Based on what was mentioned in the previous chapter regarding the criteria set by al-Shatibi for the causes (illal) or objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), he establishes a new matter and considers it a reconciliation between those who differ on the issue of justifying rulings through appropriate attributes or legal meanings, or other matters that some have considered pathways for justification.
For example: the disagreement regarding the reasoning behind the prohibition of alcohol due to intoxication, where the reasoners stated that intoxication is a reason even if it is not explicitly mentioned or understood from the discourse, based on the principle of reasoning and the appropriateness of the description. On the other hand, those who oppose this reasoning argue that it is not indicated by the Sharia nor understood from the discourse, but rather it is something that the reasoner inclines towards and imagines its correctness in his heart; thus, it is a product of the whims of the mind and not from the indications of the Sharia. Al-Shatibi came with his methodology or criteria for reasoning to say that this reasoning or this description, in itself, is not indicated by the legal discourse. Therefore, reasoning based on it is a matter of beautification and vilification. However, since it has been established that legislation was for the sake of goals, reasons, objectives (maqasid) or rulings, it has been established that it is permissible for the jurist (mujtahid) to search for the wisdom behind the prohibition of alcohol. With Al-Shatibi's condition for certainty regarding the reason or objective or that it returns to something certain, the reason determined by the jurist cannot merely be an appropriate description, nor is that a requirement at all; rather, it must be something established through induction or returning to a principle or meaning that is certainly established through that method. Accordingly, the claim of the reason of intoxication, if this meaning is not certain, has no value in the Sharia. However, since the preservation of the intellect is a meaning that the legislator intended to achieve with certainty and has become a firmly established principle in the religion, and since reasoning based on intoxication returns in its meaning to this certain principle - which is the preservation of the intellect - therefore, reasoning based on intoxication is valid.
