Based on this, what has come in legal texts such as His saying, Exalted is He: { Prohibited to you are dead animals, blood, and the flesh of swine } (1), and His saying, Exalted is He: { Indeed, intoxicants, gambling, stone altars [to other than Allah], and divining arrows are but defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it } (2), and His saying, Exalted is He: { As for the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands } (3), and His saying, Exalted is He: { The female and male adulterer, lash each one of them with a hundred lashes } (4), the prohibition of the mentioned items of dead animals, blood, swine, and intoxicants... and the prohibition of theft and adultery are among the original meanings and the original implications of the speech. As for what may be argued as a rationale for these rulings or what wisdom may be derived for them without the existence of a term indicating that or texts from which it can be inferred, such as saying that the rationale or wisdom is the harm caused by dead animals, blood, or swine, or the intoxication caused by alcohol, or that the preservation of life, preservation of intellect, preservation of wealth, and preservation of lineage is the meaning of these rulings or the intent of the Legislator behind them, this is not among the implications of the texts; if it were, it would be among the original meanings. Rather, the notion that it is intended by the Legislator is a presumption that lacks legal evidence, and it is additional to the original meanings. Can rulings be derived from it or built upon it?

There is no ruling for the secondary implication: Al-Shatibi discusses this issue, presenting the arguments of those who affirm it and the arguments of those who oppose it, concluding with the statement: "The result is that inferring rulings from the second aspect does not establish, so it is not valid to apply it at all" (5).
