As for the follower (muqallid), he has no consideration for the legal evidences (adillah shar'iyyah), and his pursuit of the intention of the Legislator (al-shari') is through weighing the opinions of the jurists (mujtahidun), as they are akin to legal evidences for him. It is not permissible for him to choose between the opinions, as this would be following whims. As for the condition of understanding the objectives (maqasid) in general and in detail, when the jurist (mujtahid) reaches it, he can issue a legal opinion (fatwa), and his statement is considered valid if he is pious, even if he does not know the Arabic language, as he can understand the objectives through reception and translation. He said: "If ijtihad pertains to deriving rulings from texts, it is necessary to condition knowledge of Arabic. However, if it pertains to meanings of benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) abstracted from the texts or accepted from the one who exercises ijtihad in the texts, then knowledge of Arabic is not required; rather, knowledge of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in general and in detail is necessary" (1). He also said: "Thus, whoever understands the objectives of the Sharia from the establishment of rulings and reaches the rank of knowledge of them, even if his understanding comes through translation in a non-Arabic tongue, there is no difference between him and one who understands them through the Arabic language" (2).

Section Two
Discussion of al-Shatibi's Idea
General Principles and Induction

The subject of this section:
After presenting the idea of objectives (maqasid) according to al-Shatibi in detail, and then summarizing its foundation and pillars in the previous section, we will discuss this foundation and these pillars in this section and the next. The ideas that will be discussed in this section are:
1 - Al-Shatibi's condition that the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) must be definitive (qat'iyyah).
2 - Induction (istiqra) and its being the only path to certainty (qat').
3 - His statement that general principles (kulliyat) are not invalidated by the opposition of particulars (juz'iyyat).
4 - The moral transmission (tawatur ma'nawi).
