The second approach: the explicit text (1). This is when a proof from the Book and the Sunnah is mentioned for the reasoning with a term that is designated for it in the language, without needing any further consideration or inference. It is of two types:

The first type: what is explicitly stated as the description being a reason for the ruling, such as stating explicitly that the reason for the ruling is such and such, or that the cause of its legislation is such and such (2), or stating explicitly what implies that according to the linguistic usage, like his saying - peace and blessings be upon him -: "I had prohibited you from storing the meat of the sacrificial animals for the sake of the drafters." (3) And like his saying - peace and blessings be upon him -: "The permission to enter is made for the sake of sight." (4) And like His saying, Exalted is He: { For that reason, We decreed upon the Children of Israel } (5). Thus, the phrase (for the sake of such and such) or (for the reason of such and such) is explicit in mentioning the reason.
