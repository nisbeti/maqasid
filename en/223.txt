His seventh proof: { Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain piety } (1). The evidence in this proof lies in { that you may attain piety }. Al-Shatibi establishes that the obligation is for specific purposes, and the fact that these purposes are intended by the legislator makes them beneficial. There is no appropriate meaning in this text to refer to. If it is said that piety is a benefit, this contradicts the essence of the idea and does not support the claim of reasoning based on benefits, because piety is obedience. This text is similar to the text: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } in its indication of this matter.
His eighth proof: { Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing } (2). Here, al-Shatibi also uses the text to establish a result or outcome of the ruling, and that this outcome is intended by the legislator, thus it is a benefit. This proof is like the previous one.
His ninth proof: { So turn your faces toward it, so that the people may have no argument against you } (3). The obligation is for the sake of achieving the result { lest it be }, so the result becomes a purpose of the obligation. There is no reason to call this purpose a benefit except because it is intended by the legislator.
His tenth proof: { Permission is given to those who are fought against because they have been wronged } (4). Al-Shatibi uses this text to establish that the ruling prescribed by Allah, which is the permission to fight, is for the purpose of lifting the oppression that has occurred against them, thus this purpose is intended by the legislator in the ruling, and therefore the permission to fight has a purpose. His next proof is similar.
His eleventh proof: { And there is for you in legal retribution [qisas] a saving of life, O you who possess intellect } (5).
