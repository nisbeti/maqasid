2 - The second section: He says: "As for the second, there is no doubt about the prohibition of intending harm in itself, as there is evidence that there is no harm and no reciprocating harm in Islam. However, the consideration remains regarding this action that combines the intention of benefiting oneself and the intention of harming others. Should it be prohibited, thus becoming impermissible, or does it remain under its original ruling of permissibility while the one who intends it bears the sin of their intention? Al-Shatibi believes that this is a matter where disagreement can be imagined and where detailed ijtihad (jurisprudential reasoning) is possible. If preventing the obligated person from acting does not prevent them from achieving their goal through another action, then there is no doubt about their prohibition, as there is no meaning in insisting on the first action except to harm others. However, if they have no alternative to the first action, their right to act takes precedence over the right of others to eliminate the harm, but they remain prohibited from the intention" (1).

3 - The third section: He states: "If the harm is general and unintended, in this case, it is considered. If preventing the action leads to harm that cannot be compensated, then their right takes precedence absolutely, and the action remains permissible. If it can be compensated, then considering the general harm is preferable, and they are prohibited from the action. He said: 'They have increased in the Mosque of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) from other than what its people approved and what they did not. This necessitates prioritizing the public interest over the private interest, but in a way that does not cause harm to the private interest'" (2).

4 - The fourth section: He says: "The right of the one who brings or repels is prioritized even if it causes harm to others, because bringing benefit or repelling harm is desired and intended by the legislator" (3). He said: "Thus, it has become clear that prioritizing the right of the one who is late over the one who is early is not intended by the Sharia except with the waiver of the right of the one who is early, and that is not obligatory for them" (4).
