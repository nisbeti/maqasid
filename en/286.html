In Islamic law, there are well-known principles such as: "Necessities permit the prohibited," "Hardship brings about facility," "When the matter becomes narrow, it expands," "Need takes the place of necessity," or others that may or may not be valid. Some contemporary scholars use such principles in their excuses, justifications, or fatwas. Should such principles be taken at face value? Is the prohibited allowed by any necessity or merely by need, and is hardship taken as evidence for leniency? No, and no one has said this. So what are the controls? This is the essence of this type of objectives of the legislator.

The principles and foundations that al-Shatibi diligently sought to verify and establish are primarily aimed at resolving issues of this kind, that is, establishing the foundations and rules that govern particulars and meanings when they are a source of doubt and confusion or of disagreement and divergent views.

It is worth noting that the principles established by al-Shatibi in this type of objectives, such as not being obliged to do what is beyond one's capacity, and not being obliged to endure extraordinary or excessive hardship, and the lifting of hardship, are principles of need (hajiyat), because they revolve around ease, alleviation, and kindness towards the obligated person, which is the definition of needs.

Obligation and ease:

It has been established in the principles that Allah, Exalted is He, does not impose on the servant what is beyond his capacity. Therefore, al-Shatibi addresses this quickly, but he discusses what follows from it. He says: "It has been established in the principles that the condition for obligation or its cause is the ability of the one obligated. What the obligated person cannot do is not validly imposed upon him legally, even if it is rationally permissible (1)... And we say: If it appears from the legislator at first glance the intention to impose what is beyond the capacity of the servant, that is actually related to its precedents or its consequences or its indicators. The saying of Allah, Exalted is He: { And do not die except while you are Muslims } (2), and His saying in the hadith: "Be the servant of Allah who is killed and do not be the servant of Allah who kills" (3), and His saying: "Do not die while you are an oppressor" (4), and what is similar to that is not required from it except what falls under the capacity, which is Islam, refraining from oppression, abstaining from killing, and submitting to the command of Allah, as well as everything else of this nature" (5).
