Al-Shawkani says: "The appropriate is either influential or non-influential. The non-influential is either suitable or unsuitable. It can be strange, transmitted, or abrogated." Irshad al-Fuhul, p. 218.

Al-Ghazali said: "The appropriate is divided into influential, suitable, and strange... The meaning of being influential is that its effect is evident in the ruling by consensus or text, so there is no need for the appropriateness" (1). Al-Amidi said: "If it is considered by a text or consensus, then it is the influential one" (2). This means that, according to al-Ghazali, the appropriateness is not a condition for the description that establishes its influence or causation by text or consensus. The reasoning based on the influential is considered one of the strongest types of analogy. Al-Ghazali said: "Analogy is of four types: the influential, then the appropriate, then the similar, then the extension. The lowest of these is the extension, and every proponent of analogy must reject it. The highest is the influential, and every denier of analogy must accept it" (3).

Al-Shawkani said: "The influential is that the text or consensus indicates that it is a cause that shows the effect of the essence of the description in the essence of the ruling or its type in its type" (4).

Thus, the influential is the description that has been established by evidence to have an effect in the ruling; its consideration is not merely a matter of the ruling coming in accordance with it or the ruling testifying to it, but rather its consideration is akin to the testimony of the text or consensus.
