And the method of the later scholars, in my view, was nothing but an influence from the followers of the jurists' method through the approach of the theologians, by relying on the principles of logic and theology in the foundational discussions. It was not a third method as much as it was a drift towards logical and theological means in debates and responses, and as much as it was attempts to encompass the various opinions that were in agreement, similar, or contradictory in comprehensive books, in eras when the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) knew no creativity except in compilation, explanation, abbreviation, annotation, arrangement, and classification. The situation remained stagnant until al-Shatibi came and examined this great legacy and made his reformist attempt to reconcile between the two methods, the method of the jurists and the method of the theologians.
The most notable difference between the two approaches appears in the discussions of the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), particularly in the topics of analogy (qiyas), reasoning (ta'leel), and the pathways of reasoning.
This difference will be clearly manifested in the first and second chapters of this book. It will also show how the idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) emerged as a result of this difference through the efforts of Imam al-Juwayni (may Allah have mercy on him), and how it subsequently became a part of the discussions in the science of Usul al-fiqh.
The idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) is based on the premise that the Sharia was established for the benefit of the servants in both realms, this world and the Hereafter. The benefits of the Hereafter are attaining the pleasure of the Merciful (Ar-Rahman), achieving bliss and gardens, and being saved from punishment and the Fire.
As for the interests of this world, they are what a person strives to attain or preserve, or to avert and protect oneself from, for the sake of their livelihood, and this is according to what Allah has created in them and determined from their characteristics and needs.
Most scholars have agreed that the interests of this world are based on five matters that serve as the foundations or necessities to which all interests return, namely the preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth. The rulings of the Sharia (Islamic law) lead to the preservation of these interests.
