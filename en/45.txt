And the method of the later scholars, in my view, was nothing but an influence from the followers of the jurists' method in the manner of the theologians, through reliance on the principles of logic and theology in the foundational discussions. It was not a third method as much as it was a drift towards logical and theological means in debates and responses, and as much as it was attempts to encompass the various opinions that are in agreement, similar, or contradictory in comprehensive books, in eras when the science of usul al-fiqh (foundational jurisprudence) knew no creativity except in compilation, explanation, abbreviation, annotation, arrangement, and categorization. The situation remained stagnant until al-Shatibi came and examined this great legacy and undertook his reformist attempt to reconcile between the two methods, the method of the jurists and the method of the theologians.
The most apparent difference between the two methods in the discussions of usul al-fiqh is in the topics of qiyas (analogy), ta'leel (reasoning), and the pathways of the cause. This difference will be clearly manifested in the first and second chapters of this book. It will also show how the idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) emerged as a result of this difference through the efforts of Imam al-Juwayni (may Allah have mercy on him), and how it later became part of the discussions in the science of usul al-fiqh.
The idea of the objectives of the Sharia is based on the premise that the Sharia was established for the sake of the interests of the servants in both abodes, this world and the Hereafter. The interests of the Hereafter are attaining the pleasure of the Merciful, achieving bliss and gardens, and being saved from punishment and fire. As for the interests of this world, they are what a person strives to obtain or preserve or to avert and protect himself from for the sake of his livelihood, according to what Allah has created in him and decreed of characteristics and needs.
Most scholars have concluded that the interests of this world return to five matters that serve as the foundations or necessities to which all interests revert: the preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth, and the rulings of the Sharia lead to the preservation of these interests.
