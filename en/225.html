This is the essence of al-Shatibi's intention behind these arguments. In this last text, we do not perceive a reasonable benefit, for al-Shatibi used this last text as evidence for the reasoning based on public interest (maslaha). The benefit is so that they do not say on the Day of Resurrection, "Indeed, we were heedless of this." The action that is reasoned is God's witnessing to people against themselves after taking them from the backs of the children of Adam. A person can do nothing but affirm and believe in this text and what it contains. While he may be able to comprehend the necessity of this affirmation, he cannot understand, with the mental capacity he has been given, how the establishment of this proof is contingent upon this witnessing. And where is the benefit for the servants in the cessation of their proof? Based on their testimony against themselves, a testimony in which they have no power or strength? Accordingly, this verse does not indicate any benefit for the servants, neither closely nor remotely. It contains no evidence in this context except that the actions of God, Exalted is He, have a purpose or intended outcome. The purpose of this action by God is to nullify the servants' proof on the Day of Resurrection.

Since al-Shatibi referred to it as a reason and called it a benefit, this means that he names the objectives of the Lawgiver (maqasid al-sharia) as reasons and benefits. Based on what has been mentioned, those who cite some texts from al-Shatibi and rely on his statements intending something other than what he intended, and establish rulings based on worldly interests, goals, and desires, citing his book or his methodology - according to their claim - have not taken the trouble to reflect and think lightly about his arguments.

If al-Shatibi's intention in reasoning based on benefits were what is considered a benefit according to sense, nature, or intellect and observation, his methodology would have been invalid from the outset, because these arguments he presented do not support this indication; rather, most of them contradict it.
