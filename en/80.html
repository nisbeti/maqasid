1- They agreed that the cause (illa) must be indicated by the Sharia. This is to be understood as coming in one of the paths of reasoning. This matter has been clarified previously.

2- The cause must be understood as a motivator and not merely as an indication. Al-Amidi said: "They differed on the permissibility of the cause in the original meaning being that of a mere indication, and the preferred view is that the cause in the original must be understood as a motivator, meaning it must include a valid wisdom that is intended by the legislator in establishing the ruling. Otherwise, if it were merely a descriptive characteristic without wisdom, but rather a mere indication, then reasoning based on it in the original is impossible" (1). This matter has been clarified earlier in explaining the meanings of the cause.

3- The cause must be effective in the ruling. Al-Zarkashi said: "In mentioning the conditions of the cause: the first is that it must be effective in the ruling; if it does not affect it, it cannot be a cause... Thus, it was mentioned by Al-Mawardi and Al-Ruyani, and what they meant by effectiveness is suitability. Better than their phrasing is the statement of the scholar Abu Mansur: that its description must be one that is valid for linking the ruling to it" (2). He also said: "Some permitted reasoning based solely on mere descriptive indications. The truth is the opposite, and it must include a ruling that motivates the obligated person to comply and serves as a witness for linking the ruling to it" (3). He said: "And the judge said in (Al-Taqrib): The meaning of the cause being effective in the ruling is that it is judged that the jurist is most likely to believe that the ruling exists when it is established because of it, rather than anything else" (4). There is significant disagreement regarding the condition of effectiveness and its meaning between the Shafi'is and the Hanafis, which I will mention in its place, if Allah wills.
