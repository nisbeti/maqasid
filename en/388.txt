He said: "If the action is contrary while being ignorant of the contradiction, there are two perspectives. The first is that the intention is in accordance, so it is not contrary from this angle. Although the action is contrary, actions are judged by intentions, and the intention of this worker is one of compliance; however, ignorance has led him to the contradiction. One who does not intend to oppose the Lawgiver outright does not fall under the category of intentional contradiction in both intention and action. Thus, his action, from this perspective, is generally considered, and there is no absolute rejection of it. The second perspective is that the action is indeed contrary, for the intention of the Lawgiver in commanding and prohibiting is compliance. If he does not comply, then he has opposed that intention, and the contradiction of intention does not negate the compliance that motivates the action because the intention of the Lawgiver was not achieved in that action in any way, nor did the intention align with the action, resulting in the whole being contrary, just as if both were contradicted together, thus compliance is not achieved" (1). He then said: "Both perspectives oppose each other in themselves and also oppose each other in terms of preference, for if you prefer one, the other presents a reason for preference, leading to mutual opposition. Therefore, this matter has become obscure in the Sharia" (2).
