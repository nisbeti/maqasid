One of the most important introductions to al-Shatibi's methodology is his third introduction, where he says: "The relied-upon intention (qasd al-awwal)(1) is the legal evidence, and the existence of certainty in it regarding the commonly accepted usage is nonexistent or extremely rare, I mean in the singular pieces of evidence. If they are from the reports of the singular (akhbar al-ahad), then their lack of providing certainty is evident. And if they are mutawatir (consecutively transmitted), then their provision of certainty is contingent upon all or most of its premises being speculative. What is contingent upon the speculative must necessarily be speculative. For it depends on the transmission of languages, the opinions of grammar, the absence of ambiguity, the absence of metaphorical usage, the legal or customary transmission, the implicitness and specification of the general, the restriction of the absolute, the absence of abrogation, the precedence and postponement, and the rational opposition. The provision of certainty while considering these matters is impossible."(2) Thus, al-Shatibi denies the existence of definitive evidence in singular texts, even if they are from the texts of the Noble Qur'an or the mutawatir hadiths. And if we understand his requirement for certainty in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), a question arises: how will this certainty be achieved according to him? He says: "The provision of certainty while considering these matters is impossible, and those who claim its existence have held that it is speculative in itself, but if it is accompanied by observable or transmitted indications, it may provide certainty, and all of this is rare or impossible. The considered evidence here is that which is extrapolated from a group of speculative evidences that converge on one meaning until it provides certainty in it, for the gathering has a strength that separation does not have, and for this reason, the mutawatir provides certainty, and this is a type of it. If a collection of evidence on the issue is obtained that provides knowledge(3), then it is the required evidence, and it is similar to the moral mutawatir, rather it is like the knowledge of the bravery of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and the existence of Hatim, derived from the abundance of transmitted incidents about them."(4)

(1) The first intention: that is, when a command or prohibition comes from the legislator. Thus, the performance of the action is the first intention of the command, and refraining from it is the first intention of the prohibition.

(2) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat. 1/14.

(3) Knowledge is beneficial: that is, it is beneficial knowledge that is certain. Knowledge is opposed to conjecture.

(4) al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat 1/14.
