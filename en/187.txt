The first text above states that particulars do not fall within the meaning of preservation intended by the noble verse, while the second text states that the branches are preserved based on the verse itself. By understanding that particulars are the basis for universals, and the meaning of returning to the certain (qat'i), the aforementioned distinction is confirmed(1).

Thus, it becomes clear that al-Shatibi does not adopt the approach of analogy (qiyas) and suitability (munasaba) in the manner that the scholars of the Shafi'i school assert; he also does not dismiss it as the scholars of the Hanafi school have done. Rather, he stipulates that the meaning or description proposed for reasoning must be related to the certain (qat'i), and without that, he does not accept it. He also does not reject it, as there may be particulars that combine with it to form a certain meaning. This is something that must be noted to understand al-Shatibi's methodology.
