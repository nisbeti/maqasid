The reason for this error is that those who confuse the cause (illah) and the objective (maqasid) divert their minds from considering the ruling and its cause to examining the method of the Legislator in legislation. They imagine that the Legislator has laws or a method for legislation upon which the rulings are based, and they attempt to discover these laws. Whether this is correct or not, the diversion of the mind to this direction and the attempt to uncover the principles upon which the Legislator legislates is an overstepping of human boundaries that involves a hidden analogy of the Creator to the created. Such a matter may be valid for humans and in rational matters, but it is not valid concerning Allah, Exalted is He. As a practical example for further clarification and distinction:

If the person responsible for organizing traffic sees the need to place signs on the roads to organize the flow and eliminate its problems, and then does so and the flow becomes organized, he may ask: What is the reason for placing the signs? The answer is: organizing the flow. He may then ask: What is the reason for the organized flow? The answer is: the signs. Thus, these two answers present a confusion; one makes organizing the flow the reason for the signs, while the other makes the signs the reason for organizing the flow. The correct understanding is that if we are considering the ruling and its reason, this corresponds here to considering the organized flow and the signs. The signs are what led to the order, and if they are removed, problems arise. They precede the organization in existence; thus, they are the cause, and it cannot be that the order is the cause.
