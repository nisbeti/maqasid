Based on this, the obligation to consider the outcomes (mālat) arises because there may be situations where an action deemed permissible is intended for a benefit that is not achieved and may lead to harm, or it may lead to both benefit and harm. Conversely, an action that is prohibited due to harm may lead to a benefit, or its abandonment may result in a greater harm than the harm it causes. Therefore, the jurist (mujtahid) must examine these occurrences and situations, and all of this relates to the realization of the relevant criteria (taḥqīq al-manāṭ), either in a general sense or in a specific sense. In the general sense, it means that the reality has circumstances and conditions that allow for different outcomes of the action, such as going to markets, schools, and attending funerals, and similar activities. These are permissible or legally desired, but these actions may lead to immoralities, such as witnessing nakedness or unlawful mixing, which transforms this reality into a new situation that requires consideration. Similarly, visiting beaches and swimming pools is originally permissible; however, if these places are frequented by naked men and women, then the outcome of this action is witnessing nakedness. Therefore, the jurist must consider the outcome of the action along with the action itself, as this required or permitted action may lead to harm. This does not mean that the action has become forbidden because it has a forbidden outcome; rather, it means that it is necessary to consider the outcome, as considering the outcome is legally required and intended just as considering the action is required and intended. The degree of this harm varies; it is different in visiting beaches frequented by naked individuals compared to public markets where some immoralities may occur or some nakedness may be exposed. Likewise, the benefits that are compared to these harms differ in degree; they are different in leisure and visiting public places and beaches compared to seeking knowledge or attending funerals, and they are different in attending Friday prayers and congregational prayers.
