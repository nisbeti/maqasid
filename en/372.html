And if it pertains to customs, it is according to intention. If the intention is lawful, then the action is valid; if the intention is unlawful, then it is invalid (1). Ibn Nujaym al-Hanafi said: "Selling juice to someone who makes it into wine is not prohibited if the intention is for trade, but if the intention is for fermentation, it is prohibited, and the same applies to planting a vineyard. Likewise, grape juice intended for fermentation or for wine. The prohibition of severing ties is threefold depending on the intention; if the intention is to sever ties with a Muslim, it is prohibited, otherwise it is not. The mourning of a woman for a deceased other than her husband is also threefold depending on the intention; if she intends to refrain from adornment and perfume for the sake of the deceased, it is prohibited for her, otherwise it is not" (2). However, even if the intention for benefit is lawful, being devoid of the intention of compliance and drawing closer to Allah renders the action devoid of reward, for there is no reward without intention.

(1) Our discussion is confined to validity and invalidity in terms of the consequences of sin or reward, and is not a ruling in this world.

(2) Ibn Nujaym al-Hanafi, al-Ashbah wa al-Nazair, p. 22.
