The Emergence of the Idea of the Objectives of the Sharia

A brief overview of the emergence of the idea:
I will present below a brief overview of the emergence of the idea, followed by a detailed explanation and documentation from the statements of the imams and their arguments, starting from where I left off in the previous chapter, namely the disagreement regarding the concept of suitability, whether it is a method of reasoning or not.
It has been clarified - previously - the meaning of suitability, and that some of the imams adopted it as a method of reasoning - standing on its own - and set conditions for it, thus categorizing suitability into sections. Some others rejected its status as a method and considered it a condition for the validity of reasoning by description through any method used. Some made it a condition in certain methods but not others.
The scholars of the Shafi'i school state that the suitable description in the ruling is one that is related to the presumption of being a reason, and presumption is applicable in legal matters; therefore, the method of suitability must be followed. The imams of the Hanafi school counter this by asserting that not all presumptions are legal, and they require that the description be influential for the reasoning to be accepted.
If we take rulings such as the prohibition of usury or the obligation of zakat on certain wealth but not others, or the prohibition of men wearing gold and silk but not women, or the prohibition of using gold and silver utensils for food, the intellect seeks suitable descriptions in these rulings or the wisdom behind them. Those who advocate the method of suitability argue for reasoning based on these rulings. Those who oppose it consider this reasoning to be arbitrary without evidence, akin to mere opinion. They require that the description be influential, meaning that its effect must be established by evidence.
Those who adopt this method have delved deeply into their research and elaborated on it in defense of it and others as well, such as the method of analogy. The debate intensified, particularly regarding the topic of the unspecific suitability or inference.

(1) The term "suitability" refers to the appropriateness of certain descriptions in legal rulings.
(2) The term "reasoning" refers to the process of deriving legal rulings based on evidence and principles.
(3) The term "usury" refers to the practice of charging excessive interest on loans, which is prohibited in Islamic law.
(4) The term "zakāt" refers to the obligatory almsgiving in Islam, one of the Five Pillars.
(5) The term "analogy" refers to the method of deriving legal rulings based on similarities between cases.
