This example is to demonstrate al-Shatibi's intended meaning regarding the ruling of the principal (matbu) and its application to the subordinate (tabi). The mentioned issue is based on the assumption of the permissibility of the contract on the person (raqabah) and its permissibility on the benefit, such that if a contract is established on one of them, the other is considered subordinate to the contracted matter, meaning that the subordinate and the principal, which are interrelated, are both permissible. Our issue is whether one of the interrelated matters is permissible and the other is prohibited. In this case, al-Shatibi also states that the ruling applies to the principal, and the subordinate is given the same ruling. He says: "When a command and a prohibition pertain to interrelated matters, and one of them is commanded and the other is prohibited in the case of separation, and one of them is in the status of a subordinate to the other in existence and non-existence, then what is considered from the two implications is what pertains to the principal, while what pertains to the subordinate is nullified and has no legal consideration" (1).

For example, the purchase of grapes may be intended for a permissible purpose, such as eating, or it may be intended for something prohibited, such as making intoxicants. The purchase of a female slave may be intended for concubinage or for service, or it may be intended for prostitution. So, which of the intended purposes is the one that is considered, and which is the principal and which is the subordinate? The intention of the obligated person is not something that can be discerned; it is a purpose that varies from person to person, and this is something that the jurist does not know. Rather, the obligated person is the jurist of himself and knows the prohibition of his action from his intended unlawful purpose. Therefore, the jurist in such cases looks at what is primarily intended according to common understanding and custom, and that is the principal, while he nullifies the consideration of the subordinate and gives the ruling based on the principal. For instance, the original intention behind the purchase of grapes, according to common understanding and custom, is not for wine; the purchase of weapons is not for highway robbery; and the purchase of a female slave is not for prostitution, thus this purchase remains permissible.
