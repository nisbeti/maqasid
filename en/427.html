The commands and prohibitions are among the discussions of the Qur'an and the Sunnah. What is meant by discussing them here is to refer to al-Shatibi's methodology in determining the meanings of texts or formulations. A formulation may appear to imply a duty, while its intended meaning is different. It may indicate a command or prohibition, but it is necessary to specify the intended meaning of the command, whether it is obligation, recommendation, or permissibility, and the intended meaning of the prohibition, whether it is prohibition or dislike. Likewise, a term may arise that requires the observer to determine its meaning, whether it is literal or metaphorical, or if it contains an implied meaning or something else. Furthermore, the rulings have objectives, so are these objectives considered in the command and prohibition? Among these indications is what was previously mentioned, especially in the discussion of the intention of the legislator in establishing the Sharia for understanding (1). And there is what will come in the discussion of the principle of blocking the means (2). What we will discuss here is al-Shatibi's general methodology in determining the meanings of the formulations of command and prohibition.

The command necessitates the performance of an action, and the prohibition necessitates refraining from an action, regardless of the intended purpose of the action or the abstention. The action or refraining from it is the primary objective of the legislator in the command and prohibition (3). He said: "The meaning of command and prohibition is the necessity of action and the necessity of abstention. The meaning of necessity is demand, and demand entails a desired outcome, with the intention of achieving that desired outcome, and there is no meaning to demand except this" (4). Until he said: "The command in this is clearer than to require evidence for it" (5).
