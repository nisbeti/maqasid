He says that the original objectives (maqasid) are divided into essential (ainiya) and sufficient (kafiya). As for the essential, it is upon every accountable person to preserve their religion, self, intellect, lineage, and wealth. As for the sufficient: "It is contingent upon others to fulfill it generally among all accountable individuals so that public affairs, which cannot be established without it, may be rectified" (1). He speaks regarding actions that involve the attainment of benefits through lawful means: "This category is divided into two: one category involves fulfilling interests directly without intermediaries, such as fulfilling one's own interests directly (2), and the other category involves fulfilling interests through the benefits of others, such as fulfilling the duties of spouses and children, and earning through what benefits others, like rents, leasing, trade, and other forms of crafts and earnings. All of these seek the individual's share, and in doing so, they serve the interests of others, creating a cycle among people, just as some parts of the human body serve one another so that the benefit is achieved for all" (3). This means that the benefits that are attained and the advantages that result from the rulings of the Sharia have outcomes that circulate among people; they are related to a general system in society and a network of relationships and public interests. Not all of this is grasped by the observer; if one perceives certain benefits or rulings, there are others that remain unrecognized. He states: "If we understand through necessity or choice an independent wisdom in the ruling of the law, it does not necessitate that there are not other wisdoms and benefits, second, third, and more. Our ultimate understanding is that we have comprehended a worldly benefit that is suitable to independently justify the ruling, and we have considered it under the ruling of legal permission, while we do not know that the benefit and ruling are limited to what has appeared. If we do not attain knowledge or conjecture from this, we cannot decisively claim that there is no benefit in the ruling except what has appeared to us, for that is a decisive claim on the unseen without evidence, which is not permissible. There may be another wisdom for which the ruling was established, and we have become from those wisdoms standing with devotion" (4)
