He says: "The seeker of his share, if he does not intend compliance in any way but seeks his share in a manner that if he could achieve it in a non-permissible way, he would take it, but he is only able to do so through the permissible way—does the initial intention exist in his case...? The answer is that it exists... because if he has no way to reach his share through the non-permissible, then his return to the permissible way is an intention towards it, and the intention towards the permissible way includes compliance with the command or acting according to the permission, which is the original first intention even if he is not consciously aware of it in detail" (1).

He says: "To act with the awareness of compliance out of necessity, like one who intends to gain pleasure from a certain woman, and when he cannot do so through fornication due to her refusal or the prohibition by her family, he contracts a marriage to reach his goal—this is also invalid in the second sense because he only returns to the ruling of compliance out of necessity and insofar as it leads to his objective, not because of the permissibility of the Sharia, even though it is not invalid in the first sense. An example of this is the zakat taken under duress, which is valid in the first sense if it absolves the obligation and clears the debt, but invalid in this second sense" (2). The meaning of the two texts above is that the act itself in marriage or zakat, there is no sin in the first and it absolves the debt in the second; the act itself is valid, while the invalidity, and perhaps the sin attached to it, is due to another act, which is his intention and purpose of disobedience, and this is from the actions of the heart.
