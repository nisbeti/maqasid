And from this, it follows that the meanings understood from the texts are those that are grasped by the general public, not just the elite. It also follows that the accepted meanings of the texts regarding doctrinal and practical obligations are what the general public, for whom the Qur'an was revealed, understands. It is not correct to require philosophical and logical premises or sciences that are only available to the elite in order to understand them. He says: "What is valid in the realm of understandings and comprehension is what is general for all Arabs, so it should not be forced beyond what they can grasp according to the words and meanings, for people in understanding and the obligation therein are not on the same level or close to one another, except that they are similar in public matters."(1)

He says: "The doctrinal and practical obligations must be such that an unlettered person can comprehend them, so that they can enter under their rule"(2). And he says: "If they were only understood by the elite, the Sharia would not be general, nor would it be suitable for the unlettered, and it has been established that it is so. Therefore, the meanings that must be known and believed in must be easy to grasp. Moreover, if they were not so, it would necessitate imposing upon the general public what they cannot bear, which is not the case, as mentioned in the fundamentals"(3).

Al-Shatibi indicates that he does not mean to allow the common people to derive rulings and establish legal truths, but rather that the plausible meanings of the texts, the valid ones, are those that follow that course. The one who engages in deriving and establishing meanings and legal truths is the qualified mujtahid (jurist) for that purpose. This matter will be elaborated upon in the discussion of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning).
