One of the implications of this is that the meanings derived from the texts are those understood by the general public, not just the elite. It also follows that the accepted meanings of the texts regarding doctrinal and practical obligations are what the general populace, for whom the Qur'an was revealed, can comprehend. It is not correct to require philosophical or logical premises or sciences that are only accessible to the elite in order to understand them. He says: "What is valid in the realm of understanding and comprehension is what is general to all Arabs, and one should not overexert themselves beyond what they can grasp in terms of words and meanings, for people in understanding and the obligation therein are not of the same measure or proximity, except that they converge in public matters" (1). He also states: "The doctrinal and practical obligations must be such that even an unlettered person can comprehend them, allowing them to fall under their ruling" (2). He further states: "If they were such that only the elite could grasp them, then the Sharia would not be general, nor would it be for the unlettered, and it has been established as such. Therefore, the meanings that must be known and believed in must be easily accessible; otherwise, it would necessitate imposing what is beyond the capacity of the general public, which is not the case, as mentioned in the fundamentals" (3).

Al-Shatibi indicates that he does not intend to allow the common people to derive rulings and establish legal truths, but rather that the acceptable potential meanings of the texts are those that follow this line. The one who engages in deriving rulings and establishing meanings and legal truths is the qualified mujtahid. This matter will be elaborated upon in the discussion of ijtihad.
