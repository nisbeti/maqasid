Then he said: "The second is what the Sharia has witnessed to reject; there is no way to accept it since the analogy does not necessitate the ruling for itself, but rather this is the view of the proponents of rational improvement. Rather, if the meaning becomes apparent and we understand from the Sharia that it is to be considered in necessitating rulings, then we accept it. For what we mean by public interest (maslaha) is what is understood to be taken into account in relation to people by bringing about benefits and repelling harms in a manner that reason cannot independently grasp. Therefore, if the Sharia does not witness the consideration of that meaning, but rather rejects it, it is rejected by the consensus of Muslims" (1). This text indicates that analogy is neither a proof of consideration nor a condition for it; rather, the proof of that is the Sharia's consideration of the meaning, which is understood through induction. This category here is the canceled analogy, so it has no consideration. Among the aberrations of this time, and among the tyranny of ignorance, are the statements and opinions that some consider fatwas, which permit prohibitions under the pretext that they are interests or aimed at interests, attributing this to the unregulated interests (masalih mursala) and to the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and sometimes to al-Shatibi. How astonishing!
Then he said: "The third: what the specific evidences have remained silent about, neither witnessing its consideration nor its cancellation; this has two aspects: one of them is that a text is reported in accordance with that meaning, such as the reasoning for prohibiting killing for inheritance. The application of the opposite of the intended purpose is to assume that if a text is not reported in accordance with it, then this reasoning has no precedent in the actions of the Sharia by assumption nor in its compatible matters, such that there exists a recognized type for it; thus, it is not valid to reason with it, nor to base a ruling upon it by consensus. Such a thing is legislation by the one who claims it, so it cannot be accepted" (2).
