What has been reported from the Hanafis is that it is a preference for the narration (athar), consensus (ijma), or necessity (darura), and here it states the preference for the unqualified reasoning (istidlal al-mursal), which includes these things and others, as it encompasses every interest (maslaha) or meaning that returns to a general meaning through induction (istiqra). The general meanings in the outcomes of actions do not fall outside of necessities (darurat), needs (hajiyat), enhancements (tahsinat), and the complements of each. However, in istihsan (juridical preference) as in the unqualified interests, there is no effect of the interests or particulars that return to enhancements or their complements. It states: "This (i.e., istihsan or the preference for unqualified reasoning over analogy) often coincides with the necessary in relation to the needy, and the needy with the complementary, so that applying analogy absolutely in the necessary leads to hardship and difficulty in some of its instances, thus the place of hardship is exempted, as well as in the needy with the complementary, or the necessary with the complementary, which is evident" (1). Al-Shatibi has provided numerous examples of istihsan in his two books, al-Muwafaqat and al-I'tisam (2). He said: "There are many examples in the Sharia, such as the loan (qard), for it is usury (riba) in its essence because it is a dirham for a dirham for a term, but it has been permitted due to the convenience and expansion it provides to the needy, such that if it remained on the original prohibition, it would cause hardship for the obligated" (3). Thus, a dirham for a dirham for a term is forbidden in sale because it is one of the six usurious categories, and the generality of this ruling necessitates the prohibition of lending a dirham. The prohibition of usury relates to the preservation of wealth from necessities, while the permissibility of the loan relates to needs because needs revolve around expansion and facilitation. Therefore, this particularity, which is lending a dirham, did not follow the prohibition of usury in the necessary but followed the permissibility in the needy, and this is istihsan. If we understand that establishing whether it is from the necessities or from the needs returns to the induction of the texts of the Sharia and their outcomes, we realize that the process of specification or clarification here does not return to mere desire but to the Sharia, and likewise in other examples.
