This approach establishes the cause by negating what is besides it, rather than by examining it.

Al-Zarkashi said: "It is linguistically the act of testing, and from it is the probe that is used to test the wound, which is called the 'mismar'. It is named this way because the observer of the ailment divides the attributes and tests each one of them to see whether it is suitable for the cause or not?"(1).

Those who assert that the basis of rulings is the reasoning behind them differ on the validity of this approach. As for those who reject this view, they do not adopt this method. Al-Sarakhsi said: "The evidence that the existence of a proof of invalidity in other attributes does not establish the validity of the attribute claimed by the reasoner in legal matters is that there are some rulings in the Sharia that are not based on reasoning at all. Thus, the existence of evidence for the invalidity of other attributes does not negate the possibility of evidence for the invalidity of this attribute"(2).

Al-Zarkashi said: "Ibn al-Hajib transmitted in his discussion on the division and classification the consensus of the jurists that a ruling must have a reason."(3)

The fifth approach: analogy and consideration. This is also based on the principle that the original basis for rulings is reasoning. This approach and its details, especially the meaning of consideration (i'tibar) and its types, have a fundamental relationship with the concept of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi. Therefore, it will have - if Allah wills - a separate discussion.
