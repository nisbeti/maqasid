This approach establishes the cause by negating everything else, rather than by examining it. Al-Zarkashi said: "It is, in language, the method of testing, and from it is the inclination used to test the wound, which is called the probe. It is named this way because the one examining the cause divides the attributes and tests each one to see if it is suitable for causation or not?"(1).

There has been a disagreement among those who assert that the original principle in rulings is the justification regarding the validity of this approach. As for those who reject this view, they do not accept this method. Al-Sarakhsi said: "The evidence that the establishment of evidence for the invalidity of other attributes does not confirm the validity of the attribute claimed by the one providing justification in legal matters is that some rulings of the Sharia are not justified at all. Thus, the establishment of evidence for the invalidity of other attributes does not eliminate the possibility of establishing evidence for the invalidity of this attribute"(2).

Al-Zarkashi said: "Ibn al-Hajib reported the consensus of the jurists regarding sifting and division that a ruling must have a cause"(3).

The fifth approach: suitability and analogy. This is also based on the principle that the original principle in rulings is justification. This approach and its details, especially the meaning of consideration and its types, have a fundamental relationship with the idea of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi; therefore, it will have - if Allah wills - a separate discussion.
