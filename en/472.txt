Thus, all branches of Sharia (Islamic law) return to general principles that are all found in the Qur'an, and these general principles were revealed earlier, just as these principles refer in their meanings to fundamental origins, and so on, until the Sharia returns to a single principle. These general principles are established through the many aspects that support them, as the following text shows: It states, "The majority of the Meccan (surahs) are established for three meanings, the root of which is one meaning, which is the call to the worship of Allah, Exalted is He. The first is the affirmation of the oneness of the one true God, although it comes in various forms, such as the negation of partners in general, or the negation of what the disbelievers claimed about it being a means of drawing closer to Allah or that He has taken a son, or other types of false claims. The second is the affirmation of the prophethood of the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) and that he is the Messenger of Allah to them all, truthful in what he has brought from Allah, although it also comes in various forms, such as affirming that he is truly a Messenger and refuting what they claimed against him, that he is a liar, a magician, or mad, or that he is taught by a human, or similar accusations from their disbelief and obstinacy. The third is the affirmation of the resurrection and the Hereafter, and that it is true without a doubt, with clear evidence and a refutation of those who deny it in every way that the disbeliever can deny it, responding in every way that establishes the proof and silences the opponent and clarifies the matter. These three meanings are what the Qur'an revealed in Mecca in general, and whatever seems at first glance to deviate from them should be referred back to them in the essence of the matter" (1). Due to the importance of this text - above - in his methodology, I have quoted it in full, and its meaning is spread throughout many places in his book, and he repeats himself in another place, saying: "This is because it (2) contains knowledge of three types, which are the primary objectives: the first is knowledge of the one to whom one is directed, the second is knowledge of how to direct oneself to Him.
