This introduction does not aim to explain and clarify these foundational terms, which will be addressed in full in this chapter and the next. Rather, its purpose is to indicate that in order to fully grasp the concept of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi, one must understand the emergence of the concept of objectives among the usuliyyin (foundational scholars) who preceded him, and one must also be familiar with the meanings of some terms and foundational concepts related to analogy (qiyas) and its discussions. While some of these terms may require only a basic understanding, others necessitate deeper exploration and insight.

For this reason, this chapter serves as an introduction to the concept of objectives in general, and subsequently to the concept of objectives according to al-Shatibi in particular. Therefore, the discussions in this chapter will introduce analogy, its cause, its pillars, its conditions, and its pathways. It will elaborate on the pathway of suitability, and on the meaning of the alignment of the ruling with public interest (maslaha) or description, which is consideration (i'tibar) and its categories. It will also clarify the meaning of inference (istidlal) and the meaning of unregulated public interest (maslaha mursala). Throughout and after this, the differences among the usuliyyin on these issues will be presented, which will reinforce what has been mentioned earlier and illustrate how the concept of objectives emerged, how al-Shatibi's idea of objectives was new and different from those that preceded it.

The first section
Analogy

The concept of analogy and its discussions:
Analogy in language means estimation. It is said that one measures something by another if he estimates it by it, such as estimating distance in meters when measuring it, or weight in pounds when measuring it. It is stated in Lisan al-Arab: "To measure something by something means to estimate it by its example" (1), and it is said: "And to measure something means to estimate it; it is a linguistic variation of measuring" (2), and it is said: "You compared between two things if you measured between them" (3).
