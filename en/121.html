1 - The inclusion of the unspoken matter with that which is explicitly stated and the differing with the agreed upon due to its similarity in meaning(1).

2 - Or the suspension of a ruling in a manner that is suggestive of it, appropriate to it in the context of the Sharia, while referring it back to an original where the ruling is established according to consideration.

3 - And linking a ruling as we have mentioned(2), without the observer finding an original with an agreed ruling that testifies to it, and this is what is called: inference (istidlal).

4 - And likening something to specific similarities that it encompasses without the obligation of it being suggestive of appropriateness, which is called analogy of resemblance (qiyas al-shabah)(3). These are the avenues of consideration in the Sharia"(4).

The focus here is on the second and third categories, where the second refers to the path of appropriateness, stating: "while referring it back to an original where the ruling is established according to consideration," while the third states: "without the observer finding an original with an agreed ruling that testifies to it," and it is termed inference. Although inference is not analogy since it does not fulfill all its conditions, there is a closeness between them in that inference is linking a ruling in a manner according to the statement of al-Juwayni above; therefore, it is included in the categories of analogy, just as the first category is counted among the categories of analogy due to the inclusion of the unspoken matter with that which is explicitly stated.

(1) This category does not hold up to being called analogy, as there is no analogy of a branch to an original; rather, the unspoken matter takes the ruling of the stated one by the indication of the word, and it is not analogy. This is akin to the concept: what is understood from the word in a place other than the place of utterance. See: al-Amidi, al-Ihkam 3/63. This is like His saying, Exalted is He: { So do not say to them [even] "uff" } [al-Isra 23], thus hitting is prohibited because it is more deserving of prohibition than expressing annoyance towards them, this is from the concept because it is understood from the address and is not from the utterance of the address. This is not an analogy of hitting to annoyance, rather it is more deserving of the ruling than annoyance, and it is from the indications of language. See: al-Amidi, al-Ihkam 3/64.

(2) As we have mentioned: that is, as we mentioned in the previous point, meaning: in a manner suggestive of it appropriate to it in the context of the Sharia, but it lacks the original.

(3) We mentioned this category in the discussion of the paths of reasoning. pp. 65 - 66.
