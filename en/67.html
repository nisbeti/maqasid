Since the basis of the ruling is the reality or the subject to which the ruling pertains, and since the rulings are reasoned - according to those who say that reasoning is the original basis for rulings - there must be a description in the subject that is suitable for reasoning, and for this reason the ruling was established. Other descriptions in the subject have no effect on the ruling. An example of this is the prohibition of alcohol. The fact that something is alcohol is the basis for its prohibition. Considering that the original basis for rulings is reasoning, there must be a reason for the ruling that is one of the descriptions of alcohol, meaning it is a description in the basis. If we look at descriptions such as liquidity, color, or smell, we find that they are not suitable for prohibition. However, if we consider the description of intoxication, we find it suitable for prohibition, thus it qualifies as a reason - according to them.

The process of proving that other descriptions have no effect on the ruling is called refining the basis (tanqih al-manaat). This is the meaning of al-Amidi's text above. Al-Asnawi said: "Refining the basis means extracting what the legislator has linked the ruling to, that is, associating it with it and making it the reason" (1).

Establishing the basis (takhrij al-manaat):

Al-Amidi said: "As for establishing the basis, it is the consideration and reasoning in proving the reason for the ruling indicated by the text or consensus without addressing its reason at all, such as reasoning in proving that the intoxicating strength is the reason for the prohibition of alcoholic drinks" (2). Al-Zarkashi said: "As for establishing the basis, it is the reasoning in extracting the reason for the ruling indicated by the text or consensus without addressing its reason at all" (3). It is a process of proving or identifying the description present in the basis of the ruling, which is the reason for the ruling. This is different from refining, which is proving the unsuitability of the descriptions for reasoning so that only one description remains, thus it becomes the reason without proving it, but based on the premise that the original reasoning is by description, and there is no other than it, so it becomes the reason.
