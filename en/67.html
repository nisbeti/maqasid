Since the basis of the ruling is the reality or the subject to which the ruling pertains, and since the rulings are reasoned—according to those who say that reasoning is the original basis for rulings—there must be a description in the subject that is suitable for reasoning, and for this reason the ruling was established. The other descriptions in the subject have no effect on the ruling. An example of this is the prohibition of alcohol. The fact that something is alcohol is the basis for its prohibition. Considering that reasoning is the original basis for rulings, there must be a reason for the ruling that is one of the descriptions of alcohol, meaning it is a description in the basis. If we look at descriptions such as liquidity, color, or smell, we find that they are not suitable for prohibition. However, if we consider the description of intoxication, we find it suitable for prohibition, making it a valid reason—according to them—.

The process of establishing that other attributes have no effect on the ruling is called the refinement of the legal basis (tanqih al-manat). This is the meaning of what al-Amidi stated above. Al-Asnawi said: "The refinement of the legal basis means clarifying what the legislator has linked the ruling to, that is, what he has associated it with and made it contingent upon, which is the cause."(1)

The extraction of the legal ruling:

Al-Amidi said: "As for the extraction of the legal ruling (takhrij al-manat), it is the consideration and ijtihad in establishing the reason for the ruling indicated by the text or consensus without its cause. This is similar to the ijtihad in establishing that the intoxicating severity is the reason for the prohibition of alcoholic beverages."(2)

Al-Zarkashi said: "As for the extraction of the legal ruling (takhrij al-manat), it is the juristic effort (ijtihad) in deriving the reason (illah) for the ruling indicated by the text or consensus without addressing its reason at all"(3). It is a process of establishing or identifying the description present in the legal ruling's basis, which is the reason for the ruling. This is unlike refinement (tanqih), which is the establishment of the unsuitability of descriptions for reasoning, so that only one description remains, thus becoming the reason without establishing it, but based on the premise that the original reasoning is through a description, and there is none other than it, so it becomes the reason.
