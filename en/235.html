As for necessities, he says: "The foundations of acts of worship are related to the preservation of religion in terms of existence, such as faith, the utterance of the two testimonies, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, and similar matters. Customs are related to the preservation of the self and intellect in terms of existence as well, such as consuming food and drink, clothing, and housing, and similar matters. Transactions are related to the preservation of lineage and wealth in terms of existence... and crimes, which are encompassed by the command to enjoin good and forbid evil, relate to the preservation of all in terms of non-existence" (1).

Regarding needs, he says: "They pertain to acts of worship, customs, transactions, and crimes. In acts of worship, they are like the concessions that ease the burden of illness and travel (2). In customs, they include the permissibility of hunting and enjoying permissible delights in food, drink, clothing, housing, transportation, and similar matters. In transactions, they include partnerships, agricultural contracts, and forward sales... In crimes, they include rulings on blood money, compensation, oaths, and the liability of craftsmen, and similar matters" (3).

(1) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat, 2/4.

(2) It was clarified in the previous chapter that concessions are partial and do not relate to general principles, and here he brings them as an example of needs, which are general objectives. The explanation is that some legal concessions do not consider the existence of hardship or difficulty, such as the travel of one who does not experience hardship. Some concessions, however, do show hardship; those that display undue hardship are classified as needs not because of the existence of legal concessions, but because the absence of obligation to endure undue hardships, whether there is a concession or not, is among the foundational needs according to al-Shatibi. Therefore, he did not say "like the easing concessions," but rather qualified it by saying: "in relation to the occurrence of hardship due to illness or travel."

(3) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat, 2/5.
