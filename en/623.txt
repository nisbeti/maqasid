And { a mother should not be harmed by her child } (1) is the verse, and from it comes the prohibition against transgressing upon souls, properties, and honor, as well as against usurpation and injustice, and everything that is in the meaning of causing harm or injury. This includes offenses against the soul, intellect, lineage, or wealth, which is a meaning that is extremely general in the Sharia, with no dispute or doubt about it. And when you consider the reports of the singular narrations (akhbar al-ahad), you will find them to be so (2). Thus, the intention of the Legislator to eliminate harm is definitive in the Sharia, and this hadith relates its meaning to this definitive meaning; therefore, it is accepted. This is also the case for all reports of singular narrations according to his statement above.
Indications of rulings:
__________
(1) Surah Al-Baqarah, 233.
(2) Al-Shatibi, Al-Muwafaqat, 3/8.
