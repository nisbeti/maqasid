The other definitions in various books of usul (principles of jurisprudence) do not deviate from what has been mentioned above.

It is noted about these definitions that their fundamental pillar is that it must logically follow from linking the ruling to the description that a benefit is achieved or a harm is averted. Some have added, particularly the scholars of usul from the Shafi'i school, that the attainment of benefit or the averting of harm must be something that is suitable to be intended by the legislator. This excludes the possibility that the suitable is purely a rational benefit, meaning a benefit that is not supported by a legal ruling, in addition to the requirement that it must be free from invalidators.

As for the definitions that restrict the suitable description to being apparent and precise, this is for the sake of its suitability for reasoning, as one of the conditions of a reason is that it must be an apparent and precise description, regardless of the method by which it is derived.

Categories of suitability:

The suitable description is divided according to the strength and weakness of the suitability and according to its consideration or lack thereof. As for the strength and weakness of the suitability, it is divided into two types: imaginary persuasive and real rational. The first is the description that, the more one examines it, the weaker it becomes, leading to its suitability being dismissed. The second is the description that, the more one researches it, the stronger and clearer it becomes, and it has three levels: necessities, needs, and embellishments. Each of these levels has its complements(1).

From the perspective of consideration and lack thereof, the suitable is divided into considered, unconsidered, and abrogated. Al-Shawkani said: "Know that the suitable is divided according to the testimony of the Sharia to its compatibility and effect, or lack thereof, into three categories: either it is known that the Sharia has considered it, or it is known that it has abrogated it, or neither is known"(2).

1- Considered:

The term "considered" is used in relation to two meanings: the first is the description or meaning that is indicated by a legal evidence; this description or meaning is supported by a text or legal evidence, thus it is considered. This is agreed upon for reasoning by the analogists.
