Thus, the characteristic of universality has been attributed to the permissible (mubah) in order to preserve the foundation of the idea of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), so that interests are intended in the permissible even if both sides are equal.

After illustrating the universality and particularity in the permissible, al-Shatibi also establishes that what is recommended in the particular is obligatory in the universal, and what is disliked in the particular is prohibited in the universal, and what is obligatory in the particular is obligatory in the universal. The same applies to prohibitions. He then states: "If the illustration of universality and particularity in the five rulings is established, then evidence may be sought for its validity, and the matter is clear upon reflection on what has preceded in the course of the report; rather, it is, in the consideration of the Sharia, at the level of certainty for whoever examines the Sharia in its contexts" (1). He then said: "It is established in these matters that the considered interests are the universals and not the particulars" (2).

However, the observer of this illustration of universality and particularity in rulings may object to what has been mentioned regarding the permissible in the third and fourth sections (3). The permissible in the particular that is required to be abandoned in the universal, as the action that is required to be abandoned in the universal serves to undermine the universals; how can it then be considered optional, or equal on both sides? This is another issue that al-Shatibi investigates; how does he address it?
