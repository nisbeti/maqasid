The evidence of al-Shatibi on this matter is his examination of the narratives that appeared in the Qur'an and his thorough investigation of them, both those that were responded to and those that were not (1), upon which he based his aforementioned principle. He then said: "Due to the consistency of this principle, the scholars have relied on it, as a group of usul scholars have argued that disbelievers are addressed with the branches of the Sharia based on His saying, { They said, 'We were not of those who prayed (43) and we did not feed the poor.' } (2), for if their statement were false, it would have been refuted when narrated" (3). Among the interesting points he mentioned based on this principle is his argument that the companions of the cave were seven and their eighth was their dog. He said: "He argued that the companions of the cave were seven and their eighth was their dog because when Allah narrated their statement that they were three, with their dog being the fourth, and that they were five, with their dog being the sixth, He followed that with the statement: { A guess by mere conjecture } (4), meaning they had no evidence or knowledge other than following conjecture, and conjecture does not avail against the truth at all. And when He narrated their statement: 'Seven and their eighth is their dog,' He did not follow it with a refutation but rather said: { Say, 'My Lord knows best their number; none knows them except a few.' } (5), indicating the correctness of this statement over the first two. It has been narrated from Ibn Abbas that he used to say: 'I am among that few who know them' " (6).
