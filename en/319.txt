He says: "The answer is that the establishment of the Sharia (law) is, if it is accepted that it is for the interests of the servants, returning to them according to the command of the Lawgiver and to the extent that He has defined, not according to their whims and desires. Hence, the legal obligations are burdensome on the souls... The commands and prohibitions remove a person from the inclinations of his nature and the pursuit of his desires until he takes them from beneath the legal limit, and this is precisely the intended meaning, which is the very essence of opposing whims and desires. As for the fact that the interests of the obligation return to the obligated person in this world and the Hereafter, this is correct, and it does not necessitate that attaining them is outside the bounds of the Sharia, nor that he can attain them by himself without the Sharia providing them to him, which is evident. This clarifies that there is no contradiction between this statement and what has preceded, because what has preceded looked at the establishment of interest and desire as affirmed by the Lawgiver, not as dictated by whim and desire." (1)
What is noticeable in this text, and in the entire book of al-Muwafaqat, is that al-Shatibi's intended meaning by the term "considered interest" (mashlaha) in the Sharia is different from the commonly understood meaning among others. The commonly understood meaning uses other terms such as interest, desire, benefit, and whim.
