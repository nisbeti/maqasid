After clarifying that urging people towards moderation is the highest degree in ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), he said: "If it is established that urging towards moderation aligns with the intent of the Lawgiver and is what the righteous predecessors adhered to, then the follower should consider which school of thought is most consistent with this path; it is more deserving of adherence and consideration. Although all schools of thought are paths to Allah, preference among them is necessary because it is further from following whims, as previously mentioned, and closer to discerning the intent of the Lawgiver in matters of ijtihad" (1).
As for the fourth description, which is the fear of issuing fatwas (legal opinions), some renowned scholars have mentioned this, specifically Imam Malik ibn Anas (may Allah have mercy on him). They noted their fear of fatwas or of making mistakes in them. Among these characteristics is their frequent saying: "I do not know," and the change in the mujtahid's (jurist's) color when a question is posed to him, and that before answering, he seems as if he is standing between Paradise and Hell. Additionally, he may defer the questioner and postpone the answer until he reflects on it, gather knowledgeable individuals for consultation, and be cautious of his opinion if it appears erroneous or contradicts a legal text. He may also regret if he issues many fatwas, among other things. Regarding this matter, it was reported from Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) that he said: "I have pondered a matter for over a decade, and I have yet to reach a conclusion on it." He also said: "Sometimes a question comes to me, and I think about it for nights." When he was asked about a matter, he would tell the questioner: "Go away until I reflect on it," and the questioner would leave and ponder it. When this was mentioned to him, he wept and said: "I fear that I will have questions on the Day of Judgment, and what a day that will be." When he would sit, he would lower his head and move his lips in remembrance of Allah, not turning right or left. If he was asked about a matter, his color would change; he would turn red, then pale, lower his head, and move his lips, then say: "What Allah wills; there is no power and no strength except with Allah." He might be asked about fifty matters and not answer a single one. He would say: "Whoever wishes to answer a question should consider his own fate before answering, in terms of Paradise and Hell, and how he will be saved in the Hereafter, then he should answer" (2).
