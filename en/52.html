The discussions on qiyas (analogy) branch out in the foundational books of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) to establish it as a principle and source of legislation, then to respond to its deniers and refute their arguments and doubts, followed by defining it, categorizing it, and clarifying its pillars, with detailed research on each pillar regarding its conditions and the validity of its consideration. Among its most important discussions are the cause (illah), its conditions, its pathways, its proof, and the objections raised against it. Al-Juwayni (1) says: "Qiyas is the basis of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and the origin of opinion, from which jurisprudence and the methods of Sharia branch out... Whoever understands its sources, categories, valid and invalid forms, what objections are acceptable and what invalidates them, and comprehends their ranks in clarity and obscurity, and knows their pathways and locations has encompassed the essentials of jurisprudence" (2).

Qiyas is categorized based on its clarity or obscurity, or based on the type of its cause in terms of its evidence or pathway, whether it is a text, a hint, or an inference.

Its authority:

Qiyas is considered one of the agreed-upon sources of legislation, although some have denied it. Al-Zarkashi said: "The author of al-Qawati' stated: All the imams from the Companions and the Followers, as well as the majority of jurists and theologians, have agreed that legal qiyas is a fundamental principle of Sharia, used to derive rulings for which there is no textual evidence" (3).
