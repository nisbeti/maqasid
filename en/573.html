This silence is not silence in the sense of not clarifying; rather, it is like the silence regarding the ruling on computers, the use of weapons of mass destruction, cars, or cloning. The clarification of these matters is not like the clarification of the ruling on carrion, jihad, marriage, or adultery, as they did not exist during the time of the Prophethood. However, their clarification exists in the Sharia, through the general texts and descriptions and reasons it has brought. This is the meaning of the silence mentioned here. As for the second category of what is left unaddressed, it states: "The second is that it is left unaddressed while the reason for it is present, and no ruling has been established regarding it at the time of the incident, beyond what was in that era. This type of silence is like a text indicating that the intention of the Legislator is not to add to it or reduce from it, because when this reason necessitating the establishment of a practical ruling is present and yet the ruling is not legislated in reference to it, it is explicit that any addition to what was there is an innovation (bid'a) and contrary to what the Legislator intended, as it is understood from His intention to remain at what was defined there, neither adding to it nor diminishing from it" (1). Therefore, al-Shatibi completely refutes the claim that public interest (maslaha) is a proof for the ruling of an action. He also counters what might be expected to be said, that public interest is the arbitration of interests in the areas where the Legislator has remained silent about clarifying them. He presents the rejected statement: "What is left unaddressed by the Legislator does not imply a contradiction nor does it indicate a specific intention from the Legislator without its opposite and contrary. If that is the case, we return to examining the aspects of interests; whatever we find to be beneficial, we accept it in the application of public interests, and whatever we find to be harmful, we abandon it in the application of public interests. What we do not find in this regard, either way, is like all other permissible matters in the application of public interests as well" (2).
