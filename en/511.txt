As for the error that arises from a valid ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), he said: "The error in ijtihad may occur either due to the obscurity of some evidence, leading to misconceptions about what was not intended, or due to a complete lack of awareness of it" (1). He warned against it, citing the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "I fear for my nation after me from three actions." They asked, "What are they, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "I fear for them from the slip of a scholar, from an unjust ruler, and from a following of desires" (2). He also said: "And from Ibn Abbas: Woe to the followers from the missteps of the scholar. It was said: How is that? He said: A scholar may say something based on his opinion, then finds someone who is more knowledgeable about the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) than he is, and he abandons his statement, and then the followers proceed" (3). After clarifying the necessity of being cautious about this, he said: "This is most prevalent when there is negligence regarding the consideration of the objectives of the Lawgiver (Shari) in that matter in which he has exerted effort, and stopping short of the utmost diligence in researching the texts concerning it. Although it may not be intentional and the one who commits it is excused and rewarded, it nonetheless carries a great danger in terms of following his statement" (4). Based on this, he said: "The slip of a scholar cannot be relied upon from any angle, nor can it be taken as a tradition from him, because it is based on a contradiction to the Sharia" (5).
