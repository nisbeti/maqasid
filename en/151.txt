Thus, the establishment of the principle of causation (ta'leel) by this group was only through theological discussions about the essence of God. Therefore, we see another group that decisively rejects this principle, even though it accepts analogy (qiyas), causation, inference, and the method of suitability. Among the most famous of these is Fakhr al-Din al-Razi. He said: "The suitability implies the presumption of causation, and presumption is (3) acted upon"(1). He also said: "It has been established that suitability implies causation while being certain that the actions of Allah, Exalted is He, are not subject to causation"(2).
Al-Razi attempted to prove the reasoning through the method of suitability, mentioning two approaches in this regard. The first is the method or the evidence presented by al-Amidi. He mentioned it and then returned to it to refute it. As for the second, he affirmed it and supported it.
He said: "The relationship (al-munasaba) indicates the presumption of causality, and this presumption must be acted upon. The first premise(3) is established from two angles: the first(4): that Allah - Exalted is He - legislated the rulings for the benefit of the servants, and this is a benefit, thus there arises a presumption that Allah - Exalted is He - legislated it for this benefit. These are three premises that must be proven with evidence. As for the first premise, its evidence is established through various angles"(5). What is meant by his statement: "As for the first premise" is his statement that Allah - Exalted is He - legislated the rulings for the benefit of the servants, which refers to the essence of causation. He then presented six angles to prove this premise, which do not differ from what al-Amidi presented, but in a more detailed manner(6).
