After that, al-Amidi began to respond to the objections that arise against this approach in reasoning and to the issues he presented within that context, particularly his statement that Allah does not legislate except for a purpose and an objective. Then, assuming the acceptance that Allah does not legislate except for a purpose and an objective, how can it be established that this presumed wisdom of the jurist is indeed the same as the intended objective of the legislator in the ruling? He said: "If it is said: We do not concede that the legislation of rulings necessitates the wisdom and the objectives, because the legislation of rulings is the work of Allah, Exalted is He, and His work either necessitates wisdom and objectives, or it does not. The first is impossible for seventeen reasons" (1). He then presented the objections to the necessity of rulings for wisdom and objectives and refuted them one by one (2). The debate on these issues is entirely outside the framework of the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) and falls within the realm of logic and discourse. Below are examples of these objections and the responses to them:
The first objection as presented by al-Amidi: "There are two positions regarding the actions of servants: one position states that the actions of servants are created by Allah, Exalted is He, and the other position states that they are created by the servants themselves. Whoever claims that they are created by Allah, Exalted is He, must accept that He is the creator of disbelief, sins, and all kinds of evils, while there is no wisdom or purpose in creating these things. And whoever claims that they are created by the servants, then they are created for them through Allah's creation of the ability for them to do so, and His creation of the ability that necessitates these matters is also not for a wisdom" (3).
The second objection: "If His action necessitated wisdom, then why did He cause the deaths of the prophets and grant respite to Iblis, and why did He necessitate the eternal punishment of the people of Hell in the Fire, due to the lack of wisdom in that?" (4).
