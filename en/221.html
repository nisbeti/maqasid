This text was brought by al-Shatibi here because it states a purpose or result of creation, which is {to test you which of you is best in deed}. This is added to his other proofs for the purpose of establishing induction. The common or shared meaning that these texts converge upon is the indication of a purpose or result of action. The inference from this text regarding causation serves as evidence for al-Shatibi's designation of the intended purpose or result as a cause. If we consider what al-Shatibi calls a cause here, we would recognize the difference between it and the cause of analogy (qiyas). The test (ibtila) is not a cause of analogy. There is no ruling in this text that can be applied to a specific incident by analogy based on the commonality of the test. All that these texts, which al-Shatibi uses as evidence, affirm is the existence of purposes for actions.

As for the fact that this purpose is a public interest (maslaha), there is no dispute that it is inappropriate to say regarding the actions, commands, and prohibitions of Allah, Exalted is He, anything other than that they are public interest, truth, and justice, and not merely what a servant perceives as a public interest or a suitable description or wisdom in the eyes of the wise and discerning. In fact, the matter seems to be quite the opposite, as this text indicates the control of the Creator over His creations. Where is the public interest that a person feels or that the mind perceives in creating a human to be tested, either towards Paradise or towards Hell? Before a person is created, how can they comprehend or understand the assertion that their creation and testing is a public interest for them? This does not reveal any aspect of public interest, and this confirms that al-Shatibi uses the term public interest for what has been established as intended by the legislator, even if it is not rationally comprehensible.

__________

(1) The phrase "to test you which of you is best in deed" is from the Qur'an, Surah Al-Mulk (67:2).

(2) The term "public interest" (maslaha) refers to the benefits that serve the welfare of the community in Islamic jurisprudence.

(3) The term "causation" refers to the reasoning behind legal rulings in Islamic law.

(4) The term "analogy" (qiyas) is a method of deriving legal rulings based on established precedents.

(5) The term "legislator" refers to Allah, Exalted is He, as the source of Islamic law.
