He says regarding the change of rulings under the pretext of public interest (maslaha) or objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia): "If they (i.e., the interests) were established in such a way that their system could be disrupted or their rulings could be invalidated, then legislation would not be based on them (1). For their being interests in that case is no more valid than their being evils, but the Legislator intends for them to be interests in absolute terms, so their establishment must be in that manner eternally, comprehensively, and generally across all types of obligations and the obligated, and in all circumstances, and thus we have found the matter in it, and praise be to Allah" (2).

Interpretation of some texts of al-Shatibi: Many contemporary scholars have approached the texts of the Muwafaqat without being aware of the true meanings, attributing to al-Shatibi opinions that he does not hold, but rather refutes and clarifies their invalidity. In reality, many of al-Shatibi's texts, if not viewed based on the premises he established and the principles he set forth, and in a manner where the subsequent texts are built upon the preceding ones, will lead to ambiguities and suggest contradictions between some texts.

Some writers in the field of principles have produced statements that hold no legal value, mistakenly believing they are relying on the idea of the objectives of the Sharia, such as the claim that wherever there is an interest, there is the law of Allah, or that the path to the permissible is permissible, or like someone who says that Allah has prohibited what our minds perceive as harmful and permitted what our minds perceive as beneficial, or something similar.

While some do this claiming to rely on al-Shatibi, there are those who have realized that al-Shatibi's ideas do not apply to these meanings. For instance, Sheikh Muhammad al-Tahir ibn Ashour points out, in the context of his assertion that the Sharia is justified by the interests of the servants, that most of al-Shatibi's arguments on this matter are not valid, and this stems from the fact that what is being argued for according to al-Shatibi differs from that of the Sheikh (3).

Thus, Dr. Abdullah Daraz saw a contradiction between some texts of al-Shatibi.
