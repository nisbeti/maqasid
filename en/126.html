Al-Juwayni said: "Al-Shafi'i and most of the followers of Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with him) went to rely on reasoning even if it does not stem from an agreed-upon ruling in principle, but he does not permit deviation, distance, or excess; rather, he allows the suspension of rulings based on interests that he sees as similar to the recognized interests in agreement and to the interests based on established, firm rulings in the Sharia" (1).

Al-Ghazali said: "Any interest that relates to the preservation of a legal objective known to be intended by the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus is not outside these principles; however, it is not called analogy (qiyas) but rather a remote interest (mursala). For analogy is a specific principle, and the fact that these meanings are intended is known not by a single proof but by many proofs that are countless from the Book and the Sunnah, and indications of circumstances and various signs, which is why it is called a remote interest. If we define interest as the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, there is no room for disagreement about it; rather, it must be affirmed that it is a proof" (2).

As an example of this, the meaning of preserving wealth, even if there is no specific ruling attesting to it—according to some observers—there are many rulings whose meanings return to the generality of this meaning. The prohibition of theft, the punishment for it, the prohibition of looting, embezzlement, and fraud, the prohibition of betrayal in the work of partners, the prohibition of extravagance and waste, the restriction of the foolish, the prohibition of misappropriation, the prohibition of consuming wealth unlawfully, the encouragement to fulfill trusts and to announce lost property, and others, all can indicate this general meaning and that it is considered in these rulings. This is stronger than relying on the ruling of the prohibition of theft—for example—to argue for the preservation of wealth as a reason of analogy and relevance in the path of relevance. Therefore, they made the preservation of wealth a principle that is observed and relied upon in legislation.
