The first category has already been discussed, which is the permissible option (mubah al-mukhayyar). The second has also been addressed, which is the permissible in the sense of lifting hardship (mubah bi-ma'na raf' al-haraj). As for the fourth, since it does not serve any purpose, it means that it neither serves nor undermines any essential, necessary, or enhancing principle, as it does not benefit the servants nor avert any harm from them. Thus, it is fundamentally contradictory to the requirement of being for the benefit of the servants. Therefore, al-Shatibi referred it back to what is required to be abandoned, stating: "As for the fourth, since it does not serve any significant purpose, it is considered frivolous or akin to frivolity in the eyes of rational individuals, and thus it is also required to be abandoned, for it serves only to waste time without any benefit for religion or worldly matters; hence it is a servant of what is required to be abandoned, making it, in totality, required to be abandoned" (1).

As for the third category, which is the permissible part that serves a choice. It is essential to note here that this choice is served, and consequently, it is a universal principle. According to al-Shatibi's idea, a universal principle cannot be a matter of choice; otherwise, it undermines it. In essence, the depiction of universality and particularity in the permissible was intended to prevent the existence of the permissible or the choice in universals. Therefore, after al-Shatibi discussed the fourth category, he returned to this third category to say: "The third category is similar as well, for it serves it, and thus it is also required to be abandoned" (2).
