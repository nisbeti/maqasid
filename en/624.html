Among the meanings that can be definitively established are the meanings of rulings, not just the meanings of texts. The meanings of rulings are what one can imagine as intended by the ruling, such as wisdom, benefit, cause, or outcome. All of these, if they are what the text can accommodate, must be established through induction, as has been clarified. However, if there is no indication from the text, neither in its explicit wording nor in its implied meaning, then this is something that can be imagined as intended by the legislator in the ruling and can be used to justify the ruling, even if it does not fall under one of the established paths of reasoning, which are the text, consensus, or hints and implications. Yet, it is only acceptable if it is established with certainty, which is done by examining many rulings or particulars that can accommodate this meaning or this reason. Without useful induction for certainty, these meanings are mere illusions and fantasies, and they are not considered because they do not return to a definitive origin; they may even contradict a foundational principle. Among his statements regarding such meanings or justifications is: "What is counted among the salt of knowledge is not part of its essence unless it is definitive and does not return to a definitive origin, but rather to something speculative, or it returns to something definitive but lacks a specific characteristic of those characteristics (1) or more than one characteristic; it is an illusion and something that provokes the intellect at first glance and initial consideration without compromising its essence or the meaning of another" (2). He also said: "Because if it is valid in minds, it does not yield any present benefit other than mere comfort for the souls" (3). The imagined meaning may also contradict a definitive principle; he said: "What is not part of the essence nor the salt: what does not return to a definitive or speculative origin, but rather its nature is to contradict its origin or another by nullification" (4).
