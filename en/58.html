As for the second opinion: that it is self-evident in itself, meaning that the legislator has made it self-evident, I do not see it differing from the meaning of the motivator. The statement that the legislator has made it self-evident means that it has come within a legal framework or is indicated by a legal proof, and the statement that it is self-evident means that it includes what makes it suitable for reasoning. This is the same meaning as the motivator itself as it has been defined. Wherever it is mentioned in the words of the imams that it is in this sense, it is an indicator, the intended meaning is that it is an indicator in the usage of the theologians and not the jurists, as it is known that the legal proofs from the texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah are, for the theologians and the usulists, indicators of the rulings, while for the jurists, they are proofs of them. In this sense, al-Amidi said: "The rational cause necessitates the ruling by itself and not by designation, whereas the legal cause is in the sense of an indicator or a sign or in the sense of a motivator" (1). Al-Zarkashi said: "It is divided into rational: which does not become a cause by the designation of a designator but by itself, and it is self-evident and does not change with time like the movement of the mover, and legal: which has become a cause by the designation of a designator" (2). He then said: "The legal causes are only called causes metaphorically or broadly; otherwise, in reality, the cause is what necessitates the ruling by itself, which is the rational cause, while that which necessitates it by another is not a cause in the view of the theologians but is an indicator of the ruling" (3).

As for the third opinion: that it is self-evident for the ruling by itself and not by the designation of Allah, this is the opinion of the Mu'tazila and aligns with their doctrine of rational good and evil, and that Allah must act in accordance with what is best and legislate what is best. Al-Zarkashi said: "The Mu'tazila and those who agreed with them among the jurists have greatly restricted this, claiming that His actions are constrained by wisdom and limited by the aspect of what is beneficial" (4).
