He says: "Essential matters or other needs or complementary issues, if they are surrounded by external matters that do not conform to the Sharia, then the endeavor to bring about benefits is valid on the condition of exercising caution to the best of one's ability without hardship, such as marriage, which requires the sustenance of dependents amidst the narrow paths of what is lawful and the vast avenues of what is unlawful and questionable... If such considerations were taken into account in marriage in our time, it would lead to the invalidation of its essence, which is not correct. Likewise, seeking knowledge if it is accompanied by abominations that one hears and sees, and attending funerals and performing religious duties if one can only perform them by witnessing what is not acceptable, does not remove this incidental factor from these matters because they are the foundations of religion and the principles of benefits" (1).

As for al-Shatibi's use of the term "worldly" when he mentions worldly benefits and harms, this is in contrast to the term benefits and harms of the Hereafter, which are the delights of Paradise and the torments of Hellfire. The worldly benefits and harms, although they may not be purely existent in reality, the benefits of the Hereafter are pure benefits, meaning they are untainted by any harms, such as punishment. The harms, that is, the punishment, is pure and not mixed with any benefit for those who are eternally in Hell, while it is mixed with mercy for the monotheists who will return to Paradise. From this mixture, the Fire does not reach their places of prostration and the seat of faith (2).
