Based on what was mentioned in the previous chapter regarding the criteria set by al-Shatibi for the causes (illal) or objectives (maqasid), he establishes a new matter and considers it a reconciliation between differing opinions on the issue of justifying rulings by appropriate descriptions or legal meanings, or other matters that some have considered pathways for justification.

For example, the disagreement regarding the justification for the prohibition of alcohol based on intoxication, where the justifiers argued that intoxication is a cause even if it is not explicitly stated or understood from the discourse, based on the principle of justification and the appropriateness of the description. On the other hand, those opposing this justification argued that it is not indicated by the Sharia nor understood from the discourse, but rather it is something that the justifier leans towards and imagines its correctness in his heart; thus, it is a product of the whims of the mind and not a sign from the Sharia. Al-Shatibi introduced his methodology or criteria for causes to state that this justification or description, as it stands, is not indicated by the legal discourse, and therefore, justifying it falls under the category of beautification and vilification. However, since it has been established that legislation was for the sake of goals, causes, objectives, or rulings, it has been confirmed that it is permissible for the jurist (mujtahid) to search for the wisdom behind the prohibition of alcohol.

With al-Shatibi's condition for certainty regarding the cause or objective or that it returns to something certain, the cause that the jurist establishes cannot merely be an appropriate description, nor is that a requirement at all; rather, it must be something established through induction or related to a principle or meaning that is definitively established through that method. Accordingly, the claim of the causality of intoxication, if this meaning is not certain, holds no value in the Sharia. However, since the preservation of the intellect is a meaning that has been definitively established as the intention of the Legislator (Exalted is He) to achieve it, and it has become a firmly rooted principle in the religion, and since the justification of intoxication relates in its meaning to this certain principle - which is the preservation of the intellect - therefore, justifying it based on intoxication is valid.

(1) Al-Shatibi refers to the Islamic scholar who developed a comprehensive theory of the objectives of Sharia.

(2) The term "mujtahid" refers to a qualified jurist who exerts effort to derive legal rulings from Islamic sources.

(3) The term "illal" refers to the causes or reasons behind legal rulings.

(4) The term "maqasid" refers to the objectives or purposes of Islamic law.

(5) The term "Sharia" refers to Islamic law derived from the Qur'an and Sunnah.

(6) The term "induction" refers to a method of reasoning in which general principles are derived from specific observations.
