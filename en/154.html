Although al-Razi rejected the very concept of causation, he affirmed the method of analogy in a different way. He said, after mentioning the first argument in explaining that analogy implies a presumption of causation, which he mentioned and then refuted, "The second argument in explaining that analogy implies a presumption of causation is that we concede that the actions and rulings of Allah cannot be attributed to motives and purposes. Nevertheless, we claim that analogy implies a presumption of causation, and the explanation is as follows: The belief of Muslims is that the rotation of celestial bodies, the rising and setting of stars, and their remaining in their forms and lights are not obligatory. However, since Allah - Exalted is He - has established a habit of keeping them in a constant state, it is no wonder that they will remain tomorrow and the day after with these characteristics. Similarly, the falling of rain during humid clouds, the feeling of fullness after eating, the quenching of thirst after drinking, and burning upon contact with fire are not obligatory, but since the habit has been consistent with these occurrences, it is no wonder that a presumption close to certainty arises regarding their continuity in their patterns. The result is that the repetition of something many times leads to the presumption that whenever it occurs, it will occur only in that manner. Once this is established, we say: When we contemplate the laws, we find that rulings and benefits are closely related, and neither can be separated from the other, which is evident after the establishment of the conditions of the laws. If this is the case, then the knowledge of the occurrence of one necessitates the presumption of the occurrence of the other, and vice versa, without one being a cause for the other or a motive for it(1). Thus, it is established that analogy is a proof of causation, while it is certain that the rulings of Allah - Exalted is He - are not attributed to purposes"(2).

Thus, despite the disagreement between these two groups regarding the very concept of causation, they both agree on attributing causation to the appropriate description, in contrast to those who reject the method of analogy.
