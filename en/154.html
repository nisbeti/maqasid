Although al-Razi rejected the principle of causation, he affirmed the method of suitability in a different way. He said, after mentioning the first argument in explaining that suitability implies a presumption of causation, which he mentioned and then refuted, "The second argument in explaining that suitability implies a presumption of causation is that we concede that the actions and rulings of Allah cannot be attributed to motives and purposes. Nevertheless, we claim that suitability implies a presumption of causation, and the explanation is as follows: The belief of Muslims is that the rotation of the celestial spheres, the rising and setting of stars, and their remaining in their forms and lights are not obligatory. However, since Allah - Exalted is He - has established a habit of keeping them in a constant state, it is inevitable that they will remain tomorrow and the day after in these attributes. Similarly, the descent of rain during humid clouds, the feeling of fullness after eating, the quenching of thirst after drinking, and burning upon contact with fire are not obligatory, but since the habit has been consistent in this regard, it is inevitable that there is a presumption close to certainty regarding their continuity in their patterns. The result is that the repetition of something many times leads to the presumption that whenever it occurs, it will occur only in that manner. Once this is established, we say: When we contemplate the laws, we find that rulings and interests are closely related, with neither being separable from the other, which is evident after the establishment of the conditions of the laws. If this is the case, then the knowledge of the occurrence of one necessitates the presumption of the occurrence of the other, and vice versa, without either being a cause or a motive for the other." (1) Thus, despite the disagreement between these two groups regarding the principle of causation, they both agree on causation by way of suitable description, in contrast to those who reject the method of suitability.
