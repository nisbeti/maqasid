And indeed, this picture reflects the era of al-Shatibi: political turmoil and coups within, military attacks and Crusader threats from abroad, ongoing wars and the fall of Islamic cities, and the Muslims' flight to Granada after being expelled from their lands, constantly seeking assistance from the Muslims in Morocco while maintaining fragmentation and division into small states, all of which foreshadow a bleak fate for the Muslims in those lands. This fate is confirmed by the absence or weakness of political thought and the lack of interest among scholars in politics. For example, there is nothing in al-Shatibi's extensive and important works that indicates his concern for political conditions except in terms of issuing fatwas to the people not to sell to the disbeliever what he could use against them, or regarding the permissibility of imposing taxes on the people out of necessity. Therefore, when the Christians were unable to control Granada due to the latter's reliance on the Marinid state in Morocco, they plotted and took control of Gibraltar, the only outlet for supplying the Muslims, and this occurred in the year 867 AH - 1462 CE(1). This made it easier for them to eliminate the Muslims in Granada, and then to completely eradicate the Muslims in Andalusia in the year 897 AH - 1492 CE. This indicates a lack of political thinking, which is a sign of decline and degradation, regardless of the scientific and economic level of the state.
