As for al-Shafi'i, he rejected it as previously mentioned, saying: "And he does not say what is preferred, for saying what is preferred is something that is newly introduced without a previous example" (1). He also said: "Indeed, preference (istihsan) is mere indulgence" (2). His companions followed him in this. It has been said that the majority rejected it; al-Zarkashi said: "The majority rejected it to the extent that al-Shafi'i said: Whoever prefers has legislated" (3). He then said: "Our companions said: And whoever legislates has disbelieved, and al-Shafi'i remained silent about the second premise due to its clarity" (4). The correct view is that if it were mere indulgence and a statement in religion without evidence, the majority would not have rejected it alone; rather, Abu Hanifa and his companions would have rejected it as well. The rejection of preference became severe, even affecting Imam Abu Hanifa and his companions. Alaa al-Din al-Bukhari said: "Know that some of those who criticize Muslims have attacked Abu Hanifa and his companions - may Allah have mercy on them - for abandoning analogy (qiyas) in favor of preference, saying that the evidences of the Sharia are the Book, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy, and that preference is a fifth category that no one among the scholars of the Sharia, except for Abu Hanifa and his companions, has recognized as a proof of the Sharia, and no evidence has been established for it; rather, it is a statement based on desire. Thus, abandoning analogy for it is abandoning a proof to follow whims or the desires of the soul, which is invalid. He then said: If the analogy they abandoned for preference is a legal proof, then the legal proof is true, and what is after the truth except misguidance" (5). He said: "It has also been reported from al-Shafi'i that he was extreme in rejecting preference, saying: Whoever prefers has legislated, and all of this is an attack without reflection, and a criticism without understanding the intended meaning, for Abu Hanifa - may Allah have mercy on him - is of greater stature and more pious than to speak in religion based on desire" (6).
