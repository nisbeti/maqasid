Al-Ghazali said: "As for the strange (gharib) that has not shown its effect or its suitability to the type of actions of the legislator, an example of this is our saying: 'Wine is prohibited because it intoxicates,' thus in its meaning, every intoxicant is included, although the effect of intoxication has not appeared in another context, yet it is suitable. This is an example of the strange if it were not for the indication provided by His saying: { Indeed, Satan only wants to cause between you animosity and hatred through intoxicants } (1). Another example is our saying that a woman who has been divorced three times during the illness of death inherits because the husband intended to evade her inheritance, which is opposed by the opposite of his intention, analogous to the killer, for he does not inherit because he hastens the inheritance, thus it is opposed by the opposite of his intention. The reasoning for the killer's deprivation is a reasoning based on a suitability that does not align with the type of actions of the Sharia, as we do not see the Sharia in another context addressing its type, so its suitability remains merely strange. If the deprivation were reasoned by his transgression through killing and this were made a punishment for the aggression, it would be a reasoning based on a suitable correlation that is not effective, for the crime itself, although its effect appears in punishments, does not show its effect in deprivation from inheritance" (2).
It is noteworthy here that the reality of the strange suitability and its examples among those who affirm it, such as al-Amidi and al-Ghazali, as has been clarified, is the same reality and examples of the remote suitability among those who affirm it, as will be shown. Al-Amidi mentioned as an example of the strange suitability: "Considering the type of hardship shared between the menstruating woman and the traveler in the type of alleviation, for the specific hardship of the menstruating woman is not the same as the specific hardship of the traveler, but rather of its kind, and the specific alleviation for the traveler by dropping two units of prayer is not the same as the alleviation for the menstruating woman by dropping prayer, but rather of its kind" (3).
