As for the meaning of returning to a definitive origin, an example of this is the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm" (1). This is a report of singular transmission (khabar ahad) that is not definitive, and therefore, according to the methodology of al-Shatibi, it is a particularity and not a definitive universal origin. It is not correct to apply it until it is established that it returns to a definitive origin, nor can it be said here that it is from the Sunnah, which is a definitive source or universal origin, as this is not the meaning of returning to a definitive origin according to al-Shatibi. Rather, the meaning is that the meaning of the hadith is in accordance with a definitive legal meaning, that is, a universal meaning that has been established as universal through the induction of many evidences (particularities) that converge on this meaning. Al-Shatibi states: "His saying, peace be upon him: 'There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm' falls under a definitive origin in this meaning, for harm and reciprocating harm are widely prohibited in the Sharia in particular incidents and universal principles, such as His saying, Exalted is He: { And do not hold them to hurt them, so that you may commit an injustice } (2), { And do not harm them, so that you may make it difficult for them } (3), { A mother should not be harmed regarding her child } (4), and the like. This includes the prohibition of transgressing against lives, properties, and honor, as well as against usurpation and injustice, and everything that is in the meaning of causing harm or reciprocating harm. It includes harm to the self, intellect, lineage, or property, and it is a meaning that is extremely general in the Sharia, with no dispute or doubt about it" (5).
The abundance of texts and evidences used for inference is merely to complete the induction and thus reach certainty. In this way, the meaning of lifting harm becomes a universal origin, and the meaning contained in the hadith aligns with the universal origin, which is a particularity, and thus it can be said that it returns to a universal origin, making it valid.
