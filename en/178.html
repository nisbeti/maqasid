The meaning of the second characteristic is that the general principles (kulliyat) are neither abrogated nor specified nor restricted, nor do they change according to time or place. Rather, what we derive from al-Shatibi's comparison of the legal general principles with the rational general principles is that the legal general principles are not merely definitive truths, but rather absolute definitive truths. They are not restricted to a specific time over another time, nor to a specific place over another place, nor to a specific accountable person (mukallaf) over another accountable person. He says: "You will not find in them, after their perfection, any abrogation, nor specification of their generality, nor restriction of their absoluteness, nor the lifting of a ruling from their rulings, neither according to the generality of the accountable nor according to the specificity of some of them, nor according to a time over another time, nor a state over another state. Rather, whatever establishes a cause is a cause forever and does not cease, and whatever is a condition is forever a condition, and whatever is obligatory is always obligatory or recommended is recommended, and thus all rulings have no cessation or alteration. Even if the obligation were to continue indefinitely, its rulings would remain the same" (1). The meaning of the third characteristic is that there is nothing in the religious sciences that is contradicted by truths, whether they are religious or rational, or that opposes them to the extent that one is compelled to interpret it in a way that the text cannot bear, because it then becomes a ruled matter rather than a ruling (2).

(1) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat, 1/45.

(2) Accordingly, the general principles, whether rational or religious, are absolute truths, and the religious truths are also rational truths, because they are based on the definitive rational proof of the prophethood of our master Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), and on the certainty of the truth of his message and report. The certainty that the Qur'an is from Allah is fundamentally a rational path. Accordingly, the religious reports and everything that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) brought, which does not allow for rational inquiry, is based on reason in that the truth of the report is rationally certain.
