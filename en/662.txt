If there is no legal value to this meaning in each of the rulings that are being extrapolated, how will the meaning acquire legal value through their aggregation? If a person is tested, for example, with twenty questions and receives a zero on each question, how can their total be one hundred out of one hundred? How can one reach certainty about the meaning from the extrapolation of the particulars in which this meaning is imagined if it holds no legal significance in any of those particulars? This brings to mind the saying of the Hanafis mentioned earlier: "By the abundance of analogies, strength in description is not attained."
As for al-Shatibi's example of the bravery of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and the generosity of Hatim, it does not belong to the category of extrapolating the meanings of rulings. The bravery of Ali and the generosity of Hatim are indications from the texts reported about them, not indications of the meanings of the texts. They are truly from the mutawatir (consecutively reported) in meaning if the texts are authentic; however, some have contested that the bravery of Ali is from the mutawatir in wording.
Al-Shatibi's error here arises from two aspects:
First: An error in methodology, which is the claim of specific objectives of the Lawgiver without any evidence. Moreover, he asserted certainty about them, not to mention the incomplete extrapolation and what it entails.
