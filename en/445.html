Al-Shatibi has a specific or new opinion regarding how to establish the meanings of legal texts, including how to determine the meanings of the verses of the Noble Qur'an. This is based on his previously stated view that the legal evidence must be definitive (qat'i) or refer back to something definitive. This means that the meaning referred to must be definitive. His opinion has also been previously explained regarding the absence or impossibility of a definitive indication from the text, which raises a question about how Al-Shatibi derives evidence from the verses of the Qur'an. The fact that the indication is not definitive means that the meanings are potential, and determining one meaning over another without a preference or evidence supporting this meaning over others is not valid. In fact, this potential or apparent meaning, according to the meanings of the words and structure, cannot be considered unless it refers back to a definitive meaning established through induction. Since it is necessary to apply the texts and to engage in ijtihad (independent reasoning) to derive rulings in order to achieve the servitude of humans to their Creator, there must be a correct methodology for determining the meanings of the verses of the Qur'an. Therefore, there must be criteria for interpretation and means to assist in it. This discussion aims to clarify these criteria, means, and methodology according to Al-Shatibi.
