He said: "The commands in the Sharia do not all carry the same level of emphasis, nor do they fall under a single intention. The commands related to necessary matters are not like those related to needs, nor are they like those related to enhancements, nor are the matters that complete necessities like the necessities themselves; rather, there is a known distinction between them. Indeed, the necessary matters are not all demanded in the same manner; for example, the demand related to the foundation of religion is not emphasized like that of the soul, nor is the soul emphasized like that of the intellect, compared to other types of necessities (1) and needs as well... Similarly, enhancements are treated in the same manner, word for word... The criterion in this is to examine each matter to see whether it is sought in the first intention or the second intention. If it is sought in the first intention, it is in the highest ranks of that type (2). If it is among those sought in the second intention (3), one must consider whether the necessary can exist without it to the extent that the action can still be called that necessity or not. If it cannot, then that sought matter takes the place of the pillar and the part that is essential to the necessity. If it can be referred to by that name without it, then that sought matter is not a pillar but rather a complement and an addition, either from the needs or from the enhancements, and one should look at its ranks in the order mentioned or similar to it based on what inductive reasoning leads to in the Sharia regarding each part of it" (4).
__________
(1) This means that the order of necessities is as follows, from highest to lowest: religion, then the soul, then the intellect, then lineage, then wealth.
(2) That is, from the five types of necessities.
(3) The meaning of primary objectives and secondary objectives was previously explained in the last chapter. The second objective is a secondary objective that has been established as serving the primary objective, which is the first.
(4) Al-Muwafaqat: 3/120 - 121.
