Among them: the arrangement of the ruling based on the description with the conjunction "fa" (ف) indicating consequence and causation in the words of Allah or His Messenger or the narrator from the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him): As for the words of Allah, it is as in His saying: {And as for the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands} (1). And as for the words of His Messenger, it is like his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "Whoever revives dead land, it is his" (2). And his saying: "You have control over yourself, so choose" (3). And as for the words of the narrator, it is as in the saying: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) forgot in the prayer, so he prostrated" (4). Al-Amidi said: "In all these instances, it indicates that what the ruling is arranged upon with 'fa' is a reason for the ruling, because 'fa' in the language is indicative of consequence" (5). Thus, 'fa' does not imply causation linguistically. However, its presence indicates that the ruling occurs following the description, and the arrangement of the ruling following the description serves as a reminder that the description was a cause for the ruling, meaning a reason for its legislation, thus it becomes a cause. Causation is necessary for this arrangement, that is, for the arrangement of the ruling based on the description with 'fa', which provides a concept suitable for causation.
