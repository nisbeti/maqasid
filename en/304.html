In reality, the terms justice and moderation have a pleasant impact on the soul, but they, like the terms oppression, excess, and negligence, cannot be understood in isolation unless they are connected to a foundational understanding. The man-made and distorted laws have their own definitions of justice and oppression or what they call good and evil, and if we were to examine the justice within them and compare it to our Sharia, we would find it to be sheer oppression, and we would find the truth within it to be falsehood and goodness to be a vice... These terms can only be understood through what they signify, that is, through what the Sharia has come to teach us is justice, oppression, goodness, or otherwise. In other words, to say that the Sharia is justice or the Sharia is moderation, if what is meant is that we know what justice and moderation are, and the Sharia commands us to adhere to what we know, is incorrect. The correct meaning is that the Sharia has come with commands and prohibitions, and what it has commanded is justice and moderation, and by knowing this, one understands what justice and moderation are, and what is contrary to them. Accordingly, the statement that justice is the straight line between the extremes of excess and negligence is a statement that lacks meaning. For this statement to have a tangible reality, one must know what excess is and what negligence is. Could it not be that what a person sees as excess is actually moderation and justice, or it is negligence? And what they see as negligence is actually justice and moderation, or it is excess? And what they perceive as justice or moderation could be excess or negligence. Is this not also consistent with the assertion that the notions of good and evil are based on Sharia rather than reason? Therefore, it is necessary to point out the frequent misreadings of al-Shatibi regarding this matter. Promoting terms like moderation or centrism or the centrism of the Sharia without specifying a foundation to embody these terms in meanings is superficial in thought and understanding. Al-Shatibi does not define moderation based on the feeling of what is excess or negligence, but rather he defines excess and negligence by deviating from moderation.
