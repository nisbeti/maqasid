Then he said: "If it is established that there is no obligation to intend the cause, then the accountable person (mukallaf) may abandon the intention to it absolutely, and he may intend it" (1).
As for abandoning the intention to the cause, this has been discussed previously. As for the permissibility of intending it, this does not mean that the act is permitted or prohibited based on the anticipated outcome, whether it is beneficial or harmful, as this would lead to the permissibility of some prohibited acts or the prohibition of some permissible, recommended, or obligatory acts based on the results they produce. Rather, the opposite is intended, which is that it is valid for the mukallaf to consider the effects if this strengthens adherence to the cause and encourages further commitment to it. However, if it weakens adherence to the cause, meaning the legal ruling, then this itself is a harm, and it is not valid. Al-Shatibi provides several examples of this. For instance, someone who earns a living to support himself and his family, as considering the outcomes may motivate him to work harder for greater earnings and better care for his family. Similarly, someone who obeys Allah to please Him and enter His Paradise, as contemplating these goals may drive him to greater sacrifice, generosity, patience, righteousness, striving, and commitment, thus reinforcing the cause. Here, it is valid for the mukallaf to consider the outcomes. He says: "This intention, when accompanied by the causation, is correct because it is an attention to the prevailing customs. And Allah, Exalted is He, said: { Allah is the One who made the sea subservient to you, so that ships may sail upon it by His command and that you may seek of His bounty } (2)" (3). Then he said: "Thus, the essence of this is to seek what Allah prepares for him through this cause, which returns to reliance on Allah and turning to Him" (4). Then he said: "If that is the case, then paying attention to the outcomes and intending them is considered in customary matters, especially for the jurist (mujtahid)" (5)
