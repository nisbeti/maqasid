Since the benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), in terms of bringing about and averting, are the causes (illal), the objectives (maqasid), the effects (musabbabat), and the meanings of the rulings (ahkam), and since they are frequently mentioned and utilized by al-Shatibi, the failure to ascertain the true meaning of what al-Shatibi intended by benefit and harm leads the researcher in the Muwafaqat to contradictions and diverts him from understanding al-Shatibi's methodology. Therefore, it is necessary to explore and establish al-Shatibi's concept of benefits and harms in detail and separately.

The concept of al-Shatibi regarding both public interest (maslaha) and harm (mafsada):

The concept of al-Shatibi regarding public interests (masalih) and harms (mafasid) appears in many of his texts, and it is also evident from the manner in which he established that the Sharia (Islamic law) is based on public interests and harms.

Indeed, al-Shatibi attempted to prove this matter in the introduction to his book on the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), responding to al-Razi, who denied the reasoning of the Sharia based on benefits and harms and cut off that discussion. He presented texts that show that the commands of Allah, Exalted is He, and His actions are reasoned, as evidenced by the legal texts indicating that Allah has ruled this for such and such a reason, and has acted thus for such and such a reason, such as His saying: { so that you may attain piety }, { to test you }, { to worship Me }... Since al-Shatibi's method of establishing principles and foundations is through beneficial induction (istiqra), he brought forth numerous texts that explicitly state that the ruling or action was for the sake of a purpose. He pointed to the existence of countless texts indicating that rulings and actions have objectives. Based on the occurrence of induction, he establishes a universal principle that does not fail, which is that the actions and rulings of Allah have purposes and objectives. Thus, al-Shatibi sees that he has definitively proven that the Sharia is reasoned by benefits and harms. Upon examining these texts that al-Shatibi cited, we find that the majority of them do not indicate the intention of the legislator towards what is beneficial or the removal of harm in the sense of what aligns with the interests or desires of people or what rational individuals or wise men deem beneficial for people. Rather, most of them lack a rational or suitable meaning that can be said to bring about a benefit or avert a harm.
