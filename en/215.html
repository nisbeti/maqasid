Since the benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), in terms of bringing about and averting, are the causes, the objectives (maqasid), the effects, and the meanings of the rulings, and since they are frequently mentioned and utilized by al-Shatibi, the failure to ascertain the true meaning of what al-Shatibi intended by benefit and harm leads the researcher in the Muwafaqat to contradictions and diverts him from understanding al-Shatibi's methodology. Therefore, it is necessary to research and establish al-Shatibi's concept of benefits and harms in detail and separately.

Al-Shatibi's concept of both benefit and harm:

Al-Shatibi's concept of benefits and harms appears in many of his texts, and it is also evident from the manner in which he established that the Sharia is based on benefits and harms.

Al-Shatibi attempted to prove this matter in the introduction to his book on the objectives, responding to al-Razi, who denied the justification of the Sharia based on benefits and harms and asserted this firmly. He presented texts that show that the rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, and His actions are justified, as indicated by the legal texts stating that Allah ruled this for such and such a reason, and acted thus for such and such a reason, such as His saying: { so that you may attain piety }, { to test you }, { to worship Me }... And since al-Shatibi's methodology in establishing the rules and principles is through beneficial induction that leads to certainty, he brought forth many texts that explicitly state that the ruling or action was for the sake of a purpose. He pointed to the existence of countless texts indicating that the rulings and actions have objectives. Based on the occurrence of induction, he establishes a universal principle that does not fail, which is that the actions and rulings of Allah have objectives and purposes. Thus, al-Shatibi sees that he has conclusively proven that the Sharia is justified by benefits and harms.

Upon examining the texts that al-Shatibi cited, we find that the majority of them do not indicate the intention of the legislator towards what is a benefit or the removal of a harm in the sense of what aligns with the interests or desires of people or what rational individuals or wise people deem to be beneficial for people. In fact, the majority of them do not contain a rational or suitable meaning that can be said to bring about a benefit or avert a harm.
