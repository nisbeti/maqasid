It has become clear that the indication of the singular texts (akhbar al-ahad) is speculative, and this alone does not warrant their consideration according to al-Shatibi. The texts of the Noble Qur'an are speculative in their indication, and the same applies to the mutawatir (consecutively transmitted) Sunnah, while the reports of singular narrations are even more so. Thus, a singular report, even if its authenticity is established, must be approached with caution unless its meaning returns to a definitive meaning. He does not say: "unless it contradicts something stronger than it," but rather: "unless it is definitive or returns to a definitive meaning."
Similarly, the texts of the Qur'an can bear multiple meanings, and one cannot definitively assert one of them merely by considering the text. The way to understand the text and apply it is by understanding it in light of what preceded it in revelation, as the revelation of the Qur'an was such that the later verses come as clarification and explanation of the earlier ones. The general principles were revealed first in Mecca. Therefore, the Medinan texts are understood in light of the Meccan ones, and the later Meccan texts are understood in light of the earlier ones, and the same applies to the Medinan texts (1). It is necessary to gather the meanings through the abundance of texts that indicate them.
