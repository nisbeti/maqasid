Then he said: "However, the fruit of understanding only appears in inference, and for this reason it is made a second condition; the first condition is the cause for reaching this rank because it is the intended goal, while the second is a means" (1).
Among the conditions for a mujtahid (jurist) is to attain the level of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) in the sciences of the Arabic language, which is included in the condition of understanding the objectives (maqasid) since it is necessary for the inference from texts and for understanding the objectives. Whoever does not reach this level is merely a follower (muqallid) in language, and his statements regarding the meanings of texts will not be authoritative; he will rely on others whose statements are authoritative, thus lowering his status in understanding the objectives. He says: "If we assume a beginner in understanding Arabic, he is a beginner in understanding the Sharia (Islamic law), or if he is intermediate, he is intermediate in understanding the Sharia, and the intermediate has not reached the level of completion. If he reaches the ultimate level in Arabic, he will likewise in the Sharia, and his understanding therein will be authoritative" (2). He said: "Whoever's understanding is limited is not considered authoritative, nor is his statement accepted; thus, he must reach in Arabic the level of the imams (leaders) in it, such as al-Khalil, Sibawayh, al-Akhfash, al-Jurjani, al-Mazani, and others" (3). Al-Shatibi clarifies that he does not mean that one must be like these imams in all the sciences of language or to delve as deeply as they did in grammar, the subtleties of syntax, and the complexities of language, and so on. Rather, he should be capable of understanding the texts and what they may imply in terms of meanings, what they do not imply, and their generality or specificity, precedence or postponement, metaphor or ambiguity, and so forth, and to reach their level in this so that he may be authoritative in it. He said: "It cannot be said that the usuliyyin (scholars of Islamic legal theory) have denied this exaggeration in understanding Arabic" (4). Then he said: "The aim is to refine understanding so that it parallels that of the Arab to that extent, and it is not a condition for the Arab to know all of the language" (5).
