In the sections on introductions and documents, some rules or principles of al-Shatibi's methodology in establishing the objectives (maqasid) are clarified. The objectives or principles reached are a result of applying this methodology. This section aims to build upon those introductions and documents with ideas, definitions, or concepts that must be considered and relied upon when applying the methodology in order to reach the general rules and principles.

Based on this, this section is to introduce some terms or legal terminology used by al-Shatibi, and understanding their meanings is essential for grasping his methodology, particularly the terms public interest (maslaha) and harm (mafsada), in addition to the terms cause (sabab), effect (musaab), wisdom (hikmah), and reason ('illah) and the meanings of rulings.

Cause:

As for the cause, its meaning has been clarified in the previous section. It is what the legislator has made a cause for an effect, whether in religious matters or in rational matters. The relevant application in the legal objectives is what is a legal cause. He stated when discussing one of the principles: "What has been discussed in this principle pertains to the effects of causes in terms of whether the causes are lawful or unlawful, that is, from the perspective of what falls under the scrutiny of the law, not from the perspective of what are ordinary causes for ordinary effects" (1).

As has been clarified, the causes are the legal rulings that the legislator has specified, whether as commands or prohibitions, and they are the indications of the texts. Since the legislator has objectives in legislation, he has established rulings so that these objectives become the causes for adherence to them, making the legal rulings causes. The intended outcomes are rulings or effects or interests or reasons, but according to a specific methodology that will be clarified below.

Wisdom:

It is presumed that the ruling was legislated for its sake or for the sake of attaining it, thus it is a cause. However, before it is definitively established that it is intended by the legislator, it can be a plausible description or an appropriate description, making it the presumed intention of the legislator rather than a definitive one, and this has no legal consideration. The wisdoms may be known to the observer, may be presumed, or may be potentially imagined.
