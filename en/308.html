The scholars who preceded al-Shatibi and spoke of the first type of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) argued for the reasoning behind rulings based on wisdoms, on the premise that these wisdoms are the beneficial meanings or appropriate descriptions that, if presented to minds, would be accepted. However, al-Shatibi's understanding of the concept of benefit (maslaha) differed from that of his predecessors. The difference between the two is like the difference between their saying: what is presumed to be rationally beneficial or is felt in the heart as a benefit or intended wisdom behind the ruling is a reason, and his saying: what is definitively established through the induction of rulings as a goal or intended by the Legislator is a reason and a benefit. In other words, they say: the benefit is a reason. He says: the reason is a benefit.

... The difference between the two methodologies is one that turns matters upside down. This difference is not limited to what has been clarified regarding their understanding of benefits and harms, but extends to other matters(1).

(1) The idea of reasoning based on benefit or legislation based on it has become widespread in our time, and as a result, clear invalid opinions and fatwas have been issued. Some have resorted to ideas they attribute to the Sharia, such as bringing about benefits, preventing harms, necessity, facilitation, the flexibility of the Sharia, and the permissibility of changing rulings with the change of time. These individuals did not adhere to the conditions set by the imams who advocate for benefits. Among these conditions is that this reasoning should not lead to the invalidation of the ruling being reasoned for, and that the beneficial description used for reasoning should not be contradicted by a text or opposed by a beneficial meaning that is used to reason for another ruling. Some contemporary scholars have argued for the necessity of expanding the scope of what is permissible (halal) through what they call broad juristic preference (istihsan) and broad analogy (qiyas), meaning not being bound by the conditions for the validity of analogy or preference because those conditions entered the science of usul al-fiqh through Greek logic. This was advocated by Dr. Hassan al-Turabi in his book Renewal of the Foundations of Jurisprudence.
