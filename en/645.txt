As for al-Shatibi's (may Allah have mercy on him) statement that Allah, Exalted is He, has permitted the good things (tayyibat) and prohibited the evil things (khaba'ith), and that there remain things whose ruling is unclear to the jurist and it is difficult for him to determine whether they belong to the good things or the evil things, this is an attempt to adapt the issue to align with his methodology and has no basis in truth. For if it were not for the prohibition of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), no one would doubt that they are permissible based on the principle of permissibility, and thus they would be considered among the good things. The prohibition of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is what rendered them among the evil things in the sense of being prohibited, and Allah knows best. Moreover, al-Shatibi did not inform us of the basis of the evil things and the basis of the good things.
Al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him) said: "The Messenger of Allah established with the Book of Allah and legislated in matters for which there is no specific text in the Book" (1).
Al-Zarkashi (may Allah have mercy on him) stated under the title (The Sunnah Independent in Legislation): "For this reason, the Imam of the Two Holy Sanctuaries did not separate the Sunnah from the Book, and he said: Everything the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) says is from the statement of Allah, Exalted is He, so there is no point in mentioning a distinction between the Book and the Sunnah. Al-Shafi'i explicitly stated in the Risala that the Sunnah is regarded as revealed like the Qur'an" (2). Among what he cited regarding the independence of the Sunnah in legislation is: "Such as the prohibition of the meat of the domesticated donkey and every carnivorous animal with fangs, which are not explicitly mentioned in the Book" (3).
As for the hadith that instructs to present the hadiths to the Book, which al-Shatibi validated its meaning even if its chain of narration is not authentic, al-Shafi'i said about it: "No one has narrated this who has established his hadith in anything, whether small or large" (4).
