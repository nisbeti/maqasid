1. Abu Abdullah Muhammad bin Muhammad bin Ahmad al-Muqri (the grandfather), known as al-Muqri al-Kabir, who passed away in the year 759 AH. Al-Shatibi attended his lectures at the Grand Mosque, which he delivered in the presence of prominent students and scholars of Granada in the year 757 AH. This is the date of his arrival in Andalusia as an ambassador (1).
2. Abu al-Qasim Muhammad bin Ahmad al-Sharifi al-Husayni al-Sabti, the judge of the community who passed away in Granada in the year 760 AH. He was the head of linguistic sciences and the commentator on the Muqadimah of Hazm al-Qartajani (2).
3. Abu Abdullah Muhammad bin Ahmad al-Sharifi al-Tlemceni, the most knowledgeable of his time (3).
4. Abu Ali Mansur bin Ali bin Abdullah al-Zawawi, a jurist and scholar, from whom al-Shatibi studied the Mukhtasar of the End of the Pursuit and Aspiration in the Sciences of Usul (principles of jurisprudence) and Jadal (debate) by Imam Abu Amr ibn al-Hajib, all of which was studied for understanding and reflection, and he granted him a general authorization with its conditions (4).
5. The sun of the imams, Abu Abdullah Muhammad bin Ahmad bin Marzouk al-Khatib al-Tlemceni, who passed away in Cairo in the year 781 AH. Al-Shatibi heard from him in sessions at the Nasiriyah school and the Grand Mosque the two books of the Sahih of Imam al-Bukhari and the Muwatta of Imam Malik ibn Anas as narrated by Yahya ibn Yahya, and he granted him authorization for these two books and for everything that carries a general authorization with its conditions (5).
6. Abu Ja'far ibn al-Zayyat, who was among the shaykhs of the Sufis, and others from whom he met and benefited (6).
