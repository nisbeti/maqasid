It is redundant to mention, after it has become clear, that Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni (may Allah have mercy on him) was the first to attempt to establish the validity of reasoning by appropriate description through the adaptation of the Sharia for the purpose of examining it in light of its objectives of public interest (maqasid al-sharia) - and Allah knows best - where he categorized the rulings according to their purposes or objectives or meanings into five categories. The matter later evolved, at the hands of the supporters of this methodology, into three categories along with their complements, which are the necessities, the needs, and the embellishments, along with the complements of each. He stated under the title: "On the classifications of the causes and principles": "What these individuals have mentioned are the principles of the Sharia(1). We categorize it into five categories:

"One of them is what is rationally understood and is a foundation. The rational meaning of it leads to a necessary matter that must be adhered to, and this is akin to the ruling of the Sharia regarding the obligation of retribution (qisas) at its proper time, as it is justified by the preservation of the sanctity of blood and the deterrence against aggression towards it. When the observer who derives rulings clarifies this in the foundation of retribution, he can apply it and extend it to where the foundation of this meaning is realized, which facilitates the justification of its origin... It has also been established in the Sharia that when the foundations are firmly established, there is no need to seek the realization of its meaning in individual cases."(2)

He said in this section: "And it is that which is based on necessity"(3).

This section is what was later referred to as the necessities, which are the essential universals: the preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property.

(1) The term "principles of Sharia (usul al-sharia)" here refers to the legal rulings, as a legal ruling that is reasoned is a principle upon which analogical reasoning (qiyas) is based, and it is one of the pillars of qiyas. Likewise, if it is a subject of inquiry and examination to understand its objective or reason.
