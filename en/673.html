Yes, indeed, there is confusion and mixing in the matter, and the fact that the cause (illah) does not lag behind the ruling (hukm) is a consensus. It either precedes it or coincides with it in time, without disagreement, neither in legal causes nor in rational causes. Al-Zarkashi said: "Know that there is no disagreement that the cause precedes the effect in rank, and they differed on whether it precedes it in time or coincides with it, with the majority of the Mu'tazila and the jurists holding that it coincides with it, and they supported this with His saying, Exalted is He: { Allah takes the souls at the time of their death } (1)." Then he said: "The second opinion is that it is with it" (2). That is, it coincides with it in time. He then said: "The rational causes coincide with their effects because they are inherently influential, while the situational causes precede the effect, and the legal causes are from the situational ones" (3).

Based on the consensus reported by al-Zarkashi, the methodology that fundamentally and structurally relies on this confusion between the cause of the ruling and its objective is rejected from its origin. To eliminate this confusion and mixing, it is necessary to clarify the point of error and its cause.

As for the point of error, in the example of theft, the cause of the cutting (of the hand) is the theft itself, not the deterrence; deterrence is a result or objective, and theft, which is the cause, occurs before the ruling or simultaneously with it, which is the obligation of cutting. The relationship between the cause and the effect does not fail; wherever theft is found, the ruling, that is, the obligation of cutting, is found. As for deterrence, it is a result that may or may not exist, and this is the case with all punishments.

In the example of creation for the purpose of worship, worship is an objective or result that may or may not exist, as in the case of the disbeliever or the sinner, and there is no cause here, because creation is one of the acts of Allah, Exalted is He. It is worth mentioning that the cause is the cause of the ruling and not the cause of the action; theft is the cause of the obligation of cutting, not the cause of the cutting itself, because the action itself may occur or may not occur, as it depends on the choice of the accountable person who may obey or disobey, as is the case with any ruling.
