He said: "It has become clear to us that they - may Allah be pleased with them - in the long ages and extended periods did not adhere to fixed principles, but rather they would engage in reasoning as those who do not see an end to the avenues of opinion, and they viewed the paths of consideration as not limited. Then the later generations would follow the earlier ones and did not pay attention to mentioning specific avenues that could not be exceeded. Thus, we know by necessity of reason that they were receiving meanings and benefits from the sources of the Sharia that they relied upon in incidents where there were no texts. If they assumed them and their opinion was not contradicted by a principle from the principles of the Sharia, they would apply them, and it became clear that they were not seeking certain knowledge but were content with assuming something as knowledge" (1). Al-Ghazali said: "There is no way to limit oneself to the influential factor because what is sought is the predominance of assumption, and whoever examines the analogies of the Companions - may Allah be pleased with them - and their juristic efforts knows that they did not condition every analogy to have the cause known by text and consensus" (2). He said: "We have not seen from the consensus of the Companions - may Allah be pleased with them - anything except following the predominant opinion, and they did not specify the types of predominance of assumption nor distinguish one type from another" (3).

Al-Amidi said: "And if it is established that the rulings were legislated for the interests of the servants, then if we see a legislated ruling necessitating a beneficial matter, it must either be that this is the purpose of legislating the ruling, or it is something that has not been made clear to us. It cannot be what has not been made clear to us; otherwise, the legislation of the ruling would be an act of devotion, which contradicts the principle (4) that has been previously established. Therefore, it must be legislated for what has been made clear. And if that is presumed, then it must be acted upon because assumption is obligatory to follow in the Sharia" (5). Al-Razi said: "We say: the analogy provides a presumption of causality, and the presumption is obligatory to act upon" (6).
