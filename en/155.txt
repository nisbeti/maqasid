The group that does not adopt the approach of analogy does not make this approach necessary for the basis of reasoning, and this is evident from the arguments that were previously clarified when discussing their rejection of the analogy approach. Among these is that if it is established that this is a public interest (maslaha), then establishing that the ruling was legislated by Allah for the sake of this public interest requires evidence. Otherwise, the presumed conjecture here is not the legal presumption that is applied in legal matters, which they have described as mere fantasies of the hearts and whims of the minds. Furthermore, even if the basis of reasoning is established, it has also been established that some rulings are not reasoned. The ruling in question may belong to this category, that is, unreasoned, so it cannot be said to be reasoned except with evidence supporting it. Alaa al-Din al-Bukhari said: "Because the original principle in texts is reasoning, although it is established through apparent means, we have found texts that are unanimously agreed upon as being unreasoned, and it is possible that this specific text is among that group, so it is not permissible to rely on that principle and impose it on others with this possibility" (1). Al-Bazdawi said: "Thus, we do not accept the argument based on the principle that reasoning is fundamental in texts; rather, it is necessary to provide evidence that this specific text is indeed reasoned" (2).
