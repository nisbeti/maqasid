He said: "The legal proofs (adillah shar'iyyah) are of two types: the first is what pertains to pure transmission, and the second is what pertains to pure opinion" (1). Then he said: "As for the first type, it is the Book and the Sunnah, and as for the second, it is analogy (qiyas) and reasoning (istidlal). Each of these can be associated with various aspects, either by consensus (ijma) or by disagreement. The consensus, regardless of how it is articulated, is included in the first type, as is the opinion of a Companion (sahabi) and the legislation of those before us, because all of this and similar matters relate to worship based on a transmitted command, with no personal interpretation involved. The second type includes juristic preference (istihsan) and public interest (maslaha mursala) if we say that they pertain to a rational matter, and they may revert to the first type if we witness that they relate to general meanings" (2). The point here is that public interest (maslaha mursala) relates to pure transmission and also to reasoning; the first is to establish that this interest is a definitive legal interest, and this is derived from the examination of legal texts. Thus, it is related to the first type. The second is to establish that this action falls under this interest and leads to it, meaning that its relationship to the meaning or interest, which is a general principle, is like the relationship of a cause to its effect or outcome.

Accordingly, public interest (maslaha mursala) is one of the types of reasoning (istidlal) according to al-Shatibi, and it is termed "mursala" because it does not have a specific evidence that indicates it or to which it returns, but the interest is definitively established by numerous evidences that exceed enumeration. Thus, public interest (maslaha mursala) becomes a principle upon which the rulings of actions that are causes or means for the interest are built, according to legal controls and limits that will be clarified, God willing.
