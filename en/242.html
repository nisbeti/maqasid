He says: "Indeed, these three ranks serve one another and specify one another. If that is the case, then it is necessary to consider the whole in its contexts and according to its conditions. Moreover, the legislator (al-shari) considers from this what cannot be comprehended by minds except through explicit text, which is most of what the Sharia indicates in particulars. This is because rational individuals during periods of ignorance used to preserve those things according to their rational perspectives, but in a manner that did not lead them to justice among creation and equity among them. Rather, during that time, chaos occurred, and one interest (maslaha) would undermine another, or destroy one principle or another. Thus, the legislator came to consider absolute interest and equity at all times, clarifying the interests that are consistent and those that are opposed by another aspect of interest."(1)

The meaning of this text is that in the time of ignorance, when the rulings of the Sharia are obscured and only the general principles or some of them remain, rational individuals strive to preserve what remains by establishing rulings or particulars of their own that they see—through their rational perspectives—as achieving the preservation of those general principles. However, when they preserved one interest, they would destroy another, meaning that they would see evils as interests until the law came and clarified what is considered an interest and what is not.
