One of those interested in the topic of the objectives of Islamic law (maqasid al-sharia), and who asserts that the objectives are the public interests (masalih) — in a sense contrary to that of al-Shatibi — is Sheikh Muhammad al-Tahir ibn Ashour, who says: "The examination of many evidences from the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah necessitates certainty that the rulings of Islamic law are contingent upon purposes and reasons that pertain to the general welfare of society and individuals" (1).
This Sheikh — may Allah have mercy on him — did not grasp al-Shatibi's intended meaning of public interest, but he did recognize the meanings of these evidences. Therefore, he concluded that they are not suitable for this argument. He said: "Abu Ishaq al-Shatibi mentioned in the introduction of the book of objectives from his book the title of the definition (2) the sound evidence following the verse on ablution: { Allah does not intend to make difficulty for you, but He intends to purify you } and His saying: { And there is for you in retaliation the saving of life }" (3).
Thus, he considered only two of al-Shatibi's evidences and rejected ten that he deemed unsuitable for argumentation. If what the Sheikh said were correct, it would invalidate al-Shatibi's claim of having achieved a comprehensive examination in this matter and would undermine his methodology from the outset. The correct approach is not to reject al-Shatibi's arguments from this perspective (4), but rather to understand his intended meaning of public interest in the sense we have mentioned. And Allah knows best.
