The statements of the imams have multiplied regarding this matter, and reports have been transmitted in this sense, in response to those who deny the validity of acting upon a solitary report (khabar al-wahid). Although a solitary report is subject to doubt from two angles, regarding its authenticity and its indication, unlike the texts of the Qur'an, where doubt only pertains to its indication. As for consensus (ijma), in terms of its transmission, it is like a solitary report, and in terms of its indication, it may be similar or stronger. Analogy (qiyas) relates to the three, and there are conclusive and numerous evidences that establish the obligation to act upon a solitary report that has a strong attribution to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) without any condition other than that it does not contradict a stronger evidence, and this is not the subject of discussion. Al-Zarkashi said: "Al-Shafi'i authored a book to establish the validity of acting upon a solitary report, and at the beginning he mentioned the famous hadith: 'May Allah have mercy on a person who hears my words' (1). Abu Dawood objected, saying: He established the solitary report by means of a solitary report, and something cannot be established by itself, like someone who claims something and is asked: Who witnesses for you? He replies: I witness for myself. The companions said: What he mentioned is invalid, for Al-Shafi'i did not rely on a single hadith; rather, he mentioned around three hundred hadiths and outlined the ways of deriving evidence from them, and the totality is what indicates it" (2).
