The statements of the imams have multiplied regarding this matter, and reports have been transmitted in this sense, in response to those who deny the validity of acting upon the report of the individual (khabar al-wahid). This is despite the fact that the report of the individual is subject to doubt from two aspects: from the aspect of its authenticity and from the aspect of its indication. In contrast, the texts of the Qur'an are only subject to doubt from the aspect of their indication. As for consensus (ijma), in terms of its transmission, it is similar to the report of the individual, and in terms of its indication, it may be similar or stronger. Analogy (qiyas) relates to the three, and there are decisive and numerous proofs that establish the obligation to act upon the report of the individual that is deemed to be attributed to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) without any condition other than that it does not contradict a stronger evidence, and this is not the subject of discussion.

Al-Zarkashi said: "Indeed, al-Shafi'i authored a book on the validation of the report of the individual (khabar al-wahid), and at the beginning he mentioned the well-known hadith: 'May Allah have mercy on the one who hears my words'(1). Abu Dawood objected and said: He established the report of the individual by the report of the individual, and a thing cannot be established by itself, like someone who claims something and is asked: Who witnesses for you? He replies: I witness for myself. The companions said: What he mentioned is invalid, for al-Shafi'i did not rely on a single hadith; rather, he mentioned around three hundred hadiths and indicated the ways of deriving evidence from them, and the totality is what supports it"(2).
