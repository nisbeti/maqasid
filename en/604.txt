5 – The fifth category: It is that the action permitted by Sharia leads to a certain harm and that this harm is not intended. It is said that the one who brings about the benefit or averts the harm, if he acts without paying attention to the consequences and without intending to cause harm, and only focused on the fact that the action is permitted, then it is permissible and there is no prohibition in it. However, if he is aware that his action will cause harm to others while he does not suffer harm from refraining from it, then he is prohibited from acting. If he proceeds with the action, he is considered to have transgressed and is liable for the damages of the transgressor in general. Al-Shatibi does not conceive of such an action except in two cases: the first is that the one bringing about the benefit or averting the harm has failed to consider the required perspective, and the second is that he intends to cause harm himself, and both are prohibited by Sharia (1).
6 – The sixth category: He says, "As for the sixth, it is that its outcome leads to harm rarely; it is, in its essence, permitted because if the benefit is predominant, then the rarity of its breach is not considered" (2). He then said, "Thus, the action remains based on the principle of permissibility" (3).
7 – The seventh category: It is when the action leads to harm frequently and predominantly, he says: "It is what leads to harm in a way that is probable, and it allows for disagreement; either the original ruling is permissibility and allowance as previously mentioned in the sixth, or the harm and corruption are likely to occur" (4). Al-Shatibi sees here the application of probability and the lifting of the principle of permission and permissibility. He says: "However, considering probability is more preferable for several reasons: one of them is that probability in matters of actions is treated like knowledge, and it seems to apply here. The second is that what is explicitly stated regarding the prohibition of means is included in this category, such as His saying, Exalted is He: { And do not insult those they invoke besides Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge }" (5) etc., and he mentioned some evidences for the principle of prohibiting means.
