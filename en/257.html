An example of this is also: "Eating, drinking, housing, riding, and marriage, for these matters can only be attained through effort and toil." And as he said: "Those interests are mixed with obligations and hardships." If these are intended interests, then the hardships are evils that are required to be alleviated, according to al-Shatibi. He said: "If the intention of the obligated person is to cause hardship, then he has contradicted the intention of the Lawgiver, in that the Lawgiver does not intend hardship itself with the obligation, and any intention that contradicts the intention of the Lawgiver is void; thus, the intention towards hardship is void, and it is therefore among the things that are prohibited."(1) He also said: "It has been made clear by the Lawgiver that hardship is to be avoided; thus, if He commands something that necessitates it, He does not intend it."(2)

So if interests can only be attained through effort and toil, and these are evils, this means that worldly interests and evils, in terms of their existence in reality, are not absolute, and the ruling on an action is based on what predominates in the situation. He said: "The truth that the Sharia has brought is the combination of these two matters under the consideration of justice, taking from the interests what does not violate an obligation and leaving the interests that do not lead to a prohibition, while maintaining balance in the recommended and the disliked."(3) This indicates his intent with the statement: "And if the other side predominates, it is the understood evil," and with the statement: "According to what has become customary in its like." An explanation of such texts will become clearer and more evident in the discussion of the third type of the objectives of the Sharia, but I will present here a text that clarifies his intent with phrases like "the understood evil or interest" and "according to what has become customary in its like," and his intent in favoring one side over the other based on custom and habit.
