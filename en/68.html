The cause (illah) is a comprehensive description that connects the original (asl) and the branch (far'). There are many comprehensive descriptions, but not every comprehensive description is suitable for reasoning (ta'lil). Therefore, there must be a legal evidence (dalil shar'i) to consider the description in reasoning. Likewise, the intended meaning of the cause is the legal cause. Nothing can be considered legal unless it is established through a legal means, that is, indicated by the Sharia. Accordingly, the intended meaning of the paths of the cause (masalik al-'illah) is its evidences, that is, the legal evidences indicating that the description is a cause. Al-Zarkashi said: "The paths of the cause: that is, the ways indicating the cause" (1). He also said: "Know that it is not sufficient in analogy (qiyas) to merely find the comprehensive description in the original and the branch. Rather, there must be evidence that supports it in consideration. The evidences are of three types: consensus (ijma'), text (nass), and inference (istinbat)" (2). Al-Shawkani said: "The scholars have agreed that there must be evidence for the cause" (3). Abu al-Husayn al-Basri said: "The path of the legal cause is only the Sharia; we say this because the path of the legal cause is how its ruling is established and its effect in it. For example, its ruling is established with it in the original and is negated with its absence, and it is known that this depends on the Sharia because its ruling and the way it is established according to the cause are only obtained through the Sharia" (4).
