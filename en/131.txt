As for what they said regarding the principle that the basis of rulings is reasoning, if a reason is not established by text, consensus, or indication and hint, and the suitability is apparent, then reasoning must be applied. The Hanafi scholars have also rejected this. Imam Alaa al-Din al-Bukhari said: "The criterion of distinction is a condition for us just as it is a condition for them. However, for them, the criterion of distinction is the cause, while for us it is the effect... But we need, before that, that is, before clarifying the distinction and beginning the reasoning, to establish evidence that the original matter we want to reason about is a witness, that is, it is reasoned in the present case and is not limited to its instance, but its ruling extends to others, just as the ruling established from the two paths extends to the pierced navel by consensus, so reasoning may be applied to it after the evidence has been established that it is a cause, because the original in the texts, although it is reasoning, is established through the apparent, and we have found from the texts that are not reasoned by consensus, and it is possible that this specific text is among that group, so it is not permissible to rely on that principle and impose it on others with this possibility" (1). Al-Bazdawi said: "We do not accept the argument based on the principle that reasoning is fundamental in the texts, but it is necessary to establish evidence that this specific text is indeed reasoned" (2).
As for their claim that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) reasoned based on suitable descriptions and beneficial meanings and that they built rulings on meanings close to beneficial meanings, they rejected it. Al-Ansari said: "They also said: The Companions were content with considering benefits, and they were not denied, thus it became consensus. We say: Their being content with it is disputed. Rather, they only considered as causes what they were aware of regarding the consideration of its type or its kind in the type of ruling or its kind. This requires you to conduct an investigation until the reality becomes clear to you" (3).
