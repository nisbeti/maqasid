The disagreement that occurs regarding public interest (maslaha) relates, in part, to the differences in the validity of reasoning based on the sent analogy (munaasaba mursala) or the analogy itself. This matter has been discussed in detail. On the other hand, it also stems from differences in terminology. If we establish that public interest does not revert to analogy, which is correct, but rather to reasoning, then the differing opinions on it are almost merely disagreements in terms and terminology. This is without taking into account the position of those who reject analogy, and the application of the meaning of public interest—at that point—seems to be agreed upon. The meaning that those who reject public interest have dismissed is not the same as that intended by its proponents in the context of reasoning, and the meaning intended by its proponents has been accepted by those who oppose it under different names. For example, al-Amidi rejected public interest, stating: "Public interests, as we have explained, are divided into those that the legislator has acknowledged and those that he has annulled. This category (i.e., public interest) is uncertain between these two categories, and it is not more appropriate to associate it with one than the other, so it is not permissible to use it as evidence without a witness of acknowledgment that it is of the acknowledged kind and not the annulled kind." It has been previously established that al-Amidi and the imams of the Shafi'i school are among the strongest defenders of the principle of analogy and its consideration in reasoning, as well as the consideration of the nature of the description in relation to the nature of the ruling, which is the public interest as understood by its proponents in reasoning and analogy.
