Al-Shawkani mentioned an example of the strange (ghareeb) by saying: "Considering the type of description in the type of ruling, such as the reasoning for the punishment of drinking being eighty lashes, as it is a presumption of slander due to it being a presumption of accusation, it is necessary to establish it in its place by analogy (qiyas) to seclusion (khalwa), as it is a presumption of illicit sexual intercourse, thus it is established in its place."(1)
The scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) among the Shafi'is have stated that the strange analogy (al-qiyas al-ghareeb) is valid for reasoning. Al-Amidi said: "Some have rejected it, and their rejection is not justified because it provides a presumption for reasoning"(2). Al-Ghazali said: "As for the strange analogy, this is a matter of ijtihad (independent reasoning), and it does not seem unlikely to me that this may prevail in the presumption of some mujtahids (jurists), nor is there a decisive proof indicating the invalidity of their ijtihad"(3).
As for the Hanafis, they rejected it and dismissed every avenue of analogy altogether. This is a type of statement from their imams: "As for imagination... it is a false matter because it is merely a form of conjecture, as imagination and conjecture are one and the same, and conjecture does not avail against the truth at all. It cannot be said that conjecture is considered in the Sharia in terms of the obligation to act upon it like the report of a single narrator or analogy, for we say that what is considered is that which has a definitive proof establishing its obligation to act upon it, not mere conjecture. There is no proof here establishing its consideration in the Sharia, so it must be disregarded."(4)
