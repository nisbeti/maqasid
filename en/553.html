The importance of clarifying al-Shatibi's (may Allah have mercy on him) intent regarding the principle of the outcomes of actions in our research lies in the grave errors that are attributed to the Sharia or are adopted as a methodology in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), upon which rulings are based that directly conflict with the texts. This is attributed to al-Shatibi and his methodology, such that un-Islamic ideas become legitimate juristic opinions under the pretext of objectives (maqasid) and beneficial outcomes (mamalat). The examples of the excesses of some contemporary scholars are numerous, and below is one of them, where reliance was placed on al-Shatibi and his ideas regarding benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) in terms of bringing about and repelling, especially concerning the principle of the outcomes of actions, where his statements were cited in contradiction to their intended meaning.

Discussion of a Misunderstanding of the Principle of Considering Outcomes:

The magazine "Al-Sirat Al-Mustaqim," published in America, in its issue "59" in the year 1417 AH, published an article titled: "Political Action in American Society" by Dr. Salah Al-Saawi, the director of the Open Islamic University in Virginia. The magazine described the published article as: a foundational jurisprudential perspective being published for the first time. The magazine stated that this study was a response to a question sent by Mr. Nihad Awad, the executive director of the American Islamic Relations Council. What is important here is not the question or the answers but rather the methodology of understanding and deduction. In this foundational perspective, there were many strange statements such as: "Since means take the ruling of objectives, and means take the ruling of ends," etc. He also stated: "Thus, considering means is one of the established principles in determining rulings, and means are those that lead to something permissible or prohibited, so the path to the forbidden is forbidden, and the path to the permissible is permissible," etc.

Among what the author claimed was the permissibility of committing a wrongdoing under the pretext of intending a benefit, and that this wrongdoing is excusable in light of the intended benefit. He attributed this understanding to al-Shatibi and cited his statements mentioned on the previous page. Therefore, I will proceed to explain this text in detail, as it further clarifies al-Shatibi's intent in the text.
