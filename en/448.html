The Qur'an is the primary source of legislation. It contains the entirety of the Sharia (Islamic law), and it clarifies everything within it. People are obligated to submit to its rulings until the Day of Resurrection, and occurrences and actions are ever-renewing. The previous jurists did not provide explanations for all rulings concerning every occurrence; therefore, it is necessary to derive meanings from the Qur'an, and it is essential to engage in ijtihad (independent reasoning) and express opinions about it. Al-Shatibi said: "The Book must be addressed by clarifying meanings, deriving rulings, interpreting words, and understanding intentions, and all of this has not been provided by those who came before. Thus, either this must be halted, resulting in the suspension of all or most rulings, which is impossible, or there must be discourse on it in a manner that is appropriate"(1).

The interpretation of the Qur'an by opinion means understanding the intent of Allah from the text of the Qur'an where this has not been clarified by a statement from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), nor has it been conveyed to us by the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them).

This requires conditions that must be met by the holder of the opinion and by the opinion itself. The discussion here is not about the mujtahid (scholar qualified to exercise independent judgment) or the interpreter and their conditions, as this will be addressed in a later chapter; rather, the focus here is on the opinion itself.

Al-Shatibi said: "The application of opinion in the Qur'an has been condemned, and there has also come what necessitates its application"(2). He then said: "Opinion is of two types: one that aligns with the speech of the Arabs and conforms to the Book and the Sunnah; this cannot be neglected by one who is knowledgeable of them"(3). He then said: "As for the opinion that does not align with the Arabic language or that which aligns with the legal evidences, this is the opinion that is condemned without question"(4).
