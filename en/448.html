The Qur'an is the primary source of legislation. It contains the entirety of the Sharia (law), and it clarifies everything within it. People are obligated to submit to its rulings until the Day of Judgment, and occurrences and actions are ever-renewing. The previous jurists did not provide a statement of all rulings for every occurrence, so it is necessary to derive meanings from the Qur'an, and it is essential to engage in ijtihad (independent reasoning) and express opinions about it. Al-Shatibi said: "The Book must be addressed by clarifying meanings, deriving rulings, interpreting terms, and understanding intentions, and all of this has not been provided by those who came before. Therefore, either this must be halted, leading to the suspension of all or most rulings, which is impossible, or there must be discourse on it in a manner that is appropriate" (1).

Interpreting the Qur'an through opinion means understanding Allah's intention from the text of the Qur'an where this has not been clarified by a statement from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), nor has it been conveyed to us from the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them). This requires conditions that must be met by the one expressing the opinion and by the opinion itself. The discussion here is not about the mujtahid (jurist) or the interpreter and their conditions, as this will come in a later chapter; rather, the focus here is on the opinion itself. Al-Shatibi said: "The application of opinion in the Qur'an has been condemned, and there has also come what necessitates its application" (2). He then said: "There are two types of opinion: one that aligns with the speech of the Arabs and conforms to the Book and the Sunnah, and this cannot be neglected by one who is knowledgeable of them" (3). He then said: "As for the opinion that does not align with the Arabic language or that which is based on legal evidence, this is the condemned opinion without question" (4).
