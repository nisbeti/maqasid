Another point is that the examination of particulars and texts is intended to reach that overarching legal objective so that one can base their fatwas on it and refer to it in their judgments of ijtihad. If this objective is already attained, then delving into particulars is merely an attempt to obtain what is already obtained, which is impossible. A third point is that the overarching legal objective is indeed organized through the understanding of particulars and specifics, and through their meanings, one ascends to what they aspire to. If the situation does not govern him due to the predominance of the overarching meaning, it is, in fact, governing because the overarching meaning is derived from it. For this reason, you will not find someone at this level issuing a ruling on a matter unless there is supporting and corroborating evidence for him; otherwise, it would not support or assist him. Thus, it is established that someone at this level is very capable of deriving rulings and exercising ijtihad, which is the objective (1).
All the arguments of those who permit this revolve around the lack of necessity to examine particulars, as the benefit of examining them is already obtained from examining the universals. As for his clarification of what can be used as evidence by those who oppose the ijtihad of someone at this level, he said: "And the opponent may argue against the permissibility from several angles" (2). The first: This involves neglecting the particular, which is an error, and one who does not engage with the particular cannot ascend to the rank of ijtihad, "for it has been established in the Book of Evidence that considering the universal while disregarding the particular is an error, just as in the opposite case" (3).
