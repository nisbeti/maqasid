The fourth: what pertains to ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), including qiyas (analogy), where qiyas is the linking of a branch to a root. Ijtihad leads to giving a ruling on a matter after the mujtahid (jurist) sees that the legal meaning related to the ruling is such-and-such. Some matters are clear, and the jurist gives them a ruling based on the clear related meaning, or he refers it back to its clear root. In some matters, the jurist may be confused about which Qur'anic root to refer to, whether it is this root or that one. Here, the Sunnah (tradition) comes to clarify the confusion; otherwise, the matter remains a matter of ijtihad. This meaning falls under the category of clarification without dispute (1). Al-Shatibi said: "Among them is the consideration of the field of ijtihad that occurs between the two clear sides" (2). He then said: "And the field (3) of qiyas revolves between the roots and the branches" (4). He explained this meaning by saying: "This is because there is a text in the Book that addresses two sides clearly in it or in the Sunnah, as previously mentioned in the second source, and the intermediary remains subject to ijtihad. The difference arises from the interaction of the two sides, and perhaps the aspect of consideration in it is close to the source, so it is left to the views of the mujtahids as it becomes clear in the Book of Ijtihad. Perhaps it is far from the observer or is a matter of worship that does not follow the path of suitability, and the clarification comes from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) regarding it, indicating that it is linked to one of the two sides or takes from each one of them in a precautionary manner or otherwise. This is the intended point here" (5).
