This statement indicates that since the view of justifying the Sharia (Islamic law) based on public interest (maslaha) is the opinion of most later jurists, it is necessary to establish proof for that. The proof that is valid for this issue pertains to the justifications if they are understood as signs of knowledge, that is, abstracted from any wisdom that the jurist may find. This is a reference to his intent regarding public interest, and this is entirely contrary to the methodology of justification adopted by the jurists before him, who justified the Sharia based on public interest. He then said: "The relied-upon position is that we have inferred from the Sharia that it was established for the interests of the servants, an inference that neither al-Razi nor anyone else disputes" (1). He then mentioned twelve texts as evidence, by way of example and not limitation. He said: "Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, says regarding the sending of the messengers, which is the foundation: { Messengers bringing good news and warning, so that people will have no argument against Allah after the messengers } (2), { And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds } (3). And He said regarding the origin of creation: { And He is the One who created the heavens and the earth in six days, and His Throne was upon the water, that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed } (4), { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } (5), { Who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed } (6). As for the justifications for the details of the rulings in the Book and the Sunnah, they are more than can be counted, such as His saying after the verse on ablution: { Allah does not intend to make difficulty for you, but He intends to purify you and complete His favor upon you } (7),
