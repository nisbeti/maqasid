Among al-Shatibi's statements on this matter is: "The Arabs use general terms based on what they intend to generalize, which is indicated by the meaning of the speech itself, rather than what those terms indicate according to their individual usage. They also use them intending to generalize what they indicate in their original meaning, and all of this is indicated by the requirements of the situation" (1).

An example of the specification that is rejected here is the specification by reason in the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: { Allah is the Creator of all things } (2). Reason dictates that neither His essence nor any of His attributes, Exalted is He, can be created; therefore, reason specifies the generality of the term "all." Al-Shatibi rejects this statement, arguing that the term "all" in this context does not encompass the essence of the Creator or anything in His attributes, stating: "The speaker may use a general term that includes, according to its usage, itself and others, while he does not intend itself nor does he mean that it is included in the implication of the general" (3). He further states: "Similarly, none of the attributes of the Creator, Exalted is He, falls under the statement in the verse: { Creator of all things } because the Arabs do not intend that nor do they imply it" (4).
