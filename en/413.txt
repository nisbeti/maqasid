This is because it does not have a decisive original that opposes the principles of the Sharia, as the lack of agreement constitutes a violation, and everything that contradicts a decisive principle is rejected; thus, this is rejected (1). Let us examine this text. The legal evidence is considered contradictory, and one cannot categorically state its acceptance even if it does not oppose a decisive principle, and the reason for this is that it is not supported by a decisive original, even though this evidence is a legal text from the Qur'an or the Sunnah. Nevertheless, he stated that it is in a state of consideration.
He continues: "One may argue that the application of conjecture in general is established in the details of the Sharia, and this is one of its instances, and although it does not conform to a principle, there is no contradiction in it either, for if the lack of agreement supports rejection, the lack of contradiction supports acceptance, and they oppose each other. The principle of working with conjecture is acknowledged. An example of this is found in the hadith where he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'The killer does not inherit' (2). Scholars have applied the appropriate and unusual in the chapters of analogy. Even if it is rare in its category, that does not harm if the evidence indicates its validity" (3).
This text is significant in illustrating al-Shatibi's perspective on legal texts and how to derive meanings from them. He does not accept the meaning unless it is supported by a decisive meaning or falls under it. As for his statement: "One may argue that the application of conjecture is generally established," this is not his opinion, and thus he concluded the sentence with: "And although this is rare in its category, that does not harm if the evidence indicates its validity." In addition to his previous statement: "And induction indicates that it is nonexistent."
