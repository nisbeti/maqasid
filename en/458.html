He said in explaining the conditions of the intended meaning of the discourse, which is the inner meaning: "However, two conditions are required: the first is that it must be correct according to the implications of the apparent meaning established in the Arabic language and must align with Arabic objectives. The second is that it must have a witness, either explicitly or implicitly, in another location that supports its correctness without any opposition" (1). As for the first condition, it has been discussed when talking about how to understand the apparent meaning. Regarding the second, he said: "As for the second, it is because if it does not have a witness in another location or if it has an opposing witness, it becomes one of the claims made against the Qur'an, and a mere claim is not accepted by the consensus of scholars" (2). The reason he did not mention here the condition of referring to something definitive (qat'i) is that this condition is general for every proof, and this matter has been emphasized previously. His discussion here is specific to the conditions of interpreting the texts of the Qur'an; therefore, this condition is implicitly considered.

Objections to al-Shatibi's methodology in understanding the meanings of the Qur'an: This is al-Shatibi's methodology in interpreting the Qur'an or in establishing meanings. However, there are interpretations of certain texts made by the Companions, the Followers, or recognized Imams that do not fully align with what he has mentioned. Therefore, he finds them problematic and attempts to justify them or bring them back to his methodology if possible. He says: "The definition of the Qur'an in terms of legal rulings is mostly general, not specific. Where it comes in a specific form, its basis is on the generality either by consideration or by the meaning of the principle, except for what the evidence specifies, such as the characteristics of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)" (3).
