The first: The reality of people as individuals and as a community. A person is in need, and these needs can be divided into these categories. Some of them must be secured or fulfilled and satisfied, and if they are not satisfied, it leads to death or to a significant corruption of life and relationships. Some of them, if not satisfied, cause the person to suffer from hardship, difficulties, and distress. And some, if not satisfied, do not lead to this or that, but they are what the soul inclines towards or what the body requires. Based on this reality, a person is driven to satisfy and fulfill these needs. This drive increases and strengthens according to the intensity of the need. And since a person is a social being, and everyone is driven to achieve satisfaction and is keen on it, they need a system or law that organizes the relationships between people and the ways of satisfaction, clarifying for each of them their rights, powers, restrictions, and obligations.
By nature, a person views what fulfills their needs or satisfies their instincts, or what leads to that, as a benefit or interest, and views what corrupts their fulfillment of needs and satisfaction of instincts or prevents them from that, and what causes them pain or leads to that, as a corruption and harm.
The second: An examination of the rulings of Islam and the conditions of the societies in which Islam was applied shows that Islam came with commands and prohibitions that lead to the satisfaction of these needs in varying degrees, which results in the security of the individual and their tranquility, as well as the security, prosperity, and advancement of society.
