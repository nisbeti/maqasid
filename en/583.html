As for what has been mentioned above regarding the principles of the school in general and specific matters, al-Bazdawi said: "Abu Hanifa (may Allah have mercy on him) said: The specific does not abrogate the general, but it is permissible for the specific to be abrogated by it" (1), and Alaa al-Din al-Bukhari said: "The response to Abu Hanifa (may Allah have mercy on him) is that the general in establishing a ruling is like the specific, then if both are presented in a case and their dates are known, the latter is abrogating if it is the general and is a specification if it is the specific" (2).

If istihsan (juridical preference) is nothing but acting according to the Sharia and acting upon the stronger of the two evidences in cases of conflict, which is the specification, then there is no room for disparaging istihsan or those who advocate for it. However, it may be said that the examples presented for istihsan contain instances that appear to show a deviation from the evidence to something without evidence or a preference for the weaker over the stronger. The response is that this does not pertain to istihsan or its meaning, but rather it relates to the differences in jurisprudential opinions regarding the details of the evidence. A jurist may abandon what is considered evidence from the perspective of another, while it is not considered evidence from his own perspective, and he may advocate for an opinion that his opponent believes has no supporting evidence, leading to the misconception that it is mere caprice and following desires. Additionally, it may be that some followers of the school do not grasp the true statement of their Imam or his evidence, building upon that which is not valid, and this may serve as a pretext for disparaging the school.
