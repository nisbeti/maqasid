The concession (rukhsah) also contradicts the foundational purpose of the Sharia in serving interests (masalih) if it does not pertain to a hardship that is truly significant, such as the travel of a wealthy king. This is because there is a conflict between it and the firm command (azimah); if the intent of the legislator is present in the firm command, then the concession undermines the original principle if it is permissible without necessity or need. Therefore, it has been argued that concessions should be classified under the category of what is permissible (mubah) as part of what is required to be abandoned entirely. However, the concession is a legal ruling that exists, and this does not align with the overarching principle according to his understanding of universality. Thus, al-Shatibi was compelled to oppose his own principles, stating: "What has been legislated from concessions is that the legality of concessions is partial, limited to the place of need; for when the traveler’s journey is interrupted, he must return to the original principle of completing the prayer and observing the fast" (1). He also said: "The conclusion is that the firm command returns to an initial universal principle, while the concession returns to a particular exception from that universal principle" (2). The evidence is that al-Shatibi was forced to accept exceptions from the universals, which contradicts the characteristics of universals in his view, and consequently, it contradicts the methodology.
