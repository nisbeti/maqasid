For although the branch does not have a specific origin, it has a general origin that supports it, and the general origin, if it is definitive, may equal the specific origin or even surpass it depending on the strength or weakness of the specific origin... Likewise, the principle of istihsan (juridical preference) according to Malik is based on this principle. We observe in this text that the general (conceptual) origin is stronger than the text in its indication, because the general origin is definitive in its meaning, whereas the definitiveness of the text's meaning is difficult or rare if it exists.
The primary and secondary objectives:
Al-Shatibi says in his seventh introduction: "Every religious knowledge is sought by the legislator only as a means to worship Allah, Exalted is He, and not for any other reason. If another consideration appears in it, it is secondary and by way of consequence, not by primary intention." This is because when a command or prohibition comes from the legislator, the legislator's intention is to establish what is commanded and to prevent what is prohibited, and this is the primary intention. In other words, the primary intention is obedience without regard to the outcomes or benefits; therefore, religious knowledge is what aids in understanding Allah's commands and prohibitions, in order to know how to obey and how to worship, and this is the meaning of "a means to worship."
