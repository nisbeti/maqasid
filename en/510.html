It has been established that the mistaken mujtahid (jurist) is rewarded. However, this applies to the mujtahid who has reached the level that enables him to engage in ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning). Thus, following him is permissible even if the possibility of his error exists, as the correct answer is not definitively established nor is it determined with a specific individual. However, this does not mean that it is permissible to accept the opinion of the mujtahid unconditionally. Some opinions are definitively wrong, even if they are issued by a recognized mujtahid, and this is what al-Shatibi refers to as the scholar's slip. There are also opinions that are judged to be erroneous, even if they may have a chance of being correct or aligned with the intent of the Lawgiver. These are the opinions issued by those whose ijtihad is not valid, meaning those who have not fulfilled the requirements necessary for ijtihad.

As for the first case, his ijtihad is originally valid, but he may misinterpret the text, mistakenly assuming something that the Lawgiver did not intend, or he may not be aware of the text at all. He may even retract his statement, but the statement is known while the retraction is not, so it is not permissible to follow a scholar in their slip or to imitate him in it.

As for ijtihad that comes from those who are not qualified, it is the source of deviation, innovation, disagreement, and division in religion. Those who do not fully understand the objectives (maqasid) tend to cling to aspects of particulars while undermining the universals. Al-Shatibi has warned against such opinions and statements with great caution, repeatedly refuting them and warning against them in al-Muwafaqat, and he elaborated on this extensively in al-I'tisam.

(1) The mujtahid is a jurist who exerts effort to derive legal rulings from Islamic sources.

(2) Ijtihad is the process of making a legal decision by independent interpretation of the legal sources.

(3) Al-Shatibi refers to the scholar's slip as a mistake made by a recognized authority in Islamic jurisprudence.

(4) The objectives (maqasid) refer to the higher purposes and goals of Islamic law.

(5) Al-Muwafaqat is a significant work by al-Shatibi discussing the principles of Islamic jurisprudence.

(6) Al-I'tisam is another of al-Shatibi's works that deals with the concept of adhering to the Quran and Sunnah.
