Based on this, what has come in legal texts such as His saying, Exalted is He: { Prohibited to you are dead animals, blood, and the flesh of swine } (1), and His saying, Exalted is He: { Indeed, intoxicants, gambling, stone alters [to other than Allah], and divining arrows are but defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it } (2), and His saying, Exalted is He: { As for the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands in recompense for what they earned as a deterrent [punishment] from Allah } (3), and His saying, Exalted is He: { The female and the male adulterer, lash each one of them with a hundred lashes } (4), the prohibition of the aforementioned items such as carrion, blood, swine, and intoxicants... and the prohibition of theft and adultery are among the original meanings and the original implications of the speech. As for what may be argued as the rationale for these rulings or taken as wisdom for them without the existence of a term indicating that or texts from which it can be inferred, such as saying that the rationale or wisdom is the harm caused by carrion, blood, or swine, or the intoxication caused by alcohol, or that the preservation of life, preservation of intellect, preservation of wealth, and preservation of lineage is the meaning of these rulings or the intent of the Legislator behind them, this is not among the implications of the texts. If it were among them, it would be one of the original meanings; rather, the fact that it is intended by the Legislator is a conjecture that is not supported by legal evidence, and it is additional to the original meanings. So, can rulings be derived from it or built upon it?
There is no ruling for the dependent indication:
Al-Shatibi presents a discussion on this issue, providing the evidence of those who affirm it, and he presents the evidence of those who oppose it, concluding with the statement: "The result is that inferring from the second aspect regarding rulings is not established, thus it cannot be applied at all"(5).
