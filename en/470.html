Al-Shatibi sees that the general principles (kulliyat) are the first to be revealed in the Qur'an, and most of them were revealed in Mecca. Therefore, what was revealed afterward serves as an explanation and elaboration of those general principles. Thus, for anyone who wishes to explore the meanings and establish the general principles, they must follow the order of the Qur'an in its revelation. He said: "Know that the general rules are those that were established first and with which the Qur'an was revealed to its Prophet in Mecca, then followed by other matters in Medina that completed those rules whose origin was established in Mecca" (1). He said: "The specific rulings revealed in Mecca were few, while the general principles were more in terms of revelation and legislation" (2). He said: "The Qur'an contains an explanation of everything in that prior order" (3), and he said: "The Medinan surahs should be understood in relation to the Meccan ones, and likewise the Meccan ones among themselves, and the Medinan ones among themselves according to their order in revelation; otherwise, it would not be correct. The evidence for this is that the meaning of the Medinan address is generally based on the Meccan one, just as the later parts of each are based on their predecessors, as evidenced by induction" (4).
