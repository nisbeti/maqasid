Chapter Seven: Al-Shatibi's Methodology in Understanding the Sharia. It contains three sections explaining al-Shatibi's methodology in establishing the rules and principles solely through induction. His methodology in understanding the Sharia and deriving rulings in general, as well as his methodology and views on understanding the Qur'an and the Sunnah. This is one of the most important applications of his ideas in the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia).

Chapter Eight: Ijtihad and Taqlid. This chapter discusses al-Shatibi's views on ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), taqlid (following a legal school), and fatwa (legal opinion) and the criteria for each, as well as the qualified mujtahid (jurist) and its conditions. It is similar to the previous chapter in terms of its importance as it is a result of his ideas on the objectives of the Sharia, and it includes a section on the principle of the consequences of actions according to him.

Chapter Nine: Applying the Idea: Three General Principles. This chapter explains the application of his idea of induction and establishing principles through the study of three general principles in three sections. The first: on the principle of public interest (maslaha) that is not explicitly stated, the second on the principle of istihsan (juridical preference), and the third on the principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i), which includes a discussion on the principle of harm.

Chapter Ten: The Idea of Maqasid According to Al-Shatibi: Presentation and Discussion. It contains three sections, the first presenting his idea and highlighting its foundation and pillars briefly. The second and third sections discuss al-Shatibi's idea and methodology through examining its foundation and pillars.

Introduction to Al-Shatibi and His Era

Introduction to Al-Shatibi:
He is Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Musa ibn Muhammad al-Lakhmi al-Gharnati, known as al-Shatibi(1), one of the scholars of the Maliki school in al-Andalus.
He became known as al-Shatibi due to his ancestry from Shatiba, and his family sought refuge in Granada after the fall of Shatiba to the Christians.
Al-Shatibi lived his entire life in Granada during the eighth century of the Hijra. There is some disagreement regarding his birth and death, but it is generally accepted that he died in the year 790 AH - 1388 CE.
