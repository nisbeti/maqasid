This is unlike the permissible and praiseworthy differences. He said: "We found the companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) after him differing in the rulings of the religion, yet they did not separate nor become factions because they did not deviate from the religion. Rather, they differed in what they were permitted to do in exercising independent judgment (ijtihad) and deriving rulings from the Book and the Sunnah in matters for which they did not find a text. Their opinions differed on this, and they became praiseworthy because they exerted effort in what they were commanded."(1)

The sign of valid ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning):

It has become clear that the one who possesses valid ijtihad (ijtihad mu'tabar) is the one who understands the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in their entirety and is capable of deriving rulings from the texts based on his understanding of them. The question that arises is how it can be known that he has attained this rank. Al-Shatibi says: "The consideration remains regarding the extent to which, if he reaches it, the address of ijtihad will be directed to him as Allah has shown him"(2). What appears from the examination of al-Shatibi's methodology is that the sign of valid ijtihad consists of three matters:

The first: the application of both universals and particulars together.

The second: considering the consequences of actions.

The third: examining the conditions of the legally responsible individuals in particular.

These three matters arise from the understanding of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), and they must manifest during the derivation of rulings, which is the fruit of that understanding.

As for the first, it has been clarified previously, and understanding the objectives (maqasid) is essential; for one who does not understand them cannot apply them, and one who understands the general principles cannot do without the specific details. Thus, understanding the objectives includes understanding the objectives of the detailed rulings. For example, applying the principle of preserving wealth, which is a general principle, in the act of theft, usurpation, or fraud without considering the specific texts regarding these actions is a mistake, as the punishments of amputation, execution, imprisonment, or others all revert to the general principle. This is a clear error; it is not sufficient to apply the general principle alone. Likewise, it is not sufficient to apply the specific details without considering the general principles; this, as has been shown a moment ago, is an entry point for destruction in the Sharia. The concept of objectives refutes it.
