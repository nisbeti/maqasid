Al-Amidi said: "Since the cause (illah) is the subject of the ruling and its basis, the examination and ijtihad (juridical reasoning) in it is either in establishing the basis, refining it, or deriving it. As for establishing the cause, it is the examination of the existence of the cause in individual cases after knowing it in itself... as in the direction of the qibla, for it is the basis for the obligation to face it... and the determination of this direction as the qibla in cases of ambiguity is presumed through ijtihad and examination of the indicators" (1). He also said: "Like justice (adalah), for it is the basis for the acceptance of testimony... as for whether this witness is just, it is presumed through ijtihad" (2). He stated: "Like the intoxicating strength (shidda al-mutriba), for it is the basis for the prohibition of drinking wine; thus, the examination of its presence in the beverage is the establishment of the basis" (3).

Accordingly, establishing the basis is the process of verifying whether the reality corresponds to the reality for which the ruling was given or not. The ruling is the obligation to face the qibla, and the subject of the obligation is facing the qibla. Verifying the direction of the qibla is the establishment of the basis, and it is known that the direction of the qibla is not the cause of the ruling, that is, of the obligation. Likewise, the description of justice in a witness; the legislator has stipulated that the witness must be just, and verifying his justice for the acceptance or rejection of his testimony is the establishment of the basis. Similarly, the intoxicating strength, if it is intoxication, for intoxication is the basis for prohibition, and verifying whether something is intoxicating or not is the establishment of the basis.

Refining the basis:

Al-Amidi said: "As for refining the basis, it is the examination and ijtihad in determining what the text indicates as a cause without specification, by eliminating what has no relevance in consideration from the accompanying descriptions" (4).
