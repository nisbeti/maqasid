This has been detailed in the preceding part of this chapter, but I mention it here in terms of the division of actions into rights of Allah and rights of the servant.

What pertains to the obligations that are a right of Allah or where Allah's right is predominant must be performed with intention, that is, with the aim of obedience and compliance. What pertains to the rights of the servant, where the servant's right is predominant, can be valid without intention; however, if it is done without intention, there is no reward for it, and if done with intention, there is a reward. Thus, the obligated person has performed the action for the sake of Allah's right therein. Al-Shatibi said: "It has been established that every obligation is not devoid of worship, and if it is not devoid, it requires intention, like purifications and other acts of worship. However, the obligations that involve the right of the servant include some that are valid without intention, which we understand from the legislator as prioritizing the servant's side, such as returning deposits, wrongful gains, and obligatory expenditures. Among them are those that are not valid except with intention, which we understand to prioritize Allah's right, such as zakat, sacrifices, and hunting. Those that are valid without intention, if done without intention, will not be rewarded; if done with the intention of compliance, they will be rewarded. Likewise, abstentions, if left with intention. This is agreed upon."(1) He said: "What predominates in the direction of the servant, the servant's right is obtained without intention, so the action here is valid without intention and is not worship of Allah. If the direction of the command is observed, then from that direction it is worship, so intention is necessary, meaning it does not become worship except by intention; it is not that intention is obligatory or required, but rather that the intention in compliance makes it worship, as if one lends in compliance with the command to facilitate for the Muslim or if one lends for a worldly purpose, as well as in buying, selling, eating, drinking, marriage, divorce, and others."(2)
