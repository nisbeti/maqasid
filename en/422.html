Al-Shatibi argues that the ambiguous (mutashabih) in the Sharia is abundant, based on the presence of abrogated (mansukh), ambiguous (mujmal), general (aam), and unrestricted (mutlaq) texts, which frequently occur in the Sharia. He counters this by stating that the abrogating (nasikh) is paired with the abrogated, the clarifying (bayyan) with the ambiguous, the specific (mukhassas) with the general, and the restricted (muqayyad) with the unrestricted; all of this is clarification. He says: "This is indicated by the statement of Ibn Abbas: 'There is no general except that it is specified.' So where is the ambiguity in it when clarification has been achieved? The same applies to other types. It is only considered ambiguous in the absence of clarification" (1). He further states: "If this is established, then clarification is coupled with the clarifier. If the clarifier is taken without clarification, it becomes ambiguous, but it is not inherently ambiguous in the Sharia" (2).
