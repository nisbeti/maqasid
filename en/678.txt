Knowledge of Arabic is for the purpose of reaching the universals from the particulars; once this is achieved, there is no need for Arabic, and there is no difference thereafter between one who receives it through translation while not knowing Arabic and one who understands it in Arabic. This statement indicates the possibility of dispensing with examining the texts for the sake of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning).
What also points to this is his statement regarding the conditions of a valid mujtahid (one who exercises ijtihad): "The degree of ijtihad is attained only by one who possesses two qualities, the first being a complete understanding of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), and the second being the ability to derive rulings based on that understanding" (1). One might think that the second quality includes the condition of applying the particulars, but this is not the case, because the second—according to al-Shatibi—is a means to the first. It is the one that demonstrates the attainment of understanding the objectives, and it is what shows the possibility of deriving rulings based on this understanding. He said: "The fruit of understanding only appears in derivation; therefore, it was made a second condition. The first is the reason for reaching this rank because it is the objective, while the second is a means" (2).
These texts prompt a careful examination of the true intent of al-Shatibi regarding this matter, which leans towards the idea of dispensing with the particulars, especially since he emphasizes the complete understanding of the objectives: "Knowledge of the objectives of the Sharia in general and in detail," "Understanding the objectives of the Sharia in its completeness," as well as his statement: "When a person reaches a level where he understands the intent of the Legislator in every issue of the Sharia and in every chapter of its chapters, he has attained a quality that is the reason for his being placed in the position of the successor to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) in teaching, issuing fatwas, and judging by what Allah has shown him" (3).
