Al-Shatibi's dispute was led by one of his teachers, Abu Said ibn Lub (1), who was known for his leniency in issuing fatwas. Al-Shatibi referred to this in one of his statements, noting that leniency was something students learned from their teachers. He said: "And he said (2): I wanted to alert you to a principle in fatwa that is very beneficial and well-known among the traditions of scholars, which is that they did not impose strictness on the questioner when he came seeking a fatwa" (3). He then said: "Before this session, various issues regarding the statements of Malik and his companions were overwhelming me, but after that session, Allah illuminated my heart with the light of those words, and the darkness of those issues was lifted all at once; all praise is due to Allah for that" (4). However, he later changed his position on leniency and the fatwas of his teacher, leading to disputes and a fierce attack against him, along with numerous accusations directed at him. For example, this statement from al-Muwaq (5): "Look at the belittlement of this al-Shatibi and his denial of the chief of scholars, Ibn Lub, whose fatwas we follow in matters of belief, permissible and forbidden, and his school of thought regarding necessary oaths and other rulings on blood, marriages, and divorce" (6).

This atmosphere had a significant impact on al-Shatibi's thought and his call, leading to his book al-I'tisam being a clarification of these ideas and similar ones, as well as a response to them. The Muwafaqat established principles for regulating the methodology of thinking and deduction.
