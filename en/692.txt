Al-Shatibi believed that there were others who were confused like him, and he expected them to embrace what he had brought forth and to follow him in it. He took great pride in this, which matched his struggles and efforts in that regard, so he refined his idea and called for its completion, not realizing that he was stifling it at its birth. He said: "As for what follows, O seeker of the truths of the highest sciences, aspiring for the most splendid results of dreams, thirsting for the sweetest sources of understanding, hovering around the sanctuary of the apparent decree, hoping to grasp its hidden meaning, meanings intertwined in the unraveling of those decrees, it is time for you to listen to one whose desires align with yours, and to engage with the sorrow of one who shares your grief, and to return, as you share with him the essence of his whispers, until he conveys to you his complaint, so that you may walk with him on this path as he has walked, and traverse in its murkiness, where its light mingles with darkness as it flows, and in the morning, if Allah wills, you will praise the outcome of the journey" (1). He also said, describing his book to those who would approach it: "Let this book be, O pure friend and loyal companion, a support for you in traversing the path, and an explanation of the meanings of harmony and success" (2). He said: "Indeed, it has brought you closer in your journey, and informed you how to ascend in the sciences of the Sharia (Islamic law) and where to proceed, and it has guided you from the paths of the travelers on the back, and has sought for you the brides of wisdom and then granted you the dowry, so take a step of determination, and by the grace of Allah, you will arrive" (3). Then he said: "At that point, it is the right of the contemplative observer, if he finds a deficiency in it, to complete it, and to have good thoughts about one who has endured the nights and days, exchanging toil for comfort and wakefulness for sleep, until he has gifted him the result of his life and bestowed upon him the orphan of his era" (4).
