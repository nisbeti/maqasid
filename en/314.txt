The interests and harms are known only through the Sharia:
... This objective of the legislator in establishing the Sharia is the servitude of man to Allah, which is definitive and there is no disagreement about it. If any disagreement arises regarding it, it pertains to differences in the meanings of words and expressions, not to the essence of its meaning. If we consider the interest in the reasoning based on the perspectives of rational individuals and those who reflect, this falls under the category of rational enhancement and deprecation, which contradicts this objective of compliance. This raises issues for those who advocate for interests, some of which have been mentioned by al-Qarafi and Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam. Al-Shatibi has also responded to them, asserting that they do not exist according to his methodology because he has a different understanding of interests and harms. Al-Shatibi emphasizes, after establishing the principle of compliance, that the determination of interests and harms is a Sharia matter that relates to enhancement and deprecation, and therefore it must return to the principle of compliance. If a wisdom appears in reasoning that is suitable for justifying the ruling, it should not be considered before obtaining conclusive induction, as there may be other wisdoms, and it may not be established through induction, making its consideration an arbitrary judgment without evidence and a misinterpretation of the legislator's intent. He states: "The fact that an interest is an interest intended by the ruling and a harm is a harm likewise is something that is specific to the legislator; there is no room for reason in this based on the principle of negating rational enhancement and deprecation. If the legislator has enacted the ruling for a particular interest, he is the one who establishes it as an interest; otherwise, it could logically be that it is not so, as all things are equal in relation to their original state, with no judgment from reason regarding their goodness or badness. Therefore, the existence of an interest as an interest is from the legislator, such that reason affirms it and the soul finds reassurance in it. Interests, as they are interests, have been concluded to be acts of worship, and what is built upon acts of worship can only be worshipful" (1).

(1) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat, 2/219. And see the nineteenth issue of the fourth type of matters regarding the intent of the legislator.
