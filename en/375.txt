Al-Qarafi and al-Shatibi opposed him in this matter. As for al-Qarafi, he said: "Similarly, one who performs Hajj and associates with it the intention of trade, such that most or all of his intention is to travel for trade specifically, and Hajj is either intended alongside that or not intended at all, and it occurs as a secondary matter by agreement, this does not invalidate the Hajj nor does it incur sin or disobedience. Likewise, one who fasts to improve his health or to achieve the removal of an illness that fasting contradicts, and healing is his primary intention or part of his intention, and he undertakes fasting alongside these intentions, these intentions do not invalidate his fast. Rather, the Lawgiver commanded it in his saying - peace and blessings be upon him -: 'O young people, whoever among you is able to marry, let him do so, and whoever is not able, then let him fast, for it is a shield' (1), meaning it cuts off desires. Thus, he commanded fasting for this purpose, and if it were invalidating, he would not have commanded it in acts of worship and their like, including renewing one's ablution and intending to cool off or cleanse oneself. All these purposes do not involve glorifying the creation" (2). He then said: "Indeed, it does not prevent that these purposes mixed with worship may diminish the reward, and that if the worship is free from them, the reward increases and the reward becomes greater. As for sin and invalidation, there is no way to it" (3).

(2) Al-Qarafi, Shihab al-Din, Al-Furuq, difference 122, 3/23.
