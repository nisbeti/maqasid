The transmission of the scholars' statements on this matter without verifying the intended meaning for each of them has led to conflicting reports. Dr. Muhammad Hasan Hayto said: "Know that the issue of the use of the mursal (transmitted) evidence has encountered much confusion, leading to conflicting reports and divergent opinions"(1). The attribution of this opinion to Imam Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) is well-known, and it has been attributed to him in an unregulated manner. Some have attributed it to the Shafi'is and the Hanafis, while others have attributed it only to the Shafi'is without the Hanafis. Some have claimed its presence in all schools of thought, while others have agreed on its rejection and denied its attribution to Malik (may Allah have mercy on him). Below are some of those reports:
Al-Juwayni said: "Al-Shafi'i and most of the companions of Abu Hanifa went to rely on reasoning even if it does not stem from an agreed-upon ruling in principle"(2), while al-Amidi took the opposite view and said: "The appropriate reasoning is the unqualified... and the jurists from the Shafi'is, Hanafis, and others have agreed on the prohibition of relying on it, which is the truth"(3). Al-Qarafi said: "What is unknown in this matter is the unqualified public interest (maslaha murasala) that we advocate, and upon investigation, it is general in the schools of thought"(4). The transmitted statements from al-Ghazali have posed difficulties, as he said: "Whoever seeks public interest has legislated just as whoever approves has legislated"(5). He also said: "Any public interest that returns to preserving a legal objective known to be intended by the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus is not outside these principles, but it is not called analogy (qiyas) rather it is called unqualified public interest (maslaha murasala)... If we interpret public interest as preserving the objectives of the Sharia, then there is no basis for disagreement regarding it, rather it must be definitively established as evidence"(6).
