If we scrutinize the two definitions, we will find them practically intertwined. The second means that the benefit (maslaha) is typically associated with the ruling, so the benefit is contingent upon the relationship between the cause and the effect, and this association serves as a reason for justifying the ruling based on the description(1). This is not a justification for the actions of Allah, while the first definition implies – in his view – a justification for the actions of Allah, which he rejects(2).

Al-Ghazali said: "Establishing the cause by demonstrating its suitability to the ruling and being satisfied with mere suitability in establishing the ruling is a matter of disagreement. It arises from the fact that what is meant by suitability is that which aligns with the objectives of benefits (masalih) such that if it is added, it becomes coherent. An example of this is our statement: 'Wine is prohibited because it removes reason, which is the basis of obligation, and this is suitable.' Not like saying: 'Because it produces foam' or 'Because it is preserved in a container,' for that does not correspond"(3).

He said: "As for the benefit, it originally refers to bringing about a benefit or averting harm, and we do not mean that. For bringing about benefit and averting harm are the objectives of creation and the welfare of creation in achieving their objectives. Rather, we mean by benefit the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, and the objectives of the Sharia concerning creation are five: to preserve their religion, their lives, their intellect, their lineage, and their wealth. Thus, everything that entails the preservation of these five foundations is a benefit. And everything that undermines these foundations is a harm, and averting it is a benefit. When we use the term 'suitable' and 'appropriate' in the Book of Qiyas (analogy), we mean this category"(4). This clarifies what he means by benefit, which is what has been established as valid in Sharia. This aligns with what is found in al-Juwayni's works. It also agrees with what is found in al-Amidi regarding the definition of suitability, as will be discussed later.
