As for the four categories, al-Shatibi says: "The doer of the action or the one who leaves it may either have his action or his abandonment in accordance with or in opposition to the law, and in both cases, his intention may be to comply with or to oppose the law, thus all are four categories. The first is that he acts in accordance with the law and intends to comply, such as prayer, fasting, charity, pilgrimage, and others, which are intended to fulfill Allah's command and perform what is obligatory or recommended. Likewise, abandoning adultery, alcohol, and other immoralities is intended for compliance, so there is no doubt about the validity of this action. The second is that he acts in opposition and intends to oppose, such as abandoning obligatory acts and committing prohibitions intentionally; this is also evident in its ruling. The third is that the action or abandonment is in accordance with the law while the intention is to oppose, which has two types: one is that he does not know that the action or abandonment is in accordance, and the other is that he knows it. The first is like a man who has intercourse with his wife thinking she is a foreign woman, or a drinker of jallab thinking it is alcohol, and one who abandons prayer believing it is still due upon him while he has actually performed it and is free from it in reality. This type has the intention of disobedience through opposition" (1). He said: "When we look at his intention, we find him violating the sanctity of the command and prohibition, so he is disobedient merely by intention, but not disobedient merely by action. The reality is that he is sinful in relation to the right of Allah, but not sinful in relation to the right of the servant, like the usurper who thinks he is taking the property of the one from whom it is usurped, while it is actually the property of the usurper himself. Thus, there is no claim against him from the one he intended to usurp from, but there is a claim against him in relation to the sanctity of the command and prohibition. The principle is that every obligation includes the right of Allah and the right of the servant" (2).
