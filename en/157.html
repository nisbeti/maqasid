This elaboration and the interruption from al-Razi regarding the lack of reasoning for actions and rulings was objected to by al-Shatibi, but he did not engage in a verbal dispute with him. Rather, he affirmed the reasoning for rulings and actions through a different approach than that adopted by al-Amidi and rejected by al-Razi. He relied on a new methodology, which is the inductive reasoning (istiqra) that he deemed conclusive. Consequently, he reached a definitive conclusion that the actions of Allah - Exalted is He - and His rulings are reasoned by the interests of the servants. He said: "Al-Razi claimed that the rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, are not reasoned by any reason whatsoever, just as His actions are not" (1). He then said: "What is relied upon is that we have inductively derived from the Sharia that it was established for the interests of the servants, an induction that al-Razi and others do not dispute" (2).

Then al-Shatibi mentioned many texts from the Qur'an and referred to others from the Sunnah, all of which indicate purposes for actions or rulings. With the abundance of these texts, he establishes that, in his view, the actions of Allah and His rulings are reasoned by their purposes.

Thus, the idea of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) emerged with al-Shatibi, through his new methodology in proving the reasons and foundations, which is the inductive reasoning that is beneficial for certainty.

As for the disagreement regarding the presumption of interest and whether it is a reason for the ruling, whether it is a legal presumption or merely an illusion, al-Shatibi did not adopt either position, nor did he refute either of them. Instead, he sought to reconcile between them, not being satisfied with such a presumption on the grounds that it is not supported by evidence, but rather it is something that the mind may entertain that this ruling was for the sake of this interest. Likewise, he did not reject this presumption on the grounds that the rulings were established for the interests of the servants, and that this interest may have been intended by the legislator with this ruling. However, he stipulated that there should be a multitude of rulings that give rise to the presumption that they were established for that interest, and thus the beneficial inductive reasoning for certainty is achieved, leading to certainty regarding the reasoning of this interest in legislation. This also illustrates how this idea arose with al-Shatibi regarding the consideration of interests and harms.
