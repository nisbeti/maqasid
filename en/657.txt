This text is important in the methodology of al-Shatibi. Among its points is that particulars are the source of universals, and without particulars, universals would not be known. Universals contain no meaning that is not present in particulars, and if a universal contradicts a particular, this means that the universal has not yet been realized as a universal. Therefore, one must return to the particular to verify the true meaning of the universal and to regulate it to resolve the contradiction. If the universal is established as a universal and contradicts the particular, the contradiction must be resolved, and neither can be disregarded. The universal is established as a definitive universal, so its contradiction is not valid, and the particular cannot be disregarded for the sake of considering the universal. The universal, as he said, is sourced from particulars, and doubt in the particular is doubt in the universal.
However, this differs in another context. He said: "The established rules are universals, and particulars cannot undermine the universals. Therefore, the rulings of the universals remain applicable to the particulars even if the meaning of the universals does not specifically appear in them" (1). This contradicts what was previously stated, as how can the meaning of the universals apply to the particulars without appearing, when universals are originally known only through particulars, and the particular is the manifestation of knowledge of the universal?! He said: "If they contradict, then either both are to be applied together, or both are to be neglected, or one is to be applied without the other, meaning in the case of contradiction. Applying both together is invalid, as is neglecting both, because that would be applying the contradiction between the speculative and the definitive. Applying the particular without the universal gives preference to the particular over the universal, which is contrary to the principle. Thus, the only remaining option is to apply the universal without the particular, which is what is required" (2). This is a clearer contradiction with what was previously stated, for here he says: "Applying both together is invalid," while there he said: "One cannot apply one and disregard the other." Here he states: "Applying the universal without the particular is what is required," while there he said that doubt in the particular is doubt in the universal, and that one must look to reconcile between them. This contradiction is caused by the methodology, which is induction.
