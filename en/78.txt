Al-Amidi cited an opinion from Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani, who said: "Qadi Abu Bakr went to interpret it by analogy of indication, which is the combination of the original and the branch in a way that does not suit the ruling but necessitates what suits the ruling" (1). He then chose another meaning, which is what is below the suitable and above the rejected, and said: "Perhaps the basis for calling it a resemblance (shabah) is this meaning" (2). He said: "An example of this is al-Shafi'i's statement regarding the issue of removing impurity: 'Purification intended for prayer is not valid without water, for the commonality is purification, and its suitability for specifying water after investigation is not apparent'" (3). He stated that this is what most of the scholars have concluded (4).
The seventh method: rejection and opposition. It is based on the principle of reasoning. Rejection (tard) is the existence of the ruling in all cases where the description exists, provided that the description is neither suitable nor resembling (shabah) (5). For if it were suitable, it would return to the method of analogy and suitability. And if it were resembling, it would return to the method of resemblance.
Opposition (aks) is the negation of the ruling with the negation of the description, and both rejection and opposition are referred to as circularity, meaning that the ruling revolves with the description in existence and non-existence (6). Al-Amidi said: "There has been disagreement about this; a group of usul scholars have gone to the view that it indicates that the description is a cause... and what the scholars among us and others hold is that it does not imply causation, neither definitively nor probabilistically, and this is the preferred view" (7). Al-Juwayni considered it a method and regarded opposition as a condition in the rejected description (8).
