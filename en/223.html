His seventh proof: { Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain piety }(1). The evidence in this proof is in { that you may attain piety }. Al-Shatibi establishes through this that the obligation is for specific purposes, and their being intended by the Legislator makes them a public interest (maslaha). There is no appropriate meaning in this text to be pointed out. And if it is said that piety is a public interest, this contradicts the essence of the idea and does not support the claim of reasoning based on public interests, because piety is obedience. This text is similar to the text: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } in its indication of this matter.

His eighth proof: { Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing }(2). Here, al-Shatibi also uses the text to establish a result or outcome of the ruling, and that this outcome is intended by the Lawgiver, thus it is a public interest (maslaha). This proof is similar to the previous one.

His ninth proof: { So turn your faces toward it, so that there will be no argument against you from the people } (3). The obligation is for the sake of achieving the result { so that there will be }, thus the result becomes an objective of the obligation. There is no reason to call this objective a public interest (maslaha) except that it is intended by the legislator.

His tenth evidence: { Permission is granted to those who are fought because they have been wronged }(4).

Al-Shatibi uses this text as evidence to establish that the ruling ordained by Allah, which is the permission to fight, is aimed at lifting the oppression that has befallen them. Thus, this objective is intended by the legislator (al-shari) with the ruling, and for this reason, the permission to fight has a purpose. An example of this is his following evidence.

His eleventh proof: { And there is for you in legal retribution (qisas) a life, O you who possess intellect }(5).
