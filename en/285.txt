It has been mentioned regarding his third introduction that he said: "Unlike the principles, they are derived from a comprehensive examination of the implications of the evidence, not from individual cases specifically" (1). This is because the implications of the evidence are the meanings of the texts and the meanings of the rulings. That is, they are the linguistic indications and the causes of the rulings. The legal meanings are the meanings that are derived, whether from the texts or from the rulings, and they are the universals or definitive principles of the Sharia. These universals must be applied when considering the texts, meaning that the meanings of the texts should be directed in such a way that the meaning derived from them does not contradict the principles, and any meaning that contradicts a principle is rejected, which is the meaning of applying the universals with the particulars. When the Exalted says: { And do not die except while you are Muslims } (2), the text can imply more than one meaning, and it has been established in the principles that there is no obligation for what is beyond one's capacity, so any meaning that contradicts this principle is rejected; therefore, the meaning shifts from the obligation of not dying to its implications.
The obligation that entails hardship is to be considered, as it has been established that the legislator intends to alleviate hardship and that he intends to impose what is commanded. These are two principles from which a contradiction arises, and they are from the implications of the Sharia, that is, from its universal principles. So what does the Sharia necessitate in this case?
This is the reason al-Shatibi chose this title. As for the term: "obligation" in the title, it carries the meaning of burden and hardship. What is meant by this is that there is no action without some burden, and the commitment of the obligated person to the commands of the Sharia necessarily entails hardship, regardless of its extent. Does the legislator intend to remove all hardship? Al-Shatibi answers in the negative. This opens the door to a complex topic, or one that requires understanding in the law and in the policy of the law, which is the estimation of the hardship that lifts the obligation.
