This condition being a condition in the derived ruling only means that the specified reason (illa) can invalidate the original ruling, and indeed this is the essence of reasoning (ta'leel) itself. The reason is what the ruling was established for, and its removal means the removal of the ruling, which is the meaning of invalidation here. Therefore, the agreement on this condition in the derived ruling indicates a lack of confidence in reasoning based on it, and this is only because all paths of derivation are based on the principle that reasoning is fundamental to rulings, which in turn leads the jurist (faqih) to search for a strong reason in their view. If they find that reasoning with this theoretical reason leads to the annulment of the ruling with the annulment of the reason, they will abandon reasoning based on it. The example of al-Amidi above is clear in this regard; reasoning for the obligation of zakat (almsgiving) based on the need to support the poor implies the cancellation of the obligation of zakat if there are no poor people, which negates the original obligation of zakat, thus invalidating reasoning based on this description. Al-Zarkashi said: "If it is derived, the condition is that it does not invalidate the original ruling or part of it, lest it lead to abandoning the stronger for the weaker, as the presumption derived from the text is stronger than that derived from derivation, because it is a branch of this ruling, and a branch does not invalidate its original; otherwise, it would lead to its own invalidation" (1).

And one of the conditions of the reason is that it should not be the subject of the original ruling; if it were, then applying the ruling in other cases would not be by analogy (qiyas) and these cases would not be branches. Rather, it would be an application of the text, and these cases would be the basis of the ruling. The reason for the original ruling would be limited.
