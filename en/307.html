The Second Topic

The Intention of the Legislator in the Obligated Person Submitting to the Rules of the Sharia

Introduction to this Type:

... This is the fourth type of the objectives of the legislator (maqasid al-sharia), and it is the most important according to al-Shatibi, while the previous three types serve it. The first type, which is that the obligations relate to the preservation of necessities, needs, and enhancements, clarifies the goals and reasons for the Sharia so that they serve as foundations upon which the particulars and branches are directed and built. The second type, which is the establishment of the Sharia for understanding, clarifies the principles for comprehending the meanings of words, structures, and texts, in order to know the meanings and rulings. The third type, which is the establishment of the Sharia for obligation according to it, clarifies how to implement the texts while preserving the foundations or universals, and it essentially refers to the application of both the universals and particulars without neglecting either, in order to derive the subsidiary rulings. Thus, the three types serve the process of arriving at the legal ruling. The subject of the fourth type, the obligated person submitting to the rules of the Sharia, is obedience; when a command or prohibition comes from the legislator, obedience is required, and it is not permissible to issue a fatwa contrary to that or to deviate from it for any reason. If there is an exception or concession in a particular case, then the legal demand does not originally encompass that case.

... While the second and third types do not raise any significant jurisprudential or foundational disputes that affect methodology in reasoning and derivation, the first and fourth types provoke considerable debate.
