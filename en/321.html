So if the purposes of the servants and their benefits and desires are found in fulfilling the needs of hunger, thirst, cold, heat, sovereignty, security, women, leadership, and other matters, did the Sharia come with the most upright system to ensure that people satisfy their true needs, and thus the Sharia is for their sake? Or is the Sharia merely commands and prohibitions from Allah that people must comply with, as Allah created in people what He created of needs, desires, and instincts that drive them to obedience? The Sharia commands the worship of Allah and submission and humility before Him, and Allah created in people the innate disposition towards faith and worship as a kindness to them and assistance in obedience, and commanded them to preserve life, to marry, to care for the family, and to provide for them, and created in them desires and emotions that assist them in that? Which of the two is the correct interpretation? Answering this requires evidence, whether rational or textual. If the arguments of those who assert the primacy of interests in the sense of benefits and advantages are based on the first interpretation, al-Shatibi adopts the second interpretation. He says: "The wisdom of the All-Wise and All-Aware has decreed that the establishment of religion and the world is only valid and sustained by motives from humans that drive them to acquire what they need for themselves and others. He created in them the desire for food and drink when they experience hunger and thirst to motivate them to seek to fulfill this need as much as they can. Likewise, He created in them the desire for women to motivate them to acquire the means to reach them. He also created in them the discomfort from heat and cold and unexpected occurrences, which serves as a motivation to acquire clothing and shelter. Then He created Paradise and Hell and sent the messengers, clarifying that stability is not here, but rather this world is a cultivation for another abode, and that eternal happiness and eternal misery are there; however, the means to attain them are acquired here by adhering to what the legislator has defined or by deviating from it...
