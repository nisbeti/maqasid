Therefore, al-Juwayni criticizes those who rely on inference, and he emphasizes the need to adhere to established principles in the Sharia. He said: "As for al-Shafi'i, he is the most knowledgeable of Allah's creation regarding the principles of the Sharia and the most precise in them, and the most astute and quick-witted in deriving them and applying them appropriately and arranging them in their ranks... However, his time was short and his opportunity limited... and he passed away at just over fifty, and that period did not allow for more than the mastery of the principles therein, so it was easy for his companions to build upon them"(1). He said: "If it is asked: What is the meaning of the approximation that you attributed to al-Shafi'i? We say: This is the crux of the matter, and we say: There are established principles with known reasons that the jurists have agreed upon, and al-Shafi'i said: I take those reasons as my refuge and I make the inferences close to them even if their specific instances are not present, so that they are as if they are, for example, principles and the inferences are considered based on them, and considering the meaning by meaning in approximation is preferable to considering one form by another in a comprehensive sense"(2). He said: "Al-Shafi'i and most of the companions of Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with them both) went to rely on inference even if it did not stem from a unanimously agreed-upon ruling in principle, but he does not permit distance, excess, or extremism; rather, it is permissible to link rulings to interests that he sees as similar to the recognized interests in agreement and to the interests based on rulings that are firmly established in the Sharia"(3).
He also said: "Not every public interest (maslaha) is an accepted face."(4)
