Thus, al-Juwayni criticizes those who rely on reasoning and emphasizes the need to adhere closely to the established principles of the Sharia. He said: "As for al-Shafi'i, he is the most knowledgeable of God's creation regarding the principles of the Sharia and the most precise in them, and the most astute and quick-witted in deriving them and applying them appropriately and arranging them in their ranks... However, his time did not allow him to expand upon them, and he passed away having just turned fifty, and that period did not permit more than the mastery of the principles within it, so it was easy for his companions to build upon them" (1). He said: "If it is said: What is the meaning of the approximation that you attribute to al-Shafi'i? We say: This is the crux of the matter, and we say: There are established principles with known rationales that the jurists have agreed upon, and al-Shafi'i said: I take those rationales as my refuge and make the reasonings close to them even if they are not their specific instances, so that they are as if they are, for example, principles and the reasoning is considered based on them, and considering the meaning by meaning in approximation is preferable to considering form by form in a comprehensive sense" (2). He also said: "Al-Shafi'i and most of the companions of Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with them both) relied on reasoning even if it did not stem from an agreed-upon ruling in principle, but he does not permit straying far or excess, rather he allows linking rulings to interests that he sees as similar to the recognized interests in agreement and to the interests based on established rulings that are firmly rooted in the Sharia" (3). He also said: "Not every interest is an accepted justification" (4).
