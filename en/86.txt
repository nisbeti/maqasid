And since this condition is a condition for the derived (mustanbata) rulings only, it means that the specified cause (illah) can invalidate the original ruling, and indeed this is the essence of reasoning (ta‘leel) itself. The cause is what the ruling was established for, and its removal means the removal of the ruling, which is the meaning of invalidation here. Therefore, the consensus on this condition in the derived rulings indicates a lack of confidence in reasoning based on it, and this is only because all paths of the derived rulings are based on the principle that reasoning is fundamental in rulings. This, in turn, leads the jurist (faqih) to search for a strong cause in his view, so if he finds that reasoning with this theoretical cause leads to the removal of the ruling with the removal of the cause, he will abandon reasoning based on it. An example from al-Amidi above is clear in this; reasoning for the obligation of zakat based on providing for the poor implies the cancellation of the obligation of zakat if there are no poor people, which negates the original obligation of zakat, thus invalidating reasoning based on this description.
Al-Zarkashi said: "If it is derived, the condition is that it does not invalidate the original or invalidate part of it, so that it does not lead to abandoning the preferred (rajih) for the less preferred (marjuh), as the presumption derived from the text is stronger than that derived from derivation, because it is a branch of this ruling, and the branch does not invalidate its origin; otherwise, it would necessitate returning to itself in invalidation."(1)
11- One of the conditions of the cause ('illa) is that it should not be the same as the subject of the original ruling (hukm al-asl); if it were so, the application of the ruling in other cases would not be by analogy (qiyas) and these cases would not be branches (furu') but rather a direct application of the text, and these cases would be the basis (manat) of the ruling. The cause of the original ruling would then be limited.
