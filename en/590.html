The principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) is based, according to al-Shatibi, on the foundation of the outcomes of actions (mālat al-afʿāl). This means that if an action leads to something forbidden or prohibited, then it may be deemed necessary to prohibit that action. The means that are blocked are actions that, upon consideration, necessitate their prohibition based on the outcome. However, this generalization does not apply uniformly in Islamic law, and the majority of legal theorists (usuliyyin) argue against it. Nevertheless, al-Shatibi states that both al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa have applied this principle, even though it is explicitly stated by them that it is rejected. He mentions that the scholars have agreed on its application in general, and it is consistent among them. The disagreement lies only in certain cases. He responded to al-Qarafi regarding the contention among scholars about considering the blocking of means: "There are many arguments they use, but they do not benefit, as they indicate the consideration of the law in blocking the means in general, which is a consensus. The dispute is only about specific means, such as deferred sales and the like" (1). He said: "As for al-Shafi'i, it is presumed that he has conducted a general induction regarding the means" (2). He stated: "As for Abu Hanifa, if it is established that he permitted the use of legal tricks (hiyal), then his original stance on deferred sales is only permissibility, and this does not necessitate abandoning the principle of blocking the means, and this is clear" (3). He also said: "The scholars have relied on this meaning and made it a principle that is consistent, which returns to the blocking of means that the scholars have agreed to apply in general, even if they differ in details" (4). Since the induction of legal rulings prevents the unrestricted assertion of prohibiting the permissible to avoid leading to the prohibited, al-Shatibi concluded that the means are divided into three categories, which are the same categories mentioned earlier regarding al-Qarafi: some means are blocked by consensus, some are not blocked by consensus, and some are disputed.
