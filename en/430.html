He said: "There is no escape from considering whether we take into account the interests (masalih) in commands and prohibitions or not. If we do not consider them, then it is more likely that we will remain with them in their mere form, and if we do consider them, we will not obtain from their rational basis anything that we can grasp without considering the commands and prohibitions. For although we know the interest in general, we are ignorant of it in detail. We know that the punishment for adultery, for example, is stoning for the married person due to the deterrent effect, rather than beheading, or flogging to death, or a specified number of lashes, or imprisonment, or fasting, or paying a financial penalty like expiations. In the case of the unmarried person, it is one hundred lashes and exile for a year, rather than stoning or killing or increasing the number of lashes beyond one hundred or decreasing it, among other possible forms of deterrence that reason can conceive. All of this indicates that we have not reached a definitive understanding of the interest in what has been specifically defined, as opposed to others. And if we cannot comprehend that, and it is not possible for minds to do so, it indicates that there is an interest in what has been defined that we do not know. The same ruling applies to everything else that we can understand its meaning. As for acts of worship, they are even more so in this regard. Thus, we have no choice but to adhere to the mere commands and prohibitions. Often, an apparent interest may seem to arise from a command or prohibition, while in reality it is contrary to that, as clarified by another text that contradicts it; thus, we must refer back to that text without considering that meaning. Furthermore, it has been mentioned in the book of objectives (maqasid) that every command and prohibition must have a ritual meaning. If this is established, then there is no way to neglect it, as any meaning that leads to disregarding the mere command and prohibition cannot be referred back to. Therefore, if the understood meaning of the command and prohibition is neglected, there is no way to it; otherwise, the result is to revert to the command and prohibition without it. Thus, the matter in the discussion of considering interests is that there is no way to consider them alongside the command and prohibition, which is the point at hand."(1)
