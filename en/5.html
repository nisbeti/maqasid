They said: Al-Shatibi and the new al-Shatibi, the Muwafaqat, and how much we need what is in it!

Is al-Shatibi to sacrifice the religion on the thresholds of its objectives, and to dissect the belly of the Sharia at the doorsteps of public interest (maslaha)?

The researcher looked into the Muwafaqat and studied it from beginning to end, and from end to beginning. It contains depth, and its author is one with intention, and it requires reflection and re-reflection; so let it be, the researcher immersed himself in it and contemplated it well. The result was that al-Shatibi is slandered by one group and misunderstood by another.

In summary, al-Shatibi is innocent of what is attributed to him regarding public interests and objectives. The researcher continued studying al-Shatibi through al-I'tisam, and found that those who take him as a veil behind which they hide are, in his view, innovators.

The Muwafaqat does not agree with these people, and al-Shatibi warns against them and their methodology. From here arose the motivations for researching this topic, which can be summarized as follows:

1 - Religion is a duty and a trust upon everyone who comprehends it. The researcher saw it as his duty to clarify that the objectives of the Sharia do not negate the Sharia, nor do they invalidate its rulings. If the objectives are the objectives of the Sharia, they are a branch of it, and the Sharia is the root; it is not correct for the branch to invalidate the root. There must be a confrontation with any thought or proposal that calls for disregarding the Sharia or transgressing its limits, especially if it is done in the name of Islam.

2 - Since some have clung to al-Shatibi and his Muwafaqat, attributing to him what he does not say, and what he has consistently warned against, it was necessary to clarify the reality of this matter, and that there is no basis in al-Shatibi or in the Muwafaqat for anyone who wishes to alter or change the rulings of the Sharia under the pretext of its objectives, whether with good or bad intentions.

3 - As the discussions about al-Shatibi and his ideas regarding the objectives or his theory in them increased, and about his new approach to understanding the Sharia, research was presented on this and around it, in books, on the pages of magazines and newspapers, and on television screens, and the researcher found that all of it was far from al-Shatibi and from his ideas, methodology, or innovations; he felt that he had to clarify what he sees as correct in this regard.

(1) The Muwafaqat is a foundational work by al-Shatibi that discusses the objectives of Islamic law.

(2) Public interest (maslaha) refers to the welfare of the community and is often used in discussions of legal rulings.

(3) Al-I'tisam is another work by al-Shatibi that addresses issues of innovation in religious practice.

(4) Innovators refer to those who introduce new practices into the religion that are not based on the original texts or teachings.
