It is worth mentioning here that the issues of legal tricks (hiyal) intertwine with the issues of legal means (dhara'i) since the trick itself is a means. Just as al-Shatibi categorized legal tricks into three types, the means are also categorized into three types. He said while discussing the tricks whose rulings are disputed: "The statement of the one who says that this is based on the principle of the means is not beneficial here, for the means are of three types: some of which are agreed upon to be prohibited ... some of which are not agreed upon to be prohibited ... and some of which are disputed" (1).

Furthermore, the principle regarding the ruling on tricks is based on the original outcomes of actions. He said: "Among them (2) is the principle of tricks, for its well-known reality is the presentation of an action that appears permissible to nullify a legal ruling and to superficially transform it into another ruling, so the outcome of the action here is a breach of the principles of the Sharia in reality" (3). He then said: "But this is on the condition of intending to nullify the legal rulings, and those who permit tricks, like Abu Hanifa, also consider the outcome" (4).

He then said: "But this is on the condition that one does not intend to nullify the ruling, for this specific intention is prohibited as it is defiance against the legislator, just as if one refrains from paying zakat. Abu Hanifa does not disagree that the intention to explicitly nullify the rulings is clearly prohibited. As for nullifying them implicitly, that is not the case; otherwise, gifting would be prohibited at the end of the year altogether, and no one among them would say this. Therefore, they agreed on the prohibition of intending through faith, prayer, and others merely to secure oneself and wealth, like the hypocrites and the show-offs, and the like. Thus, it becomes clear that trickery regarding legal rulings is generally invalid when considering the outcome" (5).
