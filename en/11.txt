Charlemagne, the king of the Franks, attempted, in collaboration with the rulers of the Spanish cities, to eliminate the emirate of Abd al-Rahman, but he failed.
Abdul Rahman revitalized and developed his emirate, encouraging the sciences and arts, agriculture, and industry, so that Cordoba became the capital of Al-Andalus. The civil and architectural manifestations were evident through its mosques, institutes, palaces, and gardens. Development continued after him, and the civilization of Al-Andalus was reflected in the active intellectual and cultural movement. Cordoba received special attention in terms of urban development, beauty, the construction of hospitals, houses of knowledge, and places of worship. Sciences and knowledge spread, seminars and intellectual and philosophical discussions were held, translation flourished, and Al-Andalus thrived, enjoying security and stability. Many Spaniards embraced Islam, and among them emerged writers who contributed to the spread of the Arabic language(1).
Then weak princes succeeded to the rule, and weakness spread within the body of the state for sixty-two years, until Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir took power in the year 300 AH. During his reign, al-Andalus reached its golden age, prosperity prevailed, and urban, agricultural, and industrial projects were realized, in addition to the mosques, schools, and hospitals that were established. Palaces were built, streets were illuminated with lanterns, lush gardens spread, and students from all over al-Andalus came to study in its institutes and universities. The wealth of the country increased, and the state's resources multiplied. A great deal was spent, and much was saved. Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir's rule lasted fifty years, from 300 to 350 AH, and he called himself caliph(2).
Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir was succeeded by his son al-Hakam, who ruled for 16 years, during which he focused on the development of the country. He was then succeeded by weak rulers who handed over the governance to others, leading to its loss, and the ambitious seized power from them, resulting in the fall of the caliphate in the year 417 AH. Small states emerged in the cities, and their rulers were known as the kings of the factions.
