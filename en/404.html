The first: It is that the action is invalid because it contradicts the intention of the legislator, so its effects do not follow from it, and it is akin to what is considered non-existent in terms of its effects. The difference between them is that there was no agreement on the return of the gift, or it did not revert to the gathering after separation or to the separation after gathering. He said: "The intention in it has become non-legitimate, so the action in it is contrary to the intention of the legislator, and it is in the ruling of what has not been acted upon, and it is united with the first category(1) in ruling, so no ruling follows from this action. An example of this is if one spends the nisab before the year in its benefits or gifts it in a gift that he does not return, or gathers the separated or separates the gathered, all with the intention of evading zakat, but he did not return to what he was before the year."(2) Then he said: "So it is like the nonexistent, invalid, and it is joined with the first category."(3)

The second: It is that this action with this intention is non-legitimate and involves disobedience, yet its effects do follow. So the one intending to travel for the purpose of breaking the fast and shortening the prayer achieves that, and the marriage of the divorced woman three times with the intention of making her lawful for her ex-husband is valid by her divorce. He said: "And upon this principle is based the validity of what al-Lakhmi says regarding the one who donates part of his wealth to exempt himself from zakat, or travels in Ramadan with the intention of breaking the fast, or delays a prayer that is due from its preferred time to pray it in travel as two rak'ahs. Or a woman delays a prayer after its time has entered in the hope that she will menstruate so that it is lifted from her. He said: All of this is disliked, and it is not obligatory upon this in travel to fast nor to pray four rak'ahs, nor for the menstruating woman to make up her prayer."(4)
