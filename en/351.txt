And His saying: "Allah does not accept the prayer of any one of you if he becomes impure until he performs ablution" (1), and His prohibition of prayer at the two ends of the day, and His reasoning for that being that the sun rises and sets between the horns of the devil (2), as well as what the scholars of dissent use in analogizing ablution to the necessity of intention... and similar matters that do not indicate a clear, precise meaning suitable for establishing a ruling upon without dispute, but rather are termed as resembling (shabah), such that those who assert it do not agree upon it, and it is only used for analogy by those who do so when they find nothing else. If we do not have a clear reason supported by evident pathways, then the solid foundation that we should resort to is to adhere to what has been defined without transgressing to anything else, for we have found that the Sharia, when we examined it, revolves around acts of worship in the realm of worship, thus it is a fundamental principle therein (3). Accordingly, acts of worship are not to be reasoned or analogized.
