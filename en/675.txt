As for the one who answers that the organization of movement is the cause of the signs, this is a mistake. The organization of movement is indeed the intended purpose of establishing the signs and a result of them. It is only thought to be a cause if one looks at what is in the mind or reasoning of the one who decided to establish them. This person observed the movement and found it needed organization, so he decided to set rules for that purpose and established signs that lead to the desired objective. Thus, if the purpose of organizing movement is considered a cause, it is not a cause for the signs or for establishing the signs; rather, it is a cause for the decision to establish them by the one responsible for that. The organization of movement, in reality, is a result and not a cause. If this result is considered a cause, it is a cause for what is in the mind of the decision-maker. When the answers differ, as mentioned above, the reason for that is that each answer pertains to something different from the others. If distinction is not made, confusion occurs.
Therefore, the objective is not a cause, and the cause is not an objective, and the objectives of the Legislator (Shari) are not causes for the rulings. If we move from this example, that is, from the motivations of people and their objectives in their rulings and actions to the causes of the legal rulings and their objectives, two matters should be considered:
First: The objective intended to be achieved by this decision may be attainable through means other than this decision, and this does not apply to legal rulings. If we know the intention of the Legislator behind the ruling, the ruling cannot be changed under the pretext of achieving the objective. For example, if we know that cutting off the hand for theft does not deter, this does not justify changing the ruling. This is not contradicted by al-Shatibi but rather affirmed by him(1).
