It cannot be said that the Qur'an contains an explanation of everything in this sense, which is that every meaning indicated by the Sunnah (tradition) is present in the Qur'an. Rather, it can be said that there is nothing brought by the Sunnah except that it has a basis in the Qur'an, and this is a point of contention. As for the assumption that the Sunnah does not bring anything that is not in the Qur'an, how can this be the case?! Does the Qur'an command prayer, fasting, almsgiving, and other matters that have been mentioned in a general way and clarified by the Sunnah? Is this clarified in the Qur'an? What the Sunnah has brought, is it not an independent legislation even if it has a basis in the Qur'an?

The issue of the independence of the Sunnah in legislation or lack thereof is intended to address whether the Sunnah brings something that has no basis in the Qur'an or not, and not whether it contains additional clarification or not. The Sunnah certainly contains additional clarification; otherwise, how would we be guided in our prayer if it were not for the Sunnah?! All that the Sunnah has brought in terms of specification, restriction, and clarification of what is general in the Qur'an that is not explained therein is additional legislation that the Sunnah has independently established.
