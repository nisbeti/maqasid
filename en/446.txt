Al-Shatibi held that the Qur'an encompasses all the rulings of the Sharia (Islamic law); there is no issue except that its ruling can be derived from the Qur'an. He argued that other sources of Sharia are merely explanations and clarifications of what is contained within it, which may be obscure to those who have not reached a level of mastery in knowledge, or which may be confusing to scholars and jurists. Thus, there is no addition to it from other sources. He said: "The Qur'an contains the explanation of everything in that prior arrangement (1), so the one who knows it with certainty is knowledgeable about the entirety of the Sharia and lacks nothing from it. The evidence for this includes several matters, among them the Qur'anic texts, such as His saying: { This day I have perfected for you your religion } (2), and His saying: { And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for everything } (3), and His saying: { We have not neglected in the Book anything } (4), and His saying: { Indeed, this Qur'an guides to that which is most upright } (5), meaning the straight path. If it did not encompass all its meanings, it would not be correct to truly apply this meaning to it. And similar verses indicate that it is guidance and healing for what is in the hearts, and it cannot be healing for all that is in the hearts unless it contains clarification of everything" (6). He said: "The one who knows the Qur'an is knowledgeable about the entirety of the Sharia" (7). He said: "You will not find in the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet) any matter except that the Qur'an has indicated its meaning in either a general or detailed manner" (8). He said: "Because Allah made the Qur'an a clarification for everything, it follows that the Sunnah is included in it in general" (9).
