He says: "Moderation is known by the Sharia, and it may also be known by customs and what most rational people witness, as in extravagance and stinginess in expenditures" (1). Thus, when he places the truth with moderation, he defines moderation as that which is indicated by the Sharia, or you could say that which is indicated by the Sharia is moderation or the middle ground. As for his statement: "and what most rational people witness," he has placed this in matters where the Sharia has designated the resolution to custom or to those with knowledge and expertise, such as the dowry of the equivalent (mahr al-mithl), the wage of the equivalent (ajr al-mithl), and the expenses of the equivalent (nafaqa al-mithl). And in defining the rich and the poor, extravagance and stinginess, all these matters are relative.

In this sense, al-Shatibi's statement that the Sharia operates in obligations according to its requirements on the middle and just path comes. He then says that if there comes in the obligations of the Sharia a tendency towards one of the extremes, that is part of the policy of the Sharia in either strictness or leniency, depending on the inclination of the obligated person towards the other side. He says: "If you look at a general Sharia ruling, you will find it advocating moderation; if you see a tendency towards one of the extremes, that is in response to a reality or expectation on the other side... Accordingly, if you see in the transmission from those considered in the religion a deviation from moderation, know that this is in consideration of a reality or expectation on the other side" (2).
