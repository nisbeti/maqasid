Here we are faced with two general principles that appear to contradict each other, and al-Shatibi holds that reconciling them by specifying one or both of them is not permissible. Al-Shatibi's response is that the essence of this specification is clarification, meaning that the definitive general rule was not originally applicable to that particular case nor does it correspond to it. The seemingly opposing specific case is not truly opposing because it does not fall under that general rule. Therefore, its opposition to the general rule is not an exception to some of what the general rule indicates; rather, it clarifies what the general rule indicates, meaning that the generality did not encompass it at all. From this specific case, the clarification and elucidation of the limits of that generality increase. Thus, he said: "The rulings of those determinations are directed towards their generality without specification, and if it is said that the excuses specified them, it is in a figurative sense, not in reality" (1).
