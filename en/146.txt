The third: ... If we find a legal ruling that entails a certain benefit, the assertion that Allah - Exalted is He - legislated this ruling for this benefit is a conjecture, and Allah has commanded us regarding conjecture, so this conjecture is legal, and the reasoning based on this benefit is legal reasoning.
The debates regarding the first and second issues have led to theological discussions related to the creation of actions and attributes. Some, like al-Amidi, for example, argued that the rulings are justified by the benefits of the servants and that this is based on occurrence and consensus. The Mu'tazila also held this view, asserting it as a necessity. Al-Razi firmly rejected the notion of justifying Allah's actions and rulings, and he established the justification of benefits for the rulings without needing a foundational justification. Then came al-Shatibi, who countered al-Razi's position and firmly established the justification of actions and rulings in all details of the Sharia, but in a manner different from that adopted by his predecessors in order to prove this principle.
Thus, we are faced with three different approaches to establishing the justification of rulings by means of wisdoms and benefits: the first is that of al-Amidi, the second is that of al-Razi, and the third is that of al-Shatibi. As for the approach of the Mu'tazila, its subject is not analogy (qiyas) or objectives (maqasid), but rather the necessity of doing what is best in relation to Allah, which is a rejected claim because Allah does what He wills and decrees what He wants, and His actions and decrees are the measure and cannot be compared to anything.
As for the third issue, which is that Allah - Exalted is He - has commanded us regarding conjecture, and whether this ruling is legislated for this benefit is a legal or non-legal conjecture, the opinions on this have been clarified in the previous discussion, and they will be referenced here to the extent that the research necessitates.
