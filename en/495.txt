It relies on the cause (illah), so if it comes through the pathways of text or indication and hint, it returns to the meanings of words and structures, and what was previously stated applies regarding the necessity of referring its meaning to a general principle established through induction. If it comes through the pathway of consensus (ijma), then what was mentioned a moment ago applies regarding the necessity of referring the transmitted matter to a definitive origin. However, if the cause comes through any of the other pathways that rely on the principle of reasoning, it has become clear from his methodology that this is a conjecture that is not supported by any legal evidence, and thus it is among the illusory interests (masalih mawhuma). Therefore, he does not accept any of these causes unless they are established with absolute certainty through the induction of the meaning being reasoned in many particulars, an induction that provides certainty, as mere conjecture is not sufficient for this purpose. He said: "It cannot be said that conjecture is also considered legally in legal rulings, as inferred from the reports of the singular narrators (akhbar al-ahad), analogy (qiyas), and others. What we are discussing, if it is conceded that it does not provide knowledge with consistency and conformity, then it provides conjecture and is thus considered. We say: What is considered among conjectures legally must be based on a legal origin as previously mentioned in its place in this book (1), and what we are discussing has not been based on a definitive or conjectural origin" (2).
