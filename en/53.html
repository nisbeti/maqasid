The deniers of it have acknowledged some of its types, but they have not gone beyond them as a form of analogy. Al-Zarkashi said: "And Abu Bakr al-Sirafi stated in his principles: 'The deniers of analogy are as if they have denied the naming, otherwise they acknowledge it'" (1).

And al-Shawkani said: "Then know that the deniers of analogy did not claim to disregard everything that is called analogy, even if its cause is explicitly stated or it is definitively established with the absence of a distinguishing factor; rather, they made this type of analogy indicated by the evidence of the original, included under it" (2).

And al-Amidi said: "This is also the view of the predecessors among the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), the Followers, al-Shafi'i, Abu Hanifa, Malik, Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and most of the speaking jurists. The Shi'a, al-Nizam, and a group of the Mu'tazila of Baghdad, such as Yahya al-Iskafi, Ja'far ibn Mubashshir, and Ja'far ibn Harb, argued against the rational acceptance of it" (3). The Zahiri (literalist) school also rejected it, and Ibn Hazm expressed this trend clearly in his book: Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam in its eighth volume (4).

The leader of this school is Abu Sulayman Dawud ibn Khalaf al-Isfahani, who died in the year 270 AH. He was originally a Shafi'i and learned his sciences from the companions of al-Shafi'i, then he abandoned the Shafi'i school and chose for himself a specific school that relies only on the text, and it is called the Zahiri school.
