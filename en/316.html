... To establish the Ash'ari (Ash'ariyya) position of al-Shatibi, al-Raisuni mentions what he calls the essence of the Ash'ari theory, along with some texts from al-Shatibi. He says: "They do not consider anything good except through the enhancement of the Sharia; without the enhancement or condemnation of the Sharia, there is neither good nor evil, and all matters are equal. This is precisely what al-Shatibi reiterates" (1). He states: "The Ash'ari influence is evident in his words, as it entails the denial of the goodness and badness of actions, meaning that they, in themselves, are either beneficial or harmful, for actions and omissions, in reason, are 'equally similar'; thus, there is no enhancement or condemnation by reason, and this is the essence of the Ash'ari theory" (2). He then defends the idea that goodness and badness in actions are intrinsic and that reason can, and has the right to, enhance and condemn. He observes that some scholars have refrained from this statement to avoid being associated with the Mu'tazila, not because they see it as incorrect, mentioning among them Abu al-Ma'ali al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali, for example. He says: "It seems to me that Abu al-Ma'ali... has deviated from the bounds of the Ash'ari theory" (3). He adds: "And although the Imam of the Two Sanctuaries was compelled to this extent of acknowledgment of enhancement and condemnation." He remarks: "It is strange what disputes and conflicts can do, that you find Imam al-Ghazali—who is a giant of Islamic thought—fearing and warning against being attributed to him what has been attributed to these individuals regarding the influence of the Mu'tazila, as he was inclined to assert the understanding of reason regarding benefits" (4). Dr. al-Raisuni then attempts to prove the validity of the claim regarding the enhancement and condemnation by reason (5).

The intent behind these statements by Dr. al-Raisuni is to highlight the contradiction between al-Shatibi's methodology and others regarding the issue of reasoning based on benefits and harms. In fact, the assertion of the causality of bringing about benefits and repelling harms for legal rulings necessitates the claim of enhancement and condemnation by reason.
