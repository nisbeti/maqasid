The second: The general ruling that extends from what has been inferred to what has not been inferred does not extend to that which does not contain the cause. Thus, the ruling on iron does not extend to anything that is not like it. Likewise with wood, and with humans, as well as with the sun and the moon and the celestial bodies; one cannot rule on the moon tomorrow in the same way as one rules on the sun tomorrow, nor vice versa, because the cause in both cases, although it is the system, is different: the system of the sun is not the same as the system of the moon. The reason for the generality is the cause, not the inference. This is the incomplete inference in material phenomena, which is only beneficial if one knows that a cause exists. So how can it be applied to the texts of the legislator, which have multiple meanings? If inference requires a cause to be beneficial, and al-Shatibi uses inference to understand the causes, then each of the two requires the other and does not exist without it. Therefore, the method of incomplete inference is invalid and rejected, and it has no existence at all in the legal texts. These causes will not be found through this method, and if they are found, they will be found through deduction as legal causes, not as material or logical causes.
The opposition of the particular (al-juz'i) to the universal (al-kulli):
Al-Shatibi argued that the universal (kulli) is not contradicted by the opposition of the particulars (juz'i) to it, based on the fact that the universal has been established universally through induction, thus it is definitive, while the particular is speculative. Therefore, either the particular should be interpreted to agree with the universal, or it should be rejected.
He also went on to state that it is necessary to apply both the general principles (kulliyat) and the specific cases (juz'iyat) together; one cannot do without the general principles in place of the specific cases, nor without the specific cases in place of the general principles.
These two matters are correct in terms of their outcomes, but they do not hold up based on their methodology, meaning they do not hold up based on the fact that the universals are inductive universals derived from particulars. There is a contradiction in the matter that al-Shatibi tried to resolve.
