And since these interests (masalih) are the results of the Sharia (Islamic law) or are intended by it, some scholars have argued that these interests are the causes (illal) of the Sharia. Others have maintained that they are objectives (maqasid) and not causes. This difference has had its impact on the foundational studies (usul al-fiqh) among the imams of jurisprudence, as well as on the concept of the objectives of the Sharia in the thought of Imam Abu Ishaq al-Shatibi.
Al-Shatibi (may Allah have mercy on him) held that the objectives of the Sharia are its causes, but the establishment of a purpose as a legal objective requires legal evidence. Al-Shatibi had a methodology in this matter that distinguished him from his predecessors, through which he sought to reconcile between the differing opinions, and hence the name of his book: al-Muwafaqat.
In order to understand the reality of this disagreement regarding the reasoning based on interests, and the difference in how an interest is considered a legal interest, or how an objective is deemed a legal objective, it is necessary to clarify the meaning of a cause in the Sharia and how it is established, along with its conditions and pathways.
The reasoning of a legal ruling based on an interest that the jurist sees as intended by the ruling is reasoning in a sense or description that he deems appropriate or suitable to be intended by the Legislator. From here, the disagreement among the imams emerged regarding the issue of reasoning based on the appropriate description. Is the appropriateness of the description evidence of it being a cause, that is, is appropriateness a pathway among the pathways of the cause? The answer to this question is the root of the differing opinions among the imams.
Therefore, it is also necessary to understand the meaning of appropriateness, and whether it is evidence of the cause, or a condition for it, or neither? And what is the difference between the two?
