If the consideration is for the essence of the description in the essence of the ruling, then it is the influencing factor, and there is a detailed explanation. As for the three categories, which are the consideration of the essence in the genus, the genus in the essence, and the genus in the genus, if there are parallels that testify to the description, meaning other rulings in which the consideration of the description appears, it is called a suitable description. If there are no parallels, meaning the description is only testified to by one ruling, it is called strange. Al-Ghazali said: "The suitable is divided into what aligns with the meanings of the Sharia and corresponds with its actions in considering the meanings, and into what is strange that has no genus" (1). He also said: "As for the suitable, we mean that its genus is influential in that ruling even if its essence is not known in that ruling in another place" (2).
Accordingly, the considered suitable is divided into three categories: influential, suitable, and strange.
