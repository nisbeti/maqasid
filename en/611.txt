As for his methodology, despite its novelty, it is considered and relied upon by scholars. In addition to his statement in the text above: "For these are the accepted principles among scholars, and the rules established by the ancients," his assertion that "if someone opposes you with anything other than this book, it is an opposition of denial, and they are blind to the aspect of innovation and creativity in it, and they are deceived by the thought that it is something unheard of, nor has anything in the original or subsidiary Islamic sciences been composed in its manner or shaped like it, and you have enough of the evil of hearing it, and of every innovation in the Sharia that it has introduced, so do not pay attention to the objection without testing it, nor throw the possibility of benefit onto something unconsidered, for it is, by the grace of Allah, a matter established by the verses and reports, and its foundations have been built by the insights of the observers. When the path is clear, denial is not required, and acceptance of what it contains and consideration of the validity of what it has presented and acknowledgment of it is necessary, except for what may occur to humans of error and misstep" (1), etc.
His attempts at reconciliation appear in many places, including those related to the reasoning of rulings by the purposes and beneficial meanings. He spoke of reasoning after establishing that the described attribute used for reasoning returns to a meaning that is accepted in Sharia, and he conditioned the acceptance of the meaning on certainty regarding it. Thus, he did not disregard reasoning by the purposes, nor did he leave the matter to mere opinion. He thus approached the Shafi'is by accepting reasoning by the purpose, and he approached the Hanafis by rejecting the notion that the purpose is speculative. However, in reality, he did not agree with either of them; he was distant from the Shafi'is by his condition of certainty regarding the meaning and his rejection of the speculative meaning in several rulings, and he remained distant from the Hanafis as well, for they do not say this type of certainty, as the abundance of analogies does not yield strength in the description according to them (2).
