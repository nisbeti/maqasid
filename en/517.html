He said: "Among these is that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was asked at different times about the best deeds and the best actions, and he made this known at certain times without being asked. He responded with different answers, each of which, if taken in its absolute or general sense, would imply contradiction with others in detail. In the authentic narrations, it is reported that he (peace and blessings be upon him) was asked, 'Which deeds are best?' He said, 'Faith in Allah.' He was asked, 'Then what?' He said, 'Jihad in the way of Allah.' He was asked, 'Then what?' He said, 'A righteous pilgrimage' (1). He was also asked, 'Which deeds are best?' He said, 'Prayer at its proper time.' He was asked, 'Then what?' He said, 'Kindness to parents.' He was asked, 'Then what?' He said, 'Jihad in the way of Allah' (2). In al-Nasa'i, it is narrated from Abu Umamah that he said: I came to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and said, 'Command me with something that I may take from you.' He said, 'You should fast, for there is nothing like it' (3). In al-Tirmidhi: 'Which deeds are of the highest rank with Allah on the Day of Resurrection?' He said, 'Those who remember Allah much, and the women who remember' (4) (5). This shows that the answers varied according to the circumstances of the accountable individuals, and this is what al-Shatibi intended by the verification of the specific context.

These three signs must all be present in the mujtahid (jurist); having only two, for example, is not sufficient, even if their presence is significantly established. If all are present, then the degree of the mujtahid's mastery of them will elevate his rank, allowing him to reach the pinnacle of excellence in ijtihad (independent reasoning).
