He said: "This, if it inclines towards a people and they find it pleasing and seek it, is due to a superficial resemblance and confusion between it and what preceded it, so the foolish may consider it based on an original principle and thus lean towards it for that reason. The reality of its origin is delusion and imagination, with no truth to it, along with the additional interests and desires that accompany it" (1).
Accordingly, the indications of rulings are the objectives or interests (masalih) that the observer imagines are intended by them; these must be established through induction. This is what is meant by his statement: "similar to moral transmission (tawatur ma'nawi)." However, it should be noted that there is a difference between this transmission and moral transmission in the terminology of hadith science. In hadith science, moral transmission refers to the abundance of texts indicating a specific meaning with variations in wording and narrations; it is an indication of texts, while the transmission that al-Shatibi refers to is an indication of rulings. Therefore, he did not describe his intent as moral transmission; rather, he said: "similar to moral transmission."
This meaning that al-Shatibi seeks to establish through the abundance of rulings that align with it has no indication in the text whatsoever. Searching for it and proving that it is intended by the Legislator requires evidence. It has been previously mentioned that there are those who reject the claim of these reasons and attribute them to mere opinion and the imaginations of hearts. So what is the evidence for the legitimacy of reasoning with them and the permissibility of seeking their induction in rulings? The answer lies in the reasoning behind the actions and rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, and that He does not legislate except for the interests of the servants.
