Al-Shatibi presents arguments from those who claim that one in this condition is very capable of deriving legal rulings, and all of these arguments return to the fact that there is no necessity to apply the particulars since they are encompassed within the universals. If we assume that the universals are complete in his understanding, then applying the universals is equivalent to applying the particulars (1).
As for the arguments of those who prohibit ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) for one in this condition, they all return to the notion that he is not capable of applying the particulars or that he does not apply them. Thus, he does not consider the specifics of circumstances, actions, and the obligated individuals, making it inconsistent for him to be among the people of ijtihad (2).
Al-Shatibi provides two examples of individuals at this level, stating: "Among the examples of this level is the position of those who entirely reject qiyas (analogy) and rely on the texts absolutely, and the position of those who apply qiyas absolutely without considering what contradicts it from the reports in general" (3). He then said: "The people of opinion stripped the meanings and looked at the Sharia through them, discarding the specifics of the words, while the literalists stripped the implications of the words and looked at the Sharia through them, discarding the specifics of the meanings of analogy" (4).
The discontinuation of ijtihad cannot be total:
It has been clarified that ijtihad is of three types, one of which is ijtihad for the purpose of determining the applicable rule (tahqiq al-manat), and this has two types: general meaning and specific meaning. Al-Shatibi argued that all types of ijtihad can cease except for ijtihad in determining the applicable rule in the general sense. If this ceases, it leads to the complete cessation of obligation, unlike the other types. The explanation for this is that determining the applicable rule must occur in every incident; if it is absent, it becomes impossible to apply the rulings. However, if the other types of ijtihad cease, the breadth of the ijtihads of the early scholars suffices for the actions of the obligated individuals, and the new issues that may require rulings are few in comparison to them, which, according to him, does not cause any harm.
