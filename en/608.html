Dr. Darraz answered the question by stating that this is due to two matters: the first is that al-Shatibi's discussions are innovative and unprecedented, while the known sciences of usul (principles of jurisprudence) were well-established and their path was paved, so those engaged in the sciences of Sharia relied on the well-known explanations and did not feel any deficiency, nor did their ambition extend to addressing the book and exercising thought upon it. The second matter is the difficulty of the book, as if there is a meaning under every word in it(1).

However, the matter—as I see it—does not return to either of the mentioned reasons. For al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him) authored the Risala, which was like a spark that ignited a revolution in the science of usul, and had the idea of al-Shatibi succeeded, it might have been a spark of a similar revolution. It is inevitable that there exist many students of knowledge and scholars who would be interested in it. Al-Shatibi must have had students and scholars who revered him and learned from him. If his methodology had the potential for dissemination, it would have spread; as for the difficulty of the book, this would not prevent its understanding, explanation, and development upon it, so the reason must be something else.

The reason relates to the methodology itself, that is, to al-Shatibi's ideas. Although what is in his book is of high value, quality, and level, and most of it adheres to the established norms of the science of usul, it contains what is new, and this newness is foundational, indeed the foundations of the foundations upon which the rules and principles are built. The first of these is the method of induction, the rejection of certainty in Sharia without it, and the rejection of specification in Sharia in the conventional sense. It also introduces new meanings in returning to the certain, and although his new ideas are few in number, they are principles that dominate and govern many branches, and thus the few new principles are significant enough to form an independent methodology that stands on its own and is fundamentally different from all other known methodologies.
