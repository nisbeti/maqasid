It also responds to al-Qarafi, as he raised an issue that is considered a refutation of the idea of reasoning based on public interest (maslaha), and it is necessary for all those who consider public interests and harms. Al-Shatibi says: "Indeed, al-Qarafi raised an issue regarding public interests and harms and did not respond to it, and it is, for him, necessary for all scholars who consider public interests and harms" (1). Al-Qarafi confirmed that this issue applies to everyone, including the Mu'tazila and others, saying (and the statement is attributed to al-Qarafi): "Because deviating from the principle of public interest and harm is rejected by the principles of Mu'tazilism, for it is foolishness, and they cannot say that the criterion for that is that every public interest for which Allah has threatened punishment for neglecting it and every harm for which Allah has threatened punishment for committing it is the intended goal, and what Allah has neglected is not included in our objectives" (2). Al-Qarafi's intention here is that the existence of rulings that do not follow the principle of public interests and harms undermines the idea, and he adds (al-Qarafi): "For we say that the threat in your view and the obligation is contingent upon public interest and harm, and it must be, according to you, by reason that Allah threatens punishment for neglecting public interests and committing harms. If you derived the recognized public interests and harms from the threat, it would necessitate circular reasoning, and if the derivation in public interests and harms were valid, it would necessitate that you allow the obligation to be imposed regarding neglecting public interests and committing harms, and the realities would then be inverted, for what is considered is the obligation, so whatever Allah has commanded is a public interest, and this invalidates your principle" (4). As for those who are not Mu'tazila and who affirm the causality of bringing about public interests and averting harms, al-Qarafi presents them with the following issue: "As for our companions' share of this issue, it is that it becomes impossible for them to say: Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, considers absolute public interest and absolute harm... Rather, their way is merely to extrapolate from the occurrences, and although this detracts from a certain type of insight into some of the secrets of jurisprudence, they say: Allah does what He wills and rules as He desires, and Allah considers what He wills and leaves what He wills, nothing more" (5)
