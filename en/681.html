And a second point is that the examination of particulars and texts is intended to reach that general legal objective so that one can base their fatwas upon it and refer to it in their judgments of ijtihad. If this objective is already achieved, then descending to particulars is a pursuit of obtaining what is already obtained, which is impossible. And a third point is that the general legal objective is only realized through understanding the particulars and specifics, and by their meanings one ascends to what they aspire to. If the situation does not govern him due to the predominance of the general meaning, it is, in reality, governing because the general meaning is derived from it. For this reason, you will not find someone at this level issuing a ruling on a matter unless there are supporting and affirming evidences for him; otherwise, it would not support or affirm him. Thus, it is established that someone at this level is very capable of deriving rulings and exercising ijtihad, which is the objective(1).

All the arguments of those who permit this revolve around the lack of necessity to consider the particulars, because the benefit of examining them is achieved through the examination of the universals.

As for his clarification of what the opponents of the ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) of the one in this rank can use as evidence, he said: "The opponent can argue against the permissibility from several angles"(2). He mentioned three angles:

The first: This involves neglecting the particular, which is an error, and one who does not consider the particular cannot rise to the level of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), "for it has been established in the Book of Evidence that considering the universal while disregarding the particular is an error, just as in the opposite case."(3)
