However, this state was unable to protect the Muslims in Andalusia, and cities and fortresses fell, which aroused the ambitions of the Christians who attempted to eliminate Granada, but they could not, and Granada grew stronger (1). Granada became a refuge for Muslims fleeing from the advance of the Christians since the establishment of the Nasrid state there. Al-Maqri states: "When the Franks seized most of the lands of Andalusia, its people migrated there, and it became the sought-after city" (2).

During the era of Granada, there were three powerful Christian kingdoms: Portugal, Aragon, and Castile, the latter being the strongest of these kingdoms. Each of these kingdoms was individually stronger than Granada. These powers united and sought assistance from others, launching crusades against Granada, and they were able to achieve some victories, but they did not accomplish their objectives.

Then the crusading forces gathered and attacked Granada, and the Muslims sought help from Morocco, leading to events and wars in which the Muslims achieved a tremendous victory in the year 674 AH. The conflict continued, and in the year 718 AH, the Castilians attacked Granada and advanced upon it with the help of the English. The Muslims called for aid from the Marinids in Morocco, who responded, and the Muslims were able to achieve victory and defeat the Franks in a battle near Granada (3).

Confrontations between Muslims and Spaniards continued, and the Muslims were defeated in a naval battle in the year 740 AH - 1339 CE, which involved forces from Aragon, Castile, and Portugal. A battle occurred in the year 741 AH, known as the Battle of Tarifa, in which the Muslims used cannons that fired flames for the first time, but they lost the battle. The Crusaders, when victorious in a battle, committed the most heinous atrocities against the Muslims (4).
