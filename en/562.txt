The concept of public interest (maslaha) according to al-Shatibi:
The public interest (maslaha) according to al-Shatibi relates to the meaning of unrestricted reasoning, and it contains new elements as does his methodology. For a public interest to be considered, it must be established as valid by the Sharia with absolute certainty through induction, or it must pertain to a general, definitive meaning that has been established in that manner. He stated in his third introduction, after clarifying that the principles must be certain and that mere conjecture is insufficient, and that the path to certainty is through induction: "Based on this premise, there is another meaning, which is that every legal principle that does not have a specific text to support it and is consistent with the actions of the Sharia, and whose meaning is derived from its evidences, is valid and can be relied upon if that principle has become certain through its collective evidences. This is because the evidences do not necessarily have to indicate certainty about the ruling in isolation without the addition of others to them, as previously mentioned, since that is nearly impossible. This includes a type of unrestricted reasoning that was adopted by Malik and al-Shafi'i, for although the branch does not have a specific principle to support it, it has been supported by a general principle. A general principle, if it is definitive, may equal a specific principle or may surpass it depending on the strength or weakness of the specific principle" (1). Accordingly, an action that is not explicitly mentioned in the Sharia as either permitted or prohibited will have its ruling based on the principle that has been established with certainty and has become a foundation to rely upon if this action falls under this meaning or serves it or leads to it.
