Therefore, the issue present in the permissible (mubah) regarding the idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) is the same as that in the concession (rukhsah). Thus, for him, concessions do not return to general principles nor serve the generalities; they are merely specific, in contrast to the original ruling (azimah). He says: "The original ruling is what has been legislated from the general rulings from the outset, and the meaning of it being general is that it does not pertain to some of the obligated individuals as they are obligated, nor to some situations over others, like prayer for example, which is legislated absolutely and universally for every person and in every situation, as are fasting, almsgiving (zakat), pilgrimage (hajj), jihad, and other rituals of Islam" (1). He says: "As for the concession, it is what has been legislated for a difficult excuse, as an exception to a general principle that necessitates prohibition, while being limited to the places of need therein. The fact that it is legislated for an excuse is the particularity mentioned by the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence). And the fact that it is difficult means that the excuse may simply be need without any existing hardship, and that would not be called a concession, like the permissibility of partnership (qarad), for example" (2). He said: "The original ruling returns to a general foundational principle, while the concession returns to a specific exception from that general principle" (3). These definitions and statements indicate the mentioned issue, which appears here once again, as he said that the original ruling is general and does not pertain to some obligated individuals or situations, then he said that the concession is an exception from that generality, which means that the original ruling has become specific.
