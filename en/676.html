The second point: The intention of the decision-maker can be understood by examining his statements and actions, and by understanding his mentality and analyzing his psychology, as this falls within the limits of human perception. This cannot be applied to the Creator, as one cannot speak of an intention or a cause without revelation from Him. Attempting to comprehend a method of legislation or laws for Him involves an error of comparing the Creator to the created. This is what al-Shatibi did through what he called the universal laws, the fundamental principles, and the true interests (masalih haqiqiya). He built all of this on the objectives of the legislator (maqasid al-sharia), which he sought to understand through induction and established as definitive causes.

His methodology is also criticized from this perspective, which is considering the objective as a cause and the resulting comparison of the Creator to the created. This methodology led to a dangerous conclusion, and indeed serves as a sign of the error in the methodology, prompting a reconsideration of it, which is the reliance on universals over particulars, the next topic.

Reliance on universals over particulars: It has become clear from al-Shatibi's methodology that legislation has objectives that are its causes or universal principles upon which particulars are based or for their sake, which are the texts and rulings. The rulings stem from the objectives of the texts, and both of their objectives are those universal principles.

Universals can only be understood through the induction of particulars, as they are their source and evidence. Without particulars, universals would not be known. It is also evident that both particulars and universals must be applied together; applying the universal without the particular is an error, just as applying the particular without the universal is an error (1).

One might object to al-Shatibi by asking what is the need to apply the particular if the universal has already been understood, as the meaning of the particular exists within it, and examining the particular afterward is a burden. If a contradiction is assumed between the particular and the universal, or if a specificity of the particular is not considered in the universal, this means that the universal is no longer universal, contrary to the assumption, and thus there is no need to apply the particular (2).
