7- The reason must be transitive. That is, the ruling must be transitive from the original case to the branch. Everyone has agreed on the validity of this condition for the validity of analogy (qiyas). However, they have differed on the validity of the reason if it is not transitive. Some of the usulists consider the description to be a reason for the original case even if the ruling cannot be extended to a branch by analogy. Others have prohibited considering it a reason on the basis that there is no benefit in reasoning with it. The benefit of the reason is the transference of the ruling from the original case to the branch. Al-Amidi said: "Everyone has agreed that the transitivity of the reason is a condition for the validity of analogy, and on the validity of the non-transitive reason, whether it is explicitly stated or agreed upon. They only differed on the validity of the non-transitive reason if it is not explicitly stated or agreed upon, such as the reasoning of the followers of al-Shafi'i regarding the prohibition of usury in the two currencies based on the essence of their value."

And Shams al-A'imma al-Sarakhsi said: "The disagreement is about the condition of transitivity, and our position is that reasoning with the text in a way that does not transfer is not permissible at all. According to al-Shafi'i, this reasoning is permissible, but it does not constitute analogy. Based on this, he permitted reasoning with the text of usury in gold and silver based on their value even though it does not transfer. We do not permit that, and our position is that the ruling of reasoning is the transference of the ruling of the original case to the branches."

(1) The intended meaning of the non-transitive reason is the description that has been established as a reason for the ruling of the original case, and it cannot be transferred to a branch, such as theft being a reason for cutting off the hand. The text indicates the reasoning for the ruling of the original case with it. However, there is no branch to which the ruling can be extended by analogy based on the commonality of the reason. Applying the ruling of cutting in another context is based on the text itself, that is, by the generality of the text and not by analogy. Among the related disagreements of the analogists here is another disagreement, which is whether the evidence for the reason is the reason itself or the evidence of the address that indicates it and the reason? The Shafi'is and the theologians went with the first view. The Hanafis went with the second.
