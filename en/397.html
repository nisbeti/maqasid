Al-Shatibi (may Allah have mercy on him) held that legal stratagems (hiyal) in religion are generally not permissible, and this means that there are very few cases in which legal stratagems are allowed. Therefore, it is necessary to have a criterion to determine the cases in which legal stratagems are permissible. Legal stratagems are actions undertaken by a legally responsible person with the intention of changing a legal ruling, which contradicts the intention of the Lawgiver. So, what are the cases in which this contradiction is valid? Al-Shatibi's answer is that if this contradiction is intended to oppose the objectives of the Lawgiver, then it is prohibited. However, if this contradiction is intended to preserve the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), then it is permissible.

He said: "The legal ruses (hiyal) in religion in the mentioned sense are not generally permissible"(1). It has been previously stated that legal ruses can occur in causes, conditions, and impediments. An example of this in causes is as he said: "In the school, if someone swears by divorce that so-and-so will pay his due by a certain time, then fears breaking his oath due to non-payment, he may divorce his wife until the time elapses and the oath is broken, and he is not considered to have a wife, as the breaking of the oath does not apply to him, even though his intention is blameworthy and his action is blameworthy because he has resorted to a ruse that invalidated a right. Thus, the violation is prohibited even if it resulted in the avoidance of breaking the oath"(2). Another example is: "The ruses of the people of 'aina in making the commodity an intermediary in selling one dinar for two dinars for a deferred payment"(3).
