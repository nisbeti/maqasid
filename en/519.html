As for the one who has a clear state on the other side, he is the one who exhibits the three signs. It is said about him: "He achieves through the legal meanings a status concerning the particular subsidiary issues such that the depth of insight into one side does not prevent him from the depth of insight into the other side; he does not adhere to the generality of one of them without comparing it to the other, and he then reflects on the application of what he has summarized to what is appropriate in the actions of the obligated" (1). And it is said: "There is no disagreement regarding the validity of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) from one who possesses this rank" (2). It is said about him: "And among his characteristics are two matters: the first is that he answers the questioner according to what is appropriate for him in his particular state" (3). "The second is that he considers the outcomes before answering the questions" (4).

As for the one whose state is unclear, he is situated between the two clear sides. He has completed the understanding of the legal universals and the extrapolation of the particulars, and he has not fallen short in this regard at all. However, when he examines the issues, he applies the universals without paying attention to the particulars. It is said about him: "He should conclude by examining what has been established according to what the legal proof has led him to, such that he attains certainty without being opposed by doubt; rather, doubts that arise against him become like the proofs indicating the correctness of what he holds" (5). Then it was said: "But he remains in this state until his memorization slips from his retention of a ruling, even if it is present with him, so he does not care about definitively ruling on the issues, whether he has stated them or opposed them" (6). And it was remarked: "So if the student reaches this rank, is it valid for him to exercise ijtihad in the legal rulings or not? This is a matter of consideration and ambiguity, and it is one in which disagreement arises" (7).
