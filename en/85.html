As for those who associate the following rationale with the method of indication and hinting at the specified rationales, like those that come through the method of text or consensus, they argue about the difference in the permissibility of specifying the specified rationales due to the difference in specifying those that come through the method of indication and hinting. The derived rationale for this group is the rationale that comes through all other methods such as analogy (qiyas), counter-analogy (al-qiyas al-‘aks), examination and division (sabr wa taqsim), relevance (munasaba), and similarity (shabah), and they do not permit specifying it. They assert that it is not permissible to specify the derived rationale, because the failure to consider the described attribute through these methods is akin to nullifying the attribute or akin to its non-establishment altogether. This is due to the lack of trust in these methods in the first place. Thus, the branching of disagreement in this issue and the confusion in the transmission from the imams is attributed to the difference in the intended meanings of the terminologies, which is something that should be noted when studying the books of the imams of usul (principles of jurisprudence) concerning analogy and rationale. Al-Zarkashi said: "Know that the rationale is either rational (aqli) or textual (sam'i). The rational rationale cannot be specified by consensus of the scholars... They only disagreed regarding the textual rationale, which can either be derived or specified. If it is derived, then al-Mawardi and al-Ruyani firmly stated that it is not permissible to specify it, meaning that the rationale does not remain a proof beyond the specific ruling due to the invalidity of relying on it" (1).

One of the conditions for the derived rationale is that it should not return to its origin by nullification, and this condition applies only to the derived and not to the specified. Al-Amidi said: "The derived rationale must not be something that returns to the ruling it was derived from by nullification. This is like justifying the obligation of a sheep in the case of zakat by the need of the poor, as this would negate the obligation of the sheep, and the elevation of the original ruling from which it was derived necessitates the nullification of the derived rationale due to the necessity of its validity being contingent upon its consideration" (2).
