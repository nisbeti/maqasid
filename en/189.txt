As for the study of the cause, it is an important foundation for his idea, as it is based on the notion that the legal rulings have objectives, and that the Sharia was established to achieve these objectives. These objectives are causes or results, and their causes are the legal rulings. Thus, the legal ruling is a cause for an effect, and the required action has an end result that arises from it. Just as the legislator intended to bring about the required action, he also intended for the end result to occur. The prohibited action has an end or result that the legislator intended to prevent from occurring, and just as the legislator intended to prevent the action, he also intended to prevent its result. Therefore, the study of causes and effects is the foundation upon which the idea of objectives (maqasid) rests according to al-Shatibi.
The permissible (mubah):
A reader may be surprised by al-Shatibi's lengthy and detailed discussion of the permissible, and the differences in his reports compared to other usul scholars, to the extent that his discussion of the legal obligations seems to focus solely on the permissible, while his discussion of other rulings—namely, the obligatory, recommended, disliked, and forbidden—is limited to a few sentences or paragraphs.
However, studying these discussions, after understanding his idea of objectives, sheds light on this approach, as al-Shatibi considers the foundational principles he has established and the relationship between these principles as the basis for his opinions and reports regarding the permissible. This necessitates presenting some of the foundational principles or rules of his idea of objectives.
Al-Shatibi concludes—through induction—that the Sharia was revealed for the interests of the servants, and these interests are the objectives of legislation, which can be categorized into three sections: necessary, needful, and embellishing, with each of these sections having its complements.

(1) The objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) refer to the goals and purposes that the Sharia aims to achieve for the welfare of humanity.
(2) The three categories of interests—necessary, needful, and embellishing—are classifications that help in understanding the different levels of importance of various legal rulings.
