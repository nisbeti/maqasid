Al-Shatibi says: "The Sunnah (tradition) refers back in its meaning to the Book (Qur'an); it is a detailed explanation of what is general, a clarification of what is ambiguous, and an elaboration of what is concise. This is because it is an explanation of it, as indicated by His saying, Exalted is He: { And We have sent down to you the Reminder that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them } (1). You will not find in the Sunnah any matter except that the Qur'an has indicated its meaning either in a general or detailed way. Furthermore, everything that indicates that the Qur'an is the totality of the Sharia (Islamic law) and its source is evidence for that" (2).

The Sunnah comes second in its indication of rulings after the Book, as it is an explanation and elaboration of it, so it cannot precede it. Also, the reports of the Sunnah are of uncertain authenticity, while the Qur'an is entirely definitive. The certainty in the Sunnah is in its authenticity as a source of legislation, that is, as a general proof. In fact, al-Shatibi, just as he denied the existence of definitive indications of the texts on meanings, he also denied—or nearly denied—the existence of mutawatir (consecutively reported) reports other than the Qur'an. Thus, the indication of the Sunnah is uncertain from two aspects: from the aspect of authenticity and from the aspect of indication.

He said: "The rank of the Sunnah is to be lower than the Book in consideration, and the evidence for this is as follows: one of them is that the Book is definitive, while the Sunnah is presumed; and the certainty in it is valid in general, not in detail, unlike the Book, which is definitive in both general and detailed terms, and the definitive is prioritized over the presumed, so it follows that the Book is prioritized over the Sunnah" (3). He mentioned other matters. Regarding the existence of mutawatir reports transmitted from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he said: "What has been mentioned about the mutawatir reports is mostly a hypothetical matter, and you may not find in the prophetic reports anything that can be adjudged as mutawatir up to the time of the incident. Thus, the discussion mentioned in the issue is a discussion about something that is not real or is rarely occurring, and there is little benefit in it, and Allah knows best" (4).
