He said in explaining the conditions of the intended meaning of the speech, which is the inner meaning: "However, two conditions are required: the first is that it must be valid according to the implications of the apparent meaning established in the Arabic language and must align with the Arabic objectives. The second is that there must be a supporting evidence, either textual or apparent, in another place that attests to its validity without opposition"(1). As for the first condition, it has been discussed when talking about how to understand the apparent meaning. Regarding the second, he said: "As for the second, it is because if there is no supporting evidence in another place or if there is opposition, it becomes one of the claims made against the Qur'an, and mere claims are not accepted by the consensus of scholars"(2). As for the absence of mention here of the condition of referring to the definitive (qat'i), it is because this condition is general for every proof, and this matter has been emphasized previously. His discussion here is specific to the conditions of interpreting the texts of the Qur'an; therefore, this condition is implicitly considered.
Issues with al-Shatibi's Methodology in Understanding the Meanings of the Qur'an:
This is the methodology of al-Shatibi in interpreting the Qur'an or in explaining meanings, but there are interpretations of some texts given by the Companions (sahaba), the Successors (tabi'un), or recognized Imams that do not fully align with what he mentioned. Therefore, he finds them problematic and attempts to justify them or return them to his methodology if possible. He says: "The definition of the Qur'an in terms of legal rulings is mostly general, not specific, and where it is specific, its basis is on the generality either by consideration or by the meaning of the origin, except for what the evidence specifies, such as the characteristics of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)"(3).
