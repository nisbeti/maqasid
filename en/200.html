Based on this understanding of al-Shatibi's intent in his discussion of the permissible (mubah) and its details, it can be judged that most of the explanations and commentaries by the commentators on the book al-Muwafaqat regarding this topic are far from the truth, and indeed have no connection to al-Shatibi's intended meaning, let alone that some of them are not free from confusion and meaningless discourse(1).

The rank of exemption:

This is what leads al-Shatibi to assume a sixth type of rulings, which applies to these particular rulings and others that resemble them in being particulars that do not serve the universals. He says: "It is valid for there to be a rank of exemption between the lawful (halal) and the unlawful (haram), so it cannot be judged as one of the five"(2).

Included in this rank are matters that are not burdensome, concessions (rukhas), and actions devoid of intention, among others. He states: "It makes no difference whether we assume those actions are commanded or prohibited or whether there is choice in them, because if they are neither prohibited nor commanded nor subject to choice, they revert to the category of what has no ruling in the Sharia, which is the meaning of exemption. Even if there is a command or prohibition attached to them, the condition for holding someone accountable for it is the mention of the command and prohibition and the ability to comply, and this is impossible in the case of the mistaken, the heedless, and the forgetful, as well as the sleeper and the insane... Among them is the error in ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning)... Thus, the essence of this is that there is no burden on him for what he has left and for what he has done, and among them are the concessions of various kinds, as the texts indicate by stating the lifting of burdens and the removal of hardship and the attainment of forgiveness"(3).
