This is due to his methodology regarding causes and effects. As for when the action aligns with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), but this alignment is incidental or due to necessity, meaning it was not intended to align, then the action is valid in one sense and invalid in another. It is valid in the sense that its effects will be realized in this world; it absolves one of liability, acquires property, permits sexual relations, and nullifies any judicial claims where there is a claim. It is invalid in the sense that no reward will be assigned to it. The doer may incur blame and sin, depending on the intention and purpose, as he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Actions are judged by intentions." The rulings regarding acts of worship differ in this matter from those regarding customary practices. Al-Shatibi has a thorough discussion on this, which I will summarize briefly. He states: "What the servants are commanded to do falls into two categories: the first is acts of worship... and the second is customary practices. As for the first, it cannot be devoid of a worldly or otherworldly benefit; if it is otherworldly, this is a benefit established by the Sharia (1)... If it is established by the Sharia, then seeking it is valid... If the benefit sought through acts of worship is in this world, it is of two types: one type relates to the improvement of one’s reputation and the good opinion of people, and the belief in the virtue of the doer due to his action, and the other type relates to attaining one’s benefit in this world. This can be divided into two: one pertains to what concerns the individual himself, while being heedless of seeking the admiration of people through the action, and the other pertains to seeking admiration in order to gain wealth, status, or other benefits. Thus, there are three categories: the first relates to improving the opinion of people and believing in virtue; if this intention is pursued, there is no doubt that it is showing off (riya) because it is driven by the desire for praise and to be thought well of, while he still performs his obligatory or voluntary prayers, which is clear. If it is secondary, it is a matter of consideration and scholarly interpretation, and the scholars have differed on this principle" (2). Al-Shatibi tends to validate the act of worship in this case, which is when the pursuit of a good opinion of him is secondary and not primary.
