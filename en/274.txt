Therefore, al-Shatibi turns away from delving into the discussions of the attributes of Allah, which are beyond the comprehension of the servant, and directs attention to the texts that are the subject of disagreement and dispute in this matter to what is actionable. He considers deep investigation into them a departure from the requirements of the Sharia. He says: "Thus, you find that the Sharia has only defined divine matters in a way that can be understood, and has deferred other matters, defining them according to the implications of names and attributes, and has encouraged reflection on the created beings as a semblance of that, and has referred what leads to confusion to a general principle, which is His saying, Exalted is He: { There is nothing like Him } (1)" (2). He says: "The Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) did not convey to us any engagement in these matters that would serve as a foundation for researchers and those who overreach, nor did it come from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), nor from the followers who are to be emulated" (3). He said: "Thus, deep investigation into them and seeking what the majority does not share in understanding is a departure from the requirements of the original Sharia, for the soul may rush to seek what is not required of it, leading it into a darkness from which it cannot escape" (4).
Based on this - also - he refutes the claim that the Qur'an contains all sciences, including natural sciences and logic, citing His saying, Exalted is He: { And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things } (5), and His saying: { We have not neglected in the Book anything } (6). Such meanings are not known, and no one among those who preceded has claimed them, so they cannot be established. It is not permissible to attribute to the Qur'an what it does not imply, just as it is not correct to deny from it what it does imply, and one must limit oneself in seeking to understand it to all that is attributed to the Arabs, including the meanings of words and structures, and their styles (7).
