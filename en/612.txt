Among his attempts at reconciliation is his statement regarding generality and specificity. It is known that the Hanafis do not restrict the definitive (qat'i) by the speculative (dhanni); they do not restrict the Qur'an or the mutawatir (consecutively transmitted) Sunnah by a single report (khabar ahad) or analogy (qiyas). This is based on their belief that the indication of the general (al-'amm) towards its specific instances (afrad) is definitive, so nothing from these instances can be excluded by speculative evidence. This is in contrast to the majority of usul scholars, who do restrict the definitive general by the speculative. What al-Shatibi brings forth here is the denial of restriction in its entirety; wherever he speaks of it, it is in the sense of clarification (bayān) (1). Thus, if some instances are specified from the general, this does not mean they are excluded from the generality of the text; rather, it clarifies that they were not included in it from the outset. Here, he approaches the Shafi'is in that the indication of the general towards all its instances is speculative. He has reiterated the texts of al-Shafi'i, stating that the general is used and intended to mean the specific. This is speculative in his view, as he denies definitive indication in the texts, and thus, he does not affirm definitiveness in meaning except through induction (istiqra). He also aligns with the Hanafis in stating that if the meaning is definitively established, it is not restricted. Thus, it becomes a universal principle, and universals cannot be restricted; therefore, the definitive universal cannot be restricted by a particular or by the speculative.
While he approaches both groups, he differs from them; he differs from the Shafi'is by rejecting restriction altogether and by not restricting universals by particulars, that is, the definitive by the speculative. He also differs from the Hanafis by rejecting restriction altogether, which means not restricting the universal by the universal, that is, the definitive by the definitive. Wherever he speaks of restricting some universals by others, he does not intend exclusion but rather clarification, as previously mentioned.
One can observe the approximation and reconciliation in the two mentioned examples, but the reality is that he was different—in both cases—with both groups; therefore, his statement will not be accepted by either of them. This may be one of the factors for the lack of spread of this methodology and its development. In both cases, the method of definitiveness, whether through wisdom or through the indication of the general towards its instances, can only be through induction, which is not customary among either of the groups.
