As for the ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) issued by someone who is not qualified for it, where he believes himself or others believe him to be qualified, he said: "It is likely that he believes about himself or he believes others believe about him that he is among the people of ijtihad and that his opinion is valid, and his opposition may sometimes be in a particular matter, which is lighter, and sometimes in a general principle of the Sharia and its overarching foundations, whether they are from the fundamentals of beliefs or actions. You see him taking some particulars that undermine its universals until he reaches what appears to him from his initial opinion without comprehending its meanings nor referring back to the necessity of relying on them" (1). He then said: "What drives this is some hidden desires in the souls" (2). He said: "And this ignorance is aided by a lack of understanding of the objectives of the Sharia" (3). He said: "The root of this category is mentioned in the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: { He it is Who has sent down to you the Book; in it are verses that are precise—they are the foundation of the Book—and others that are ambiguous } (4), and in the authentic narration: 'The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) recited this verse and then said: When you see those who follow what is ambiguous therein, they are the ones whom Allah has named, so beware of them' " (5) "(6).

Al-Shatibi clarified that such ijtihad is the source of innovations (bid'ah) and sects. It is the cause of differences in religion and division within it. From it emerged the Khawarij (secessionists), and its signs include division, which is the separation from the community, and then the fragmentation among them, and deviation, which is the following of desires and the following of ambiguous matters (7).
