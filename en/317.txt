It should be noted here that the issue of whether the good and the bad are determined by Sharia (shar'i) rather than by reason (aqli) is a definitive matter. As for the disagreement regarding the ability of reason to determine good and bad, it does not imply, for those who affirm its ability, that it grants legitimacy to what is deemed good by reason. On one hand, neither al-Juwayni nor al-Ghazali leans towards the correctness of rational good and bad. Al-Juwayni states: "The judgment attributed to its object is not a fixed attribute within it; thus, when we say that drinking alcohol is forbidden, the prohibition is not an inherent quality of drinking. If we mandate drinking in necessity, it is like the forbidden drinking in choice. The meaning of it being forbidden is that it is related to the prohibition, and of it being obligatory is that it is related to the command" (1). He also says: "Among the rulings of Sharia are the good and the bad, which pertain to command and prohibition; nothing is deemed bad in the judgment of Allah, Exalted is He, for its own sake, just as nothing is deemed good for its own sake" (2).
Al-Ghazali states: "If this address from the legislator does not exist, then there is no ruling; thus, we say that reason does not determine good or bad, nor does it mandate gratitude to the benefactor, nor is there a ruling for actions before the arrival of Sharia" (3).
Following the words of the two imams, al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali, leaves no room for what Dr. al-Raisuni has imagined. Those who justify based on public interest (maslaha) do not see this as legislation by reason; rather, they view it as mandated by Sharia, contrary to their opponents. If the matter of this justification becomes problematic and necessitates a rational good or bad, it is rejected without hesitation. If Dr. al-Raisuni believes that the resolution of the issue lies in affirming the legitimacy of rational good and bad, he has strayed far from the path.
