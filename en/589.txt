Al-Qarafi said: "The blocking of means (sadd al-dhara'i) is not a distinctive feature of the Maliki school as many Malikis mistakenly believe. Rather, the means are divided into three categories: the first category is one that the ummah has unanimously agreed to block and prohibit, such as digging wells in the paths of Muslims, as it is a means to their destruction, and likewise, poisoning their food and insulting idols when it is known that such insults are directed at Allah, Exalted is He. The second category is one that the ummah has unanimously agreed not to prohibit, as it is a means that should not be blocked, such as preventing the cultivation of grapes for fear of wine, as no one has ever said this. Similarly, preventing cohabitation in houses for fear of adultery. The third category is one in which scholars have differed on whether it should be blocked or not, such as deferred sales in our view, like someone who sells a commodity for ten dirhams to be paid in a month, having bought it for five before the month. Malik says that he has given away five now and will take ten at the end of the month, so this is a means of lending five for ten with a deferred payment by means of showing the form of sale for that. Al-Shafi'i says that one should look at the form of the sale and take the matter at face value, so it is permissible. These sales are said to reach a thousand issues that are specific to Malik, in which he disagreed with Al-Shafi'i."(1)
Similarly, Abu Hanifa disagrees with the Malikis in this regard, as his opinion on the permissibility of legal tricks (hiyal) means he does not adhere to blocking means. Although those who advocate for it are the Malikis, others disagree with them, Al-Shatibi, Al-Qarafi, and others say that it is applied by all, and the intention is: in some cases. These are the cases that fall under the first category of the three categories of means mentioned by Al-Qarafi.
