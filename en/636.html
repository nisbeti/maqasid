The principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) that are related to beliefs (aqa'id) are those that resemble beliefs in that they are foundational for other matters, while they themselves are not built upon anything else. If they are built upon something else, then they are considered branches, and their validity or invalidity depends on that other matter. Accordingly, the scholars of usul have classified the principles of jurisprudence as the four general proofs. Additionally, there are other rules and principles that are relied upon in ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and istinbat (deduction), for which certainty is not a prerequisite, as they are based on other matters that have been established with certainty. Some have even excluded qiyas (analogy) from the requirement of certainty, considering that qiyas relies on the cause (illah) and its extension, and the source of the cause is the three proofs: the Qur'an, the Sunnah, and consensus (ijma). Thus, reliance on the cause and its extension goes back to these three, and qiyas thereafter is merely the action of the one making the analogy, which is speculative. This is similar to reasoning based on reports of the individual narrators (akhbar al-ahad), which is also speculative, but its foundation, which is the Sunnah, is certain, and the obligation to act upon reports of the individual narrators is certain.
