Through discussing them, the correctness or incorrectness of some of his ideas becomes evident, such as the meaning of returning to the definitive (qat'i) in his view, and that the legal evidence (dalil shar'i) is only accepted if it is definitive or refers back to something definitive. The next section will, God willing, discuss the foundation of the idea, which is the principle of reasoning for the actions of Allah (Exalted is He), and will address a necessary consequence of his methodology, which is the possibility for a jurist (mujtahid) who is well-versed in the general principles of Sharia to dispense with examining its particulars. This includes the non-requirement of knowledge of Arabic for a jurist who is knowledgeable about the objectives of the Sharia in general and in detail.
The requirement of definiteness in the principles of jurisprudence:
Al-Shatibi held that the principles of jurisprudence must be definitive and that they are akin to beliefs (aqa'id) in this regard. He clarified that his understanding of the principles differs from that of other scholars of jurisprudence. His understanding of the principles refers to the definitive meanings that jurisprudence relies upon or is derived from. Indeed, he differs in this from the foundational scholars and jurists, which is a new perspective. I see this as a mistake. The danger of this mistake is magnified because it pertains to the principles, and it is not merely a principle among principles but rather a principle that governs the principles, indeed, it governs the entire methodology. The error lies not in its being new or in its contradiction to the established methodology of the scholars, but rather in its subject matter, that is, in the meaning of this principle.

(1) The term "qat'i" refers to definitive evidence or knowledge that is certain and indisputable in Islamic jurisprudence.
(2) The term "dalil shar'i" refers to legal evidence or proof derived from Islamic sources.
(3) "Aqa'id" refers to the beliefs or doctrines in Islam that are considered essential and foundational.
(4) "Mujtahid" refers to a jurist who is qualified to exercise independent reasoning in Islamic law.
(5) "Sharia" refers to Islamic law derived from the Qur'an and Sunnah.
(6) "Objectives of the Sharia" refers to the goals and purposes that Islamic law aims to achieve.
