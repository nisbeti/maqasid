The reason for this error is that those who confuse the cause (illa) with the objective (maqsad) divert their minds from considering the ruling and its cause to examining the method of the legislator in legislation. They imagine that the legislator has laws or a method for legislation upon which rulings are based, and they attempt to discover these laws. Whether this is correct or not, the diversion of the mind to this direction and the attempt to uncover the principles upon which the legislator legislates is an overstepping of human boundaries that entails a hidden comparison of the Creator to the created. Such a matter may be valid for humans and in rational discourse, but it is not valid in relation to Allah, Exalted is He. As a practical example for further clarification and distinction: if the person responsible for organizing traffic sees signs placed on the roads for the purpose of organizing movement and resolving its issues, and then does so and the movement becomes organized, one might ask: what is the cause of placing the signs? The answer would be: to organize the movement. One might then ask: what is the cause of the organized movement? The answer would be: the signs. These two answers create confusion; one makes the organization of movement the cause for the signs, while the other makes the signs the cause for the organization of movement. The correct approach is that if the focus is on the ruling and its cause, this corresponds here to the focus on the organization of movement and the signs. The signs are what led to the order, and if they are removed, problems arise. They precede the organization in existence; they are the cause, and it cannot be that the order is the cause.
