It should be noted here that the issue of whether good and evil are determined by the Sharia (shar'i) rather than by reason (aql) is a definitive matter. As for the disagreement regarding the ability of reason to determine good and evil, for those who affirm its capability, it does not imply that it grants legitimacy to what is deemed good by reason. On one hand, neither al-Juwayni nor al-Ghazali leans towards the correctness of the rational determination of good and evil. Al-Juwayni says: "The ruling attributed to its subject is not an inherent quality within it. Thus, when we say: drinking alcohol is forbidden, the prohibition is not an intrinsic quality of drinking. If we deem drinking permissible in necessity, it is like forbidden drinking in choice. What is meant by it being forbidden is that it is related to the prohibition, and what is meant by it being obligatory is that it is related to the command"(1). He also says: "Among the rulings of the Sharia are the determination of good and evil, which are related to commands and prohibitions. Nothing is deemed evil in the ruling of Allah, Exalted is He, for its own sake, just as nothing is deemed good for its own sake"(2).

Al-Ghazali says: "If this discourse from the Legislator does not exist, then there is no ruling. For this reason, we say that reason does not determine what is good or bad, nor does it necessitate gratitude to the Benefactor, and there is no ruling for actions before the arrival of the Sharia."(3)

Following the words of the two imams, al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali, leaves no room for what Dr. al-Raisuni has imagined.

Those who justify by means of public interest (maslaha) do not see this as legislation by reason; rather, they view it as being in accordance with the legal command, unlike their opponents. If the matter of this justification becomes problematic and necessitates a rational improvement or condemnation, it is rejected without hesitation. If Dr. al-Raisuni believes that the resolution of the issue lies in asserting the legitimacy of rational improvement and condemnation, then he has strayed far.
