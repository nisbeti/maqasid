And indeed, there is a speaker who says that incomplete induction (istiqra' naqis) leads to certainty in the universal ruling and results in its application to what has not been induced, and in a decisive manner. For we find that iron expands with heat, and the observation is repeated without exception, thus the same ruling is applied to all iron. Wood floats on water, and this is ruled for all wood, and people die, leading to a ruling on the living and those who will be born. The sun rises every day and sets, leading to the conclusion that this will happen tomorrow and the day after, and that it was happening before our existence. We have a strong feeling that these rulings are definitive, even though what has been induced is much less than what has not been induced. Therefore, it is not correct to object to the validity of incomplete induction and its ability to yield certainty.

The response is that induction in such cases can only be incomplete, and even if it may be extensive, it will not be majority-based. Nevertheless, it does provide a ruling on what has not been induced. Yes, but this is not due to the induction itself, rather it is due to something else in addition to this induction, and this thing is akin to the cause that leads to the extension of the ruling from what has been induced to what has not been induced, and to everything that contains this rational or logical cause (and not a legal one). This cause, along with induction, leads to the generalization of the ruling, and a ruling is made on what has not been induced by analogy. In the example of iron that expands with heat, the cause is the similarity or the absence of difference between what has been induced and what has not been induced. This leads to the establishment of the universal and its application, even if those who apply this cause here do not realize it. People carry the concept that what is established for one thing is established for its like, and this operates within them even without their intention, just as blood circulation occurs in the body; it exists within a person as their mind forms and grows. The failure to recognize the effect of this similarity or other potential hidden causes for the ruling is what leads to error, causing one to mistakenly believe that incomplete induction is beneficial, and the same can be said for all the other mentioned examples.

__________

(1) Incomplete induction refers to a method of reasoning where conclusions are drawn from a limited set of observations rather than a comprehensive analysis.

(2) The concept of analogy (qiyas) in Islamic jurisprudence is a method used to derive legal rulings for new situations based on established precedents.

(3) The term "rational cause" refers to a logical basis for drawing conclusions, distinct from legal or Sharia-based reasoning.

(4) The mention of blood circulation serves as a metaphor for innate human reasoning processes that operate subconsciously.
