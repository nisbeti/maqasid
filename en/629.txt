Thus, the fact that the public interest (maslaha) is a public interest is from the perspective of the legislator, such that reason affirms it and the soul finds reassurance in it. The interests, as they are interests, have been viewed as acts of worship (ta'abudiyyat), and what is based on the act of worship can only be an act of worship (1). Accordingly, al-Shatibi's reasoning for the Sharia based on interests means that it must be established for him that the intention of the legislator is present; if it is established, then it is a public interest regardless of human sentiment. This is a new perspective that contradicts the conventional understanding of the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) regarding public interest, and the same can be said for harm (mafsada).
The meaning of reasoning for the Sharia based on interests:
The rulings are causes for effects that are the intended goals of the legislator, and the rulings are established based on this intended goal. From here, this intended goal becomes a reason for legislation, and the jurists discover these reasons through examining the outcomes and effects of the rulings. The intended goal of the legislator exists prior to the rulings, and based on it, the rulings were revealed. For the jurist, the intended goal can only be known through the rulings. Hence, al-Shatibi considers the intended goal to be a reason, even though in meaning, the reason for an action precedes it, while the intended goal comes after it.
This meaning of reasoning, along with the previously mentioned meaning of public interest, leads to the conclusion that the appropriateness of the described attributes is not a condition. If the reason or intended goal is a suitable description that makes the obligated person feel that it aligns with their human nature and purposes, then this is an incidental matter and not the original basis according to the principle of negating beautification and vilification. This differs from the intention of the scholars of usul who spoke of reasoning for the Sharia based on interests; indeed, even if it resembles their expressions, it undermines their idea.
