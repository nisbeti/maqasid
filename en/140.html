"The fifth category: that which does not present any meaning at all to the jurist (mustanbit), nor any necessity or need or encouragement towards a virtue. This is very rarely the case. For if it is impossible to derive a specific meaning, it does not prevent one from conceiving a general one. An example of this category is purely physical acts of worship. They do not relate to any defensive or beneficial purposes. However, it may be said that the continuity of these acts maintains the worshippers' flexibility in adhering to the rules of submission, and the renewal of the covenant through the remembrance of Allah, Exalted is He, serves as a deterrent against immorality and wrongdoing. This applies in general, and when the discussion in this category reaches determinations such as the number of units of prayer (rak'ahs) and similar matters, the one making the analogy (qiyas) does not hope to derive a meaning that necessitates the determination in matters whose origins cannot be analogized" (1). Al-Razi referred to this classification in Al-Mahsool, following al-Juwayni in it (2).

After al-Juwayni, scholars began to classify the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) into three categories: necessary, need-based, and complementary. Each category has its own supplements or complements. This does not mean the elimination of some of the five categories mentioned by al-Juwayni, but rather their inclusion within the three.
