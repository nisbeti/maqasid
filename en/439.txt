Al-Shatibi explains that this is one of the reasons for the disagreement between him and the usuliyyin (jurisprudents) regarding the meaning of specification (takhsees). He says: "There is no doubt that generality has established forms" (1). He then states: "This is because the generality indicated by the forms, according to their establishment, has two perspectives: one is in consideration of what the form indicates in its original establishment absolutely, and to this perspective the usuliyyin aimed; therefore, specification occurs for them through reason, sensory experience, and other separate specifications. The second perspective is based on the practical objectives that customs dictate to be intended, even if the original establishment contradicts that. This consideration is practical, while the first is analogical" (2). He then adds: "The rule in Arabic principles is that if the practical origin conflicts with the analogical origin, the ruling is for the practical" (3). Understanding the practical meaning requires understanding the reasons for revelation, the current and contextual evidence, and the methods of the Arabs in expression and in understanding words and structures. At that point, what some present as an objection to his rejection of specification, he counters by asserting that the generality they understood does not exist at all. In the previously mentioned example regarding His saying: { Indeed, you and what you worship besides Allah are the fuel of Hell } he adds to what he said about "what" being for the non-rational, thus excluding the angels and Christ, stating: "What applies to our original issue is that the address appears to be directed at the disbelievers of Quraysh, who did not worship angels or Christ; rather, they worshipped idols. Thus, His saying: { And what you worship } is general concerning the idols they worshipped, and nothing else is included in the practical generality, so the objection of the objector was due to his ignorance of the context and negligence of what was intended in the verses" (4).
