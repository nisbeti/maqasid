Al-Shawkani said: "Ibn al-Hajib stated in his Mukhtasar al-Muntaha: 'The non-accepted (hadith) is the mursal (unattributed report); if it is strange or its abrogation is established, it is rejected by consensus. If it is appropriate, Imam al-Ghazali explicitly stated its acceptance and mentioned that Malik and al-Shafi'i held this view, while the preferred opinion is its rejection. Al-Ghazali conditioned its acceptance on the necessity of the public interest (maslaha) being certain and comprehensive'" (1). He also said: "The strange (hadith) that is not appropriate is unanimously rejected" (2). The transmission of consensus on the rejection of the strange (hadith) contradicts what was previously reported from al-Ghazali and al-Amidi, where al-Ghazali said: "The appropriate strange (hadith) is a matter of ijtihad (independent reasoning)" (3). Al-Amidi said: "Some have denied it, and their denial is unfounded because it leads to conjecture through reasoning" (4). The conflicting statements regarding the strange (hadith) stem from differing interpretations of its meaning. In fact, al-Ghazali himself, who stated the possibility of its acceptance, is the same one who reported its rejection without disagreement. However, he clarified his intent each time; he rejected it if it meant mursal, and stated its acceptability if it meant that it was supported by a ruling.
