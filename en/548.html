Similarly, in the case of prohibited actions, their outcome may, in some instances, lead to a benefit (maslaha), or the avoidance of such actions may result in a greater harm than the harm they cause. There are many examples of this, including the avoidance of consuming what is unlawful in a situation where one is on the brink of destruction, or the amputation of a limb that poses a threat to the entire body. Additionally, killing a Muslim without justification is a major sin, but if the disbelievers use him as a shield in war and begin killing Muslims in such a way that he can only be stopped by his death, the matter requires careful consideration, which may lead to the obligation of killing him, or its permissibility, or its prohibition (1).

As for what pertains to the realization of the specific context, it relates to understanding the condition and intention of the obligated person. An action may be recommended (mandub) but could lead the obligated person to cease worship, or it may be obligatory (wajib) but result in harm to his body or impairment of his mind. There are many examples of this. Al-Shatibi said: "In the hadith of the Bedouin who urinated in the mosque, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) commanded that he be left until he finishes urinating, and said: 'Do not interrupt him' (2)" (3).

And there is the hadith that prohibits being overly strict with oneself in worship for fear of ceasing it. All that has been mentioned regarding the realization of the specific context carries this meaning, where an action is originally permissible but is prohibited due to the harm it may lead to, or it is forbidden but the prohibition is overlooked due to the benefit it entails. Likewise, all the evidence indicating the closure of avenues (sadd al-dhara'i) generally involves a permissible act leading to an impermissible one. The original ruling is permissibility, but its outcome is impermissible. The evidence indicating expansion and the lifting of hardship generally allows for actions that are originally impermissible due to the benefit they lead to that is permissible (4).
