The intensification of the debate between those who accept and those who reject the approach of suitability and reasoning has led to the emergence of the idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and to its formulation. I will summarize below the arguments of those who advocate for them, then I will substantiate them with texts from the imams and clarify the responses of the refuters to show how the idea of the objectives emerged. Here are their arguments in brief:

1 - They argued that the texts and their meanings are not sufficient for the issues and events. There is no event that is devoid of a ruling from Allah, Exalted is He, attributed to the Sharia of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him). Therefore, it is necessary to provide reasoning for the rulings, and thus the original principle in rulings is reasoning. This necessitates reasoning based on suitability, and it requires a disciplined pursuit of meanings. What does not have its reason established through the paths of text, consensus, indication, and allusion, if a suitable meaning appears in it, it must be reasoned with unless it is annulled or opposed.

2 - They stated that the companions (may Allah be pleased with them) would pursue the understanding of beneficial meanings, and those who followed them did so without establishing criteria for what should be followed and what should not be followed from the meanings. This indicates the validity of this pursuit, provided that these meanings and benefits are derived from the sources of the Sharia. The evidence is established in the actions of the companions.

3 - They said that the descriptions and suitable meanings, and what is similar to them or close to them, are matters that are subject to conjecture regarding their causation. The legal rulings are taken based on conjecture, so reasoning based on suitability is legal, as is the reasoning itself. Here are the texts that indicate this: Al-Juwayni said: "As for al-Shafi'i, he said: We know with certainty that no event is devoid of a ruling from Allah, Exalted is He" (1). He also said: "The previous imams did not leave any event, despite the multitude of issues and the abundance of cases and fatwas, without a ruling from Allah, Exalted is He... Thus, we know that they - may Allah be pleased with them - pursued the establishment of rulings with confidence in their applicability to events, striving to affirm them in what is relevant and appropriate, looking forward to what will occur" (2).
