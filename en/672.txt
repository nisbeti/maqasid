Al-Shatibi aimed to establish the principle of reasoning by affirming objectives (maqasid) for the legislator from the rulings, which are the causes and outcomes of adherence to the legal rulings. He considered these objectives as reasons without addressing or proving that the intended purpose of the ruling is its reason.
According to what Al-Shatibi presented, the objective is a reason, and the objective itself is a cause, result, and outcome. The ruling is the cause that leads to the objective, and based on this logic, the legal ruling is the cause of its reason. An example of this is the ruling of cutting off the hand for theft. The legislator's intent behind the ruling is deterrence, so deterrence is the reason for the ruling, and the ruling is the cutting off of the hand, making deterrence the reason for the cutting. Deterrence is a result of the cutting, which is the ruling, thus the ruling is a cause for it, and the ruling is the cause of its reason. Is this correct? And is the reason for the ruling a result of it? Therefore, does the reason for the ruling exist after it? In other words, is the objective a reason?
Another example from his evidence in proving the reasoning of the Sharia is His saying, Exalted is He: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } (1). Worship is an objective for the legislator, and it occurs after creation. Is it a reason for creation, making the reason exist after its effect? Is the depiction of the matter in this way in these two examples, the example of theft and the example of creation for worship, correct, or is there confusion and ambiguity in the matter?
No, the matter is not as such, even if it appears straightforward at first glance. The objective is not a reason, and the reason is not an objective, and the ruling is not a cause for its reason, and the reason for the ruling does not exist after it; how could it, when it only exists with it and through it and ceases to exist with its absence! It is the motivator for it or against it.

(1) Surah Adh-Dhariyat, 56.
