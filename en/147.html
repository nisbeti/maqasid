Evidence of those who affirm the principle of reasoning:

A group of the imams of usul (principles of jurisprudence), after dividing the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) into necessities, needs, and enhancements, sought to establish evidence for this reasoning. Al-Amidi said under the title (Establishing evidence that suitability and consideration are proof that the description is a reason): "The rulings were legislated for the interests of the servants. As for the fact that they were legislated for objectives and purposes, this is indicated by consensus and reason. The consensus is that the jurists are unanimous that the rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, are not devoid of wisdom and purpose, even if they differ on whether this is by way of obligation, as the Mu'tazila said, or by way of agreement and occurrence without obligation, as our companions said. As for reason, it is that Allah, Exalted is He, is wise in His creation. The consideration of purpose in His creation must either be obligatory or not obligatory: if it is obligatory, then it is not devoid of purpose; and if it is not obligatory, then acting for the purpose is closer to being in accordance with reason than acting without purpose. Thus, the purpose is necessarily inferred from His actions, and if the purpose is necessary in His creation, then the rulings are from His creation, and they are for a purpose and objective. The purpose must either return to Allah, Exalted is He, or to the servants. There is no way to the former due to His transcendence above harm and benefit, and because it contradicts consensus, so only the latter remains." (1)

__________

(1) Al-Amidi, al-Ihkam 3/250. It is noteworthy in this text and similar ones that the establishment of the principle of reasoning has shifted the discussion of the reason from the meaning that the legislator has made a reason for the ruling to discussions related to the essence of Allah.
