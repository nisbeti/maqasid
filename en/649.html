And when the meaning of induction (istiqra) is understood, it becomes clear that it has no place where al-Shatibi placed it. The imams relied on deduction (istinbat) as a rational method for understanding and arriving at rulings, not induction. So, what is induction?

Al-Asnawi said: "Induction is the inference of a ruling in particulars based on its establishment for the general rule" (1). He said: "It is divided into complete and incomplete; the complete is the establishment of a general ruling in a category for its establishment in all its particulars" (2). As for the incomplete: "It is the establishment of a general ruling in a category for its establishment in some of its individuals, and this does not provide certainty, as it is possible that the ruling of what has not been induced from the particulars is contrary to what has been induced from them" (3).

An example of this is to say: in this box, there is a collection of pieces; if one examines them one by one and finds that they are all iron pieces, one can say: all the pieces in the box are iron. This is complete induction, and this ruling is general. If one takes a piece from the box randomly, it is certainly iron based on the general ruling. However, if one examines most of the pieces or all but one and finds iron, this is incomplete induction, and if one takes a piece randomly, one cannot rule that it belongs to the same category as the induced ones, and it is not correct to issue the general ruling at all.

Thus, induction is the establishment of a ruling for the whole or the totality through its establishment for the parts one by one. If the subject to be induced is known and limited, complete induction can be achieved by examining all one by one. If it is not limited, then complete induction is not possible. Induction is different from deduction; induction leads to the general ruling based on the ruling on the particulars, while deduction leads to the ruling on the part based on the general or overall ruling. The general ruling resulting from complete induction is also considered deduction, because the general ruling is derived from the ruling on the whole.
