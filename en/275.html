And this is undoubtedly without dispute. The speech and the texts were intended for a specific purpose, which is their meanings. Therefore, attention was given to the meanings of words and their implications, to the implications of structures, and to the meanings of letters. This is why the scholars of jurisprudence (fiqh) and the scholars of principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) paid attention to the sciences of language and their implications. Al-Shatibi says: "The concern for the meanings dispersed in the discourse is the greatest objective based on the fact that the Arabs were concerned with meanings, and they only refined the words for their sake. This principle is well-known among the people of Arabic. The word is merely a means to attain the intended meaning, and the meaning is the objective, and not every meaning; for the individual meaning may not be taken into account if the composite meaning is understood without it" (1). The individual meaning refers to words like hand, eye, their hands, our eyes, and with you, among others, in the verses that raised discussions about attributes, and to the word abba in His saying: { And fruit and abba } (2). He says: "In the collection of al-Isma'ili, which is narrated on the authority of Sahih al-Bukhari from Anas ibn Malik, that Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) recited { And fruit and abba } and said, 'What is abba?' Then he said, 'We were not tasked with this,' or he said, 'We were not commanded with this.' It is also reported from Anas that a man asked Umar ibn al-Khattab about His saying { And fruit and abba }, 'What is abba?' Umar said, 'We were forbidden from delving deeply and being overly concerned.' It is well-known that he admonished Dubay' when he frequently asked about the verses of the sent ones and the violent winds and similar matters. The apparent meaning of all this is that he only forbade it because the composite meaning is generally known, and no legal ruling can be based on performing these actions...
