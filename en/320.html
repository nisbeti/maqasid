... Al-Shatibi's intention in raising the contradiction is that the interests of the servants, their purposes, and their desires are not realized or attained by following them, but rather they are realized by adhering to the rulings of the Sharia. A servant may perceive the fulfillment of his benefit, purpose, or satisfaction of his need through a certain means, and if he follows it, then that is following one's desires (hawa), even if he believes that this is a benefit and that the Sharia was revealed for the sake of benefits. This belief is not legitimate, and this is not a benefit according to al-Shatibi. If the realization of benefits and purposes and the satisfaction of needs is permissible, not every means to that is permissible; the purpose may be legitimate, such as ownership, but the means to it may be forbidden, such as gambling, deceit, usury, and theft. If ownership is permissible, it can only be attained through permissible means. This is the meaning of his statement: "The commands and prohibitions remove him from the inclinations of his nature and the pursuit of his desires until he takes them from beneath the permissible limit," and his statement: "Nor should he attain them by himself without the Sharia providing them for him."

... Thus, al-Shatibi states that the Sharia was established for the interests of the accountable individuals in both abodes, but interests can only be known through the Sharia. It is not correct for the accountable individual to seek his share except through the means prescribed by the Sharia, and his meaning ultimately leads to the conclusion that there is no difference between saying that the Sharia was established for the servants and saying that the servants were created for the Sharia, which is the implication of His saying, Exalted is He: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } (1).
