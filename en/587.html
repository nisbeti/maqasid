The difference between public interest (maslaha mursala) and juristic preference (istihsan) is that public interest is based on the principle that what is necessary for fulfilling an obligation is itself an obligation for achieving something in necessities, and on the principle of alleviating hardship in needs. As for juristic preference, it involves the specification of the necessary by the need or by the complementary, or the specification of the need by the complementary. Accordingly, they may converge in some cases. The issue may become ambiguous as to whether it is from public interest or from juristic preference, or it may be validly associated with both principles, in the case where the alleviation of hardship in needs from public interest coincides with the specification of needs from juristic preference.

An example of this is what al-Shatibi mentioned among his examples of istihsan (juridical preference), which is the liability of the common worker. He said: "The abandonment of evidence for a benefit, as in the liability of the common worker, even if he is not a craftsman, for the position of Malik in this matter is based on two opinions, like the liability of the bathhouse owner for the garments"(1). Then he said: "If it is said: This is from the category of maslaha mursal (unrestricted public interest) and not from the category of istihsan, we say: Yes, except that they framed istihsan as an exception to the rules, unlike masalih mursal. Such a situation can be envisioned in the matter of liability, as the workers are entrusted by evidence and not by original innocence, so their liability falls within the scope of the exception from that evidence, thus it is included under the meaning of istihsan in that regard"(2).
