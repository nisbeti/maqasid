The difference between public interest (maslaha) and juristic preference (istihsan) is that public interest is an action based on the principle that what is necessary for fulfilling an obligation is itself obligatory for achieving something in necessities, and based on the principle of alleviating hardship in needs. As for juristic preference, it is the specification of the necessary by the need or by the complementary, or the specification of the need by the complementary. Accordingly, they may converge in some cases. The issue may become confused as to whether it is from public interest or from juristic preference, or whether it can be correctly associated with both principles, in the case where the alleviation of hardship in needs from public interest coincides with the specification of needs from juristic preference. An example of this is what al-Shatibi mentioned among his examples of juristic preference, which is the liability of a shared worker. He said: "The abandonment of evidence for a benefit, as in the liability of the shared worker even if he is not a craftsman, for Malik's position on this issue is based on two opinions, like the liability of the bathhouse owner for the garments" (1). He then said: "If it is said: This is from the category of public interest and not from the category of juristic preference, we say: Yes, except that they have framed juristic preference as an exception to the principles, unlike public interest. Such a situation can be envisioned in the matter of liability, as workers are entrusted by evidence and not by original innocence, so their liability falls under the category of exceptions to that evidence, thus entering under the meaning of juristic preference in that regard" (2).
