Imam Jamal al-Din Abdul Rahim al-Asnawi said: "As for the reports of the single narrators (ahad), they are invalid because the narration of a single narrator, if it provides any benefit, only provides conjecture. The legislator has only permitted conjecture in practical matters, which are the branches, and not in scientific matters such as the principles of the faith and likewise the principles of jurisprudence, as conveyed by al-Anbari, the commentator on al-Burhan, from all the scholars" (1).

Al-Baydawi said: "The principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) are the knowledge of the evidence of jurisprudence in general, how to benefit from them, and the state of the beneficiary" (2).

Al-Asnawi, in his commentary on the above text, said: "What is meant by knowing the evidence is to know that the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas) are evidence that can be relied upon... Know that the expression 'evidence' excludes many principles of jurisprudence such as generalities, reports of single narrators, analogy, presumption of continuity (istishab), and others. For the jurists, even if they accept working with them, they are not considered evidence for jurisprudence but rather indications of it, for the term 'evidence' among them is only applied to what is certain" (3).

(1) Jamal al-Din Abdul Rahim al-Asnawi, Nihayat al-Sul fi Sharh Minhaj al-Usul, 2/270. Thus, conjecture is acted upon if it is based on a firmly established principle that it is one of the sources of legislation. The hadith attributed to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is obligatory to act upon even if it is not of certain authenticity, because it is firmly established that the Sunnah is one of the sources of legislation, meaning it is revelation.

(2) Al-Qadi Nasir al-Din ibn Umar al-Baydawi, Minhaj al-Wusul ila Ilm al-Usul, 1/5. Along with Nihayat al-Sul of al-Asnawi, Dar al-Ilm - Beirut.

(3) Al-Asnawi, Nihayat al-Sul fi Sharh Minhaj al-Usul, 1/9 - 11.
