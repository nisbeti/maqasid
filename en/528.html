The first description, which is justice, is recognized by the follower (muqallid) through the behavior of the mufti in his words and actions. If he contradicts what is upheld by the people of religion and knowledge, he should not be followed, and his statements should not be taken. This disqualifies him from being preferred over others, as he lacks justice. He said: "As for his fatwas in speech, if his statements are contrary to what is lawful, and this is among his statements, it is possible for them to be contrary to what is lawful, so they should not be trusted" (1). The meaning of the text is that if his statements, which are outside the scope of fatwas, contradict the Sharia, then the same applies to his statements in fatwas, and they may also contradict the Sharia, thus they should not be considered valid. He said: "As for his actions, if they are contrary to the actions of the people of religion and knowledge, it is not permissible to follow them or to take them as a model among the actions of the righteous predecessors, and likewise his approval, as it is among his actions" (2). He said: "For the one who contradicts with his limbs indicates his contradiction in speech, and the one who contradicts in speech indicates his contradiction with his limbs, for all derive from one heart matter. This is a clarification of the invalidity of his fatwa in general" (3). Therefore, the principle he establishes is: "That a fatwa is not valid from one who contradicts the requirements of knowledge" (4).
