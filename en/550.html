Here, one can refer to some of his statements in the Book of Rulings regarding causes and effects, which may seem contradictory to his text above, where he said: "It is not necessary for the one responsible (mukallaf) to pay attention to the effects or to intend them when engaging in the causes." (1) He also said: "If it is established that there is no obligation to intend the effect, then the one responsible may abandon the intention to it in general, and he may also intend it." (2) From these texts and others, it is understood that although the effect results from the cause, the one responsible is not concerned with the effects or the outcomes; rather, he is concerned with knowing the ruling and adhering to it, regardless of its outcomes. Al-Shatibi responds by saying: "It cannot be said that it has been mentioned in the Book of Rulings that the effects do not need to be considered when entering into the causes, for we say, and it has also been established that it is necessary to consider the effects in the causes, and the discussion has been made regarding that and reconciling the two matters. Our issue is of the second type, not the first, as it pertains to the jurist (mujtahid) who is looking into the ruling of others with regard to being free from personal interests. The jurist is a representative of the legislator in ruling on the actions of the responsible individuals, and it has been established that the legislator intends the effects in the causes." (3)
