Al-Shatibi asserts that he opposes the earlier usuliyyin (principle scholars) in his statement regarding specification (takhsis) and critiques them. He says: "If it is said: Thus the usuliyyin say that specification clarifies the intended meaning of the mentioned phrases, as it removes the misconception of the specific being included under the generality of the phrase in the listener's understanding, and it is not intended to be included under it; otherwise, specification would be abrogation (naskh)" (1). He goes on to say: "How do you differentiate between what I have mentioned and what the usuliyyin mention?" (2). Al-Shatibi answers: "The response is that the difference between them is apparent, as what is mentioned here pertains to clarifying the status of general phrases in the original Arabic or legal usage. What the usuliyyin mentioned pertains to clarifying the transition of the phrase from its general status to a specific one. We have clarified that it is an explanation of the status of the word, while they said it is an explanation of the departure of the word from its status, and there is a difference between the two" (3).
He said: "If it is said: The essence of what has been discussed is that it is a debate over wording while the meaning is agreed upon, and such a matter does not lead to a ruling. The response is: No, rather it is a discussion that leads to rulings" (4). He stated that the methodology of the usuliyyin in specification is reprehensible. He said: "Indeed, the difficulty of this matter has led to another reprehensible issue (5), which is that the generalities of the Qur'an do not contain anything that is genuinely significant in its essence of generality, even if it is said to be a proof after specification, and it entails the invalidation of the Qur'anic universals and the invalidation of deriving evidence from it altogether, except through a form of leniency and good opinion, not based on thorough examination and certainty of the ruling. In this, if contemplated, it weakens the legal evidences and diminishes reliance upon them" (6). He then concluded by saying: "The truth regarding general phrases, when they are reported, is that they are in their original usage general, such that the place of their generality is understood in Arabic, with an understanding that is aware of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)" (7).
