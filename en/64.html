"A land where my amulets were tied upon me, and the first land that touched my skin is its dust."(1)

The term "manat" has been used in the discussions of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) in the same linguistic sense.

Sheikh Jamal al-Din al-Asnawi(2) said: "The 'manat' is the name of the place of attachment, and 'attachment' means hanging and sticking. Habib al-Ta'i said:

A land where my amulets were tied to me, and the first earth that touched my skin was its dust.

Any amulet that is tied to me. When the ruling is linked to the cause and is dependent on it, it is called a point of reference (manat) (3).

The reason that is the cornerstone of analogy (qiyas) is not the legal basis (manat), nor is the legal basis the reason, but when the ruling that is based on reasoning is linked to its reason and dependent upon it, it is called a legal basis. This is a figurative term. Al-Zarkashi said: "The legal basis is the reason." Ibn Daqiq al-Eid said: "Their expression of the legal basis for the reason is a matter of linguistic metaphor, because when the ruling is dependent upon it, it is like a tangible thing that is attached to something else; thus, it is a metaphor in the sense of likening the abstract to the tangible. This has become established in the terminology of jurists to the extent that nothing else is understood when it is mentioned in general."(4)

(2) He is: Jamal al-Din Abu Muhammad Abdul Rahim bin al-Hasan bin Ali bin Umar bin Ali bin Ibrahim al-Qurashi al-Umari al-Shafi'i al-Asnawi. The Sheikh of Islam and the master of the eminent imams, the head of the Shafi'is in the Egyptian lands. A jurist (faqih), a scholar of principles (usuli), a poet (arudi), and a grammarian (nahwi). He was born in Asna, a town in Upper Egypt, in the year 704 AH and died in Cairo in the year 772 AH. He was prayed upon three times due to the large number of mourners. See: his biography at the beginning of the first volume of Nihayat al-Sul in the explanation of Minhaj al-Usul, with the marginal commentary of Nihayat al-Sul by Sheikh Muhammad Bakhit al-Muti'i, Al-Alam al-Kutub.
