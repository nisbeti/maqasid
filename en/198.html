The permissible (mubah) that one has a choice in is as if it means the obligatory (wajib) that one has a choice in, like the attributes of expiation (kafara). He says: "It can be summarized that every permissible act is not permissible in an absolute sense, but rather it is permissible in part specifically, and as for in totality, it is either an action that is required or an action that is required to be abandoned. If it is said, 'Is this explanation not a contradiction to what has been previously stated, that the permissible is that which has equal extremes?' The answer is no, because what was previously mentioned is in terms of considering it in itself, without taking into account any external matter, and this consideration is in terms of its relation to external matters. When you look at it in itself, it is what is referred to here as permissible in part. And when you look at it according to external matters, it is what is called required in totality. You see that this nice garment, for example, is permissible to wear; it has equal standing in the view of the Sharia regarding its action and its abandonment, so there is no intention behind either of the two matters. This is a rational reality when considering it in this limited regard to the essence of the permissible as it is. And from the perspective of being a protection against heat and cold, a cover for the private parts, and beauty in appearance, it is required to be acted upon. This consideration is not limited to this specific garment nor to this specific time; it is a consideration in totality, not in part."
