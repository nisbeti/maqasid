Another aspect of the attention given to the book is its printing and commentary. Abu Ubaida, mentioned earlier, referred to five editions (1). The first was in 1302 AH - 1884 CE at the Tunisian State Press. The second was in 1327 AH - 1909 CE, where the first part was printed in Kazan, the capital of the Republic of Tatarstan in Russia. The third was in 1341 AH - 1922 CE in Egypt, with commentary by the two scholars Muhammad Khidr Hussein and Muhammad Hassanein Makhlouf, where the first commented on the first and second parts, and the second commented on the third and fourth parts. The fourth was in Egypt with commentary and verification by Sheikh Abdullah Daraz. The fifth was in Egypt with verification by Muhammad Muhyi al-Din Abdul Hamid. Thus, the edition verified by Abu Ubaida himself is the sixth edition.
In addition to this, there have been books and articles discussing the book al-Muwafaqat, the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), and al-Shatibi (2). Most of them appear in this book through references to or discussions of them.
2 - Al-I'tisam. Al-Shatibi authored it after al-Muwafaqat, but did not complete it; it consists of two parts and its subject is innovations (bid'ah). No one has written anything like it in this regard - and Allah knows best - and people have relied on it in this subject to this day. The importance of this book comes from several aspects, including its comprehensiveness regarding types of innovations, and the immense ability of al-Shatibi to penetrate into the depths of the soul and understand its motivations, as well as the depth and precision that allows for distinguishing between similar or intertwined matters, such as distinguishing between public interest (maslaha) and preference (istihsan), and distinguishing each from innovations.
