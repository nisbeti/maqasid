Al-Shatibi said: "Some people claim that the Qur'an has an apparent meaning and a hidden meaning" (1). He went on to state that this claim is valid in a specific sense and not in others, namely that the apparent meaning is the literal recitation or the words themselves and their meanings. As for the hidden meaning, it is what Allah, Exalted is He, intends from the discourse. He said: "The apparent meaning is the literal recitation, and the hidden meaning is the understanding of Allah's intent" (2). He also stated: "The apparent meaning refers to the Arabic understanding, while the hidden meaning is what Allah, Exalted is He, intends from His words and discourse. If the intent of those who used this phrase is what has been interpreted, then it is correct and there is no dispute about it. However, if they mean something else, then it is an assertion of something additional to what was known by the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) and those after them, and there must be conclusive evidence to support this claim, as it is a principle by which the Book is judged, and it cannot be speculative" (3).
