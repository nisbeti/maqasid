He said regarding fasting: { Fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may attain piety } (1), and regarding prayer: { Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing } (2), and he said regarding the qibla: { So turn your faces toward it, so that there will not be for the people a reason to argue against you } (3), and regarding jihad: { Permission is given to those who are being fought because they were wronged } (4), and regarding retribution: { And there is for you in retribution a saving of life, O you who have understanding } (5), and regarding the affirmation of monotheism: { Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes, we have testified, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection, "Indeed, we were of this unaware" } (6). The aim is to draw attention, and if induction indicates this and is beneficial for knowledge in such a matter, we assert that the matter continues in all details of the Sharia (Islamic law) (7).
In this way, al-Shatibi sees that he has established conclusive evidence that the legal rulings are based on public interest (maslaha), and we will look - in the following - at this text analytically to understand his intention regarding the reasoning based on public interest. Let us take - then - the texts he used as evidence, each of which is beneficial for reasoning based on public interest, and their abundance forms an induction that suggests something akin to moral consensus.
His first evidence: His saying: "Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, says regarding the sending of the messengers: { Messengers as bringers of good tidings and warners, so that there will not be for the people a reason to argue against Allah after the messengers }" (8).
