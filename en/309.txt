He called in his book for an approach that does not adhere to individual texts, but rather relies on a collection of texts from which the general meaning or the type of public interest (maslaha) is derived, and then legislation is based on that general meaning. In this case, there is no concern for the new legislations contradicting individual texts. Sheikh Rashid al-Ghannouchi followed him in this approach in his book (Public Freedoms in the Islamic State). In a press interview with the Lebanese magazine al-Shiraa, Sheikh al-Ghannouchi responded to the notion that his ideas are strange by Islamic standards, saying: "We are still governed by the complex of the text," and he said: "Al-Turabi is the teacher of a generation, and I am one of them." Legal rulings in our time have been given according to the whims of those in power, to the extent that it has been attributed to some the saying: "You have the opinion, and I have the evidence." And to another, the saying: "Present to me what benefits the people, and I will present to you the evidence." See: an article titled (Response to Fabrications Against al-Shatibi) by Mahmoud Abdul Karim Hassan, al-Wa'i magazine, issue: 88, Rabi' al-Awwal 1415 AH - 1994 CE. And see: al-Muwafaqat, edited by Abu Ubaida, in his introduction to the book, vol. 1, footnote, p. 42.
