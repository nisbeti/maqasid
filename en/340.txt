Thus, the causes are intended by their effects, and they are intended by the one who established the causes as causes; they are intended by the legislator (al-shari) and not by the obligated person (al-mukallaf), and their attainment is through these causes. Therefore, the obligated person must adhere to the causes, that is, to the legal rulings, and it is not upon him to consider or take into account the effects, which are the interests (masalih) and harms (mafasid).
He said: "The establishment of causes necessitates the intention of the establisher towards the effects, I mean the legislator" (1). Then he said: "It follows from the intention to establish them as causes that there is an intention towards what arises from them, namely the effects" (2). Then he said: "The legal rulings were legislated to bring about interests or to avert harms, and these are certainly their effects" (3). Then he said: "If the effects were not intended by the causes, their establishment as causes would not be valid; however, they were imposed as such. Therefore, they must be established as causes, and they cannot be causes except for effects. Thus, the establisher of the causes intends the occurrence of the effects through them" (4), and if this is established, and the causes are intended by the legislator, it follows that the effects must be so as well" (5).
Thus, the legislator's intention with the Sharia is to bring about interests and avert harms, and this is achieved through what he has legislated. It is not upon the obligated person to consider the intended interests and harms; rather, it is upon him to adhere to what has been legislated for him. However, is it permissible for him to consider the results and strive to attain them? Al-Shatibi says: "There is no explicit evidence in the Sharia that mandates seeking the intention of the effect" (6). He said: "If it is said to you: The legislator commanded and prohibited for the sake of interests. You say: Yes, and that is for Allah, not for me; for what is upon me is to cause the means, and the attainment of the effects is not upon me. So I direct my intention towards what has been made obligatory upon me and entrust what is not upon me to the One who is responsible for it" (7).
