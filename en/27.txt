He said in al-Muwafaqat: "Many of the followers of the jurists give fatwas to their relatives or friends that they do not give to others, following their own interests and desires or the interests of that relative or friend" (1). He also said: "Some of them might justify this in situations where they claim necessity and urgent need, based on the principle that necessities permit the prohibited, thus taking what aligns with their interests" (2).

Al-Shatibi established this position regarding these circumstances as a point of contention between him and the scholars of his time, to the extent that he was accused of the aforementioned charges and others. He expressed this, as has been shown, and among his expressions is also his saying (al-Basit):

"I have been afflicted, O people, with a variety of trials ... from whom I have to manage until it nearly destroys me.
To avert harm, not to seek a benefit ... so sufficient for me is Allah in my intellect and in my religion" (3).

Dr. Hamadi al-Abidi says: "There were violent confrontations between al-Shatibi and those jurists to whom he directed his criticism and warned people against. Whenever he issued a fatwa permitting something, they issued a fatwa prohibiting it, and whenever he issued a fatwa prohibiting something, they issued a fatwa permitting it" (4).
