As for what one exerts effort in (ijtihad), he said: "The places of valid ijtihad are those that oscillate between two extremes, where the intention of the Legislator is clear in affirming one and negating the other, so it does not definitively lean towards the side of negation or the side of affirmation" (1). He also said regarding the actions of the accountable person (mukallaf): "If there is a command regarding them, it may either show the intention of the Legislator in negation or in affirmation, or it may not show any intention at all. If no intention is shown at all, it falls under the category of ambiguous matters (mutashabihat), and if it does show intention, it may sometimes be definitive (qat'i) and sometimes non-definitive (ghayr qat'i). As for the definitive, there is no room for consideration after the truth is clear in negation or affirmation, and it is not a place for ijtihad; it is a category of clear matters (wadhihat) because the ruling is truly clear, and anyone who deviates from it is certainly mistaken" (2).

It has been mentioned in the previous chapter when discussing the various opinions on how the Sunnah clarifies the Qur'an that one of them is clarifying what is ambiguous and oscillates between two origins, where the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) clarified the origin that should be adhered to. For example, Allah, Exalted is He, has permitted good things (tayyibat) and prohibited harmful things (khaba'ith), so the status of some items, such as those with fangs among beasts and those with claws among birds, became ambiguous: should they be classified with the good or with the harmful? The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) clarified this in hadiths that prohibit those with fangs among beasts and those with claws among birds, as well as the flesh of domesticated donkeys (3). Thus, the valid ijtihad is that which clarifies what is ambiguous to people regarding its ruling and oscillates between two origins that appear to be equal in terms of classification.
