He said: "As for the enhancements, their meaning is to adopt what is appropriate from the virtues of customs and to avoid the disgraceful conditions that rational minds abhor, and this encompasses the category of noble character (makarim al-akhlaq)"(1). He also said: "The absence of these does not negate a necessary or a needful matter, but rather it falls under the category of enhancement and adornment"(2). To further clarify the limits of each of these ranks, he states: "The needful matters revolve around the necessary ones, as do the enhancements"(3). He says: "For whoever seeks more, the needful matters revolve around expansion, facilitation, alleviation of hardship, and kindness. In relation to religion, this appears in legal instances of concessions in purification, such as tayammum (dry ablution), the lifting of the ruling of impurity when its removal is difficult, shortening the prayer, lifting the obligation of prayer due to fainting, praying while seated or on one's side, and breaking the fast while traveling or ill"(4). He states: "The category of enhancements also operates similarly to the needful matters, as it pertains to acting upon noble character and what is appropriate in the course of customs, such as purifications in relation to prayers according to the opinion of those who consider it to be in this category(5), taking adornment from clothing, the virtues of appearances, perfumes, and similar matters, selecting the best and highest in zakat (almsgiving) and expenditures, and the etiquette of kindness in fasting, as well as in relation to souls, such as kindness and benevolence, and the etiquette of eating and drinking, and so forth, and in relation to offspring, such as maintaining good conduct and parting with kindness"(6). In his book, there are more examples for those who wish to seek further clarification(7).
