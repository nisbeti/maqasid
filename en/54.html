The proponents of qiyas (analogy) have supported their position with evidence from the Qur'an and the Sunnah, as well as the consensus of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) on the authority of qiyas and its status as a source of legislation. These evidences are discussed in the foundational texts of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), and it is not our purpose here to delve into them(1).

The pillars of qiyas:

Qiyas has pillars that must be present for it to be valid. These are the original case (asl), the ruling of the original case as indicated by the Sharia, the branch (far') that is being compared to the original, and the reason (illah) for the ruling of the original, which is a characteristic present in the original case, and by its presence in the branch, the ruling can be extended from the original to the branch. Al-Amidi said: "Once the meaning of qiyas is understood, it consists of four pillars: the original, the branch, the ruling of the original, and the common description" (2). Therefore, all four pillars must be present for qiyas to be valid. Al-Zarkashi stated: "The original, the branch, the reason, and the ruling of the original must all be mentioned in qiyas" (3).

Its conditions:

Qiyas has conditions that must be met for it to be valid, and these conditions are the conditions of its pillars. The aim here is not to elaborate on the discussions of qiyas, but rather to mention some of these conditions as examples of these discussions, and to provide further detail on the reason and its conditions, which are necessary for research.

For example, one of the conditions of the original case is that it should not be compared to another case, meaning it should not be a branch in another qiyas. If it is a branch, then it is not more deserving of being compared to its original(4).

One of the conditions of the branch is that the reason present in it must share the reason of the original; otherwise, the ruling of the original cannot be extended to the branch(5).

Another condition is that its ruling should not be explicitly stated, as there is no qiyas in a ruling when there is a text regarding it(6).
