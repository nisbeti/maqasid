As for the third, it is the place of ambiguity and confusion, and the views of the scholars have diverged regarding it, as there has not been a clear, definitive evidence to establish its connection to either the first or second category. Nor has a purpose been clarified by the legislator that is agreed upon as intended, nor has it been shown that it contradicts the public interest (maslaha) for which the Sharia was established concerning the specific issue at hand. Thus, this category has become a subject of dispute among the disputants, with some testifying that it does not contradict the public interest, making the trickery permissible, while others argue that it contradicts the public interest, rendering the trickery impermissible. It cannot be said that one who permits trickery in some issues acknowledges that it contradicts the intention of the legislator; rather, they permit it based on their understanding of his intention, and that their issue falls under the category of permissible trickery that the legislator intended" (1).
It is well known that the opinion of permitting trickery is attributed to the Hanafis. Therefore, al-Shatibi's division of trickery into three categories serves as a basis for reconciling the disagreement regarding the ruling on trickery to an agreement, which is the agreement on considering the principle or rule that the ruling on trickery depends on whether it undermines a legal principle or contradicts a legal interest or not. The issues of trickery that have caused disagreement revert to this principle. He mentioned two examples of these issues. The first is the marriage in which trickery is employed to make permissible the divorced woman for her husband after three divorces. The second is the sales of deferred payments where trickery is used to sell one dirham for two dirhams on credit. He mentioned the arguments of those who permit these tricks and the arguments of those who prohibit them. He clarified while explaining the two opinions that they revert to the pursuit of aligning with the intention of the legislator and not contradicting it, as well as aligning with the legal interests that the Sharia has recognized and not contradicting them. He then said: "Let us suffice with these two examples, for they are among the most famous issues in the realm of trickery, and the reasoning applied to them can be applied to other matters" (2).
