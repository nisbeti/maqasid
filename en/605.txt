Al-Shatibi mentions in this section that the prevailing principle here is the principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) more than the principle of removing harm (raf' al-darar), based on the fact that the obligated person does not intend harm but rather seeks permissible benefits. Therefore, the principle of blocking the means operates on the basis that the intention of harm, which is prohibited, has become the norm, and it has become predominant in this action that it cooperates in sin and transgression or is a ruse (1).
8 - The eighth section: He says, "As for the eighth, which is that its performance leads to corruption frequently, not predominantly or rarely, this is a matter of consideration and ambiguity, and the original ruling is to carry it on the basis of the validity of permission, as per the school of al-Shafi'i and others. This is because knowledge and suspicion of the occurrence of corruption are absent, as there is only a mere possibility between occurrence and non-occurrence, and there is no evidence to favor one side over the other. The possibility of intending corruption and harm does not equate to the actual intention nor necessitate it" (2). He said, "Moreover, it is not correct to consider the one who brings or pushes it here as negligent or intentional, as in knowledge and suspicion, because carrying it on the intention towards them is no more valid than carrying it on the lack of intention towards either of them. If that is the case, then the permitted causation is very strong" (3). What he has stated indicates the continuation of the action on its original permissibility, although he leans towards applying the principle of blocking the means based on the fact that Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) applied it here. He then mentioned examples that justify or permit the application of blocking the means, but not necessitating it. He said, "If that is the case, then the permitted causation is very strong, except that Malik considered it in blocking the means based on the frequency of intention in occurrence" (4). He then said, "The Sharia is based on precaution, taking measures, and guarding against what may lead to corruption. If this is known in general and in detail, then acting upon it is not an innovation in the Sharia, but rather it is a principle of its principles that relates to what is complementary, whether it is necessary, need-based, or improvement-oriented" (5).
