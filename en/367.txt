... ... Imam al-Suyuti said: "Know that Qadi Husayn mentioned that the foundation of jurisprudence (fiqh) is based on four principles," then he mentioned the four mentioned above. He said: "And those who claim to be precise assert that a fifth has been neglected, which is that matters are judged by their intentions (maqasid). He said: Islam is built upon five, and jurisprudence is based on five." Al-Suyuti added: "In my view, if one intends to refer jurisprudence to the five in a forced and contrived manner, then the fifth is included in the first and also in the second. Indeed, Shaykh al-Islam Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam referred all of jurisprudence to the consideration of public interests (masalih) and the prevention of harm (dar' al-mafasid), and if he were pressed, he would say: I refer everything to the consideration of public interests, for the prevention of harm is among them. He would say regarding this: one of these five is sufficient, and it is more likely that it is the second. And if one intends a clear reference, it exceeds fifty, even over two hundred." p. 12. As for the scholar Ibn Najim al-Hanafi, he counted them as six, arranging them as follows: there is no reward except by intention, matters are judged by their intentions, certainty is not overruled by doubt, hardship brings about ease, harm must be eliminated, and custom is authoritative. See: al-Ashbah wa al-Nazair, pp. 1-3.
