One of the aspects that enhances the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in al-Shatibi's work is his clarification of his views on ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), its types, and its conditions, as well as on the mujtahid (jurist) or scholar and their ranks, and on taqlid (following a legal authority) and how to take opinions or fatwas (legal rulings). Likewise, he addresses the fatwa or legal ruling provided by the scholar, distinguishing what can be accepted legally from what must be rejected. This includes his stance on fatwas that adopt ease, permissibility, necessities, needs, leniency, and the choice between opinions as a methodology to be generalized and applied where it is valid and where it is not.

Among the most important aspects that clarify al-Shatibi's methodology and affirm what has been mentioned is the condition he deems essential for the mujtahid, which is that it is not valid for him to issue a ruling without considering the consequences of actions. This is because the Sharia was revealed to bring about benefits and avert harms, and these are its consequences and the consequences of its rulings. Therefore, it is not valid for the mujtahid to give a ruling on an action without considering its outcome.

For this reason, I have structured this chapter into three sections: the first on the criteria for ijtihad, the second on the criteria for fatwa, and the third on explaining and elucidating the principle of the consequences of actions.

Due to the importance of the principle of the consequences of actions in al-Shatibi's thought and its role as the foundation upon which he builds his argument for the rationale of the Sharia, I have followed this chapter with a section that explains three rules that are among the most important he has established based on the principle of the consequences of actions. These are applications of his idea in the objectives of the Sharia: the rule of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i), the rule of public interest (maslaha mursala), and the rule of juristic preference (istihsan). These three rules will, God willing, be the subject of the eighth chapter.
