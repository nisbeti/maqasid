These acts of worship (ibadat) and customary practices (adat), although it is not correct to pay attention to their meanings in the sense that analogy (qiyas) cannot be applied to them or based on them, they do have a comprehensible meaning, even if it is not one that is suitable for analogy. This is in addition to the general meaning, which is worship. He says: "If it is asked whether there is a specific reason for these acts of worship (ibadat) from which the intention of the legislator can be understood or not? The answer is that, as for acts of worship, their required action is mere submission without addition or reduction. Therefore, when Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) was asked about the fasting of a menstruating woman without prayer, she said to the questioner: 'Are you a Haruriyya!' in denial of her asking about such a matter, as the purpose of worship is not to understand its specific reason. Then she said: 'We were commanded to make up the fast but not to make up the prayer.' This supports the idea of worship over reasoning based on hardship, and the statement of Ibn al-Musayyab regarding the legislator's equal treatment of the fingers: 'It is the Sunnah, my nephew.' This indicates that there is no reason. As for customary practices and some acts of worship, they also have a comprehensible meaning, which is the regulation of the means of public interest (maslaha)... Thus, the legislator has set specific limits that do not exceed, such as eighty lashes for slander and one hundred lashes and exile for fornication without marriage, and the cutting of the hand at the wrist, and in the specified amount" (4).
