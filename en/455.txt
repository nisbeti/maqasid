To achieve this condition, which is understanding the apparent meaning, that is, the implication of the text as it is an Arabic speech, one must know the reasons for revelation (asbab al-nuzul), and what they entail in terms of understanding the contextual clues of circumstances and statements. The meaning varies depending on the audience, and it is possible that the matter may indicate obligation, recommendation, permissibility, or it may pertain to duty, threat, frustration, or other meanings. An order may come in a general form intended for a specific case, and it may come with a word or phrase that has one implication in its original context and another in its usage. The word may be ambiguous, metaphorical, or have one meaning in its original usage, another in Islamic law, and another in common practice. In summary, the texts are revealed with contexts and accompanied by contextual clues that affect the meaning. He said: "Knowledge of the reasons for revelation is necessary for anyone who seeks to understand the Qur'an" (1). He also said: "The science of meanings and rhetoric, which is known by the miraculous arrangement of the Qur'an, in addition to understanding the objectives of the speech of the Arabs, is fundamentally based on understanding the implications of the circumstances of the speech from the perspective of the speech itself, the speaker, the audience, or all of them" (2). He said: "Like the interrogative, which is a single word but encompasses other meanings such as affirmation and reproach, and so forth. And like the command, which can imply permissibility, threat, frustration, and similar meanings, and its intended meaning is only indicated by external matters. Its essence lies in the implications of the circumstances, and not every circumstance is conveyed, nor is every clue associated with the very speech conveyed. If some of the indicative clues are missing, the understanding of the speech may be entirely lost or only partially understood. Understanding the reasons is essential for clarifying every form in this regard, and it is undoubtedly important for understanding the speech. The meaning of knowing the reason is the same as understanding the implications of the circumstances" (3).
