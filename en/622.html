And the Sunnah does not contain new legislation; it does not independently legislate but rather clarifies the Qur'an. Therefore, it corrects the meaning of the hadith: "Whatever comes to you from me, compare it to the Book of Allah. If it agrees with the Book of Allah, then I said it; and if it contradicts the Book of Allah, then I did not say it. How could I contradict the Book of Allah, by which Allah guided me?" (1), and it is relied upon. If it is not authentically narrated, its meaning is still established and definitive for him. He said: "Its meaning is correct whether its chain of narration is sound or not" (2). Among his examples regarding the consideration of the implications of texts through induction is what he mentioned about the establishment of necessities, including his statement about prayer: "When we look at prayer, we find the command to establish prayer in various forms, and we find praise for those who establish it and condemnation for those who abandon it, and the obligation for the accountable to perform it, establishing it while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides, and fighting against those who abandon it or oppose its abandonment, and other matters of this nature" (3). He then said that through this: "We have certainly learned the obligation of prayer" (4). He said: "And likewise for all other evidences in the principles of Sharia, and this is how the principles differ from the branches, as the branches are based on individual evidences and specific sources, remaining on their original basis of reliance on conjecture, unlike the principles which are derived from the induction of the implications of the evidences in general, not from their individual cases specifically" (5). This is an example of reasoning with the texts of the Qur'an. As for the Sunnah, one of its examples is his saying: "His saying, peace be upon him: 'There should be neither harm nor reciprocating harm'" (6), for it falls under a definitive principle in this regard, as harm and reciprocating harm are prohibited throughout the Sharia in specific incidents and general rules, such as His saying, Exalted is He: { And do not hold them to hurt them, so that you may transgress } (7), and { And do not harm them, so that you may make it difficult for them } (8),
