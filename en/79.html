The eighth approach: the principle of consistency (al-tard). Some usul scholars have argued that consistency without the condition of its opposite is a cause. Thus, consistency becomes an approach for them. Al-Razi stated this in al-Mahsool. He said: "What is meant by consistency (al-tard) is the description that is not known to be appropriate or necessarily related to the appropriate when it is present with the description in all cases that differ from the point of contention, and this is what is meant by consistency and continuity" (1). However, al-Juwayni rejected it.

Those who rejected the approach of consistency and its opposite also rejected the approach of consistency as it is more deserving of rejection. Al-Amidi said: "If it is known that consistency and its opposite do not serve as evidence for causation, then consistency alone is even less likely to serve as evidence" (2). Al-Zarkashi said: "Consistency does not mean that the cause is not negated, for that is the matter of its opposite, but rather it means that its cause is neither appropriate nor effective" (3)... And those who claim that consistency and its opposite are not valid evidence, it is even more so that consistency is not valid evidence... The scholars who consider it invalid argue that reliance on it is nonsense. Imam al-Haramayn said: The judge went to great lengths in condemning those who believe that the ruling of Allah, Exalted is He, is linked to it" (4). He said: "Ibn al-Sam'ani stated: Abu Zayd called those who consider consistency as evidence and continuity as proof of the validity of causation 'the Hushawiya of the people of analogy.' He said: These are not counted among the jurists" (5).

Al-Ghazali said: "Consistency must be rejected by every analogist" (6). This is his statement in al-Mustasfa, but in Shifa al-Ghalil, his statement differs (7).
