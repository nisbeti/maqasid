Therefore, the matter that must be considered in al-Shatibi's discussion of causes is that the legal rulings are causes for intended results. These results are the effects of the rulings or the intended benefits, and the rulings are the means leading to them. Just as the Legislator intended through commands and prohibitions to bring about what is commanded and to prevent what is prohibited, He also intended to achieve certain results from the commanded action and to prevent certain results from the prohibited action. This is the foundational principle of the objectives (maqasid) in al-Shatibi's thought. For this reason, al-Shatibi discussed causes and effects in detail and at length before beginning his book on the objectives.

However, the obligation regarding causes, which are the rulings, even though the effects are intended by the Legislator, does not mean that there is an obligation regarding the effects themselves, as they are not within the capacity of the obligated person nor are they dependent on him; rather, what is within his capacity is the cause that the Legislator has commanded him to pursue. The occurrence of the effect happens within that framework. He said: "The evidence for this is what has been established in the discourse that what is required of the obligated person is to engage in the causes, while the effects are the actions of Allah, Exalted is He, and there is no earning involved for the obligated person" (1), and he said: "Thus, the obligation and address only pertain to what is acquired, so the effects are excluded from the address of obligation because they are not within their capacity; if they were to be addressed, it would be an obligation of what is not bearable, which is not the case" (2).

Furthermore, the fact that the effect is intended by the Legislator through the establishment of the cause does not imply the permissibility of intending to bring about the effect through a means other than the legislated cause, as just as the effect is intended, the primary intention is the cause because the address indicates it and the obligation is placed upon it, and the effect is only understood through it. He said: "The fact that the Legislator did not legislate this cause for this specific effect is evidence that there is a corruption in that causation and no benefit" (3). We will clarify this matter in more detail and with more documentation in the third section of the fifth chapter.
