He said: "As for the reasoning behind prohibiting killing for inheritance, the application of the opposite of the intended purpose is to be estimated if there is no text in accordance with it. This reasoning is not established in the actions of the Sharia by obligation nor by its compatible matters in such a way that there exists a recognized type for it; thus, it is not valid to use it as reasoning nor to base a ruling upon it by consensus. Such a thing is legislation from the one who claims it, and it cannot be accepted" (1).
Based on these texts, especially from those who adopt the concept of public interest (maslaha), it becomes clear that inferring from the mursal (unattributed hadith) does not mean inferring from the public interest; this implies making reason a legislator, which is unanimously rejected. Therefore, the meaning of the mursal for those who accept it can only be understood as either compatible or strange.
This matter is confirmed by what we find among some of the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) who divide the mursal itself into nullified, strange, and compatible. This means that the mursal which may be accepted or disputed is that which is strange or compatible. Al-Ansari said: "The mursal is divided into what is known to be nullified by text or consensus... and into what is not known. If no one knows in it one of the considerations of compatibility based on its type or its kind in the type of ruling or its kind in the specific ruling, but it is also not known to be nullified, then it is the strange mursal, which is called the public interests (masalih mursala). If one of the considerations of compatibility is known in it, then it is the compatible mursal" (2).
Al-Ghazali said: "The difference among the jurists regarding the strange compatible: that which does not fit, and the compatible: that which does not have a specific origin is what is referred to - in the language of jurists - as mursal reasoning, meaning relying on the compatible, interest-based meaning that appears in the branch without citing a specific origin" (3). He said: "As for the strange compatible - that which does not fit and does not have a specific origin - this is rejected: there is no known disagreement in it" (4).
