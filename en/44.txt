The scholars of usul (usul al-fiqh) among the Hanafis distinguished themselves by not advocating for a particular school in usul, but rather supporting a school in the branches of jurisprudence (furu). From here, it was said that the paths of usul branched into two ways:
The first: the way of the jurists, which refers to the Hanafi method.
The second: the way of the theologians, which refers to the Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali methods.
Then arose what was called the way of the later scholars, which refers to the methodology of scholars who combined both ways in their books. Dr. Muhammad Hassan Hayto states: "This 'Treatise' was the first book classified in usul al-fiqh, and thereafter, the imams and scholars continued to explain it and seek light from it" (1). He then says: "Numerous works were authored on it, and compilations were produced, and the paths of researchers in it branched into two ways:
The first way is known as the way of the theologians, which includes the Shafi'is and the majority.
The second way is known as the way of the jurists, which includes the Hanafis" (2). He then said: "The way of the theologians: This was concerned with clarifying issues and establishing principles and tended towards rational evidence as much as possible, abstracting the foundational issues from the legal branches, similar to the scholars of theology" (3). He then said: "The way of the jurists: This is more connected to jurisprudence and more suitable for legal branches, establishing the foundational principles based on what has been transmitted from the branches by their imams, claiming that these are the principles that those imams observed when they derived those branches" (4). After that, Dr. Hayto mentions some of the most important books that combined both ways, such as "Jam' al-Jawami'" by Imam Taj al-Din al-Subki, and "Musallam al-Thubut" by the scholar Muhibb al-Din ibn Abd al-Shakur (5).
