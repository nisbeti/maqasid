As for its application in necessities, he said: "And as for it being in the realm of necessity as a form of alleviation, this is also evident and is a stronger proof for lifting hardship, for there is nothing in it that indicates strictness or an increase in obligation" (1).

Based on this, the principle of public interest (maslaha) is not a justification for claiming that there is a zone of ambiguity in the Sharia or that there are matters about which the Sharia has remained silent without clarifying their ruling, and that rulings are applied based on public interests. Such a claim is rejected by al-Shatibi because the interests themselves can only be known through the Sharia. This is one aspect. On the other hand, there is nothing that the Sharia has remained silent about in the sense of not clarifying its ruling; rather, there are matters for which no specific evidence has been provided, but they are indicated by general principles and analogies. They fall under general or comprehensive rules, and there is no silence from the Sharia in the sense of lack of clarification. It has been previously established that the Sharia contains clarification of everything. Al-Shatibi said: "The silence of the legislator regarding a ruling is of two types: one is that he remains silent about it because there is no compelling reason for it or necessity for it, such as the events that occurred after the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), for these did not exist and then were silent about with their existence; rather, they occurred later, and the people of the Sharia needed to consider them and apply them based on what has been established in its general principles. What the righteous predecessors initiated falls under this category, such as compiling the Qur'an, recording knowledge, and the inclusion of craftsmen, and similar matters that were not mentioned during the time of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and they were not among the events of his time nor was there a necessity for them to be acted upon. This category has its branches that follow its established principles in the Sharia without any doubt" (2).
