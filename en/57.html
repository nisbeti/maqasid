"The third: it is that which necessitates the ruling by itself and not by the decree of Allah, which is the view of the Mu'tazila based on their principle of rational good and evil." (1)

"The fourth: it is that which necessitates by custom, and this was chosen by Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi." (2)

As for the fifth view, it is: "the motivator for legislation in the sense that the description must encompass a valid interest that is intended by the legislator in establishing the ruling." (3)

As for the first view: that it is the identifier, meaning a sign of the ruling, there is no doubt that the cause is an identifier of the ruling and a sign of it, as it is a description that exists in the original. However, this is not sufficient for identification or application, and it will become clear when mentioning the conditions of the cause that the cause, which is a pillar in analogy (qiyas), is not sufficient for it to be merely a sign or indicator. There must be in this sign or indicator or description something that makes it suitable for reasoning or indicative of it. For example, the smell of wine is a sign of it, but it is not suitable for reasoning with it, unlike intoxication, as it has an additional element that makes it suitable for reasoning, provided that all other conditions of the cause are met.

Al-Amidi, for example, uses the term indicator in relation to the cause, but he does not mean a mere indicator. He says: "The rational cause necessitates the ruling by itself and not by designation, whereas the legal cause is in the sense of an indicator or sign or in the sense of a motivator." (4) He also says: "They disagreed on the permissibility of the cause in its original meaning being that of a mere indicator, and the preferred view is that the cause in its original meaning must be that of a motivator, meaning it must encompass a valid wisdom that is intended by the legislator in establishing the ruling; otherwise, if it were merely a descriptive characteristic without wisdom, but a mere indicator, then reasoning with it in its original sense would be impossible." (5)
