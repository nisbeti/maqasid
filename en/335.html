... These are examples of what is meant by custom (urf). The term custom may be used to refer to what people commonly agree upon in applying certain words to specific meanings, such that the indication of the word towards the meaning becomes a matter of customary truth, and this is akin to terminology. It may also refer to what people commonly agree upon regarding estimations of wages, prices, dowries, and the values of goods...

... The intended meaning of custom here is that which was mentioned in the examples above. This is not a source of rulings; rather, it requires a ruling from the Sharia. The Sharia came to judge the actions of people and their customs and traditions. Therefore, if it becomes customary for a woman to expose her neck, hair, or legs without it being considered reprehensible, this has no legal value, and these things remain private parts (awra) whose exposure is considered ugly.

... Thus, the custom that must be taken into account is what the Sharia has indicated is permissible, and then it becomes a sign or indication of something else as in the examples above. This does not change the rulings. For example, if a particular action becomes indicative of something that is legally reprehensible, this action becomes reprehensible according to the Sharia. For instance, if a man covers only his private parts while exposing everything else, and this becomes considered ugly in the eyes of people, then its prohibition in the Sharia does not mean that the private parts have changed; rather, the prohibition is due to contradicting modesty. The Sharia has commanded modesty. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Modesty is a branch of faith" (1), and there is no doubt that such an action contradicts modesty.

... However, if the action is not permissible, it cannot become an accepted custom, and opposing such a custom is a requirement of the Sharia. This is the concept of custom and its consideration according to al-Shatibi. Below are some of his texts to document what we have mentioned.
