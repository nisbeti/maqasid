Professor Muhammad Abu al-Ajfan said: "As for the cultural climate that Granada experienced during the life of al-Shatibi, it was relatively flourishing, as the tradition of scientific interest continued, and scholars engaged in enriching the reservoir of knowledge with their writings and research, while the chain of hadith and the narration of books of knowledge and the documentation of the programs (1) of the scholars persisted" (2).

Education and authorship were active, and two large scientific institutions were established in Granada: the Great Mosque and the Nasrid School. Authorship covered various branches of empirical, legal, and philosophical sciences. Professor Abu al-Ajfan states: "Thus, the scientific movement was flourishing until the late eighth century, indicating a strength that was able to resist the period of decline that was seeping into Andalusia and pushing it toward its inevitable fate. The thinkers and scholars of Andalusia during this turbulent time made attempts at reform and healing divisions, called for jihad, instilled a spirit of determination in souls, and raised awareness of the looming danger" (3).

(1) Programs: "A type of record that includes the scholars of its authors and what they took from them in narrations, and what they read to them from books, or it includes the scholars of a particular scholar whom another person undertook to gather and mention what was taken from them." (2) See: Muhammad Abu al-Ajfan, his edition of the Al-Irfadat wa al-Inshadat of al-Shatibi, p. 15.
