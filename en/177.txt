Al-Shatibi says in his ninth introduction that the religious sciences (ulum al-sharia) correspond to the rational sciences (ulum al-aqliya) in terms of the possibility of certainty regarding them or deriving certainty from them. Just as the legal truths are situational, meaning they are established by the legislator, the truths of existence and things are also situational, established by the Creator. He states: "Situational truths may correspond to rational truths in providing certain knowledge. The knowledge of the Sharia is among them, as knowledge of it is derived from the general induction that organizes its disparate individuals until they become in the mind a collection of general, consistent, established universals that are neither transient nor changeable nor subject to judgment. These are the characteristics of rational universals. Furthermore, rational universals are derived from existence, which is a situational matter, not a rational one, thus they are equal to the legal universals in this regard, and the distinction between them is removed" (1). Accordingly, the legal universals have three characteristics: the first is generality and consistency,... the second is permanence without decay,... and the third is that knowledge is a judge, not something judged upon (2).
The meaning of the first characteristic is that legal rulings apply to the actions of the obligated individuals absolutely; there is no action that is not judged by some legal universal, and if an action is assumed not to be judged by any universal, it is judged by some other universal (3).
