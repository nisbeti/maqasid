Therefore, it is necessary to clarify the meaning of the cause (illah), which is a cornerstone in analogy (qiyas), by defining the terms that are frequently used in the books of usul (principles of jurisprudence) in the sense of cause or with the term cause metaphorically, which are not actually causes, leading to the misconception that they are the cause of analogy, such as the term reason (sabab) and the term criterion (manat).
Reason (sabab):
It is said in Lisan al-Arab: "A reason is anything that one uses to reach something else, and in another version, anything that one uses to intercede for something else, and it has been caused to it, and the plural is reasons. Anything that one uses to reach something is a reason, and I made so-and-so a reason to so-and-so in my need, and a means and a conduit" (1). This is the linguistic meaning.
As for the legal terminology, it is a sign or indicator that defines the existence of the legal ruling. Al-Zarkashi said: "In Sharia, most scholars say it is the apparent, defined description that the evidence of the hearing indicates to be a defining factor for the legal ruling" (2), and he added: "Like making the decline of the sun a defining factor for the obligation of prayer... like the decline for prayer and the sighting of the crescent in Ramadan for the obligation of fasting, and like the minimum threshold (nisab) for zakat" (3).
The fact that the reason is a defining factor for the legal ruling means that the ruling exists with its presence but not by its existence. The presence of the sun in the sky is a sign that indicates the entry of the time for the noon prayer and the existence of the ruling of the noon prayer, which is obligation. Similarly, the crescent of Ramadan is a sign that indicates the entry of the month of Ramadan and the existence of the legal ruling, which is the obligation of fasting. Likewise, the crescent of Shawwal indicates the existence of the holiday and the obligation to break the fast, and the existence of the minimum threshold for zakat is a sign of the existence of the ruling, which is the obligation of zakat.
All of these are mere signs or indicators and do not contain a rational meaning that can be used for reasoning. The sun in the sky is suitable for indicating the existence of the ruling but is not suitable for reasoning the ruling of prayer, and the same applies to other examples.
