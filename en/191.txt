The depiction of the particular and the universal in rulings:
Al-Shatibi addresses this issue by stating that rulings have two perspectives, one as they are particular and the other as they are universal.
He responds to those who say that the permissible (mubah) is to be avoided because one who engages in what is permissible may become distracted from what is required to be done, and he counters those who say that it is required to be done because one who engages in what is permissible has abandoned what is prohibited (haram), saying: "The permissible in the view of the Lawgiver is that which one is free to choose between doing and not doing, without praise or blame for either action or inaction" (1). He states: "The permissible, in its nature as permissible, is neither required to be done nor required to be avoided" (2). Here, that is, in its nature as permissible, it is viewed as a particular; however, if it is viewed as something required to be done or required to be avoided, that is from a perspective in which it serves or undermines the universal.
The permissible is viewed in terms of its particularity and its universality; however, in terms of its universality, there is nothing that one is free to choose because it would be mere play. As for its particularity, that is, a specific legal ruling for a specific action, if one is free to choose it, this means it is stripped of wisdom or objective, and this does not negate the idea of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in al-Shatibi's view because he applies them to universals rather than particulars. He says: "The recognized interests are the universals, not the particulars" (3).
