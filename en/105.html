It is a description that has not been established by a ruling of consideration, and the consideration has appeared for its opposite. Al-Asnawi said: "The appropriate description is of three types: the first is that the legislator (shari) disregards it, meaning that he presents the branches in opposition to it, and there is no doubt that it is not permissible to use it for reasoning" (1). He then said: "This is like the obligation of fasting for two months as expiation for sexual intercourse during the day in Ramadan for the owner, even though it is more effective in restraining him than emancipation, but the legislator has disregarded it by making emancipation obligatory from the outset, so it cannot be considered" (2).

Al-Amidi said: "The appropriate description that has not been established by any original consideration in any way, and yet its disregard and the legislator's disinterest in it have appeared in a particular case, is something that has been agreed upon to be invalidated and cannot be relied upon" (3). He then cited the same example mentioned above from Al-Asnawi.

Al-Shatibi said: "What the law has rejected cannot be accepted, for the appropriateness does not necessitate a ruling by itself; rather, this is the view of the proponents of rational improvement" (4). He then cited the same example mentioned above, attributing it to Al-Ghazali, and said: "This is appropriate because the purpose of the expiation in the law is deterrence, and the owner is not deterred by emancipation but is deterred by fasting, and this fatwa is invalid" (5).
