There is no contradiction between these two principles according to al-Shatibi, as the benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) are determined by the Sharia, meaning that whether something is a benefit or a harm falls under the category of commendation and condemnation. However, a contradiction arises if the benefit is considered according to the perspective of rational individuals and those who deliberate, and their determination of what is suitable or what constitutes a benefit and what constitutes a harm. Those who hold this view have their evidence for reasoning based on benefits, so do their proofs contradict the principle of worship and compliance?
... The answer according to al-Shatibi is: Yes, they do contradict. Therefore, he attempts to negate this contradiction in a way that turns matters upside down. After he concluded the proof and establishment of the principle of compliance as a definitive objective of the Lawgiver, he turned to what he fundamentally relied upon to justify the Sharia by bringing about benefits and repelling harms, and he reconfigured it in another way. He said: "If it is said: The establishment of the laws is either in vain or for a wisdom, the first is invalid by consensus, and Allah, Exalted is He, said: { Did you think that We created you in vain? } (1), and He said: { And We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them in vain } (2), { And We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them as play. We did not create them except in truth } (3). If it is for a wisdom and benefit, then the benefit is either directed to Allah, Exalted is He, or to the servants. Its return to Allah is impossible because He is Self-Sufficient, and it is impossible for benefits to return to Him as clarified in the science of theology. Thus, it remains that it returns to the servants, which is necessitated by their interests, for every doer seeks the benefit of himself and what aligns with his worldly and otherworldly affairs, and the Sharia has guaranteed this demand within the framework of obligation. So how can it be denied that the Sharia is established according to the interests of the servants and the motives of their desires? Furthermore, it has been previously stated that the Sharia came in accordance with the interests of the servants and confirmed their shares as a favor from Allah according to what the scholars assert, or as an obligation according to what the Mu'tazila claim. If this is established as a true objective of the Lawgiver, then what contradicts it is false" (4). This text presents an objection to al-Shatibi regarding how he establishes benefits, and al-Shatibi responds to this text not by denying or refuting it, but by affirming it while directing its meaning or interpretation in a way that differs from the conventional or immediate understanding.
