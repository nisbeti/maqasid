And what is not a definitive meaning cannot be applied. Al-Shatibi stipulates that for a proof to be applied, it must be definitive or refer to a definitive meaning (1). He says: "Every legal proof is either definitive or speculative. If it is definitive, there is no doubt in considering it, such as the proofs for the obligation of purification from ritual impurity, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, enjoining good, forbidding wrong, unity of the community, justice, and similar matters. If it is speculative, it is either based on a definitive origin or not. If it is based on a definitive origin, it is also considered valid; if it is not, one must be cautious with it, and it is not correct to categorically accept it. However, it is of two types: one that opposes an origin, and one that neither opposes nor agrees with it. Thus, all of them fall into four categories. As for the first, it does not require explanation, while the second, which refers to a definitive origin, is also clearly applicable, and this applies to most reports of the individual narrators, as they clarify the Book" (2). He then says: "As for the third, which opposes a definitive origin and is not supported by a definitive origin, it is rejected without doubt. Two points serve as evidence for this: the first is that it contradicts the principles of the Sharia, and what contradicts the principles of the Sharia cannot be valid because it does not belong to it, and what does not belong to it cannot be considered part of it. The second is that it has no evidence supporting its validity, and what is like that is disregarded" (3). Let us pay attention here to the second point in the evidence for rejection. It is that it has no evidence supporting its validity. This indicates something new according to Al-Shatibi, which is that the legal meaning or legal proof requires a meaning that supports it in order to be accepted. It becomes clear in the context of the entire text that the supporting evidence must be a definitive meaning. He then says: "As for the fourth, which is speculative and neither supported by a definitive origin nor opposes a definitive origin, it is subject to consideration. Its category is that of the strange analogy. It may be said that it is not accepted because it establishes a law that is not consistent with what has been established in its like."
