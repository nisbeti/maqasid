The right of the servant is not an inherent right, but rather it is by the decree of Allah - Exalted is He - that this right exists. Likewise, the right of the servant contains a right of Allah. Al-Shatibi said: "From here, the scholars say that some obligations are rights of Allah alone, which pertain to worship, and some are rights of the servant. They say regarding the latter that it also contains a right of Allah, as in the case of a murderer who is pardoned; he is to be struck one hundred times and imprisoned for a year"(1). He then said: "Thus, every obligation has become a right of Allah; what is for Allah is for Him, and what is for the servant returns to Allah in terms of Allah's right in it and in terms of the servant's right being one of Allah's rights, since it is within Allah's authority not to grant the servant any right at all"(2).

Accordingly, the right of the servant is not absolute, for every obligation therein is a right of Allah.

The rights of Allah are of two types. The first: what is a right of Allah that is purely His, which cannot be waived, such as acts of worship. The second: the rights of others, which the servant cannot waive on behalf of others; however, that other person has the right to waive their own right. Unlike the acts of worship from the first category, which cannot be waived. The rights of others include sales, exchanges, and expenditures, so the servant must fulfill the right that is owed to the one who has it. If that latter person waives their right, then that is their choice.

The right of others over the servant is one of the rights of other servants, therefore it is not absolute, and it includes a right of Allah. If the other party waives their right, it is nullified, and the right of Allah remains. This is "as in the case of a murderer who commits intentional killing; if he is pardoned, he is struck with a hundred lashes and imprisoned for a year, and in the case of a murderer by treachery, there is no pardon for him. And in the punishments (hudud), if they reach the ruler regarding matters other than retribution, such as slander and theft, there is no pardon in them even if the one entitled to the right forgives"(3).
