If we consider al-Shatibi's opinion regarding the second type of objectives of the Lawgiver (maqasid al-shari'a), which is to establish understanding, where he stated that the texts do not indicate the secondary meanings, in addition to the fact that the public interest (maslaha) is what has been definitively established through induction, it seems that al-Shatibi is rejecting the method of reasoning by appropriate description and is refuting the justification of the Sharia by bringing about benefits and repelling harms in the conventional sense held by its proponents.
... This is what led Sheikh Muhammad al-Tahir ibn Ashour to see that most of al-Shatibi's arguments for justifying the Sharia are invalid(1). This is also what prompted Dr. Ahmed Rissouni to label this opinion of al-Shatibi as one of the negative effects of theological discourse, asserting that the reason for al-Shatibi's stance is his influence by the Ash'ari perspective. Dr. Rissouni states under the title: "Understanding benefits by reason": "The well-known issue in theological discourse and in the science of Usul al-Fiqh regarding the issue of good and evil... because al-Shatibi himself did not escape some of its negative effects, specifically his influence by the Ash'ari perspective on the subject. In fact, this Ash'ari perspective is still prevalent - if not dominant - today"(2).
