And His saying: { Fighting has been prescribed for you, and it is a great hardship for you } (1) the verse. And His saying: { And there is for you in retaliation (qisas) life } (2). This is something that considers the outcome in general (3). These texts are indeed used to establish induction, and the evidence in them is that the ruling has appeared and the intended outcome has been made clear. This evidence is itself the proof that the Sharia was legislated for the sake of the interests of the servants (4). In fact, some of these texts used as evidence here are also used as evidence there.
One can critique al-Shatibi's arguments here, as his first and second proofs refer back to the third; they are essentially one proof in reality. This evidence is itself the proof for the reasoning of the Sharia in bringing about benefits and averting harms, so he has not introduced anything new in establishing the consideration of the outcomes of actions, and he did not need to provide evidence. Anyone who adopts his methodology from its origin is obligated to adhere to the principle of the outcomes of actions, and whoever rejects the principle of the outcomes of actions must also reject the methodology from its origin.
