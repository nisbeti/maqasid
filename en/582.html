And if this is the concept of istihsan (juridical preference) according to Abu Hanifa (may Allah have mercy on him), then there is no disagreement between him and those who criticize istihsan. However, the question is: why this terminology? Is there a relationship between this term and the act of leaving the general for the specific or taking the stronger of the two evidences? Al-Zarkashi mentioned statements that indicate this inquiry, including: "The disagreement between us and them is merely verbal, for the interpretation of istihsan in a way that they criticize is not something they accept. What they do accept is the deviation in ruling from one evidence to another that is stronger than it. This is something that has not been denied. However, we do not recognize this name as a term for what is said based on such evidence" (1).

What I see regarding this matter is that this terminology may relate to a particularity within the school, as it is reported from Abu Hanifa and the majority of the school that they do not consider the specification of the general by the specific unless the specific is later than the general. If the general is the later one, it abrogates the specific. However, it may be unknown which is later, or it may be presumed that the general is later, but the specific is accompanied by indications that make it more impactful and necessitate its application in consideration and ijtihad (independent reasoning), such as when it is reported that the Companions practiced it. In this case, the principles of the school dictate adherence to the general and neglect of the specific, but the indications accompanying the specific strengthen it, and ijtihad leads to its application and the specification of the general by it. From here - and Allah knows best - expressions like: "what has a strong effect even if it is hidden" and "what has a visible effect and hidden corruption" emerge, and perhaps this is a valid justification for the use of the term istihsan.
