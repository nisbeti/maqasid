These were the conditions for ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) for those who wished to derive rulings from the texts based on their understanding of them. There is a lower rank for the mujtahid (jurist) in which knowledge of Arabic is not a requirement; he may even be a non-Arabic speaker. However, what is required in this case is knowledge of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in general and in detail. There is also a type of ijtihad, as al-Shatibi calls it, which does not require knowledge of Arabic or knowledge of the objectives of the Sharia; this is the ijtihad in determining the context of the ruling. He says: "If ijtihad pertains to deriving rulings from the texts, then knowledge of Arabic must be a condition. However, if it pertains to the meanings of benefits and harms, abstracted from the texts or accepted from the one who has derived rulings from the texts, then knowledge of Arabic is not required; rather, knowledge of the objectives of the Sharia in general and in detail is necessary" (1). He also said: "Thus, whoever understands the objectives of the Sharia in establishing rulings and reaches a level of knowledge of them, even if his understanding comes through translation in a non-Arabic tongue, there is no difference between him and one who understands them through the Arabic language" (2). This rank is lower than the first rank, and its holder does not engage in ijtihad to reach the objectives, nor does he derive rulings based on his understanding of them; rather, he receives the general principles and foundations and learns them from those who have derived them, so he is constrained by his understanding and his school of thought. Likewise, he cannot apply both the general principles and the specific cases together; rather, he only applies the general principles.
