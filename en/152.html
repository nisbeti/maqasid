He said: "These six aspects indicate that the Exalted One has not legislated the rulings except for the benefit of the servants. Then people differed after that: as for the Mu'tazila, they explicitly stated the reality of this position and uncovered its meaning, saying: It is reprehensible for Allah, Exalted is He, to commit what is wrong, and to act in vain; rather, His actions must encompass a purpose of benefit and aim. As for the jurists, they assert that He, Exalted is He, legislated the ruling for this meaning and for this wisdom, and if they heard the term 'aim', they would declare the speaker a disbeliever, even though the only meaning of that 'lam' (ل) is the aim. Moreover, they say: 'Although it is not obligatory for Allah, Exalted is He, to consider the interests, He does not act except in what is beneficial for His servants, as a favor and kindness, not out of obligation.' This is the discussion regarding the establishment of this premise." (1)

As for al-Razi's refutation of the principle of reasoning behind rulings and interests, it comes in a lengthy discourse, of which we will mention some. He said: "His statement: The specification of a particular case with a specific ruling must have a reason, and that reason cannot return to Allah, Exalted is He, so it must return to the servant. We say: Either you claim that specification must have a specific reason or you do not claim that; in either case, you cannot assert that the rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, are based on interests." (2) He then presented a lengthy argument to refute the claim of reasoning behind the rulings of Allah, and all of this discussion pertains to the essence of Allah, Exalted is He.
