Thus, the intended meaning of al-Shatibi with the term "foundations" is confirmed, along with his method of establishing them, which is induction, and the meaning of returning to a definitive origin, which is part of his new contributions that provide us with some controls or principles of his methodology. He - may Allah have mercy on him - pointed out this difference between him and the earlier usuliyyin (foundational scholars). He said: "Know that the purpose of returning to the definitive origin is not to establish proof of the validity of acting upon it, like the proof that acting upon the report of a single narrator or by analogy is obligatory, for example. Rather, what is intended is something more specific than that, as previously mentioned in the hadith 'There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm' and the issues mentioned alongside it, which is a meaning contrary to that intended by the usuliyyin, and Allah knows best" (1).
The particular and the branch and the difference between them:
It has been clarified previously that the foundations of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) are general principles derived from particulars. These particulars are the indications and meanings of the legal texts. And these particulars are speculative and are not considered unless they return to the general principles. The meanings of the legal texts are their linguistic indications, and the descriptions that are conceivable or appropriate for the rulings, and the outcomes and results and causes of the rulings. Although these are speculative, they are indicated by the texts, or they do not indicate them but are merely presumed to be intended by the legislator; these are particulars.
So, if one of these meanings - which is particular - returns to a general origin, it is then called a branch. Accordingly, every branch is particular, but not every particular is a branch. The lack of the particular returning to the general is a distinction that differentiates the particular from the branch.
