He said: "Ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is of two types: one that cannot cease until the foundation of obligation ceases, which is at the Day of Judgment, and the second that can cease before the end of the world. As for the first, it is the ijtihad related to the determination of the legal rule" (1).

As for the fact that the determination of the legal rule requires ijtihad, it has been previously established. Among what he said in this context is: "When the legislator said: { And bring to witness two just men from among you } (2) and it is established for us that the meaning of justice is a legal one, we need to specify who possesses this attribute. People are not equal in the description of justice; rather, it varies significantly. For when we contemplate the just, we find that their characterization has extremes and a mean: an upper extreme in justice that is undoubtedly clear, like Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, and a lower extreme which is the first degree of deviating from the requirements of the description" (3). Then he said: "Between them are ranks that cannot be enumerated, and this middle ground is ambiguous; it is necessary to reach the limit of capacity, which is ijtihad. This is what the judge requires in every witness" (4).
