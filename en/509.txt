The jurists (mujtahidun) are rewarded, both the one who errs and the one who is correct. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "If a ruler exercises ijtihad and errs, he will have one reward, and if he is correct, he will have two rewards" (1).
What the jurist arrives at through his ijtihad is what he is most certain is the ruling of Allah, and it becomes the ruling of Allah in his case. It is not permissible for him to abandon it for the opinion of another jurist nor to issue a fatwa except based on it (2). Al-Shatibi said: "The entire Sharia returns to one opinion in its branches, even if there is much disagreement, just as it does in its foundations, and nothing else is valid in it" (3). He also said: "Correctness is in accordance with the intention of the legislator, and error is in opposition to it" (4). He stated: "If it is said that all are correct, it is not absolute but rather relative to each jurist or to those who follow him, as they all agree that no jurist is permitted to retract what his ijtihad has led him to nor to issue a fatwa except based on it" (5). He remarked: "To allow for the existence of two contradictory proofs, if those who advocate this mean contradiction in appearance and in the views of the jurists, not in reality, then what they say is permissible; however, this does not imply the permissibility of contradiction in the proofs of the Sharia. If they intend to allow this in reality, then this is not something that anyone who understands the Sharia would adopt, given the evidence that has been presented against it, and I do not think any of them would say it" (6). It was narrated from Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said: "This cannot be two differing correct opinions" (7).
