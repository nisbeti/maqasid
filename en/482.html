The first: His saying: "Some of it is very general, as if it were akin to taking evidence from the Book regarding the validity of acting upon the Sunnah and the necessity of adhering to it" (1). This meaning is based on the evidences that command taking the Sunnah, including His saying, Exalted is He: { And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it; and fear Allah } (2). This is an accepted meaning according to al-Shatibi, as taking the Sunnah is a definitive principle, but al-Shatibi requires more than that, which is that the specific meaning from the Sunnah returns to a Qur'anic meaning. Therefore, he provides an example here, saying: "And among those who adhered to it (3) is Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, for it was narrated that a woman from Banu Asad came to him and said: 'I have heard that you cursed so-and-so and so-and-so, the tattooer and the one who has tattoos, and I have read what is between the two boards and did not find what you say.' Abdullah said to her: 'Did you not read: { And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it; and fear Allah }?' She said: 'Yes.' He said: 'That is it' " (4). This is one of the meanings of clarification, which differs from what al-Shatibi intends, as previously explained. He indicates this here by saying: "The apparent statement to her is in the Book of Allah, then he explained that by saying: { And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it } , without mentioning the saying: { And I will surely order them to change the creation of Allah } (5), for that verse encompasses all that is in the prophetic hadith" (6). It appears in this statement that al-Shatibi relates the meaning of the prohibition of tattoos to the Qur'anic meaning { And I will surely order them to change the creation of Allah }.
