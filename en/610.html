The First Topic

A brief overview of the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi.

The meaning of the Muwafaqat:

The choice of the name "al-Muwafaqat" (The Concurrences) refers to a meaning intended by al-Shatibi, which is the reconciliation between the schools of thought by clarifying that although they differ in branches, they all revert to recognized principles and foundations among all. The reason for the disagreement is the differing opinions of the imams in tracing issues back to their origins; one favors tracing it back to one origin while another favors tracing it back to a different origin. As for the principles, all of them are recognized and relied upon by the imams. Despite the novelty and provocative nature of his methodology, he states that it is acknowledged by the recognized imams even if they do not mention it or draw attention to it, so it should not be dismissed by those who are unaware of it on the grounds that they have not heard of its like.

As for the meaning of reconciling between the schools of thought, he referred to this by mentioning a striking story. He said: "Due to the secrets of obligation related to this noble Sharia that I have embedded in it, I named it 'The Title of Introduction to the Secrets of Obligation.' Then I transitioned from this theme to a strange chain of narration that astonishes the perceptive and astute. The essence of it is that one day I met some of the scholars whom I had allowed to take a place of benefit from me, and I made my scientific gatherings with them a destination for travel and a place for receiving knowledge. I had begun to arrange and classify the book, casting aside distractions without refining or composing it. He said to me, 'I saw you last night in a dream with a book you authored. I asked you about it, and you told me it was "The Book of Al-Muwafaqat." He said, 'I was going to ask you about the meaning of this elegant title, and you informed me that you reconciled between the opinions of Ibn al-Qasim and Abu Hanifa.' I said to him, 'You have hit the mark with a share of the righteous vision, and you have taken from the prophetic glad tidings a good portion and share, for I have begun to compose these meanings, intending to establish those foundations, for they are the recognized principles among the scholars and the rules upon which the ancients built.' The sheikh was astonished by the strangeness of this agreement, just as I was astonished by traversing this wilderness and accompanying these companions."(1)
