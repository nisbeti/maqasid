The principle is also based on what is called the principle of cooperation, and one of its evidences is His saying, Exalted is He: { And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression } (1). Thus, an action that is permissible, if it leads to something forbidden, is a means to it and is considered as assisting in the forbidden objective; therefore, the means takes the ruling of the objective. If it leads to something that is commanded, it is a means to righteousness and assists in the legally required objective, and it also takes the ruling of the objective. If the action is not a means to anything, it is the absolute permissible (mubah). He said: "The permissible, as it is permissible, is equal on both sides" (2). Then he said: "If it is a means to something prohibited, it becomes prohibited in terms of blocking the means, not in terms of being permissible" (3). Then he said that the permissible: "is of three categories: one category is a means to something forbidden, and thus from that perspective, it is required to be abandoned. A second category is a means to something commanded, like what is assisted in an otherworldly matter" (4). Then he said: "A third category is not a means to anything, and it is the absolute permissible. In general, if it is assumed to be a means to something else, its ruling is the ruling of that other thing" (5). It must be noted that the action he is discussing is the action that is originally permissible. And his saying: "in general" means that it is not absolute. For often an action does not take the ruling of the objective. And when the outcome or objective is prohibited and the action takes the ruling of the objective based on the principle of cooperation, this is also an application of the principle of blocking the means. He said: "The principle of blocking the means applies based on the principle of cooperation in obedience or disobedience, for this principle is agreed upon in consideration, and there are matters in which there is disagreement, such as the means in sales and similar matters, although the principle of blocking the means is also agreed upon, and it also includes the principle of conflicting original and predominant" (6).
