As for what is necessary for ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), it is essentially - according to al-Shatibi - a single comprehensive matter that encompasses various aspects, which is the complete understanding of the objectives of the Sharia (Islamic law), namely the general principles and the objectives of the rulings pertaining to them. The other matters mentioned as conditions for ijtihad are included in this matter: he said, "The degree of ijtihad is attained by one who possesses two characteristics: the first is a complete understanding of the objectives of the Sharia, and the second is the ability to derive rulings based on that understanding" (1). As for the first characteristic, it means the general principles of the Sharia that fall into three categories and their complements, and it also refers to the detailed rulings and their objectives, which are the branches that relate to those general principles; thus, he said: "on its completeness." All of these relate to achieving benefits and preventing harms. Among what is necessitated by a complete understanding of the objectives is understanding what al-Shatibi meant by benefits and harms, which is that they are based on the designation of the Lawgiver and not according to personal interests, desires, and whims. This has been clarified previously, and al-Shatibi reiterated it here, thus bringing together the reasoning based on benefits and the intention of worship and compliance. He said, "It has been established in the book of the legal objectives that the Sharia is based on considering benefits, and that benefits are only considered based on how the Lawgiver has designated them, not based on the perception of the obligated person" (2). He said, "It has been established through thorough observation that benefits fall into three categories, and when a person reaches a level where he understands the Lawgiver's intention in every issue of the Sharia and in every chapter of its rulings, he has attained a characteristic that qualifies him to take the place of the Prophet's successor - peace and blessings be upon him - in teaching, issuing fatwas, and judging by what Allah has shown him" (3). As for the second characteristic, it cannot be attained except after the first characteristic is achieved, but he made it a second condition because it is a fruit of the first, and the realization of the first cannot occur without the existence of the second. He said, "As for the second, it is like a servant to the first" (4).
