Add to this, for example: Prayer and fasting are rooted in the preservation of religion, which is one of the necessities. How can breaking the fast, combining prayers, and shortening them be permitted in a journey where hardship is hardly mentioned, or for a wealthy king? However, if the journey involves hardship and significant difficulty, then according to the methodology of al-Shatibi, this elevates the concession from being merely a concession to being something that is required, recommended, or obligatory, and it serves overarching principles, such as the principle of alleviating hardship or not being burdened with what causes significant difficulty, or the principle of preserving life. For example, eating carrion in a state of necessity serves the principle of necessities, which is the preservation of life, and this is a requirement, not merely a concession. Similarly, the sitting of the followers behind a sitting imam is not a concession but rather an obligation and a necessity that complements the necessities, unlike the sitting of the imam, which is a concession if he is able to stand with difficulty. On one hand, and on the other hand, if we concede to returning concessions to overarching principles, this undermines the idea that the overarching principles support and reinforce one another, and that all rulings and principles serve and return to the preservation of necessities, which are the preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth. In His saying, Exalted is He: { Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his belief, except for one who is forced while his heart is secure in faith } (1). And in His saying, Exalted is He: { And when you travel throughout the land, there is no blame upon you for shortening the prayer } (2), the concessions do not support the principles; rather, they undermine them. Indeed, the preservation of religion takes precedence over the preservation of life, and yet uttering the word of disbelief in this case is permissible, as is shortening the prayer in a journey where there is no hardship or difficulty, which does not serve the principle of preserving religion, meaning it does not serve its universality. This leads to the conclusion that the principles do not support one another.
