What al-Amidi intended by the response in this text is to consider public interest (maslaha) when there is no evidence to indicate it, and this is unanimously rejected. However, what is meant by those who advocate for the unqualified public interest is accepted by all, including those who have rejected it. Al-Qarafi confirms this by stating: "As for the unqualified public interest, it is reported that it is specific to us, and when the schools of thought are lacking, they find that when they compare and differentiate between the two issues, they do not seek evidence by consideration for that meaning by which they compared and differentiated, but rather they suffice with mere suitability. This is the unqualified public interest, and thus it exists in all schools of thought" (1). He also said: "It has been established that the unqualified public interest exists in all schools of thought upon investigation, for they compare and differentiate based on suitability and do not seek evidence by consideration, and we mean by the unqualified public interest only that. What further confirms the practice of the unqualified public interest is that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) undertook matters for the sake of pure public interest, not due to the presence of evidence by consideration, such as the compilation of the Qur'an, for there was no prior command or analogy regarding it. Likewise, the matter of leaving the caliphate to consultation, the recording of the registers, the minting of currency for Muslims, and the establishment of prisons were actions taken by Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), as well as the demolition of the endowments adjacent to the Mosque of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and the expansion of the mosque when it became cramped, which was done by Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him), and the renewal of the call to prayer on Friday in the market, which is the first call to prayer" (2). He then said: "And this is very common for pure public interest, and the Imam of the Two Holy Sanctuaries has addressed in his book titled Al-Ghayathi matters that he permitted and ruled upon, while the Malikis are distant from them and have crossed over them for the sake of pure public interest, as well as Al-Ghazali in Shifa al-Ghalil, even though both of them strongly deny our stance on the unqualified public interest" (3).
