To clarify the difference between the two issues, I say: al-Shatibi did not present the fifth issue to define benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), but rather to indicate that they are not limited to mere existence, and that they are limited to the Sharia. As for the text upon which Dr. Daraz based his objection, which raises a problem, its meaning is that what the Sharia has brought forth in terms of commands and prohibitions, and what is established based on it as beneficial and harmful, is what returns to the human being in a way that preserves his life and the completeness of his existence through food, shelter, and other things that his sensual and rational attributes necessitate, such as security, dignity, ownership, and other pleasures of life. These can only be attained through what leads to them, which are the rulings of the Sharia. Therefore, al-Shatibi's text does not say: "By benefits I mean the sustenance of human life and obtaining what his sensual attributes necessitate," but rather says: "By benefits I mean what pertains to the sustenance of human life..." This meaning is clear in al-Shatibi's book for those who reflect upon it, and it will be elaborated upon when discussing the fourth type of the objectives of the Sharia, which is its establishment for compliance. His texts on this matter are numerous; I will mention here a text that suffices for the purpose. He says: "If it is established that the purpose of the Sharia is for the benefits of the servants, then it returns to them according to the command of the legislator and within the limits he has set, not according to their whims and desires... As for the fact that the benefits of obligation return to the obligated person in this world and the hereafter, this is correct, and it does not necessitate that obtaining them is outside the limits of the Sharia, nor that it is attained by oneself without the Sharia providing it to him, which is evident. Thus, it becomes clear that there is no contradiction between this and what has preceded, because what has preceded is a consideration in terms of establishing the benefit and purpose as affirmed by the legislator, not in terms of what is necessitated by whim and desire." (1)

The meaning of the text upon which Dr. Daraz based his objection is that the benefits indicated by the Sharia through induction are those that lead to a person obtaining his shares and purposes, and not that benefits are understood according to what returns to people based on their inclinations and perspectives.
