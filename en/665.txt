The second direction is a foundational discussion, provided that it is separated from the reasoning behind God's actions, regardless of the outcome, meaning whether the principle is reasoning or lack of reasoning. Al-Zarkashi said: "Ibn al-Hajib reported in his discussion on examination and classification the consensus of the jurists that a ruling must have a reason, and this was questioned based on the well-known principle that God's actions are not reasoned by purpose" (1). He said: "Know that the position of the Ahl al-Sunnah (People of the Sunnah) is that His rulings are not reasoned in the sense that He does not do anything for a purpose, nor is He compelled by anything to do something; rather, He is God, Exalted is He, capable of bringing about benefits without their causes and eliminating harms without their motivations. The jurists said that the rulings are reasoned, and they did not oppose the Ahl al-Sunnah, but rather they meant by reasoning: wisdom. The Mu'tazila and those who agreed with them among the jurists restricted this, claiming that His actions, Exalted is He, are bound by wisdom and limited by the aspect of benefit" (2). It is common among Muslims to separate the two matters, and that God, Exalted is He, does not act for a purpose. In the discussions of the theologians among the scholars of usul (foundations), there are arguments that support the position of those who reject the reasoning behind God's actions; indeed, their evidence is conclusive, as al-Razi said.
Rebuttal of the reasoning behind God's actions: Those who advocate for this differ in their methods of proof. The foundation of the idea for some of them is that God, Exalted is He, is wise in His creation, so the consideration of purpose in His creation must either be obligatory or not obligatory. If it is obligatory, then it does not lack the intended goal; and if it is not obligatory, then His action for the intended goal is closer to being in accordance with reason than His action without an intended goal (3).
