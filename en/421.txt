The purpose of including this research here is to clarify al-Shatibi's methodology in determining the meaning of a legal text when it is ambiguous. Therefore, we will first explain what al-Shatibi means by the definitive (muhkam) and the ambiguous (mutashabih).
He says: "The definitive is used in two senses, general and specific. As for the specific, it refers to that which is intended to mean the opposite of the abrogated (mansukh)"(1). He then said: "As for the general, it refers to the clear and evident that does not depend on anything else to clarify its meaning. The ambiguous in the first sense is the abrogated, and in the second sense, it is that which cannot be understood from its wording, whether it can be comprehended through research and contemplation or not"(2).
The intended meaning here is the second, meaning that the definitive is the clear and evident, and the ambiguous is that which cannot be understood from its wording. He said: "When one reflects on this usage, it becomes evident that the abrogated, the ambiguous, the apparent, the general, and the unrestricted are all included under the meaning of the ambiguous, just as the abrogating, what has a fixed ruling, the clarifying, the interpreted, the specified, and the restricted fall under the meaning of the definitive"(3).
Al-Shatibi clarifies that most rulings of the Sharia are definitive. He states that ambiguity is rare, saying: "It is known that ambiguity does occur in legal matters, but the consideration of how much of it exists is whether it is little or much, and what is established is the little, not the much"(4). He also said: "If it were not for the evidence proving that there is ambiguity in it, the statement would not be valid"(5).
