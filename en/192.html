As for looking at the permissible (mubah) from the perspective of the whole, it makes the permissible permissible in part and required in total; it may be recommended in total or obligatory in total, or permissible in part and prohibited in total; it may be disliked in total or forbidden in total. He says: "The permissibility according to the partial and the total is subject to the remaining rulings. The permissible is permissible in part and required in total in the sense of recommendation or obligation, and permissible in part and prohibited in total in the sense of dislike or prohibition. These are four categories: The first: like enjoying the good things of food, drink, transportation, and clothing... If some times are left out while being able to do so, it would be permissible just as if it were done. If it were completely abandoned, it would be contrary to what the Sharia has recommended... And likewise, if all people abandoned that, it would be disliked. The second: like eating and drinking, having relations with wives, buying and selling, and the obligation of permissible earnings... All these things are permissible in part, meaning if one chooses any of these things over others, that is permissible. And if a man leaves them at certain times or circumstances, or if some people leave them, that does not detract from it; if we assume that all people left that, it would be abandoning what is necessary and commanded. Thus, entering into it would be obligatory in total. The third: like strolling in gardens, listening to the cooing of doves, permissible singing, and permissible play with doves or others; such things are permissible in part, so if done one day or in a certain situation, there is no harm in it. If done constantly, it would be disliked, and its doer would be attributed to a lack of intellect and to going against the virtues of customs and to extravagance in doing that permissible act. The fourth: like the permissible things that undermine justice when done continuously, even if they are permissible." (1)
