Al-Juwayni summarized the many proofs by saying: "The usuliyyin (scholars of Islamic jurisprudence) have elaborated extensively on the methods of responding to the deniers, and our preferred approach consists of two paths: the first relies on a widely transmitted matter that only a denier would dispute and only an adversary would reject. This is that we know with certainty from our intellects that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) used to send messengers and entrust them with conveying the rulings and details of the lawful and unlawful. He would sometimes accompany them with books, and their transmission of the commands of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was on the basis of individual reports (ahad), and infallibility was not necessary for them. Thus, their reports were subject to suspicion, and this was established with certainty, widely transmitted without any refutation due to the nature of the transmission, and only a blatant contradiction could refute the widely transmitted. This is the first path. The second path is based on the consensus (ijma) of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), and their consensus on acting upon individual reports is transmitted widely. We have no doubt that in various incidents they sought rulings from the Book of Allah, and if they did not find a mention for the sought matter, they would turn to the reports of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and they would hasten to rely on the transmission of the trustworthy and reliable without disagreement" (1). Al-Zarkashi mentioned a similar statement as well (2).
The independence of the Sunnah in legislation: Al-Shatibi held that the Sunnah does not independently legislate based on the premise that the Qur'an contains an explanation of everything, and that the Sunnah is an explanation of it. Accordingly, the Sunnah does not introduce anything that has no basis in the Qur'an, neither in terms of subject matter (type or category) nor in terms of meaning. The commentary on this statement is that the fact that the Qur'an contains an explanation of everything and that the Sunnah is an explanation of the Qur'an does not mean that the Sunnah cannot independently legislate, nor that the Sunnah cannot impose obligations that have no basis in the Qur'an. For the indication of the Qur'an towards the Sunnah and the obligation to derive rulings from it is part of the explanation and completes the explanation, and the indication of the Sunnah towards matters not found in the Qur'an does not contradict the Qur'an being an explanation.
