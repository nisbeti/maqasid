The reality is that these issues arise for those who consider benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) when they are understood as meaning advantages and disadvantages, or the wisdoms (hikmah) and what human attributes necessitate. Therefore, al-Qarafi raised these issues but did not respond to them.
Al-Shatibi addressed these issues, and his response is valid according to his own methodology in considering benefits and harms; thus, his affirmation of the idea of reasoning based on benefit is a correction of the concept of this idea, for both benefit and harm. He says: "As for the Ash'arites, the induction (istiqra) of the Sharia indicates what is considered and what is not considered" (1). Thus, he directs consideration towards the induction that leads to certainty regarding the specific benefit, and he says: "As for the Mu'tazilites, it is the same, because they only consider benefits and harms based on what reason leads them to believe is the way to achieve the general and detailed welfare of the world in benefits or its disruption in harms. They have made the Sharia a revelation of what reason claims without addition or subtraction, so there is no difference between them and the Ash'arites in the outcome of the issue" (2). This means that they do not rely on reason in determining benefits and harms, but rather they rely on the Sharia. Accordingly, there is no issue according to al-Shatibi's methodology because the determination of benefit or harm is referred back to the Sharia. Al-Shatibi critiques the statement of Imam Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam, where he said: "Most of the benefits and harms of this world are known by reason, and that is the essence of most Sharia" (3), and where he said: "As for the benefits and harms of the Hereafter, they are only known through transmission" (4),
