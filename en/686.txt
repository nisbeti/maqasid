The stance towards al-Shatibi's methodology is not the same as the stance towards al-Shatibi himself, nor should it be. Rather, the researcher feels, while presenting the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) as understood by him to researchers and those interested in it and in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) in general, that the most significant person who critiques it is al-Shatibi himself. This is due to the affection that has developed within the researcher towards a man who combined genius and faith, knowledge and morality, ambition and piety. His intellect elevated him, and his soul, filled with fear and hope, urged him to rise, aspiring like the devoted and active worshippers. His psyche was exalted, and his intellect, filled with thought and wisdom, urged him to ascend, leading him to the asceticism of the grateful worshippers. The elevated intellect was coupled with the noble psyche in a believing person, which gave rise to a love for Allah, and the sweetness of faith bore fruit, guiding the seeker on his path. When the researcher saw the seeker stumbling and the paths becoming difficult, compassion and empathy were added to love and affection, as he witnessed the esteemed and enlightened scholar being obstructed by the ultimate goal from the path and being blinded to the subtleties of the truths. He wished he could be a student of his, a companion who would guide him with two eyes, sparing him from distractions that would divert him from perception or hinder his understanding. For great and pious individuals are rare and pass by infrequently, and there is no infallibility after the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) for anyone. Therefore, to assist them in obeying Allah and to serve in the service of Allah's commands is indeed an act of righteousness and obedience. To strive to correct them with etiquette, to enlighten them with wisdom, and to obey them and draw near to them sincerely for Allah and out of piety is a great endeavor, as great as their deeds, and beneficial as the fruits of their production, and it is a rarer opportunity than they are. The mistakes and missteps from them are a loss for those with them and for those around them, even if they are rewarded.

(1) The objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) refer to the overarching goals that Islamic law seeks to achieve.
(2) Principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) are the foundational rules and methodologies used to derive legal rulings in Islam.
(3) The term "the devoted and active worshippers" refers to those who engage in sincere worship and good deeds.
(4) The phrase "the esteemed and enlightened scholar" denotes a respected figure in Islamic scholarship who possesses deep understanding and insight.
(5) The reference to "the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)" acknowledges the final messenger of Islam, whose teachings are considered infallible.
