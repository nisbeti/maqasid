Then he clarified his intention with a text from al-Shafi'i, saying: "Al-Shafi'i indicated in his Risala (Message) this meaning, that Allah addressed the Arabs in His Book in their own language according to what they know of its meanings. He then mentioned what is known of its meanings, the vastness of their language, and that they are addressed in the general with the intention of the general, which includes the specific, and he proves this with some of what is included in the speech. And the general is intended to mean the specific and is known by the context, and the speech informs its beginning about its end and its end about its beginning. And that one speaks of something known by its meaning without the wording, just as one knows by indication, and one may call a single thing by many names and many meanings by a single name. He then said: Whoever is ignorant of this from their language, and in their language the Book was revealed and the Sunnah came, then to attempt to speak in its knowledge is to attempt what some of it is unknown to him. And whoever attempts what he does not know, and what his knowledge does not confirm, then his agreement with the truth, if it agrees with him from where he does not know, is not commendable, and he is not excused for his error if he speaks in matters of which he does not have knowledge of the distinction between error and truth in it. This is his statement, and it is the truth from which there is no escape" (1). Understanding the objectives (maqasid) includes understanding the reasons for revelation, as they are a condition for understanding the meanings of the texts, and this was clarified in the first discussion of the previous chapter.
