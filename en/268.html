One of the issues discussed by al-Shatibi is the disagreement among jurists regarding rulings. If one of them says that an action is permissible, and another says it is forbidden, then considering the benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), if the action is harmful, how could it be permissible according to the first, and if it is beneficial, how could the second prohibit it? This is a problem for those who consider benefits and harms.

Al-Shatibi states that there is no problem here; if it is permissible according to the first, this means that its benefit is predominant in his view, and if it is forbidden according to the second, this means that it is harmful in his view. Thus, the consideration of benefits and harms differs among jurists, and there is no issue. He said: "Some later scholars - namely al-Qarafi - claimed that the argument for benefits only holds if the correct opinion in matters of ijtihad (juristic reasoning) is singular, because the rational principle is that the predominant cannot be both the thing and its opposite. Rather, whenever one is predominant, the other must be less so, and this necessitates that the correct opinion is one, which is the opinion of the one who rules by the predominant, while the others must be mistaken as they rule by the less predominant. This contradicts the principle of those who claim correctness with those who say that the laws are based on benefits. This is what he said, and he quoted his teacher Ibn Abd al-Salam in response, stating that these individuals must say that this principle only applies to consensus rulings" (1). He then refuted this claim, saying: "The benefits or harms in matters of disagreement are established according to what is in the reality of the matter for the jurist and in his view, and there is no difference here between the mistaken and the correct. If the Maliki is convinced that the excess interest in fresh fruits and vegetables is permissible, then the aspect of benefit for him is the predominant one, and it is also so in reality as he perceives it" (2). This means that the action is permissible according to Sharia and thus is a benefit for him. "And if the Shafi'i is convinced that interest in this case is not permissible, then it falls under the ruling of forbidden interest for him, and the aspect of benefit for him is the less predominant one, not the predominant one, and it is also so in reality as he perceives it" (3).
