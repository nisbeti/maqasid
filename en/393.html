He said: "The accountable person (mukallaf) is only obligated to perform actions based on the intention of the legislator (shari') behind the command and prohibition. If the intention is something other than that, then it becomes, by the intention of the one intending (qasid), a means to what he intended, not the objectives, since he did not intend the intention of the legislator, so it becomes intended for another purpose, making the action or abstention a means to it. Thus, what is considered by the legislator as intended becomes a means for him, and what has this characteristic nullifies the legislator's decree and undermines what he has established" (1).

He said: "The one who takes what is legislated (mashru') without the intention of the legislator towards that objective is indeed taking something that is not truly legislated, because the legislator only legislated it for a known purpose by obligation. Therefore, if he takes it with the intention of something other than that known purpose, he has not brought forth that legislated matter at all. If he has not brought it forth, he contradicts the legislator in that taking, as he becomes like one who acts contrary to what he was commanded and neglects what he was commanded" (2).

Section Three

Legal Devices (Hiyal)

Definition:

Al-Shatibi said in defining legal devices: "Its essence is presenting an action that appears permissible to invalidate a legal ruling and to transform it in appearance into another ruling" (3). He said: "Like the one who gifts his wealth at the end of the year to evade zakat. For the essence of the gift is permissible, and if zakat were to be prevented without a gift, it would be prohibited" (4).
