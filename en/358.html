... And He said: { And you consume inheritance with [greedy] consumption (19) and you love wealth with [intense] love } Al-Fajr, 19-20.

... And He said: { Indeed, mankind was created anxious (19) when evil touches him, impatient (20) and when good touches him, withholding (21) } Al-Ma'arij, 19-21.

Is it permissible for the accountable person (mukallaf) to initiate actions with the intention of making his non-Sharia objectives Sharia-compliant? This is known as legal tricks (hiyal). Like someone who gives charity from some of his wealth or spends it to evade zakat, or like someone who undertakes a journey to permit himself to break his fast, for example, or like someone who marries a woman who has been divorced three times so that he can divorce her with the intention of making her lawful for her first husband.

For such actions, and for such objectives and what arises or branches from them, al-Shatibi discussed the category of the objectives of the accountable person. This chapter was written to clarify al-Shatibi's position on these issues.

Since intention and purpose and their effects on the actions of the accountable person are matters extensively discussed by the scholars, and al-Shatibi's statements are both consistent and inconsistent with each other, the opinions and views of the scholars on this matter will be addressed before presenting al-Shatibi's views on it.

Section One

Objectives and Intentions

Intention and purpose and the difference between them:

Al-Ghazali said: "Know that intention, will, and purpose are terms that convey the same meaning, which is a state and quality of the heart that is encompassed by two matters: knowledge and action. Knowledge precedes it because it is its origin and condition, and action follows it because it is its fruit and branch" (1). He also said that intention "is the will and the impulse of the soul by virtue of desire and inclination towards what is in accordance with the objective, either in the present or in the future. The primary mover is the desired objective, which is the motivator, and the motivating objective is the intended purpose, and the impulse is the purpose and intention, and the activation of the ability to serve the will by moving the limbs is the action" (2).

Ibn Nujaym al-Hanafi said: "In language, as in the dictionary, it is to intend something with intention, which can be emphasized or softened: to aim for it. In Sharia, as in al-Talwih, it is the intention of obedience and drawing closer to Allah in performing the action" (3).
