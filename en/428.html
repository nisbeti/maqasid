When commands and prohibitions come in a manner that is beyond one's ability, such as His saying, { and do not die except as Muslims } (1), or { say, "Be stones or iron" } (2), or in a way that we know cannot be required in our Sharia, such as His saying, { and whoever wills, let him disbelieve } (3), or { say, "Believe in it or do not believe" } (4), this does not mean the cancellation of obligation or the non-application of texts, or the neglect of forms. Rather, the matter was not an obligation in the first place, and the forms and texts did not imply obligation; rather, they were threats, despair, or impossibility, and this is one of the meanings of the forms themselves. It is a matter of applying both particulars and universals together to determine the intended meaning (5). He said: "As for the matter of impossibility and threat, it is not, in reality, a command, even if it is said to be a command metaphorically; for as previously mentioned, a command, even if metaphorical, necessitates intention, and thus it becomes a command in which the aspect of metaphor can be conceived; otherwise, it is not a command without the intention to bring about what is commanded in any way" (6).

Commands and prohibitions convey their implications, which are the rulings, and the rulings have their objectives, which are the interests (masalih). However, the latter can only occur within the execution of what is requested to be executed and the abstention from what is requested to be abstained from. Indeed, the legal ruling concerning its intended purpose is like the original concerning the branch. It is not correct for the branch to invalidate the original.
