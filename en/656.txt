He said regarding the application of universals and particulars together: "It is impossible for particulars to be independent of their universals. Thus, whoever takes a text, for example, in a particular while disregarding its universal is mistaken; likewise, whoever takes the universal while disregarding its particulars. The explanation of this is that acquiring knowledge of the universal is only through the examination and observation of particulars. The universal, in its essence, is not known to us before knowledge of the particulars, and it does not exist in the external world; rather, it is contained within the particulars, as established in the intelligibles. Therefore, to focus on the universal while disregarding the particular is to stand with something that has not been established in knowledge without knowledge of the particular, and the particular is the manifestation of knowledge of it. Moreover, the particular is only designated as such because the universal is fully present within it and is what sustains it. Thus, disregarding the particular as such is, in reality, a disregard for the universal itself, and that is a contradiction. Furthermore, disregarding the particular altogether leads to doubt regarding the universal, since such disregard occurs only when there is a contradiction with the universal or a presumed contradiction with it. If the universal contradicts the particular, while we only derive it from the particular, this indicates that knowledge of that universal has not been established, as it is possible that the particular contains a part of the universal that the considered part does not include. If this is possible, there is no alternative but to return to the particular in understanding the universal, and this indicates that the universal cannot be considered in isolation without considering the particular" (1). He then said: "If a universal principle is established through induction, and then a text comes regarding a particular that contradicts the principle in one of the ways of contradiction, it is necessary to reconcile the two in consideration" (2). He then said: "In this case, it is not possible for the principles to be violated, and if this is established, the universal cannot be considered while the particular is disregarded" (3).
