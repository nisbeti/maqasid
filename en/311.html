... Al-Shatibi emphasizes that adherence to commands and prohibitions is fundamental in the Sharia, and that servitude to Allah must be present in every action of the accountable person. Any action that does not involve worship of Allah is following one's desires. He stresses that determining benefit (maslaha) and harm (mafsada) is contingent upon the provisions of the legislator, and that following what one perceives as beneficial without considering the ruling of the Sharia regarding that benefit is purely following one's desires, and whatever is thus is entirely invalid.

... He states: "The legal objective of establishing the Sharia is to free the accountable person from the call of their desires so that they may be a servant of Allah by choice, just as they are a servant of Allah by necessity" (1). He then provides evidence that conclusively supports his methodology. He says: "The evidence for this consists of several matters: the first is the explicit text indicating that the servants were created to worship Allah and to submit to His commands and prohibitions, such as His saying: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. (56) I do not want from them any provision, nor do I want them to feed Me. } (2). And His saying: { And command your family to pray and be steadfast therein. We do not ask you for provision; We provide for you. } (3). And His saying: { O mankind, worship your Lord, who created you and those before you, that you may become righteous. } (4)... and other verses commanding worship in general and its details specifically. All of this refers back to returning to Allah in all circumstances and submitting to His rulings at all times, which is the essence of worshiping Allah... The second is what indicates the blameworthiness of opposing this objective, starting with the prohibition against disobeying Allah's command and the condemnation of those who turn away from Allah... The root of this is following one's desires and yielding to the obedience of immediate interests and fleeting passions... The third is what is known through experiences and customs, that religious and worldly benefits cannot be attained by indulging in following desires and aligning with interests" (5). Based on this, there is no action of the servant that does not involve worshiping Allah; thus, it must be accompanied by the intention of compliance, and this can only be with permission from the legislator. This is because what is good and bad, or beneficial and harmful, is known through the Sharia, and if the Sharia has no role in defining this, then what is followed is desire, as the matter is confined between the Sharia and desire, with no third option.
