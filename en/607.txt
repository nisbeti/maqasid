After clarifying the meaning of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi, their categories and types, and how to attain them, as well as how to understand the Sharia and derive rulings based on it, and after establishing al-Shatibi's comprehensive methodology in this regard, which attempts to integrate previous methodologies or principles and foundations of various kinds through the new principles he established for understanding, it remains to examine this idea and these principles and discuss them.
Although he diverged in his innovations from all his predecessors among the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) and presented an unprecedented methodology, he sought to relate the various principles back to his own, even though he sometimes explicitly stated his disagreement with them and insisted that it was a disagreement in substance and not merely in wording.
During this process, al-Shatibi emerged as a profound scholar and jurist, deeply versed in the sciences of the Sharia, knowledgeable about human nature and the conditions of communities, insightful in discerning implications, diving deep into the pearls of meanings, submissive to the evidence and committed to it, eager to understand both the general and specific objectives, warning against extremism just as he warned against laxity.
In light of all this, a question arises that was raised by Dr. Abdullah Darraz in his introduction to his commentary on the book al-Muwafaqat: "If the status of the book is as you mentioned, and its virtue in the Sharia is as you described, then why has it been obscured from view for all these years and not received its due attention, let alone the dedication to its exposition?"(1). The question may go further: why did al-Shatibi's ideas not appear among other scholars, whether his students or others? Why did no one follow him in this regard? Did his ideas die immediately after their birth? Or are his ideas so profound and his methodology so complex that there were no followers for his ideas or practitioners of his methodology?
