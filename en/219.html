This text is prepared by al-Shatibi as a ruling or action of Allah, Exalted is He, justified by public interest (maslaha). Upon careful consideration, we find it indicative of a goal—one of the objectives—of sending the messengers, or of a result intended to be achieved. The action is the sending of the messengers as bearers of good news and warners, and the result is establishing the proof against the people that they have been given glad tidings and warned, so they cannot say on the Day of Resurrection: "No warner came to us," nor can they say: "How can we be held accountable for doing something we were not forbidden from, and for abandoning something we were not commanded to do?" What al-Shatibi intends here is to establish that the actions and rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, have goals or outcomes for which He acted or legislated. Thus, cutting off the proof for the servants on the Day of Resurrection is a goal or result of sending the messengers that the legislator intends to achieve. It is not a cause in the sense of a cause that is a pillar in analogy (qiyas), for the cutting off of the proof for the servants on the Day of Resurrection is not a cause for a legal ruling, and there is no ruling in this text that is subject to analogy. The fact that al-Shatibi refers to the goal or result or outcome or effect as a cause prompts us to clarify the meanings of these terms in his context so that we do not misplace them.

As for the fact that this cause is a public interest, this is a matter of figurative language and not a matter of recognizing that this matter is a public interest and then observing that the Sharia has commanded or established what a person recognizes as a public interest. Yes, it is not from this perspective, and the intended figurative language is that everything Allah commands is called a public interest or truth or justice, and everything He forbids is called corruption or injustice or falsehood. This is what al-Shatibi means, and to understand the matter, let us look at the text above: is there in this goal, which is that people will not possess a proof on the Day of Resurrection by which they can escape accountability and punishment, a public interest or an aspect of public interest? If no bearer of glad tidings or warner had come to them, they would have been among those in a state of ignorance and would have escaped punishment { And We would not punish until We sent a messenger } (1), so where is the public interest here or the appropriate meaning that, if presented to minds, would be accepted? The cutting off of the proof for the servants on the Day of Resurrection does not fall within the public interest of the servants. Based on this, al-Shatibi's use of this text to argue for justification by public interest indicates that what he means by public interest is what has been established as intended by Allah, Exalted is He, regardless of whether it is rationally meaningful or not.

His second evidence: { And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds } (2).
