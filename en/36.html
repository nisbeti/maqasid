1 - The Muwafaqat. It is the most famous of his books and the deepest in content. He titled it: "The Title of Defining the Secrets of Obligation," and then he proceeded to refer to it as "the Muwafaqat." Through it, he attempted to establish a methodology for articulating the principles and deriving the branches. Therefore, it is a book on the principles (usul). If we consider al-Shatibi's (may Allah be pleased with him) intended meaning of the term principle or principles, it is more accurate to say that the book is about the principles of the principles, and this will become clear - if Allah wills - in the explanation of his idea regarding the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), which is the subject of this book.

Much has been said about the Muwafaqat, but only a little of this is correct, and it contains ambiguities that cannot be resolved or understood in terms of the true intended meaning except through extensive review and connecting the texts in various places.

The book is available in several editions and has been edited by several scholars, the most prominent of whom is Dr. Abdullah Daraz. It is published in four volumes. Al-Shatibi (may Allah be pleased with him) divided it into five sections: the first is on the scientific introductions needed as a prelude to the other sections; the second is on rulings and what relates to them, whether from the address of obligation or the address of status; the third is on the objectives; the objectives of the Legislator and the objectives of the obligated; the fourth is on the legal evidences, which are the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas); and the fifth is on the rulings of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), taqlid (following a jurist), and fatwa (legal opinion), and on the conditions of the mufti (the one giving the fatwa) and the mustafti (the one seeking the fatwa).
