One does not issue a fatwa (legal opinion) based on the weakest among them in body, spirit, or pursuit of the Hereafter, nor based on the strongest among them in that regard, but rather adopts the well-known middle path among them. The term "well-known" refers to what is established in the Sharia regarding guiding people towards this middle ground from what is permitted by law. The follower (muqallid) weighs the opinions of the jurists based on what he observes in their general fatwas, not those specific to individual obligations, as he is not among those qualified to deliberate on that. What is permitted is what the legislator intended to allow, and the middle ground in this context is the most precise intention of the legislator. Al-Shatibi said: "The mufti who reaches the pinnacle of the rank is the one who guides people towards the well-known middle path that is suitable for the public; he does not lead them towards extremism nor incline them towards the side of laxity" (1). He also said: "If he deviates from that with the questioners, he has strayed from the intention of the legislator, and thus what deviates from the middle path is condemned among the knowledgeable scholars" (2). He stated: "If the questioner is led towards hardship and difficulty, it will cause him to resent the religion and lead to a cessation from following the path of the Hereafter, which is evident" (3).

As for the mufti whose rank leads him to adopt a position above the middle ground, people may mistakenly think they should emulate him, causing them to take on burdens that are unsuitable for them. Therefore, he should conceal from the people what he imposes on himself that is above the middle ground, or he should alert them to it. He said: "It may be permissible for the jurist to impose upon himself obligations that are above the middle ground" (4). He then said: "Since he is a mufti by his words and actions, he has the right to conceal what may be emulated from him, for perhaps someone who cannot bear that action will follow him, leading to their downfall. If it happens to be known to the people, he should alert them to it, as the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) used to do, for he surpassed the people in worship and character" (5).
