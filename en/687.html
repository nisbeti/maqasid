The affection, esteem, and respect that the researcher holds for al-Shatibi makes him the first to critique his ideas. It also makes those who carry the concerns of al-Shatibi the first to present this research along with what they have concluded from the critique.

What al-Shatibi undertook was an attempt to reform the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) to correct what he sees as widespread errors, to emphasize what he considers neglected principles, to discard what does not belong to it, and to alert to areas where there is presumed disagreement but no actual disagreement. This was aimed at correcting and regulating discussions in usul al-fiqh that have grown, developed, branched out, and become detached from their foundations and controls.

For example, his rejection of the prevailing concept of public interest (maslaha) and harm (mafsada) is notable; although he spoke of reasoning based on bringing about benefits and averting harms, and it initially appeared, as many uncritical observers thought, that he made public interest the foundation of legislation, he actually dismantled this understanding and shifted this reasoning from one path to another and opposing one. This is something he is attested to. It is one of the most important objectives in his book. Therefore, he referred to it at the beginning of the preface of his book, saying: "Indeed, before the rise of this light, we were groping about like the blind, and our minds were running in pursuit of our interests in a disordered manner, due to their weakness in bearing these burdens and participating in the immediate whims, on the battlefield of the self, which is caught between the two extremes of the worst. We would place poisons in the places of remedies, seeking healing like one grasping at water, and we continued to swim between them in the sea of illusion, drifting, and wandering in our ignorance of the evidence in a dark night, and we would deduce the barren analogy, and seek the traces of health from the sick body, and we would walk face down, thinking we were walking on the straight path, until the pure compulsion appeared in the face of fate, and the reality of the hands of necessity was raised to the One, the All-Prevailing" (1).
