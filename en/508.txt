This does not mean that there is no ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) in other areas. Rather, this is the recognized ijtihad, which leads to clarifying Allah's ruling regarding an action. As for ijtihad in other matters, it occurs during the pursuit of knowledge and advancement in it and understanding the objectives (maqasid) of the Sharia. The student of knowledge learns, studies, and strives to understand the objectives and the reasons behind the Sharia, which includes the necessity of acquiring knowledge of language, hadith, understanding the reasons for revelation, and all other necessary sciences. During this process, he strives to attain knowledge of the objectives in their completeness, but before achieving complete knowledge of the objectives, if he engages in ijtihad in the aforementioned areas, his ijtihad is not considered valid. Therefore, we find him stating in previous texts that ijtihad is the exertion of effort in seeking certainty or conjecture regarding the ruling. We also find him denying ijtihad in definitive matters.
The difference among the mujtahids (those who engage in ijtihad):
The mujtahid exerts his effort to know Allah's ruling regarding an action. What he arrives at is a conjectural ruling, for if it were definitive, it would not require ijtihad, nor would there be room for differing opinions and views. Al-Shatibi said: "As for the definitive, there is no room for discussion about it" (1). He also said: "Whoever goes beyond it is certainly mistaken" (2). Hence, it is said: there is no ijtihad alongside the text, meaning one that is definitive in both its authenticity and its indication.
If the ruling is conjectural, then this is a matter in which the mujtahids differ, and their opinions vary. The ruling regarding an action is the intention of the legislator concerning the related discourse, and it is one and not multiple, which is a definitive matter. Thus, it cannot be that all mujtahids, despite their differences, are correct; rather, only one is correct. This is the case for every ruling in the Sharia. The multiplicity of the legislator's intention regarding a single issue is impossible. Allah, Exalted is He, said: { And if it had been from other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction } (3).
