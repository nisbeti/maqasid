One example of this is the sale of uncertainty (gharar), which the Sharia has prohibited. Among transactions, some are clearly uncertain, while others are clearly not, and there are those that are ambiguous as to whether they are uncertain or not. The determination of this is for the qualified jurist (mujtahid). He said: "Among these is the prohibition of selling uncertainty, and we see that the scholars unanimously agree on the prohibition of selling unborn offspring, birds in the air, and fish in the water. And it is permissible to sell a garment that is filled with something concealed from sight, even though if the filling were sold separately, it would be prohibited. It is also permissible to rent a house for a month with the possibility that the month may be thirty or twenty-nine days, and to enter the bathhouse with the differing customs of people regarding the use of water and the duration of stay" (1). He then said: "Every issue in which there is disagreement in the matter of uncertainty is intermediate between the two extremes, taking on a resemblance from each of them" (2).

Another example is the zakat on jewelry, the testimony of an unknown person, and the ownership of a slave, among others. He said: "They all agreed on the absence of zakat on goods and on the presence of zakat on the two currencies, so the permissible jewelry is situated between the two extremes, which is why there is disagreement regarding it. They agreed on accepting the narration and testimony of the just person and on not accepting that from the corrupt person, so the unknown person is situated between them, leading to disagreement about him. They agreed that a free person owns property while an animal does not own property, and when the slave takes on characteristics from both sides, they disagreed on whether he owns property or not, based on the predominance of the ruling of one side" (3). He then said: "You may not find a significant disagreement among rational people regarding intellectual matters or transmitted matters that is not based on conjecture or certainty, except that it revolves between two extremes in which the disputants do not differ" (4).
