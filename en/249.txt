And based on this, it is not permissible to disregard or neglect the particulars (juz'iyyat) under the pretext of the universals (kulliyyat), nor is it correct to object here by stating that the absence of individual particulars does not affect the establishment of the universals. He says: "There is no doubt in the necessity of intention towards the particular" (1) and he says: "The absence of the particular there is only due to the preservation of the particular in its universality from another perspective, just as we say that the preservation of lives is legislated, and this is a universal that is definitively intended by the Legislator, then retribution (qisas) was legislated for the preservation of lives, so killing a soul in retribution is a preservation of it by intention, and it necessitates the absence of a particular from the particulars of the universal being preserved, which is the destruction of this soul due to an incidental occurrence, which is the assault on the soul. Thus, neglecting this particular in its universality from the perspective of preserving a particular in its universality as well, which is the assaulted soul... Therefore, the absence of individual particulars from the requirement of the universal, if it is for no incidental reason, is not valid legally, and if it is for an incidental reason, then it pertains to the preservation of the universal from another perspective or to the preservation of another universal; the first case would render its absence detrimental to the universal, while the second case would not render its absence detrimental" (2).
