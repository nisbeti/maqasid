This has led to details and restrictions in defining the appropriate (munasib) and its conditions among the usuliyyin (scholars of Islamic jurisprudence), resulting in expressions such as recognized public interest (mashlaha mu'tabara), abrogated public interest (mashlaha mulghah), remote public interest (mashlaha mursala), and legislatively accepted public interest (mashlaha murtada shar'an), as well as the objectives of creation (maqasid al-khalq) and the objectives of the Legislator (maqasid al-shari'). There was also a division of the appropriate into real rational and imaginary persuasive. "The first is that which, the more one investigates and examines it, the more its appropriateness is confirmed, while the second is that which is initially imagined to be appropriate, but when subjected to research and scrutiny, its appropriateness falls away and reveals itself to be without substance" (1). Furthermore, it was divided into necessary (daruri), need-based (hajji), and improvement-based (tahsin). Al-Zarkashi said: "The categories of the appropriate in terms of reality and persuasion are divided into real and persuasive. The real is divided into what occurs in the realm of necessity, the realm of need, and the realm of improvement" (2). He then said: "As for the persuasive, it is that which initially appears to be appropriate, but upon thorough investigation, it turns out otherwise" (3).

Clarifying the meaning of ikhalah and munasibah:

It is noted in the context of the texts of the imams of usul that some of them use the terms ikhalah and munasibah interchangeably, while others differentiate between them. Al-Amidi says: "The fifth approach in establishing the cause is ikhalah and munasibah" (4), and al-Shawkani states: "Munasabah: it is expressed as ikhalah, public interest, inference, and consideration of objectives, and its extraction is called the derivation of the basis (takhrij al-manat), which is the essence of the book of qiyas (analogy) and its area of ambiguity and clarity. The meaning of munasibah is the specification of the cause merely by presenting the appropriateness without any defects, neither by text nor otherwise, and munasibah in language means compatibility, and the appropriate (munasib) is that which is compatible" (5).
