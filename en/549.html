Considering this principle does not mean permitting the unlawful (haram) because its outcome is permissible, nor does it mean prohibiting the lawful (halal) because its outcome is forbidden. Rather, it means examining to understand the ruling of the Lawgiver in cases of differing circumstances. Therefore, this is part of the work of the mujtahid (jurist) in his capacity as a mujtahid, who examines the action and its outcome, free from personal desires. Requiring the mujtahid to consider the outcomes is akin to exerting utmost effort and thorough examination. Al-Shatibi said: "The consideration of the outcomes of actions is a legally intended objective, whether the actions are in agreement or in opposition. This is because the mujtahid does not rule on an action performed by the obligated individuals, whether to engage in it or refrain from it, except after considering what that action will lead to. It may be permissible due to a benefit that it brings or a harm that it averts, but it has an outcome contrary to what was intended. It may also be impermissible due to a harm that arises from it or a harm that it prevents, but it has an outcome contrary to that. If one were to declare the first as permissible, it might lead to a benefit that equals or exceeds the harm, thus preventing the declaration of permissibility. Similarly, if one were to declare the second as impermissible, it might lead to averting a harm that equals or exceeds the benefit, so it would not be correct to declare it impermissible. This is a domain for the mujtahid, difficult in its source, yet sweet in taste, praiseworthy in its outcome, in accordance with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)"(1). This is the text of al-Shatibi regarding the principle of the outcomes of actions. It is directed to the mujtahid and not to others. He says: "This is because the mujtahid does not rule" and he says: "This is a domain for the mujtahid, difficult in its source." It is also clear that he does not say that the outcome of the action determines its ruling. Rather, he states that it is necessary to consider the outcome when examining, so one should not declare based solely on the examination of the action, nor should one declare based solely on the examination of the outcome. Rather, it is necessary to consider both aspects, based on the legal principles of necessities, needs, and enhancements. And based on the consideration of both general and specific outcomes.
