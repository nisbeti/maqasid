It must be noted here that the research is not about the word "qasd" or its derivatives, where and when it has been mentioned, as it is an Arabic word commonly used in the speech of Arabs both in the past and present. Rather, the research is about the objectives (maqasid) in a specific sense, which is the meaning or meanings intended by the scholars of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence). The first to speak about the objectives was Imam al-Juwayni - and Allah knows best - and he, along with the scholars who came after him - except for al-Shatibi - refers to a single meaning of the objectives, which is the necessary, need-based, and enhancement objectives of the Sharia. These can be defined by them as the goals or interests for which the Sharia was established.
As for al-Shatibi, the matter encompasses this definition and goes beyond it, as the objectives for him are the objectives of the legislator and the objectives of the obligated person (mukallaf). The objectives of the obligated person can be defined as the goals or interests that the obligated person seeks to achieve, whether they are worldly or otherworldly.
The objectives of the legislator, however, are broader than the objectives of the Sharia, and the objectives of the Sharia are merely objectives that the legislator intended through legislation, but they are not all the objectives of the legislator. Allah, Exalted is He, created things and has an objective in doing so, according to al-Shatibi, and this objective is His purpose in creation, not in legislation, as creation is not legislation. He, Exalted is He, says: { He created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed } (1), and He says: { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me } (2), so the test and worship here are objectives of the legislator and not objectives of the Sharia.
