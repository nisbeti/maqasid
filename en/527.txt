The preference of the follower (muqallid) among the jurists (mujtahidin) occurs when their opinions conflict on a particular issue. It is a preference among jurists and not among opinions or fatwas, and this preference among the jurists must be based on characteristics within them that can be discerned and judged. Therefore, the preference of the follower for a jurist depends on their actions. This relates to piety, scrupulousness, and understanding. There is no doubt that knowledge or being more knowledgeable is one of the means of preference, but this matter may be obscure to the follower, or the opinions may conflict, and two scholars may be equal or close in knowledge. Thus, preference can only be based on actions, which indicate the scrupulousness and understanding of the jurist regarding the importance of their actions in front of the public or those who follow them. They realize that they must stand as a jurist through their words, actions, and approvals, not just through their words. This is because "the mufti stands in the community in the place of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)"(1), for he has inherited knowledge from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and conveys it. For the followers, he is like the legislator through his ijtihad(2). Therefore, it is said: "The fatwa from the mufti is derived from the aspects of speech, action, and approval"(3). Accordingly, the preference of the follower depends on characteristics in the jurist that are:
1. Their actions do not contradict the legal rulings, which is a requirement of justice.
2. Their actions do not contradict their statements, even if the contradiction is within what is permissible, which is a requirement for standing as a mufti.
3. They guide people with their fatwa towards the middle ground, which is the norm of the Sharia and the intent of the legislator.
4. They fear the consequences of issuing a fatwa, which is a requirement of piety, scrupulousness, and fear of contradicting the intent of the legislator.
