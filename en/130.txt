As for the texts and their meanings not being sufficient for the issues and occurrences, since the texts are finite and the occurrences are infinite, the former cannot draw from the latter like a drop from the ocean. Sheikh Muhibullah ibn Abd al-Shakur said: "This is what is called public interest (maslaha mursala), which is considered a proof by Malik, while the preferred view among the majority is to reject it. Our argument is: there is no proof without consideration, even if it follows the path of reason. They said:
First: If consideration were not taken, many occurrences would be left unaddressed. We say: we deny the necessity of this conclusion because the generalities and analogies are comprehensive. Furthermore, the absence of a specific proof serves as a basis for permissibility (1).
Second: The Companions were content with considering public interests. We say: rather, they only considered what they were aware of regarding the type or nature of it (2).
The commentator on al-Tahrir, Ibn Humam al-Iskandari, a Hanafi, said: "This category known as public interest (maslaha mursala) is preferred to be rejected, as there is no proof for consideration, thus it must be rejected. They said: many occurrences would be left unaddressed. We say: we deny the necessity of this conclusion, meaning we do not concede that the absence of consideration leads to occurrences being devoid of rulings, as the generalities and analogies are comprehensive. Even if it were absent, the negation of every specific proof entails that its ruling is original permissibility, meaning if in a particular incident there is no source from the four evidences, one acts according to a general principle established in the Sharia by consensus, which is original permissibility, as it is the default for things (3).
