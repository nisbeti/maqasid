Based on this, the method of induction (istiqra) is not appropriate here, in addition to the fact that the legal texts are not material phenomena that can be induced; the legal texts have implications that must be acted upon, whether they are speculative or definitive. This method contradicts what the recognized imams have established, thus it is erroneous and cannot be acted upon.

Moreover, this induction is incomplete and cannot be thorough; it does not allow for certainty at all according to the reality of induction. It is not an induction of most particulars, but rather, as al-Shatibi says, it is an induction of many particulars, and this many is not the majority. Therefore, it is not correct according to the method of induction to establish the universal through this incomplete induction and to give a ruling based on what has been induced for what has not been induced. Yes, the many texts that converge on a single meaning may emphasize the importance of this meaning or increase the degree of conjecture about it, and certainty may be attained regarding it. This is not induction but rather deduction (istinbat); meanings are deduced from the texts and may imply others. If there is a shared meaning in many texts, the abundance of texts leads to a presumption that it is the intended meaning, thus the meaning is established, but this is not induction. If induction were required, the meaning would not be established. It is not necessarily the case that this meaning is universal; this depends on the meaning itself. If there are many texts indicating the prohibition of adultery or the obligation of prayer, these rulings do not become universal because they are particular in their subject, even if they are definitive. If a single text indicates a general or universal principle, it should not be dismissed on the grounds that the indicator is a single or speculative text, such as his saying - peace and blessings be upon him -: "There should be neither harm nor reciprocating harm" (1). It is a report of singular transmission (khabar ahad) and it is also a universal principle; it does not need to be definitive to be so, nor does it require what al-Shatibi has gathered of evidence on the meaning, even though the abundance of evidence may affirm the meaning and potentially make it definitive.
