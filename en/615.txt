This meaning is spread throughout various places in his book, including what was mentioned above, and one of the most important is the ninth issue of the first type of the objectives of the legislator (maqasid al-shari). He said: "The legislator's intention to preserve the three essential, necessary, and embellishing principles must be based on evidence, and the evidence for that can either be definitive or speculative. The speculative nature of it is invalid, as it is a fundamental principle of the Sharia, and indeed it is the foundation of its foundations. The foundations of the Sharia are definitive, as clarified in its place, so the foundations of its foundations are more deserving of being definitive. If it were permissible to establish them based on speculation, the Sharia would be speculative in both its roots and branches, and this is invalid."(1)
