It is a description that is appropriate, the consideration or rejection of which is unknown. It has been referred to as public interest (maslaha) that is not explicitly stated, as well as implicit reasoning and public interest (istislah). Some scholars of usul (usul al-fiqh) have included it among the discussions of reasoning. Al-Zarkashi said: "The discussion on analogy has already been addressed, regarding the appropriate matters that the Lawgiver has either considered or rejected. The discussion here pertains to what is unknown, meaning what the Lawgiver has remained silent about in terms of consideration and disregard, which is referred to as public interests that are not explicitly stated and is called implicit reasoning. For this reason, it is termed 'not explicitly stated,' meaning it has neither been considered nor rejected. Imam al-Haramayn and Ibn al-Sam'ani referred to it as reasoning, while al-Khwarizmi expressed it in al-Kafi as public interest (istislah). He stated that the meaning of public interest is the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia by preventing harm to the creation. Imam al-Ghazali explained it as finding a meaning that suggests a ruling suitable for it rationally, while there is no agreed-upon basis, and the reasoning is applicable here. Ibn Burhan explained it in al-Awsat as not being based on a general or specific principle, and there are differing opinions on it" (1). The meaning of the implicit reasoning has differed among scholars of usul, and opinions have conflicted regarding its acceptance or rejection. The majority hold that it is not suitable for reasoning. Dr. Mustafa al-Bagha stated: "The words of the scholars of usul almost converge on the fact that the claim of public interest is a matter of disagreement, and the prevailing opinion is that it is not suitable for reasoning since there is no evidence for its consideration, and only Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) has adopted this view" (2).
