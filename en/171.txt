The establishment of the preservation of religion, for example, as a necessary principle, requires—according to him—the definitive proof of the five pillars such as prayer and almsgiving, among others, through induction. He says: "From this path, the obligation of the five pillars such as prayer and almsgiving has been definitively established. Otherwise, if someone were to argue for the obligation of prayer based on His saying, { Establish the prayer } (1) or something similar, there would be several points of contention in the argument itself. However, it is supported by external evidence and the rulings that follow, which have made the obligation of prayer a necessity in religion, and only a skeptic regarding the fundamentals of religion would doubt it" (2). He said: "So when we look at prayer, it comes with { Establish the prayer }. In various aspects, there is praise for those who establish it and condemnation for those who abandon it, and there is compulsion for the obligated to perform it, whether standing, sitting, or lying on their sides, and fighting against those who abandon it or resist abandoning it, among other things of this nature. Likewise, the soul is prohibited from being killed, and killing it makes one liable for retribution, and it is threatened as one of the major sins associated with polytheism, just as prayer is associated with faith. The necessity to provide for the needy is established, as is the obligation of almsgiving and supporting those who cannot rectify themselves, and judges and rulers have been appointed for this purpose, and armies have been organized to fight against those who attempt to kill the soul. The one who fears death is obligated to sustain himself by any lawful means, even if it involves consuming carrion, blood, or pork, among other things that relate to this meaning. Thus, we have certainty regarding the obligation of prayer and the prohibition of killing, and similarly for all other evidence in the rules of Sharia. This is how the principles differ from the branches, as the branches are based on individual pieces of evidence and specific sources, remaining reliant on conjecture, unlike the principles, which are derived from the comprehensive induction of the implications of the evidence, not from individual pieces in particular" (3).
