Returning to what was previously mentioned, that the legal evidence (dalil shar'i) is only accepted if it is definitive (qat'i) or refers to something definitive, it becomes evident to conclude that the prophetic hadith, even if its chain of transmission (sanad) is authentic, cannot be applied unless its meaning refers to a definitive universal meaning (kulli qat'i). Since all universals are found in the Qur'an, as demonstrated in the previous section, the hadith cannot be applied unless it has a basis in the Qur'an. Furthermore, one of the implications of this methodology is that the Sunnah does not contain additional legislation unless the addition is in the sense of detailing a general statement (mujmal) or interpreting an ambiguous one (mutashabih). Since the Qur'an is a clarification of everything { this is a clarification for the people } (1), { and We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for everything } (2), what the Sunnah brings forth in terms of legislation is an additional clarification and explanation, and not something without a basis in the Qur'an. If something is presented in it that is thought to have no basis in the Qur'an, al-Shatibi refers it back to a Qur'anic origin; otherwise, he refrains from accepting it.

This matter, according to al-Shatibi, contradicts what the imams and jurists (mujtahidun) hold, as it is customary that a hadith necessitates action even if its authenticity is presumed (dhanni), and it is not suspended or rejected unless it contradicts something that is stronger in authenticity to the extent that the contradiction cannot be resolved by specification (takhssis) or restriction (taqyeed). Thus, al-Shatibi differs from the established methodology in that he does not suffice with the condition that the application of a text of uncertain authenticity should not contradict or oppose something stronger in authenticity; rather, he requires, in addition to that, the existence of a definitive meaning from the Qur'an upon which the uncertain (dhanni) can rely.
