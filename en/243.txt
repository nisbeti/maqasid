He said: "The concept derived from the position of the Legislator is that obedience or disobedience is magnified according to the magnitude of the benefit (maslaha) or harm (mafsada) resulting from it. It is known from the Sharia that the five essential needs are recognized in every community, and the greatest harms are those that occur from neglecting them. The evidence for this is found in the threats associated with neglecting them, such as disbelief, murder, and what pertains to it, adultery, theft, and drinking alcohol, along with other matters for which a punishment or threat has been established. In contrast, matters related to needs (hajiyat) or enhancements (tahsinayat) do not have a specific threat or a known punishment associated with them. If that is the case, it pertains to a necessary matter, and induction (istiqra) clarifies this" (1). Thus, the necessities are those that the Sharia has specified with a threat or a known punishment to protect them from non-existence. He said: "Each of these ranks, being different in the degree of consideration, has the necessities as the most emphasized, followed by the needs and then the enhancements" (2).
