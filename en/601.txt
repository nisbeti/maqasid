The third criterion for applying the principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) according to al-Shatibi is that the action permitted by Sharia must lead to harm frequently, not rarely. If its leading to harm is rare, then this does not render it prohibited; that is, the jurist does not issue a fatwa declaring it unlawful. Even if this harm is intended for a specific accountable person, this is something the jurist cannot ascertain and thus cannot rule upon. The condition that the leading to harm must be frequent, not rare, is based on the principle that rulings are made based on the majority, not the rare occurrences, just as the intention is considered based on what is commonly prevalent in society.
If the action leads to the unlawful frequently, it may be predominant, or it may be frequent but not predominant or rare; this is treated as predominant in his view and is prohibited under the principle of blocking the means if the first two criteria are met (1).
This condition has appeared in several places in al-Shatibi's book, most clearly in his elaboration on the principle of removing harm. Below is an explanation of how al-Shatibi applies the principle of removing harm and the overlap between it and the principle of blocking the means.
