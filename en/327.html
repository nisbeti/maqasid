Therefore, viewing necessities as having no share for the accountable person (mukallaf) is to prevent the share that may result from it from becoming the legal objective, as this would waste many other shares. Taking these shares or benefits as objectives under the pretext that the Sharia intended to fulfill them has repercussions on the network of relationships and public interests in society, in addition to what it may lead to in terms of neglecting commands and prohibitions.

... An example of this is the ruling on the killing of an apostate (murtad). The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever changes his religion, kill him" (1), and the companions unanimously agreed on this ruling and acted upon it. It is one of the necessities in the preservation of the religion. Researchers may mention a ruling regarding it, such as their saying that apostasy weakens the strength of Islam, confuses the weak among the Muslims, and affects others as it prevents or delays their entry into it, among other things, and killing the apostate prevents all of that. Therefore, this ruling has benefits for society and individuals. In our time, there are those who say that the apostate should not be killed, claiming that the dangers have been removed, and that Islam and Muslims today are not affected by such an act; the difference is vast between the reality of Muslims in the city and their reality today. By applying the meaning of the original objectives (maqasid) according to al-Shatibi, the ruling on the apostate must be viewed as a necessary principle stripped of share, and it should not be lifted even if circumstances change. The mentioned rulings, which may be cited, are not all the rulings, and moreover, not applying the text nullifies the act of worship, even if some of these rulings are established with certainty and thus become benefits, they do not annul the text, because adherence to it is also a principle as it returns to a definitive meaning, which is the preservation of the religion. On the other hand, any imagined wisdom that leads to abandoning the meaning of the text is rejected, because the meanings of rulings are only established through induction, and the induction here serves to contradict the principles, thus it is certainly rejected, following the methodology of al-Shatibi himself.
