Al-Juwayni summarized the many proofs by saying: "The usuliyyin (scholars of Islamic jurisprudence) have elaborated extensively on the ways to respond to the deniers, and our preferred approach consists of two paths: the first is based on a widely accepted matter that only a denier would dispute and only a stubborn person would reject. This is that we know with certainty from our minds that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) used to send messengers and entrust them with conveying the rulings and details of the lawful and unlawful. He would sometimes accompany them with written texts, and their transmission of the commands of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was on the basis of individual reports (ahad), and they were not necessarily infallible. Thus, their reports were subject to doubt, and this was established as a certainty, widely accepted without any refutation, as it is supported by the concept of tawatur (transmission of reports by a large number of people). Only something that is astonishing can refute the widely accepted. This is one of the paths. The second path is based on the consensus of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), and their consensus on acting upon the reports of individuals is transmitted widely. We do not doubt that in matters of events, they sought rulings from the Book of Allah, and if they did not find a mention for the matter at hand, they would turn to seek the reports of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), and they would hasten to rely on the transmission of the trustworthy and reliable without disagreement."(1) Al-Zarkashi also mentioned a similar statement.(2)

The independence of the Sunnah in legislation:

Al-Shatibi argued that the Sunnah (tradition) does not independently legislate based on the premise that the Qur'an contains clarification of everything, and that the Sunnah serves as an explanation of it. Accordingly, the Sunnah does not introduce anything that lacks a basis in the Qur'an, neither in terms of subject matter, meaning, or type.

The commentary on this statement is that the fact that the Qur'an contains the explanation of everything, and that the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet) explains the Qur'an, does not mean that the Sunnah cannot independently legislate, nor that the Sunnah cannot introduce obligations that have no basis in the Qur'an. The indication of the Qur'an regarding the Sunnah and the obligation to derive rulings from it is part of the explanation and completes the explanation. The indication of the Sunnah regarding matters not found in the Qur'an does not contradict the fact that the Qur'an is an explanation.
