It has been established previously that the appropriate analogy (al-mursala) in the method of analogy is not an absolute analogy; rather, it refers to what is considered valid. So why is there a distinction between them, and why is the reasoning not counted as a form of analogy (qiyas)? In other words, how can it be said that the valid appropriate meaning in reasoning, which links the ruling to it, does not have an original source (asl), while it is said that this meaning is applicable in many rulings? Can one of these rulings not be considered an original source, and the description or appropriate meaning within it be regarded as a cause, thus making the matter a case of analogy?
The answer is: Yes, reasoning is based on the justification by an appropriate description, but it differs from the method of analogy in that this meaning is not derived from a specific ruling. If we were to relate it to a specific ruling, it would be analogy. However, when claiming an appropriate description in a specific ruling, it does not hold, as there may be other descriptions in the ruling that are more appropriate than it. Thus, it is not correct to justify the ruling by this meaning while neglecting what is more appropriate. This applies to other rulings as well. Therefore, it is said: there is no specific original source that supports it. Meanwhile, the valid meaning in reasoning, even if its existence or the strength of its appropriateness is weaker than others in each individual ruling, the abundance of its presence in the rulings leads to a stronger consideration of it, and it may make it an original source or a principle in legislation, rather than merely a cause in the ruling. When examining numerous rulings, it is noted that it is not specifically present in any one of them, but the observer feels that it is an original source or is realized in these meanings through their convergence. For this reason, reasoning does not revert to analogy or to the method of analogy, even though it is based on justification, because it lacks one of the pillars of analogy, which is the original source.
