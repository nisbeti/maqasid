He said: "Similarly, the same applies to legal tricks (hiyal) according to those who permit them unconditionally, for they base their allowance on the belief that the legislator (al-shari) has an intention in bringing about benefits (masalih) and preventing harms (mafasid). Indeed, the Sharia was established for this purpose. For example, if the marriage of the one who makes permissible (muhallil) is validated, it is validated on the assumption that it is likely that the intention of the legislator is to permit the attainment of the benefit of the two spouses in it, and likewise for other issues, as evidenced by the validity of pronouncing the word of disbelief (kufr) out of fear of killing or torture and in other general and specific benefits. It is not possible to establish a proof in the Sharia that invalidates every legal trick, just as there is no proof that validates every legal trick. Rather, only those that oppose the intention of the legislator are invalidated, and this is agreed upon by all the people of Islam, while there is disagreement in the issues where the evidences conflict."(1)

The ruling on what is based on legal tricks:

If what we have explained is the ruling on legal tricks, what is the ruling on what results from legal tricks in this world? Al-Shatibi mentions that the practice of legal tricks is of two types: either it is considered existent and results follow from it, or it is considered non-existent. If its existence is acknowledged, there is a disagreement on the matter. However, if it is deemed non-existent, nothing that the obligated person intends will result from it. He says: "The ruling that the cause necessitated remains as it was before this action, and the action is null and void, of no benefit and has no ruling, such as when one gifts property before a year to someone who has persuaded him to return it to him after the year as a gift or otherwise. And like the one who gathers the separated (things) until the messenger comes, then it is returned to separation, or the one who separates the gathered (things) likewise, then it is returned to what it was."(2) Thus, the action that is a legal trick in this case is considered as non-existent or not realized.
