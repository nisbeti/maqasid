However, this presentation of the Sharia (Islamic law) serves to indicate what the application of the Sharia leads to for the individual and society, and it indicates that bringing about benefits and averting harms are results, or among the results, of applying the Sharia. Therefore, the objection and response to those who adopt the path of suitability and reasoning, and this methodology in considering the description or the beneficial meaning, remains the same; rather, this argument may provide them with further evidence for their rebuttal. It is necessary, according to the Hanafi (school of thought), to establish that these results or benefits are intended by the legislator, and this can only be proven by a legal evidence. Furthermore, the objective of the legislator in the ruling is a result of its application; so is the result of the ruling or its cause a reason for it? Thus, the matter has remained a subject of significant contention and debate. Ibn Qudamah al-Maqdisi said: "The correct view is that this is not a proof because it is not known from the legislator that he preserves lives by every means; therefore, mutilation was not legislated even though it is the most effective deterrent and warning, nor was killing legislated for theft or drinking alcohol. If a ruling is established for a benefit among these benefits, it is not known that the Sharia preserves that benefit by establishing that ruling, as it would be placing the Sharia based on opinion and ruling by pure reason." (1)

For example, cutting off the hand of the thief leads to the reduction or elimination of theft, and it leads to the preservation of wealth, which is one of the essential objectives. However, this objective, which is the preservation of wealth, does not serve as a reason for cutting off the hand of the thief, because the usurper, the embezzler, and the fraudster are not punished by the Sharia, even though this objective exists in the prohibition of embezzlement, usurpation, and fraud.
