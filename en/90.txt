The observer of these statements will understand the meaning of "ikhalah" and "munasaba." "Ikhalah" means the claim of a descriptive attribute as a cause. However, this - as he said - is a claim that must be proven, and the way to prove it is through the emergence of the appropriateness of the description and the removal of invalidating factors. His statement: "The ikhalat are divided" means that the attributes that can be claimed are numerous and varied; some are valid while others are nullified, so this alone is not sufficient. Even if it is said that this description contains a public interest (maslaha), this is also not enough. His statement: "The avenues of istislah (public interest) are negated" and his statement: "Not every istislah is an accepted avenue" mean that merely having the description be a public interest or containing a public interest is not sufficient to establish its validity for reasoning. From here, his meaning of "munasaba" is understood, which is that the described attribute - being a public interest - must have a public interest that is accepted by the Sharia. Or that the establishment of the ruling based on that description necessitates the realization of that public interest logically. As for the first, which is that the public interest is accepted by the Sharia, this is what he means by the term "removal of invalidating factors." Therefore, the meaning of "munasaba" can only be the necessity or establishment of the realization of the public interest from the establishment of the ruling based on the description. This establishment occurs through a preponderance of evidence and does not require certainty. This meaning is confirmed by his statement: "If the ikhalah appears and the meaning is free from invalidating factors and there is a preponderance of evidence, then this is among what the early scholars have certainly related."(1)
Al-Razi said: "People have mentioned two things in defining appropriateness: the first is that it leads to what is in accordance with human benefit in terms of acquisition and preservation, and it may be expressed as acquisition by bringing benefit and preservation by repelling harm... The second is that it is compatible with the actions of rational individuals in customs. It is said: this pearl is suitable for this pearl, meaning their combination in a compatible string, and this robe is suitable for this turban, meaning their combination is compatible."(2) Then he said: "The first definition is the statement of one who attributes the rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, to the rulings and public interests, and the second definition is the statement of one who rejects it."(3)
