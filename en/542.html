However, these permitted or prohibited actions may have situations or circumstances, whether related to the individual obligated (mukallaf) or the circumstances of reality, that do not lead to their intended outcomes in Sharia, or they may lead to outcomes but with results that the legislator did not intend. Here, it is noted that the ruling on the action was for a specific outcome, but the action has resulted in one or more outcomes that may contradict what the legislator intended through legislation. It is also noted that the original principle in legislation is that rulings were established for the sake of their benefits or objectives (maqasid). Thus, the rulings and their outcomes are linked as cause and effect. If this is a fundamental principle in legislation, then when the legislator commanded or prohibited, he did so intending the outcome. Therefore, one who studies the Sharia to understand it must do so on this very basis, because the ruling on the action that the legislator clarified is intended to be adhered to, and likewise, the outcome of the action is intended by the legislator. The action and its outcome are connected as cause and effect, and the legislator intends the cause and its effect. If a disconnection occurs between the action and its outcome in certain situations, or if a connection occurs between the action and an unintended outcome in certain situations, then the jurist (mujtahid) must consider this matter and take it into account when deliberating and exercising ijtihad, because it is significant in the eyes of the legislator, and when the jurist clarifies the rulings for the followers (muqallidin), he is as if he is the legislator.

__________

(1) mukallaf: an individual who is legally responsible for their actions in Islamic law.

(2) maqasid: the objectives or purposes of Sharia law.

(3) mujtahid: a jurist who exercises independent reasoning in Islamic law.

(4) muqallidin: those who follow the rulings of a jurist without exercising independent reasoning.
