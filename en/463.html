He said: "In every issue where knowledge is sought in the most complete manner, one must turn to its origin in the Qur'an. If there is a text explicitly addressing it, its type, or its genus, then that is sufficient; otherwise, the levels of consideration in it are multiple" (1). He then said: "It has been established in the first section of the Book of Evidence that every legal proof is either definitive or refers back to something definitive, and the highest reference for the definitive is the Noble Qur'an, which is the first point of reference. However, if the matter only pertains to action specifically, it suffices to refer to the Sunnah transmitted by individual narrators, just as it suffices to refer to the opinion of the jurist, which is weaker. The reference to its origin in the Book is necessary due to its need for that in establishing it as a foundational source to be referred to, or as a religion through which Allah is worshipped; thus, it is not sufficient to receive it from the reports of individuals as previously mentioned" (2).

Based on the above, the interpretation that poses a challenge to al-Shatibi's methodology is only apparent, and al-Shatibi rejects it. He exerted himself to clarify the possibility of considering it because it comes from someone whose statement is regarded as valid, and this is the source of the issue itself. Therefore, it should be noted that he rejected the interpretation of Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) regarding the opening letters of the surahs, as well as the interpretation of "partners" in the verse: { So do not make with Allah any equals } (3). Thus, he said regarding the opening letters of the surahs: "It has been reported that these letters are secrets whose interpretation only Allah knows, and this is the most evident opinion; they are among the ambiguous matters" (4). He also rejected the interpretation of "partners" by saying: "However, there is a valid perspective on this, which is that he did not say that this is the interpretation of the verse" (5). As for the difficulty of these statements and the effort to find a valid perspective for them, it is due to the prominence of those from whom they were reported. He said: "All of this was needed due to the prominence of those from whom this was transmitted among the virtuous" (6).
