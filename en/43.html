Books have been authored on the methodology of al-Shafi'i in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) that were and still are the foundation and support of this science. The most significant works known from the ancients are three books: the "Al-Mu'tamad" by Abu al-Husayn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Tayyib al-Basri al-Mu'tazili, who passed away in 436 AH. The second is the book "Al-Burhan" by Abdul Malik ibn Abdullah al-Juwayni, who passed away in 478 AH. The third is the book "Al-Mustasfa" by Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, who passed away in 505 AH. Then came Abu al-Husayn Ali, known as al-Amidi, who passed away in 631 AH. He compiled these books and added to them in his book "Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam." A similar endeavor was undertaken by Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, who passed away in 606 AH. He compiled these books and added to them in his book "Al-Mahsool." After these scholars came al-Zarkashi, who passed away in 784 AH. He gathered the aforementioned books and others from the writings of the ancients, amounting to over two hundred (1), and compiled them in his book "Al-Bahr al-Muhit fi Usul al-Fiqh."

Among the most significant books of usul for the Hanafis is "Usul al-Bazdawi" by Fakhr al-Islam Ali ibn Muhammad al-Bazdawi, who passed away in 482 AH, and "Usul al-Sarakhsi" by Imam Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Sahl al-Sarakhsi, who passed away in 490 AH.

With the development of the science of usul from al-Shafi'i to these scholars, two matters are observed:

First: The growth and diversification of this science, and the emergence of branches, details, restrictions, and exceptions to the principles and rules.

Second: The influence of theology (kalam) and logic on these branches, and the emergence of logical reasoning in establishing arguments, examples, and proofs. Thus, a theoretical approach emerged that seeks to establish the rules and to detail and explain the principles of the school in order to support it, even if this did not have an effect on independent reasoning (ijtihad) and deduction (istinbat) (2).
