"A land where my amulets were tied to me, and the first land that touched my skin was its dust."(1)
The term "manat" has been used in the discussions of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) in the same linguistic sense. Sheikh Jamal al-Din al-Asnawi(2) said: "The manat is the name of the place of tying, and tying means hanging and sticking. Habib al-Ta'i said: A land where my amulets were tied to me, and the first land that touched my skin was its dust, meaning that the charms were tied to me there. When the ruling was linked to the cause and tied to it, it was called a manat."(3)
The cause, which is the pillar of qiyas (analogy), is not the manat, nor is the manat the cause, but when the conditioned ruling was linked to its cause and tied to it, it was called a manat, and this is a figurative naming. Al-Zarkashi said: "The manat is the cause. Ibn Daqiq al-Eid said: Their expression of the manat for the cause is a matter of linguistic metaphor, because when the ruling was tied to it, it was like a tangible thing that is attached to another; thus, it is a metaphor in the sense of likening the abstract to the tangible, and this has become in the terminology of the jurists such that nothing else is understood by it when mentioned alone."(4)

(1) The same location. The mentioned verse has been attributed to Ruqa' bin Qays al-Asadi, while Sheikh Jamal al-Din al-Asnawi attributed it to Habib al-Ta'i as indicated below.
(2) He is: Jamal al-Din Abu Muhammad Abdul Rahim bin al-Hasan bin Ali bin Omar bin Ali bin Ibrahim al-Qurashi al-Umari al-Shafi'i al-Asnawi. A Sheikh of Islam and a teacher of the eminent imams, head of the Shafi'is in the Egyptian lands. A jurist, usuli, prosodist, and grammarian. He was born in Asna, a town in Upper Egypt, in the year 704 AH and died in Cairo in the year 772 AH. He was prayed upon three times due to the large number of mourners. See: his biography at the beginning of the first volume of Nihayat al-Sul in the explanation of Minhaj al-Uusul, with the marginal notes of the explanation of Nihayat al-Sul by Sheikh Muhammad Bakhit al-Mut'i, Al-Alam al-Kutub.
(3) Al-Asnawi: Nihayat al-Sul 4/138.
(4) Al-Zarkashi, al-Bahr al-Muhit 4/227.
