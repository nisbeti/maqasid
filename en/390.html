Al-Shatibi (may Allah have mercy on him) holds that it is necessary to apply both sides; what can be corrected should be corrected, such as forgetfulness (sahw) in prayer. What can be avoided should be avoided, as in the case of the missing person (mufqood) if his wife marries another man and he returns. The error can be rectified by prioritizing the first husband, or the first husband is prioritized if he returns before her marriage. What cannot be rectified is disregarded. Al-Shatibi's statement on this is: "And disregard what must be disregarded that cannot be rectified" (1). An example of this is eating forgetfully during Ramadan, and in the case of the missing person, if his wife marries and he returns after her marriage, she belongs to the second husband (2).

Among Al-Shatibi's statements regarding the application of both sides is his saying: "So apply the implications of agreement in lifting the punishment and penalty, and apply the implications of disagreement in not relying on that action and not building upon it, until what must be corrected is corrected, leaning towards the intention as well, and disregard what must be disregarded that cannot be rectified" (3). He said: "Count those who err in actions and statements as ignorant of the ruling of the forgetful" (4). He stated: "This is evident in purification (taharah), prayer (salah), fasting (sawm), pilgrimage (hajj), and other acts of worship, as well as in many customary matters such as marriage (nikah), divorce (talaq), foods, and drinks, and it is supported by evidence indicating the lifting of accountability for error and forgetfulness.

As for errors in financial matters, the aspect of intention is considered regarding the Hereafter, while the aspect of action is considered concerning the rights of others in this world. Therefore, it is liable for compensation, and ignorance in this regard is treated like knowledge or intent. He said: "In financial matters, it is liable for compensation in ignorance and intent because we say: the ruling on liability for property is different, as the error in it is equivalent to intent in the resulting liability for its destruction" (5).
