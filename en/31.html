1 ـ Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn al-Fakhar al-Biri, who passed away in 754 AH. His student Ibn al-Khatib says about him: "The imam unanimously acknowledged for his leadership in the Arabic language, opened upon by Allah, in terms of memorization, knowledge, engagement, transmission, and guidance, with no hope for anyone else to match him. He was one of the best reciters in al-Andalus in terms of recitation and performance. Al-Shatibi adorned this teacher of his with (Our great master, the significant scholar) (1)" (2).

2 ـ Abu Ja'far Ahmad al-Shuquri, the jurist, grammarian, and expert in inheritance who taught in Granada the book of Sibawayh, the laws of Ibn Abi al-Rabi', the summary of Ibn al-Banna, the Alfiyyah of Ibn Malik, the rules of instruction, and the al-Mudawwana al-Kubra (3).

3 ـ Abu Sa'id Faraj ibn Qasim ibn Ahmad ibn Lub al-Taghlibi, who passed away in 782 AH. He was the mufti of Granada and the preacher of its mosque, and a teacher at its Nasiriyah school (4). Al-Shatibi presented to him the abridgment of Abu Amr ibn al-Hajib in a single session and permitted him to narrate it from him (5). Some of his disputes with al-Shatibi have been previously mentioned.

4 ـ Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Ali al-Balansi al-Awsi, who passed away in 782 AH. He is the author of a tafsir (exegesis) and a book on the ambiguous verses of the Qur'an (6).

5 ـ Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Abi al-Hajjaj Yusuf ibn Abdullah ibn Muhammad al-Yahsubi, known as al-Lushi, and al-Shatibi granted him a general authorization with a condition (7).
