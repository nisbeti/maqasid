Among the invalid fatwas that al-Shatibi clarifies and warns against are those fatwas that deviate from the preferred opinion under the pretext of ease and alleviating hardship, which are based on legal principles. This leads to a question: should one adopt the lighter of the two opinions or the heavier? Thus, the choice is not based on what is preferred in accordance with the intent of the Lawgiver, but rather on the lighter or heavier opinion. Those who advocate for the lighter opinion cite the evidence of ease, alleviation of hardship, removal of harm, and the leniency of the Sharia (1). In response to this, he states: "The answer to this has been previously mentioned, and it also leads to the necessity of completely lifting the obligation" (2). He further states: "If hardship arises in the obligation, it necessitates alleviation based on these proofs, then this should apply to purifications, prayers, zakat, pilgrimage, jihad, and others, and it does not stop at a limit except when there remains no obligation on the servant, and this is impossible; thus, what it leads to is similar" (3).

Al-Shatibi summarized some of the harms that arise from leniency in fatwa and the selection among opinions and the pursuit of concessions, mentioning among them:
1 - "Detachment from religion by abandoning adherence to evidence in favor of following dissent."
2 - "Disregard for religion, as it becomes, in this regard, fluid and unregulated."
3 - "Abandoning what is known for what is not known."
4 - "Disruption of the law of public interest (maslaha) by abandoning adherence to what is known."
5 - "Leading to the claim of mixing the schools of thought in a manner that violates their consensus. And other harms that can be enumerated" (4).

Section Three
The Origin of the Consequences of Actions

Its Importance in Al-Shatibi's Methodology:
Al-Shatibi made consideration of the consequences of actions one of the signs of a credible mujtahid (jurist), and he made it obligatory for the mujtahid when considering and deriving rulings, as it is one of the objectives of the Lawgiver. He said: "Considering the consequences of actions is a legally recognized objective" (5).
