And regarding improvements, he says: "They are applicable in the same contexts as the two prior categories" (1), meaning the necessities and needs. "In acts of worship, such as the removal of impurities, and in general all forms of purification, covering the private parts, adorning oneself, and drawing closer through voluntary good deeds like charity and acts of devotion, and similar matters; in customary practices, such as the etiquette of eating and drinking, avoiding impure foods and drinks, and avoiding extravagance and stinginess in consumption; in transactions, such as the prohibition of selling impurities, the preference for water and pasture, depriving a slave of the position of testimony and leadership, and depriving a woman of the position of leadership and marrying herself... and in criminal matters, such as the prohibition of killing a free person with a slave or killing women, children, and monks in warfare" (2).

Complements or additions to the objectives:

Each section of these three categories is accompanied by what is like a complement or addition. This means that if it were absent, it would not undermine the objective or the foundation. For example, retribution (qisas) is essential for the preservation of life, while its equivalence is complementary. The prohibition of alcohol is essential for the preservation of the intellect, while the prohibition of a small amount that does not intoxicate is complementary. Selling, for instance, is essential for the preservation of wealth, while witnessing and pledging in it are complementary. Purification, for example, is among the improvements, and the recommended acts of purification are complementary. Shortening prayers during travel, which involves hardship, is among the needs, while combining prayers in it is complementary (3).
