An example of this is the prohibition of alcohol. The ruling is based on the description of intoxication, and if consideration is directed towards the description of intoxication, it is a consideration of the essence of the description in the prohibition. Therefore, it cannot be compared to the impairment of reason by narcotics because it is not intoxication, but it can be compared to nabidh (fermented drink) because it is intoxication.

And if consideration is directed towards the description or meaning of the impairment of reason, it is broader than intoxication and includes the impairment of reason by alcohol, narcotics, and others. It is a consideration of the generality of the description or its genus, and narcotics can be compared to it. This is the consideration of the genus of the description in the ruling. Al-Amidi said: "This is like the meaning of intoxication, for it is suitable for the prohibition of nabidh, and the essence of intoxication has been established in the prohibition of alcohol, while the effect of its genus has not been demonstrated in its essence." (1)

Another example is the description of hardship in the concession of travel; it is a suitable description supported by the rulings of shortening prayers, combining prayers, and breaking the fast while traveling. If the consideration in these concessions is for the hardship of travel, it is a consideration of the essence of the description or its particularity in the ruling. And if the consideration is for the generality of hardship or exhaustion or difficulty, it is a consideration of the generality of the description or its genus because hardship has types, such as the hardship of travel, the hardship of work, the hardship of jihad, the hardship of menstruation, and others.

However, if the consideration is for a ruling that is broader than the ruling that supports the description, it is a consideration of the description in the generality of the ruling or in its genus. An example of this is the consideration of the description of siblinghood from both parents in precedence in inheritance over siblinghood from the father. The ruling here is the guardianship of inheritance, and the description is the precedence of siblinghood from both parents over siblinghood from the father. Thus, the consideration of this description in the guardianship of inheritance is a consideration of the description in the essence of the ruling. The consideration of the description in the generality of guardianship leads to comparing the guardianship of marriage to the guardianship of inheritance, where siblinghood from both parents is given precedence in the guardianship of marriage. The generality of guardianship encompasses the guardianship of inheritance and the guardianship of marriage. This is a consideration of the description in the generality of the ruling.
