And based on that, the definitive evidence (qat'i) that they require for the proof is in the sense of the evidence that is a comprehensive source for legislation. As for other rules, principles, and rulings, they are speculative (dhanni) and are relied upon and followed in the Sharia, and no definitive evidence is required for their establishment or meaning. Rather, the condition is that they must be based on definitive evidence, that is, on the evidence that serves as their source, which are the four agreed-upon evidences. If there is disagreement regarding them, then the evidence (which is the source) must be definitive according to those who affirm it among the jurists.
Al-Zarkashi said: "Then the intended evidences are the Book, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), analogy (qiyas), and reasoning (istidlal). Imam al-Haramayn and al-Ghazali said: They are three: the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus, and they denied that speculative universal laws are part of the principles of jurisprudence. He said in the summary: What the scholars have accepted is that what cannot be known, such as reports and analogies, is not considered part of the principles of jurisprudence." (1) He then said: "If it is said: The reports of the singular narrators (akhbar al-ahad) and analogies do not lead to knowledge and they are among the evidences of the rulings of the Sharia, it is said to him: The principles are concerned with establishing evidences on the obligation of implementation, and that is what is understood through definitive evidences. As for the practice, it is derived from them, thus it pertains to jurisprudence rather than its principles. He said in al-Burhan: If it is said: Most of the legal issues are speculative, we say: Speculation is not jurisprudence; rather, jurisprudence is the knowledge of the obligation of action when speculation arises. Therefore, the scholars said: The reports of the singular narrators and analogies do not necessitate action by themselves; rather, action must be based on what necessitates knowledge of action, which are the definitive evidences regarding the obligation of action when the reports of singular narrators are narrated and analogies are established. He said: Although they are found only in the principles of jurisprudence, the role of the jurist is to clarify the definitive in acting upon them." (2)
