This means that the rulings of the Sharia (Islamic law) are preserved from change, alteration, abrogation, or distortion, and Allah has taken upon Himself the task of preserving them, so nothing of that sort will occur within them, unlike the previous laws. The evidence for this is from two aspects: the first is the legal texts, such as His saying, Exalted is He: { Indeed, We sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will preserve it } (1), and this preservation includes the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet), even if the texts do not explicitly mention it (2). The second aspect of the evidence is the reality from the time of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) until now, where Allah, Exalted is He, has appointed individuals to preserve this Sharia through their efforts, defending it and striving for it in every science that understanding the Sharia depends upon. Al-Shatibi said: "This blessed Sharia is infallible just as its Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is infallible, and just as its community is infallible in what they have agreed upon" (3). Among those whom Allah has appointed to preserve His religion are the scholars who understood the objectives of the Sharia from the Book and the Sunnah. He says: "And Allah sent from among them leaders who understood from Allah and from His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), deriving rulings and comprehending their meanings from the purposes of the Sharia in the Book and the Sunnah, sometimes from the very words, sometimes from their meanings, and sometimes from the rationale behind the ruling, until they applied the incidents that were not mentioned to those that were, and made the path easy for those who came after them, and thus the matter has proceeded in every science that understanding the Sharia depends upon, which is the very preservation that the transmitted evidences entail, and with Allah is success" (4).

(1) Surah Al-Hijr, 9.
(2) It is clarified in the third chapter that Al-Shatibi's view is that the preservation guaranteed by the verse is for the general principles and not for the particulars. The particulars follow the general principles, which are the particulars that return to the general principles, and thus he said regarding the Sunnah: "And the Sunnah, even if it is not mentioned (i.e., in the guaranteed preservation), is an explanation of it (i.e., the Book) and revolves around it; it is from it, and to it returns in its meanings, so each of the Book and the Sunnah supports the other" Al-Muwafaqat, 2/38.
(3) Al-Muwafaqat, 2/38.
(4) The same source, 2/40.
