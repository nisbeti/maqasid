As for the distinction between intention (niyyah) and purpose (qasd), it has become clear that purpose is broader and intention is more specific. This is evident upon careful examination of the texts mentioned earlier, which indicate that intention is invoked in the presence of the action; it is not sufficient for there to be merely a purpose or desire in the heart, but this must be accompanied by the intention to bring about the action. Therefore, al-Baydawi stated in his definition: "It is the will directed towards the action," and al-Suyuti said: "It is said to be concurrent with the action." As for his statement: "And that is a matter of the heart," this emphasizes that it is a type of purpose, with its place being the heart, similar to it. Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali said: "Those who differentiate between intention and will and purpose and the like do so because they believe that intention is specific to the first meaning mentioned by the jurists. Some of them said: Intention is specific to the action of the one intending, while will is not specific to that, as a person may want from Allah to forgive him without intending that" (1).
