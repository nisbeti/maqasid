The meanings of rulings are the same as the causes or intended wisdoms of the legislator, such as the preservation of religion and the preservation of life; they are meanings derived from the legal rulings that lead to these outcomes. He said: "It is necessary to pay attention to the meanings for which the rulings were legislated, and the meanings are the causes of the rulings" (1). He also said: "Legal actions are not intended for themselves, but rather for other matters that are their meanings, which are the interests for which they were legislated" (2).
Interest and harm:
It becomes clear from the above that the pursuit of interests and the prevention of harms are the goals and outcomes of the rulings. They are their causes, objectives, and meanings.
However, it should be noted that there is a fundamental difference between the meaning of both interest and harm according to al-Shatibi and their meanings according to those who preceded him among the jurists who assert the superiority of pursuing interests and preventing harms.
For the earlier scholars, interests and harms are what is deemed so by rational individuals and wise people, meaning they are what returns to the obligated individuals in accordance with their innate nature and inclinations, and aligns with their desires and objectives; therefore, they are suitable meanings or descriptions. Thus, the recognized interests for them are the suitable descriptions if a ruling or rulings come in accordance with them (3).
As for al-Shatibi, the interest is what is established as being intended by the legislator, regardless of whether it is suitable or unsuitable. This is done by deducing a meaning or description in the rulings, regardless of whether it is an interest or a harm according to the obligated individuals and their customs. If the meaning is deduced in a way that conveys what resembles a moral consensus, it is a general meaning intended by the legislator, and it is considered an interest if it causes what the Sharia requests, and it is considered a harm if it causes what the Sharia prohibits. This concept of interest and harm according to him is considered a reversal of the conventional meaning among the jurists, and failing to recognize this meaning leads to a misunderstanding of his concept of objectives, or to attributing to al-Shatibi opinions and ideas that contradict the reality of his views and thoughts.
