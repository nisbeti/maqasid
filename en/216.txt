Indeed, the utmost that these texts indicate, which al-Shatibi intended by citing them, is that rulings and actions have objectives or purposes. Since these actions and rulings are from the Legislator, their objectives are thus legal (shar'i). And since they are legal objectives, they are termed benefits (masalih) or harms (mafasid) depending on the legality of the actions or their illegality.
To prove that this is al-Shatibi's concept of benefits and harms, we will discuss the evidence he presented to demonstrate that the Sharia is based on benefits, one by one.
Analytical discussion of al-Shatibi's arguments:
Al-Shatibi said: "The purpose of establishing the laws is solely for the benefits of the servants in both this world and the Hereafter."(1) He also said: "Al-Razi claimed that God's rulings are not based on any reason whatsoever, just as His actions are not, and that the Mu'tazila agreed that His rulings are based on the consideration of the benefits of the servants, and that this is the choice of most later jurists. And when he was compelled in the science of Usul al-fiqh to establish reasons for the legal rulings, he did so on the basis that the reasons are merely signs that indicate the rulings."(2)
