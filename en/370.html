The sharing in objectives (tashrik) refers to the sharing between the intention of complying with the command of the Lawgiver or drawing closer to Him and the intention of fulfilling one's own desires. Among the desires of the self, some are for show (riya) and some are not. Show is when one acts to be seen by people, such as someone who performs an act to be noticed by a ruler or others in order to gain esteem in their eyes because they see him. However, someone who acts with the intention of profit or gain or to enjoy good things has an intention for the self that is not show. Al-Qarafi said: "To perform the commanded act and draw closer to Allah, Exalted is He, with the intention of seeking His face, and for people to esteem him or for him to be esteemed in their hearts, so that their benefit reaches him or harm is repelled from him, this is the basis of one category of show. The other category is to perform an act without any intention of seeking Allah's face at all, but only for people. This category is called show of sincerity, while the first is show of partnership (shirk), because this does not involve sharing but is purely for creation, while the first is for creation and for Allah, Exalted is He" (1). He also said: "As for absolute sharing, like one who strives to achieve obedience to Allah through jihad and to obtain wealth from the spoils, this does not harm him, nor is it prohibited by consensus, because Allah, Exalted is He, has made this for him in this act of worship. There is a distinction between his striving to have people say he is brave or to be esteemed by the imam so that he receives more from the public treasury; this and similar acts are forbidden show, and between striving to obtain captives, horses, and weapons from the enemy's wealth, this does not harm him even though he has shared, and this cannot be called show because show is to act so that he is seen by others among His creation, and seeing is only valid from creation. Thus, for one who does not see or perceive, it cannot be said that the act is show in relation to him, and the wealth taken from spoils and similar sources cannot be said to be seen or perceived, so the term show does not apply to these intentions due to the absence of sight in them" (2).

Based on the above, one can elaborate on the ruling regarding the sharing between the intention of compliance and the intention of desire.
