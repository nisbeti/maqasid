And as for His saying, Exalted is He: { And We have sent down to you the Reminder that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them }, it does not indicate that the Sunnah is not independent in legislation. Rather, it indicates that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) conveys, clarifies, details, and explains what has come in the Qur'an. There is nothing in the wording of the text or its implied meaning that indicates that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) does not clarify anything other than what has come in the Qur'an. Allah, Exalted is He, revealed to His Prophet the Sharia of Islam, which includes what He revealed to him from the Qur'an and from the Sunnah. Thus, he (peace and blessings be upon him) clarifies the Qur'an and clarifies the Sunnah, and both are revelation. He, Exalted is He, said: { And he does not speak from [his own] inclination. It is not but a revelation revealed } (1). This is confirmed by the texts that require obedience to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and clarify that Allah, Exalted is He, has given him the Qur'an and something like it with him (2).

Therefore, the inquiry of whether the Sunnah is independent in legislation or not is essentially about whether the Sunnah can bring about a command that has no basis in the Qur'an or not, meaning whether it has no kind or type to refer back to in its subject matter. The claim that the Sunnah does not come with that requires evidence, and there is no evidence; rather, the evidences allow for that.

Thus, the evidences that rely on this matter are the extrapolation of the obligations of the Qur'an and the obligations of the Sunnah, meaning their occurrence or non-occurrence, and it has been established that they do occur. This includes the prohibition of the flesh of domesticated donkeys and every predator with fangs and every bird with talons (3). So, if the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had permitted these things, would that permissibility contradict a principle in the Qur'an? Is it not the case that if the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had not prohibited them, their ruling would be permissibility based on the principle: the default ruling of things is permissibility unless there is evidence of prohibition?
