The tongue of Lisan al-Din Ibn al-Khatib speaks of the tyranny of the Bermejo and his consumption of the people's wealth, saying: "He was a tyrant (1), devouring the fodder and pasture, and by eliminating the crops, he deprived the means of prosperity, opened the doors of calamity, and permitted some taxes (2), giving a little and then withholding (3). Days did not pass before he returned to his tyranny (4) and oppressed the subjects with his evil, burdening them with the need to tether their horses after drowning their provisions for his soldiers" (5).

Economic Condition:

From what has been mentioned, it is evident that there was a rise followed by a decline in the state of Al-Andalus, which is reflected in the emergence and growth of the state, then its decline and fall. This is also evident in the growth of wealth and the increase in spending and saving, followed by a worsening of need, which was witnessed during the era of al-Shatibi.

Professor Muhammad Abu al-Ajfan states: "We learn from a text directed to al-Shatibi that Muslims needed some essential resources, and some questioned: Does this need permit them to sell to the disbelievers what they had been prohibited from selling to them because it strengthens them? The text of the question is: Is it permissible for the people of Al-Andalus to sell items that the scholars prohibited selling to the people of war, such as weapons and others, due to their necessity for essentials in other matters like food and clothing, or is there no difference between the people of Al-Andalus and others in the lands of Islam?" (6).
