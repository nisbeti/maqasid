It is important to remind ourselves of certain matters at this point, namely that the outcomes of actions are their causes, benefits, rulings, or meanings, and al-Shatibi does not accept them as either beneficial or harmful unless they are established with certainty through induction. The outcomes are definitive universal principles, and without this, they are not considered. The outcome that must be taken into account when examining an action and its result is that which is established with certainty. Since actions are particulars and their results are particulars, considering the outcome is akin to considering the universal principle to which these particular results refer. This is part of the details of his methodology, as previously explained. Below are some of his texts to remind us of this matter, as it is essential for understanding the essence of the outcomes of actions. It is also crucial to remain constantly aware when reading al-Shatibi of what he means by benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), which are what the legislator intended, regardless of human attributes. They are so in terms of what the legislator has established, not in terms of the whims of souls. He said: "The legitimate causes were established for the benefits of the servants, and they are the wisdom of legislation" (1). He said: "What is prohibited is prohibited due to a harm that results from its action. If it is acted upon, it is under the condition that it leads to the benefits or harms under the cause; ignorance of the benefit or harm or their quantities does not exempt one from this. The matter entails that there is a harm in committing the prohibited act that Allah knows, and for that reason, He prohibited it. The doer is obligated to all that results from that cause of benefits or harms, even if he is unaware of the details of that" (2). He said: "It is necessary to pay attention to the meanings for which the rulings were established. The meanings are the causes of the rulings" (3). He said: "The legal rulings were established to bring about benefits or to avert harms, and they are their causes definitively" (4). He said: "It is established in these matters that the considered benefits are the universals, not the particulars" (5).
