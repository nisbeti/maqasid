The third: distinguishing between the rights of Allah and the rights of human beings. The rights of human beings refer to the rights of the servant himself who performs the act that is a means. The effects of the action are based on whether it is a right of the servant. Its effects are nullified if it is a right of Allah. An example of the right of the servant is traveling to shorten the prayer or to break the fast. An example of the right of Allah is the distinction between the community and the gathering of the separated in zakat. He said: "The third is to distinguish between the rights of Allah, Exalted is He, and the rights of human beings" (1). However, if both rights are combined in one action, the right of Allah, Exalted is He, and the right of the servant, the matter becomes a subject of consideration and ijtihad (juridical reasoning) to favor one of the two sides, and the ruling will be for the prevailing side according to the jurist. He said: "What remains is that if both rights are combined, it is a matter of consideration and ijtihad to favor one of the two sides according to what appears to the jurist, and Allah knows best" (2).

The right of Allah and the right of the servant:

These two expressions are used to refer to what the obligated person has no choice in, which is the right of Allah, and to what he has a choice in, which is the right of the servant. The meaning of the right of the servant is that he is legally permitted to claim his right or to waive it. As for the right of Allah, the servant cannot waive it. The right of others among the servants also falls under the right of Allah; the servant cannot waive the right of others. Al-Shatibi said: "Everything that pertains to the rights of Allah has no choice for the obligated person in any case, while what pertains to the right of the servant himself allows him the choice. As for the rights of Allah, the evidence that they are not waived and do not return to the choice of the obligated person is abundant, and the strongest of it is the complete induction in the sources of the Sharia, such as purification in its various forms, prayer, zakat, fasting, pilgrimage, enjoining good and forbidding evil, the highest of which is jihad, and what relates to it from expiations, transactions, eating, drinking, clothing, and other acts of worship and customs that establish the right of Allah or the right of others among the servants, as well as all offenses; all of these cannot have the right of Allah waived in them at all" (3).
