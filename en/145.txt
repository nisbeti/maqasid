Thus, the disagreement remained as it was, returning to the dispute over how to consider wisdom (hikmah), public interest (maslaha), or objectives (maqasid). Those who reject reasoning based on appropriate descriptions see this consideration as a mere imaginary notion or a mental obsession, a conjecture that is not supported by the sacred law, and therefore has no validity. From here, the arguments of those who justify based on objectives emerged, asserting that reasoning is a legal necessity and that it is the foundation of rulings. Accordingly, reasoning based on beneficial meanings is not mere arbitrariness but rather a legal conjecture because it is the foundation; otherwise, the ruling would be merely an act of devotion, which contradicts the principle. This is the subject of the next section.

Section Three
An Introduction to the Idea of Objectives (Maqasid) According to al-Shatibi

A brief overview of this section:
The main topic of this section is the principle of reasoning, that is, the assertion that the foundation of rulings is reasoning. Some jurists have relied on the idea that the Sharia is based on bringing about benefits and preventing harms in order to establish certain pathways in reasoning, particularly the pathway of appropriateness. Establishing this reasoning necessitated proving several issues:
First: ... proving that Allah, Exalted is He, does not act except for a purpose or wisdom, and His legislation is a result of His actions, thus establishing that the legal rulings are reasoned. Since some legal rulings cannot be expected to have known reasons, such as numbers, quantities, and shares, it cannot be said that rulings are reasoned; rather, it should be said that the foundation of rulings is reasoning.
Second: ... that wisdom must be a benefit and not a harm, and the benefit either pertains to Allah, Exalted is He, or to the servant. Allah, Exalted is He, is free from benefits and harms, so the benefit must pertain to the servant.
