It is clear that al-Shatibi does not accept evidence unless it is definitive or refers back to something definitive, and the detailed evidences do not indicate the intended purpose of the legislator with definitive indication. So how can certainty be achieved knowing that the detailed evidences are the evidences of rulings? He said: "The relied upon in the first intention is the legal evidences, and the existence of certainty in them based on the commonly used is nonexistent or extremely rare, I mean in the individual evidences. If they are from the reports of the individual narrators, then their failure to provide certainty is evident. And if they are mutawatir (consecutively reported), then their provision of certainty is contingent upon all or most of its premises being speculative, and what is contingent upon the speculative must necessarily be speculative" (1). He then said: "The only considered evidences here are those that have been extrapolated from a group of speculative evidences that converge on one meaning until they provide certainty in it, for the gathering has a strength that separation does not have, and for this reason, the mutawatir provides certainty. This is a type of it. So if a compilation of the evidences of the issue is obtained that provides knowledge, it is the required evidence, and it is similar to the moral mutawatir, like the knowledge of the bravery of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and the existence of Hatim, which is derived from the multitude of incidents reported about them" (2). He held that this is the only way to establish certainties in the Sharia. He said: "From this path, the obligation of the five foundations such as prayer and zakat and others has been established with certainty. If someone were to argue for the obligation of prayer by citing His saying, the Exalted, { Establish the prayer } (3) or something similar, it would be a matter of consideration in the argument by itself from several angles" (4). This certainty, and consequently this extrapolation, is required to establish anything from the necessities, needs, or enhancements (5), and these and what falls under them are the objectives of the Sharia or its foundations. They do not deviate or falter as previously mentioned.
