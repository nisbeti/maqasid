Al-Shatibi says: "The objectives that are considered are of two types: one refers to the intention of the legislator, and the other refers to the intention of the obligated person. The first is considered in terms of the legislator's intention in establishing the Sharia from the outset. And in terms of his intention in establishing it for understanding. And in terms of his intention in establishing it for obligations according to it. And in terms of his intention in having the obligated person submit to its ruling; these are four types" (1).

The subject of this chapter is the first two types. As for the third and fourth types, they are the subject of the following chapter - if Allah wills.

The first discussion

The intention of the legislator in establishing the Sharia from the outset

The meaning of this type:

Establishing the Sharia from the outset refers to the obligation of it, meaning the commands and prohibitions. This type represents the objectives of the legislator regarding obligations, such that the obligations are the causes for their effects, which are the objectives of the legislator through them; it is correct to refer to this type as the objectives of the Sharia.

The objectives are of three categories:

These objectives are understood from the legal discourse and from the legal obligations. Al-Shatibi says: "The obligations of the Sharia relate to preserving its objectives in creation, and these objectives do not exceed three categories: the first is that they are necessary, the second is that they are needed, and the third is that they are embellishing" (2). He then defines the limits of each of these three categories and clarifies that the legislator's intention to preserve them is evident in all obligations of the Sharia, as the obligations of the Sharia are the rulings of acts of worship, customs, transactions, and crimes. In these four, the legislator's intention to preserve the necessities, needs, and embellishments is evident.
