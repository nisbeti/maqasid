Then al-Shatibi explained his meaning of companionship and inclination, saying: "Companionship is either through action, an example of which is to say, for instance: this food or this clothing, or this tangible object has been permitted for me by the Sharia to enjoy, so I enjoy what is permitted and work to obtain it because it is allowed. Or it is through potential, an example of which is entering into the causation of that permitted thing from the allowed avenue, but the very permission did not cross his mind; rather, it occurred to him that this is reached through such and such a path. If he reaches it through that, then in ruling it is like the first, provided that the path leading to the permitted is permissible from his side, except that companionship through action is superior" (1). Thus, al-Shatibi contradicts, indeed opposes, al-Ghazali significantly on this issue, especially regarding the topic of sincerity and sharing in objectives. He says: "If it is said: how can the intention of the legislator for sincerity in ordinary actions and the prohibition of sharing in them be achieved? It is said: this means that they should be performed according to what is legislated. They should not be intended for a pre-Islamic act, nor a satanic innovation, nor resemble those outside the faith, such as drinking water or honey in the manner of drinking wine, or eating what is made to glorify the festivals of the Jews or Christians even if made by a Muslim, or what is slaughtered in imitation of pre-Islamic practices, and similar acts that are a form of glorifying polytheism" (2). This is the elaboration of what he stated in the Book of Rulings, where he said: "Ordinary matters are only considered valid if they do not contradict the intention of the legislator, and the appearance of agreement is not a condition" (3).
