The legal rulings (ahkam shar'iyya) are among the meanings of the legal texts (nusus shar'iyya) and their implications, as well as the legal principles (qawa'id shar'iyya). The words and texts are those for which a meaning is sought; once the meaning is known, the intention of the Legislator (al-Shari') behind it is understood. The legal meanings are those intended by the Legislator, and the servant has no knowledge of them except through revelation from Him, Exalted is He. The sources of Allah's revelations - Glory be to Him - are the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma'), and analogy (qiyas), and considering the differences, they are the Qur'an and the Sunnah and what they guide towards. These are the sources of the legal texts, and there can be no legal meaning unless it originates from the legal texts.

Accordingly, if the meanings of the rulings that al-Shatibi refers to are the implications of the legal texts, then they are legal; if they are not, then they are not legal. If they are among the implications of the texts, then they are meanings of the texts, and thus they are considered valid.

What al-Shatibi means by the meanings of the rulings are their purposes (hikm), or their causes (musaabibat), or their benefits (masalih), or their objectives (maqasid), or their reasons (illal), and he does not mean that they are meanings of the texts.

__________

(1) Legal rulings (ahkam shar'iyya): The rules derived from Islamic law.

(2) Legal texts (nusus shar'iyya): The texts from the Qur'an and Sunnah that provide legal guidance.

(3) Legal principles (qawa'id shar'iyya): The foundational rules that govern the interpretation and application of Islamic law.

(4) Legislator (al-Shari'): The one who establishes the laws, referring to Allah in this context.

(5) Legal meanings: The intended meanings behind the legal texts as understood in Islamic jurisprudence.

(6) Revelation: The divine guidance provided by Allah through the Qur'an and Sunnah.

(7) Consensus (ijma'): The agreement of scholars on a particular legal issue.

(8) Analogy (qiyas): A method of legal reasoning that applies established rulings to new situations based on similarities.

(9) Implications: The meanings or interpretations derived from the legal texts.

(10) Purposes (hikm): The intended benefits or objectives behind legal rulings.

(11) Causes (musaabibat): The factors that lead to the establishment of a legal ruling.

(12) Benefits (masalih): The advantages or positive outcomes associated with a legal ruling.

(13) Objectives (maqasid): The overarching goals of Sharia that legal rulings aim to achieve.

(14) Reasons (illal): The underlying justifications for a legal ruling.
