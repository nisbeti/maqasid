Secondly: Al-Shatibi himself has established that these meanings do not have any legal indication; rather, they serve the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), which are derived from the texts (1). If they had a legal indication from the texts, they would be among its original objectives. Nevertheless, he has categorized them, based on his observations, as definitive general objectives, which is not sound. Consider his statement, for example: "What assures us from Allah's questioning us on the Day of Resurrection about how you understood from me that I intended such and such classification based on my statement: { And they think that they are doing well } (2), or my statement: { He said, 'Indeed, I am of those who are the warners' } (3), for to claim such a thing about the Qur'an and that it is intended by the speaker is dangerous; rather, it refers back to His statement: { When you receive it with your tongues and say with your mouths that of which you have no knowledge, and you think it is insignificant, while it is, in the sight of Allah, great } (4), and to the fact that it is an opinion in the Book of Allah" (5).

Perhaps someone might say: The assertion of these objectives or causes—according to his view—is based on the premise that the original principle is the justification of rulings; therefore, he traces the rulings to verify their legal causes.

The response is: This principle, even if accepted, permits justification, but the causes are numerous and the imaginations are more so. Identifying this cause or that without any guidance, indication, or sign from the Sharia that points to the type or nature of the cause is a claim without evidence. Therefore, alongside this principle, there must be a method for the cause or evidence for it. Thus, you find that the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) who affirm the principle of justification also establish methods for the cause, such as analogy (qiyas), suitability (munasaba), similarity (shabah), examination and division (sabr wa taqsim), and extension and reversal (tard wa 'aks), among others.
