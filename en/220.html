This text has two aspects of meaning regarding the justification of Sharia (Islamic law) based on public interest (maslaha). The first aspect is the one adopted by the scholars who argue for the justification of Sharia, stating that since it is a mercy, it must return to the servants with benefits and avert harm and evils from them. Thus, the pursuit of benefits and the prevention of evils become the justification for the rulings. This is not the aspect of reasoning that al-Shatibi relies on, as we know that al-Shatibi does not suffice with a single text or isolated texts to establish meaning. Al-Shatibi's purpose in citing this text is the second aspect, which is that the actions and rulings of Allah are justified. This text is one of the particulars that serve this meaning. In other words, Allah, Exalted is He, performed the act of sending messengers, and the text indicates that His action has a purpose, which is mercy. Thus, this meaning, which is the justification of actions and rulings, is realized in this particular instance. This is the intended meaning in all of his twelve arguments; if his purpose with this text were the first aspect, it would not support all the other texts.

Therefore, with this text, he establishes that the sending of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was for a purpose. Since al-Shatibi made this text a proof for the justification based on public interest, this indicates that he uses the term justification to refer to the purpose. As for the fact that this justification is a benefit, it can be said that mercy is a benefit for the servants, and thus the legislator intended it. However, this is not al-Shatibi's intent; rather, his intent is that the mercy for the worlds is the purpose of the legislator, and therefore it is a benefit. This text does not imply that what a person considers a benefit is what the Sharia came to indicate, because the implication of the text is that the message is a mercy for the servants, regardless of the commands and prohibitions within it; it is a benefit for the worlds with its commands and prohibitions, however they may be.

His third proof: { And He it is who created the heavens and the earth in six days, and His Throne was upon the water, that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed } (1).
