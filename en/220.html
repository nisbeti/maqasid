This text has two aspects of meaning regarding the justification of the Sharia (Islamic law) based on public interest (maslaha). The first aspect is the one adopted by the scholars who argued for the justification of the Sharia, stating that since it is a mercy, it must return to the servants with benefits and avert harm and evils from them. Thus, the pursuit of benefits and the prevention of evils become the justification for the rulings. This is not the aspect of reasoning that al-Shatibi relied upon, as we know that al-Shatibi does not suffice with a single text or isolated texts to establish meaning. Al-Shatibi's intention in citing this text is the second aspect, which is that the actions and rulings of Allah are justified. This text is one of the particulars that support this meaning. In other words, Allah, Exalted is He, performed the act of sending, and the text indicates that His action has a purpose, which is mercy. Thus, this meaning, which is the justification of actions and rulings, is realized in this particular instance. This is the intended meaning in all of his twelve arguments; if his intention with this text were the first aspect, it would not strengthen all the other texts.

Therefore, this text establishes that the sending of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was for a purpose.

Since al-Shatibi used this text as evidence for reasoning based on public interest (maslaha), this indicates that he uses the term "reason" to refer to the objective. As for the fact that this reason is a public interest, it can be said that mercy is a public interest for the servants, and thus the legislator aimed for it. However, this is not what al-Shatibi intended; rather, his point is that the mercy for the worlds is an objective of the legislator, and therefore it is a public interest. This text does not imply that what a person considers to be a public interest is what the Sharia has come to indicate, because the meaning of the text is that the message is a mercy for the servants, regardless of the commands and prohibitions it contains; it is a public interest for the worlds in whatever commands and prohibitions it includes, however they may be.

His third proof: { And He is the One who created the heavens and the earth in six days, and His Throne was upon the water, that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed }(1).
