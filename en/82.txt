7- The reason (illah) must be transitive. This means that the ruling extends from the original case to the branch. Everyone agrees on the validity of this condition for the soundness of analogy (qiyas). However, they differ on the validity of the reason if it is not transitive. Some of the usul scholars consider the description a reason for the original case even if the ruling cannot be extended to a branch by analogy. Others prohibit considering it a reason on the basis that there is no benefit in reasoning with it. The benefit of the reason is the transference of the ruling from the original case to the branch. Al-Amidi said: "Everyone agrees that the transitivity of the reason is a condition for the validity of analogy, and on the validity of the limited reason(1), whether it is explicitly stated or agreed upon. They only differ on the validity of the limited reason if it is neither explicitly stated nor agreed upon, such as the reasoning of the Shafi'i school regarding the prohibition of usury (riba) in the two currencies based on the essence of their value(2)"(3).
And Shams al-A'imma al-Sarakhsi said: "The disagreement is regarding the condition of transitivity, and our position is that reasoning with a text in a way that does not allow for transference is not permissible at all. According to al-Shafi'i, this reasoning is permissible, but it does not constitute analogy. He permitted reasoning with the text on usury in gold and silver based on their value, even if it does not transfer. We do not permit that, and our position is that the ruling of reasoning is the transference of the ruling of the original case to the branches"(4).

(1) The limited reason refers to the description that has been established as a reason for the ruling of the original case, and cannot be transferred to a branch, such as theft being a reason for cutting off the hand; the text indicates reasoning for the ruling of the original case with it. However, there is no branch to which the ruling can be transferred by analogy based on the commonality of the reason. Applying the ruling of cutting off the hand in another context is based on the indication of the text, that is, by the generality of the text and not by analogy. Among the related disagreements among the analogists here is another disagreement, which is whether the evidence for the reason is the reason itself or the evidence of the address that indicated it and the reason? The Shafi'is and the theologians went with the former, while the Hanafis went with the latter.
