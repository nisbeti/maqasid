... Al-Shatibi's focus on the universality of the Sharia and its permanence, and his mention of this matter within the objective of worship and compliance, affirms his concept of benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) and refutes the claim of the Sharia's evolution and flexibility and the permissibility of changing its rulings. He states: "Among the characteristics of the Sharia is 'universality and consistency; therefore, the legal rulings regarding the actions of the accountable individuals are absolute, even if their specific instances are countless. There is no action imposed, nor movement or stillness claimed, except that the Sharia governs it, both individually and collectively, which is the meaning of its being general" (1). Among its characteristics also is "permanence without abrogation; therefore, you will not find in it, after its completion, any abrogation or specification of its universality, nor restriction of its absoluteness, nor lifting of any of its rulings, neither in terms of the generality of the accountable individuals nor in terms of the specificity of some of them, nor in terms of one condition over another. Rather, whatever establishes a cause is a cause forever and does not cease, and whatever is a condition is always a condition, and whatever is obligatory is always obligatory, or recommended is recommended, and so on for all rulings; they do not cease or change. Even if the obligation were to continue indefinitely, its rulings would remain the same" (2).

... He says: "If it is established that the legislator intended through legislation to establish benefits for the Hereafter and this world in a manner that does not disrupt their order, neither in terms of the whole nor in terms of the part, and whether it is of the nature of necessities, needs, or embellishments, then if it were established in such a way that its order could be disrupted or its rulings could be violated, the legislation would not be intended for it, for its being benefits in that case is no more valid than its being harms. Rather, the legislator intends for it to be benefits absolutely; thus, its establishment must be in that manner eternally, universally, and generally for all types of obligations and the obligated individuals in all circumstances, and thus we have found the matter therein, and all praise is due to Allah" (3).
