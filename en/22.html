He also said: "Industries flourished in this kingdom that the inhabitants mastered and inherited from their ancestors who built the most magnificent civilization in the Andalusian peninsula. Among these were the manufacturing of weapons, silk and woolen fabrics, ceramic vessels, various types of paper and leather. The export of these industries provided income and stimulated commercial activity. The southerners and other nations with close economic ties to Andalusia had commercial establishments in Granada, and Granada entered into trade treaties with the Republic of Genoa and the Kingdom of Aragon" (1).

This atmosphere witnessed a surge in the pursuit of knowledge and in scientific and jurisprudential dialogue and discussion, and its results appeared in the enthusiasm for authorship and research, with the names of scholars shining brightly and their works becoming well-known. At the forefront of each field were prominent figures such as Ibn al-Fakhar al-Biri in the sciences of language, Ibn al-Saigh and Abu Hayyan in the science of grammar in particular, Abu Ishaq al-Shatibi in the science of usul (principles of jurisprudence), Ibn Jazi and Ibn Asim in fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence), Ibn al-Khatib and Ibn Zamrak in literature and politics, Ibn Hudhayl the Wise in philosophy, and many others besides them (2).
