Al-Shatibi explains that these universals (kulliyat) are the foundation of the sciences of Sharia and are the sources of rulings. The basis of legislation is what is universal or related to the universal; thus, whatever is like this is central to knowledge, and what is not like this has no consideration according to his methodology. He says: "Some knowledge is central to knowledge, and some is peripheral to knowledge but not central, and some is neither central nor peripheral; these are three categories. The first category is the foundation and the basis upon which the pursuit revolves, and to which the objectives of the firmly rooted (rasikhun) ultimately lead, and that is what is definitive or related to a definitive origin" (1). He said: "The second category, which is counted among the peripheral knowledge and not among the central: is what is not definitive nor related to a definitive origin, but rather to something speculative, or it is related to a definitive origin, yet it lacks some of those characteristics, or more than one characteristic, thus it is speculative (mukhayyal) (2), and it is something that provokes the intellect at first glance and initial consideration without compromising its origin or the meaning of another" (3). This category is dismissed by al-Shatibi (4).

(1) Al-Muwafaqat. 1/43. We will clarify shortly what al-Shatibi means by referring to a definitive origin.

(2) That is: it instills in the heart an illusion of correctness, so it is something that minds receive with acceptance or that souls find pleasing. In this category fall - according to al-Shatibi - the meanings or descriptions that come in the paths of analogy (masalih) and resemblance (shabah), or through the method of inference as long as they are not definitive.

(3) Al-Shatibi, Al-Muwafaqat. 1/45.

(4) Al-Shatibi's stance on the meanings that fall into this category resembles the stance of the Hanafi scholars regarding the path of analogy, as it is speculative and does not return to a definitive origin, and the speculation here is not of the type that is applicable in legal matters but rather is purely rational speculation. Therefore, this category is not considered, and meanings may have evidence that completes their three characteristics, making them considered, and they may have what qualifies them from the third category due to their harm to the Sharia. See: 1/46 - 50.
