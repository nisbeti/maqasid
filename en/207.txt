It is redundant to say, after clarifying his views on concession (rukhsah) and obligation (azimah), that he believes standing with obligation is better in terms of Sharia than taking concession. Likewise, concessions do not have a universal law; thus, their instances are limited to those mentioned in the texts, and they cannot be analogized. As for what falls under general rules, such as not being tasked with what is beyond one's capacity, the hardships that cause distress, and the removal of hardship, these are considered obligations and not concessions (1).
Causes and effects:
The cause is one of the situational rulings. In the terminology of the jurists (usuliyyin), it is a clear, defined description that the textual evidence indicates as a marker for the existence of the ruling (2). For example, the crescent moon of Ramadan is a cause for the ruling of fasting, and a sale is a cause for ownership and the permissibility of benefiting from the property. The relationship between the cause and the ruling in legal rulings is different from that between the cause and effect in rational matters. For instance, drinking alcohol is a cause of intoxication; thus, drinking alcohol results in intoxication. However, in Sharia, the crescent moon of Ramadan does not contain the ruling of fasting; rather, it exists at its occurrence, not due to a material relationship between cause and effect, but rather by a report from the Legislator who made the description a sign or indicator for the existence of the ruling. This is the understanding commonly held by the jurists regarding the relationship between cause and ruling in legal terminology.
