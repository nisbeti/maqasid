To verify that the mujtahid (jurist) has reached the degree of consideration, it is said that the student of knowledge has three states: a state in which it is clear that he is not among those qualified for valid ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), and thus he must follow (taqlid) without disagreement. A second state in which it is clear that he is among those qualified for valid ijtihad without disagreement, and between these two states lies a third state that is ambiguous and whose matter is unclear, leading to disagreement regarding it.
As for the one in the first state, he is someone who is still examining the particulars and is beginning to grasp the general meanings, but they are not yet complete. He continues to reflect and deduce to understand the objectives (maqasid), allowing his mind to roam within them. Some of these objectives are consistent and established, while others are contradicted by texts, thus failing to establish a universal principle. Therefore, he cannot engage in ijtihad. It was said: "He must alert his mind to reflect on what he has memorized and to investigate its causes, and this arises from a feeling of understanding something that has been acquired, but it remains vague."(1) It was also said: "This student, while remaining in this state, wrestles with the legal sources and is wrestled by them, opposing them and being opposed by them, in the hope of grasping their foundations and connecting with their rulings and objectives, which have not yet been clarified for him. He cannot engage in ijtihad regarding what he is examining because he has not yet established the basis for ijtihad, nor is he in a position where his heart is at ease with what he is trying to ijtihad in; thus, he must refrain and follow (taqlid)."(2)
