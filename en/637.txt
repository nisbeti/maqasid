Therefore, the scholars of usul (usul al-fiqh) did not refer to the speculative evidences as proofs, but rather considered them to be indicators; they are signs that point to their meanings. However, the obligation to act upon them or to follow them does not depend on them, but rather on something else, which is the conclusive evidence for acting upon them. The conclusive evidences are the definitive proofs for the obligation to adhere to the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas). The conclusive evidence is not the detailed proof itself, which is different from the jurists, as these indicators are considered proofs by the scholars of usul and the theologians. Al-Asnawi said: "Know that the expression of proofs excludes many principles of jurisprudence such as generalities, reports of the singular (akhbar al-ahad), analogy, presumption of continuity (istihsan), and others. For the scholars of usul, even if they acknowledge them, they are not considered proofs for jurisprudence but rather indicators of it, as the term proof is only applied to that which is certain" (1). He also said: "I say: The proofs of jurisprudence are divided into those agreed upon by the imams and those disputed. The agreed upon are four: the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy, while everything else such as presumption of continuity, public interests (masalih mursala), preference (istihsan), reverse analogy (qiyas al-aks), and taking the lesser of what has been said, and others that will come are disputed among them" (2). He further stated: "As for the auditory evidences, if they are disputed, they only provide conjecture according to those who advocate them, while the agreed upon among the imams are the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy. As for analogy, it is clear that it only provides conjecture, and as for consensus, if it reaches us through singular reports, it is likewise, and its transmission through mass reporting is very rare. Even if it is assumed, Imam in al-Mahsool and al-Amidi in al-Ihkam and al-Muntaha al-Sul have affirmed that it is speculative. As for the Sunnah, singular reports from it only provide conjecture, while the mass-reported ones are like the Qur'an, their text is definitive but their indication is speculative due to the need to negate the ten possibilities" (3).
