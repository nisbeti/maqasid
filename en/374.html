Then he said: "Know that if an action is not purely for the sake of Allah, Exalted is He, but is accompanied by a trace of showing off (riya) or personal desires, then people have differed on whether this entails reward, punishment, or nothing at all, so that it has neither merit nor demerit. As for that which is intended solely for showing off, it is certainly upon him and is a cause of condemnation and punishment. As for that which is purely for Allah, it is a cause of reward. The consideration lies in the mixed intention. The apparent reports indicate that there is no reward for it, and the reports are not free from contradiction on this matter. What seems to be the case - and knowledge is with Allah - is that one should look at the strength of the motive. If the religious motive is equal to the personal motive, they will counterbalance and cancel each other out, and the action will be neither for him nor against him. If the motive of showing off is predominant and stronger, then it is not beneficial and is, moreover, harmful and leads to punishment. Indeed, the punishment in this case is lighter than the punishment for an action that is purely for showing off and does not contain any trace of seeking closeness. However, if the intention of seeking closeness is predominant in relation to the other motive, then he will have a reward commensurate with the strength of the religious motive" (1). He also stated that seeking worldly gain is not forbidden, but seeking it through acts of religion is forbidden due to the presence of showing off and the alteration of worship from its proper place (2).
