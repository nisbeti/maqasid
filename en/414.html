It should be noted that he cites the hadith: "The killer does not inherit." The disagreement among the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) is regarding the consideration of the meaning of dealing in opposition to the intended purpose that the hadith supports, and this meaning is not one of the indications of the text of the hadith. Meanwhile, al-Shatibi discusses the application of the hadith itself, and his point is that if the idea of dealing in opposition to the intended purpose is established definitively, then it is valid to apply the hadith because it would then refer to a definitive meaning. We also notice that he considers this hadith to be of the category of the strange (gharib), and we have previously explained that the scholars of usul who reason based on the appropriate (munasib) consider the strange to be a description that is observed in a single ruling and has no witness, or has only one witness, and the term witness means a ruling that agrees with the meaning. Here, he has made the hadith strange, and it requires a definitive witness, meaning that the meaning of dealing in opposition to the intended purpose must be established definitively. Therefore, we find him in another place mentioning the principle of dealing in opposition to the intended purpose as a question with a tendency to accept it, as it has been provided with supporting particulars, but it has not been provided with the inductive reasoning that leads to certainty. He says: "But the question remains whether the causation should be considered as being contradictory to the intention of the legislator in such a way that it does not lead to what the cause intended, thus establishing the principle of dealing in opposition to the intended purpose, and the ruling is applied based on it if that intended purpose is determined, which is implied by the hadith regarding the prohibition of the killer from inheriting, and the implications of jurisprudence regarding the hadith of preventing the gathering of the separated and the dispersing of the gathered out of fear of charity. Likewise, the inheritance of the divorced woman during the illness, or the perpetuation of prohibition on one who marries during the waiting period, and much of this." If dealing in opposition to the intended purpose were a universal principle that supports the meaning of the hadith "The killer does not inherit," it would not be appropriate for al-Shatibi to cite the hadith as an example of the fourth category that does not have a universal principle supporting it.
