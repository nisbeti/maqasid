... Therefore, you find him in many instances in al-Muwafaqat addressing fatwas that are issued citing public interest (maslaha) or necessity, and addressing leniency without a license, and addressing the jurists who present such opinions based on the assertion that the Sharia of Islam is lenient and accommodating. He says, for example: "The leniency of the Hanif (haneef) is only present within the bounds of what is consistent with its principles, and it is not the pursuit of concessions or the selection of opinions based on whims that is established from its foundations" (1), and he says: "The pursuit of concessions is a leaning towards the desires of the souls, and the law has come with a prohibition against following desires... Some may permit this in situations where they claim necessity and compelling need based on the principle that necessities permit the prohibited, thus taking what aligns with their objective... This too is of the previously mentioned category, as its essence is taking what aligns with the present desire, and the locations of necessity are well-known in the Sharia" (2). He then says: "Piety and religiousness are scarce among many who position themselves to disseminate knowledge and issue fatwas" (3), and we will discuss this matter in detail - if Allah wills - in the chapter on ijtihad and taqlid.

... For this reason, we find al-Shatibi in this type of objectives (maqasid) to be thorough and meticulous, discussing the issue of legislation for the interests of the servants, and relating it to the principle of obedience and servitude to Allah. He discusses the issue of changes in people's customs with the variation of times and places, clarifying that this has no effect on the legal rulings, and he discusses the issue of adhering to the text or following meanings, indicating that following meanings must not deviate from the text under any circumstances.

... The volume of al-Shatibi's discussions in this type of objectives amounts to the total of his discussions in the three previous types, and it occupies - approximately - half of the second part of the book al-Muwafaqat.
