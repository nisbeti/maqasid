The complement, in as much as it is a complement to an essential, necessary, or enhancing aspect, must be considered a condition, which is that it does not invalidate the original. The invalidation of the original results in the invalidation of the complement. Al-Shatibi gives examples of this, including jihad, which is essential for the preservation of the religion. He says: "Jihad is necessary, and the ruler in it is necessary, and justice in it is a complement to necessity. If the complement returns to invalidate the original, it is not considered. Therefore, the command for jihad with unjust rulers came from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), as well as the command to pray behind wicked rulers, for in abandoning that, one abandons the Sunnah of the congregation, and the congregation is one of the required symbols of the religion, and justice complements that requirement, and the original is not invalidated by the complement." (1)
With the invalidation of the original, the complement is invalidated, whereas with the invalidation of the complement, the original is not invalidated, even though it may be affected in some way. Al-Shatibi considers enhancements to be like the complement to necessities, and both are like the complement to essentials. Thus, the Sharia is based on the preservation of necessities. He says: "The interests of religion and the world are based on the preservation of five matters... If religion is absent, the expected reward will not be realized; if the obligated person is absent, there will be no one to practice religion; if reason is absent, the practice of religion will cease; if progeny is absent, there will be no continuity in the usual manner; and if wealth is absent, there will be no sustenance." (2) This means that the absence of necessities leads to the absence of needs and enhancements. Necessities, along with others, are like the described with the attribute; they cannot exist without each other, while the latter can exist without the former.
