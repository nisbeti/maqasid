And the principle: "Actions are judged by their intentions" is acknowledged by al-Shatibi in the same sense mentioned by the jurists in the previous discussion, even though he does not state it explicitly. He said: "Indeed, actions are based on intentions, and the objectives are considered in transactions related to acts of worship and customary practices, and the evidence for this meaning is not limited. It suffices for you that the objectives differentiate between what is customary and what is worship, and in acts of worship, between what is obligatory and what is non-obligatory, and in customary practices, between the obligatory, the recommended, the permissible, the disliked, the prohibited, the valid, the invalid, and other rulings. A single action may be intended for one purpose and thus be an act of worship, while if intended for another purpose, it would not be so; rather, it may be intended for one thing and thus be an act of faith, while intended for another thing, it may be disbelief, such as prostrating to Allah or to an idol. Furthermore, when an action is associated with intention, it is subject to the legal rulings, and when it is devoid of intention, none of these rulings apply to it, such as the actions of a sleeping person, an inattentive person, or a madman" (1). He also said: "All actions that fall under choice do not become acts of worship except with the intention for that. As for what is established for worship, such as prayer and pilgrimage, there is no doubt about it, while customary actions do not become acts of worship except through intentions" (2).
