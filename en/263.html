Al-Shatibi clearly explained his concept of public interest (maslaha) and harm (mafsada), stating that it is subordinate to the Sharia and not something that is followed. This is not in line with what the earlier scholars considered regarding interests and harms, although al-Ghazali did point to this meaning when he said: "As for public interest, it originally refers to bringing about benefit and averting harm, and we do not mean by that that bringing about benefit and repelling harm are the objectives of creation and the welfare of creation in achieving their objectives. Rather, we mean by public interest the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, which are five: to protect their religion, their lives, their intellect, their lineage, and their wealth. Anything that entails the preservation of these five foundations is a public interest, and anything that undermines these foundations is a harm, and repelling it is a public interest" (1). By adopting the path of analogy and the strange and remote analogy, he contradicts what al-Shatibi establishes, and it is considered that he justifies public interest in the sense that he stated in this text that he does not mean. This is after incorporating this meaning into the objectives of the legislator.

What we mentioned about al-Ghazali applies to other jurists who consider public interests, such as al-Juwayni, al-Amidi, al-Razi, al-Qarafi, Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam, and others.

This meaning makes public interest—at least theoretically—something that is followed by the Sharia rather than subordinate to it, which leads to complications in the idea of justifying actions based on public interests. From this perspective, al-Shatibi, in his work on al-Muwafaqat, aimed to refine the science of usul al-fiqh.

Therefore, after establishing that public interests and harms are considered in relation to the sustenance of worldly life for the afterlife, and that they relate to enhancement and deprecation, he proceeded to critique or reject some opinions in this field. He rejected al-Razi's statement that the original ruling regarding benefits is permissibility and regarding harms is prohibition, as clarified in the previous pages.
