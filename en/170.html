Al-Shatibi has provided many examples in his book that illustrate his inductive method in deriving conclusions; here is one of them: He says, "The ummah, indeed all religious communities, have agreed that the Sharia was established to preserve the five necessities: religion, life, lineage, wealth, and intellect. This knowledge is as certain to the ummah as the necessities themselves, and we do not have a specific proof for this, nor is there a particular origin that we can refer to. Rather, we have recognized its compatibility with the Sharia through a collection of evidence that cannot be confined to a single category. If it were to rely on something specific, it would normally require specification, and the people of consensus would have to refer to it, which is not the case. This is because each of these individually is speculative, and it is not necessary in the case of moral or other forms of tawatur (transmission) that a single report be the one that provides knowledge over all other reports; likewise, it is not necessary here, as all the evidence equally contributes to the inference of speculation if considered in isolation."(1)

(1) Al-Shatibi, al-Muwafaqat, 1/15.
