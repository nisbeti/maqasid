If it is assumed regarding commands and prohibitions that the intention of the legislator is to preserve wisdom or public interest (maslaha), even if this leads to neglecting the form of the command or prohibition, this is not the case. Rather, it returns to the fact that some forms have meanings that are limited to the literal sense, while others are metaphorical. Employing the metaphor instead of the literal meaning is not neglecting the text or the form; rather, it is the very essence of employing them. However, what should be focused on is how to determine the intended meaning: the literal or the metaphorical. This is done by applying both particulars and universals, along with current and contextual indicators.
He says: "Commands and prohibitions are of two types: explicit and implicit. As for the explicit, it has two perspectives: one is from the standpoint of its essence, where no consideration is given to a beneficial reason. This is the perspective of one who treats the mere form as a matter of pure devotion without any reasoning, so there is no difference in this view between one command and another, nor between one prohibition and another" (1).
