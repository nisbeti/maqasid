Indeed, those who reject reasoning based on the appropriate description rely on the fact that reasoning requires evidence; a description does not establish a cause unless it appears in one of the legal pathways for causation, and this is not a matter of dispute. However, they believe that the appropriateness is not a pathway for causation, meaning it is not evidence for it but rather a condition for it. If the appropriate description does not have a legal evidence indicating its validity or nullification, then it is considered unsubstantiated and has no proof.

As for those who reason based on the appropriate description, they rely on the idea that appropriateness in itself is a pathway for causation. The fact that a description is appropriate is evidence of its causative nature, provided that there is no evidence indicating that it is nullified. Since this claim requires evidence, its proponents have sought to establish that the default in rulings is reasoning, and to make this claim a foundational principle (qawa'id) of jurisprudence. If a legal evidence indicates the causative nature of a certain description, then it is the cause. If we do not find evidence for that, then reasoning must be based on some description even if it does not appear in a pathway, based on that principle. Thus, the absence of reasoning contradicts the default. From here, the legitimacy of reasoning based on the appropriate description arises, and appropriateness becomes a pathway for causation.

Here, the disagreement has shifted to a dispute over this principle, where those who accept it have built upon it, and those who reject it have dismissed the consideration that the reasoners have proposed.

From here, al-Shatibi came to establish the principle of reasoning with absolute certainty, and to lay down a new methodology for how to consider the description, and consequently for how to regard the description as an objective and a cause. This is the methodology of beneficial induction - in his view - which will be clarified and detailed in its appropriate context.
