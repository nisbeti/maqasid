Likewise, the rulings that the legislator has linked to custom (urf), where the intended meaning of custom here refers to the terms or quantities that are commonly recognized, such as saying: this commodity is for a thousand. Is it a thousand lira, dollar, or dinar? Or if it is a dinar, is it an Iraqi, Jordanian, or gold dinar? And similarly, the meanings of the terms used in contracts and transactions. And like the estimation of expenses or the estimation of wages or the dowry or the price. These matters may change according to places, times, people, or conditions, and the rulings are based on these terms and estimations. Thus, although it may be said that there is a change in the rulings, it is not a change based on time or place, but rather a change in reality and conditions. For example, if the law prescribes for a divorced woman a maintenance equivalent, then what changes is the amount of maintenance, which depends on the condition of the divorced woman, and this is not a change in the ruling, as the ruling is that she has maintenance equivalent to her, and this is established.
... For example, the prohibition of carrion in normal circumstances and its permissibility in cases of necessity is not a change in the rulings, as its prohibition is established in that case, and its permissibility is established in the other case. Similarly, it is said about wine: it is forbidden, but if it turns into vinegar, it becomes permissible; this is not a change or reversal in the rulings, but rather the prohibition of wine is established and the permissibility of vinegar is established. Accordingly, the legal rulings are fixed and permanent.
... Therefore, it is not correct to say: the change of rulings is not denied by the change of time and place (1). If the change is restricted to the meanings mentioned above, it then becomes related to the bases of the rulings, that is, to changes in the realities or conditions that are part of the action to which the ruling pertains.
