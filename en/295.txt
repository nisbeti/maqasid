The term "license" (rukhsah) is used in more than one meaning, but the intended and predominant meaning is that which corresponds to the exemption (azimah) in the rulings of legal status (ahkam al-wad‘), which is what the jurists (usuliyyun) derive from. This is the meaning that al-Shatibi employed in his discussion of licenses and exemptions as types of legal rulings, and in this sense, it is: "what has been legislated for a difficult excuse as an exception to a general principle that necessitates prohibition, while being limited to the places of need therein" (1).
It can also be used in other meanings mentioned by al-Shatibi (2), among which we will mention what concerns us here. He says: "The term 'license' may refer to what has been alleviated from this nation in terms of severe obligations and burdensome actions, as indicated by His saying, Exalted is He: { Our Lord, and lay not upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us }" (3) (4). He mentioned some evidence that indicates the principle of lifting hardships. In this sense, the concession (tarakhus) refers back to a definitive general principle from the essential needs (al-usul al-hajiyyah), and not in the sense that the jurists commonly use.
... The first technical definition refers to the licenses explicitly stated by the Sharia, which are permissible to use as soon as their cause exists, even if the hardship is absent, such as the license for shortening (qasr) or combining (jam‘) prayers during travel. These licenses are specific, and thus it was said about them, "as an exception to a general principle," and therefore they cannot be analogized and are limited to their specified instances.
... As for the second definition, it is what al-Shatibi intended by concession (tarakhus) or alleviation in the presence of hardship or burdensome difficulties.
