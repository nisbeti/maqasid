The position of al-Shatibi is that the Sunnah does not bring forth legislation that has no basis in the Qur'an. He faces objections that there are many things in the Sunnah that have no basis in the Book, so he mentions these objections to refute them and to affirm his position. He cited examples of what is objected to, saying: "Induction has shown that there are countless things in the Sunnah that are not mentioned in the Noble Qur'an, such as the prohibition of marrying a woman to her aunt or her maternal aunt, the prohibition of domestic donkeys, and all creatures with fangs from the predatory animals, the freeing of captives, and that a Muslim should not be killed for a disbeliever."
He responded to the issue of the prohibition of marrying a woman to her aunt or maternal aunt by saying: "Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, has prohibited the combining of a mother and her daughter in marriage and between two sisters, and it has come in the Qur'an: { And lawful for you is what is beyond that } (2). Thus, the prohibition by him (peace and blessings be upon him) against marrying a woman to her aunt or maternal aunt is based on analogy (qiyas) because the reason for which the combining of those is condemned is present here. It may be narrated in this hadith: 'If you do that, you will sever your ties of kinship.' The reasoning indicates the basis of the analogy."
