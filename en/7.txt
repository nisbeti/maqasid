I have structured this book or this study with an introduction, followed by a definition of al-Shatibi and his era, then ten chapters and a brief conclusion, followed by indexes of the noble verses and the honorable hadiths, sources and references, and then the contents of the book.
Chapter One: The Introduction to the Emergence of the Idea of the Objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia). It contains an introduction to the emergence of the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) and its development, along with three sections: the first on qiyas (analogy), the second on the pathways of the cause and its conditions, and the third on the pathway of the relationship and suitability, which are necessary for entering the discussions of usul al-fiqh, qiyas, and ta'leel (causal reasoning). In particular, the pathway of the relationship and suitability serves as a prelude to the idea of the objectives of the Sharia.
Chapter Two: The Emergence of the Idea of the Objectives of the Sharia. It contains a brief presentation of the emergence of the idea, followed by three sections on how it originated and developed among the scholars of usul before al-Shatibi, which forms a prelude to the idea of the objectives according to al-Shatibi.
Chapter Three: The Criteria for the Objectives According to al-Shatibi. It contains three sections, the first and second on the preliminaries and criteria, then the references and foundations that al-Shatibi deemed necessary for understanding his book (al-Muwafaqat), and the third section on the results and the concept of benefit (maslaha) and harm (mafsada) according to al-Shatibi.
Chapter Four: The Intention of the Legislator in Establishing the Sharia Initially and in Establishing it for Understanding. It discusses the types of the legislator's objectives, which are four, and the statements of al-Shatibi and his methodology in understanding the two types: the first being the establishment of the Sharia initially, and the second being its establishment for understanding.
Chapter Five: The Intention of the Legislator in Establishing the Sharia for Obligatory Acts and in the Subjection of the Obligated to Its Rulings. It discusses the statements of al-Shatibi and his methodology in understanding the two types: the third being the establishment of the Sharia for obligatory acts, and the fourth being the subjection of the obligated to the rulings of the Sharia.
Chapter Six: The Objectives of the Obligated. It contains a prelude and three sections explaining the impact of intention or objective in the actions of the obligated, the impact of agreement or disagreement between the intention of the legislator and the intention of the obligated in action, and an explanation of legal tricks (hiyal) and al-Shatibi's stance on them.
