This is the first aspect of looking at benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), which is that they do not exist in a pure form. As for the second aspect, which is the relation of the legal ruling to them, here there are either pure benefits or pure harms at all times. If the legal demand is for an action, it is for a benefit intended by the legislator. If this action is preceded by, accompanied by, or followed by harms, meaning what is considered harmful by the law, this relates to the fact that benefits and harms do not exist in a pure form in reality. However, these harms are not intended by the legislator with that demand, and their relation to that demand does not imply that they are acceptable or excusable in the legal sense. The existence of certain outcomes for specific rulings is not sufficient to indicate that they are benefits or harms; it is necessary to consider the benefit or harm through the examination of a large number of rulings. Therefore, the benefit established by law remains a benefit, and the same applies to the harm. The fact that harms are related to a desired action and benefits are related to a prohibited action does not change anything regarding the nature of the benefit as a benefit and the harm as a harm, nor does it indicate that benefits have been prohibited or that harms may become desired. This means that benefits and harms are pure in the law, even if they are not pure in terms of existence in reality. Therefore, al-Shatibi says: "The conclusion from this is that the benefits considered by law or the harms considered by law are pure and not mixed with any harms, neither little nor much. Even if it is imagined that they are mixed, they are not in reality so according to the law" (1). Examples of this include work and seeking sustenance for one's dependents, which is a benefit, despite the toil and effort involved; the legislator intended the benefits therein and did not intend the toil and effort. Going out for prayers or seeking knowledge or similar activities, even though there are abominations that are heard and seen along the way, does not change anything regarding what is a benefit and what is a harm. It does not change anything in the legal rulings, and one must avoid abominations as much as possible.
