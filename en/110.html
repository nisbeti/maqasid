Al-Ghazali said: "Istislah (public interest) is not a fifth root in itself, but whoever seeks istislah has legislated just as whoever deems something good has legislated" (1). He also said: "This is one of the illusory roots, for whoever thinks it is a fifth root has erred, as we have referred the public interest to the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), and the objectives of the Sharia are known through the Book (Qur'an), the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet), and consensus (ijma). Thus, any public interest that does not return to the preservation of an objective derived from the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus, and is among the strange interests that do not align with the actions of the Sharia, is invalid and discarded. Whoever resorts to it has legislated just as whoever deems something good has legislated" (2).

Similarly, it is well-known among the Hanafis that they reject and dismiss every method of analogy as a root. They reject the analogy even if it is considered valid, as they require an effect. Ibn Humam al-Din al-Iskandari, the Hanafi, said: "This category known as istislah (public interest) is rejected, as there is no evidence for its consideration, and it is a legal evidence, thus it must be rejected" (3).

It is well-known among their scholars that they say: "No evidence, no consideration." Al-Ansari said: "Istislah (public interest) is a proof according to Malik (may Allah have mercy on him), while the preferred view among the majority of scholars and jurists is its rejection. Our argument is: there is no evidence without consideration from the legislator, even if it follows the dictates of reason, and this cannot be accepted from one who advocates analogy" (4).

Ibn Qudamah al-Maqdisi said: "This is called istislah (public interest) and we do not call it qiyas (analogy) because qiyas refers to a specific root, and the correct view is that it is not a proof because it is known from the legislator that the preservation of blood is paramount in every way. Therefore, mutilation was not legislated even though it is the most effective deterrent and punishment, nor was killing for theft or drinking alcohol legislated. If a ruling is established for the sake of one of these interests, it is not known that the Sharia preserves that interest by establishing that ruling; it would be an imposition of the Sharia based on opinion and a ruling based on pure reason" (5).
