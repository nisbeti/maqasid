Based on this, every legislation or obligation brought by the Sunnah must be presented to the Book (Qur'an); if it aligns with the Book, it is valid, and if it does not align, its validity is not established. He said: "Yes, it is permissible for the Sunnah to come with something that does not contradict or agree, but rather with what is silent about in the Qur'an, unless there is evidence to the contrary, which is what has been translated in this matter; at that point, it is necessary for every hadith to agree with the Book of Allah" (1).

However, it is worth noting that the requirement for the Sunnah to agree with the Book, and that the Sunnah does not introduce an addition that is not an explanation or detail of what is in the Book, pertains only to the rulings of obligation, that is, commands, prohibitions, and permissions. Just as there is no ambiguous or unclear verse in the Qur'an that does not lead to an obligation, it is permissible for the Sunnah to come with something that has no basis in the Book, provided it does not lead to an obligation. He said: "Where we said that the Book indicates the Sunnah and that the Sunnah has come only to clarify it, this is in relation to commands, prohibitions, and permissions, or what necessitates that, and in general, what relates to the actions of the obligated in terms of obligation. As for what falls outside of that from the reports of what has happened or what will happen that does not relate to a command, prohibition, or permission, it is of two types: one is that it occurs in the Sunnah as an interpretation of the Qur'an; this is undoubtedly a clarification of it" (2). Then he said: "The second is that it does not occur as an interpretation nor does it contain a meaning of an obligatory belief or action, so it is not necessary for it to have a basis in the Qur'an because it is an additional matter beyond the sites of obligation, and the Qur'an was revealed for that. Therefore, if the Sunnah goes beyond that, there is no harm" (3).
