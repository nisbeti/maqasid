If it becomes evident that there is a worldly benefit for the obligated person (mukallaf) in either performing or refraining from that action, and if this benefit has been established as a definitive, universal meaning that the legislator intended by enacting that ruling, then it becomes a definitive principle. It is akin to a text, and achieving it with the intention of obedience is akin to the primary intention. If that anticipated benefit—whether definitively or presumably—has not been definitively established as an objective of the legislator in enacting that ruling, it is considered the secondary objective or the dependent objective. That is, it is dependent on the primary objective. According to what al-Shatibi states in his introductions, this second meaning is speculative, thus it is not considered a principle, nor does it hold significance in legislation. However, it cannot be entirely dismissed, as it may be subject to an inductive reasoning that renders it definitive. Therefore, it is examined: if it strengthens adherence to the primary intention, it is considered; if it weakens or opposes the primary intention, it is rejected. The primary intention is worship, and the secondary intention is considered if it serves as a means to the primary intention. Hence, it is termed dependent or additional. For example, the primary intention of seeking knowledge and learning is action. He said: "Any knowledge that does not lead to action has no basis in the Sharia that indicates its commendation" (1). He said: "The Sharia has come for the purpose of worship, which is the objective of the mission of the Prophets (peace be upon them)" (2). He said: "Indeed, the proofs of monotheism have come in this manner in the course of the Qur'an: { except as a reminder } (3), except for this, which is clear in that worshiping Allah is the objective of knowledge, and the verses on this meaning are countless" (4). He said: "The essence of knowledge is action; otherwise, knowledge is a loan and of no benefit" (5). He then said: "No one denies the virtue of knowledge in general except an ignorant person, but it has a primary intention and a secondary intention. The primary intention is what has been mentioned earlier, while the secondary intention is what the majority mention regarding its possessor being noble, even if he is not originally so. And the ignorant person is base, even if he is originally noble...
