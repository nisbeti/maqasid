What this text means is that the five necessities are the primary objectives (maqasid). Other objectives are considered thereafter. If they are such that the necessity cannot be established without them, then they are among the necessities in that category which cannot be established without them. For instance, jihad preserves the religion, and thus it is a necessity for the preservation of the religion, as are the five pillars, the killing of the apostate, the supreme leadership (imama al-uzma), and the prohibition of killing and retribution, which are essential for the preservation of life; therefore, they are necessities in this category, namely the preservation of life. This applies to other categories as well. However, if that category of necessities is not negated or lost by the absence of a particular objective, then this objective is not considered a necessity but rather one of its complements, which may be classified as needs (hajiyat) or enhancements (tahsinat). For example, cleanliness in acts of worship does not undermine the essence of preserving the religion, nor does shortening prayers or breaking the fast while traveling. Similarly, drinking a drop of alcohol does not invalidate the essence of preserving the intellect, so it is not classified as a necessity. The same principle applies to the complements of necessities to determine their classification as needs, enhancements, or complements of either. All of this is based on the examination of the legal texts (nusus) and their meanings. What can be established through examination is considered an objective, while what cannot be established may either serve an objective, thus being a branch related to it, or negate an objective, in which case it is rejected, or it may neither negate nor serve, in which case it is suspended and not considered a branch but rather a detail. A careful consideration of this previous text summarizes this matter from its methodology.

(1) The five necessities refer to the fundamental objectives of Islamic law that are essential for the well-being of individuals and society.
(2) The five pillars of Islam are the foundational acts of worship that define a Muslim's faith and practice.
(3) The supreme leadership refers to the highest authority in the Muslim community, responsible for governance and maintaining order.
(4) The prohibition of killing and retribution is a fundamental principle in Islamic law that protects human life and ensures justice.
(5) Cleanliness in acts of worship is an important aspect of Islamic practice, emphasizing the need for physical and spiritual purity.
(6) Shortening prayers and breaking the fast while traveling are allowances provided in Islamic law to facilitate worship for those who are on a journey.
(7) The examination of legal texts involves a careful analysis of the Qur'an and Sunnah to derive legal rulings and understand their implications.
