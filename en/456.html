Al-Shatibi provides examples of this, including the case of Qudamah ibn Muz'oon, who was one of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) and drank alcohol, becoming intoxicated. Evidence was presented against him regarding this. Al-Shatibi said: "Omar said: 'O Qudamah, I will punish you.' He replied: 'By Allah, if I had drunk as they say, you would not have the right to punish me.' Omar asked: 'Why not?' He said: 'Because Allah says: { There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have eaten, if they fear Allah and believe and do righteous deeds, and then fear Allah and believe and do righteous deeds, and then fear Allah and do good; and Allah loves the doers of good }' (1). Omar replied: 'You have misunderstood the interpretation, O Qudamah. If you fear Allah, you will avoid what Allah has prohibited.' (2) And he said: 'Omar then said: "Will you not respond to his statement?" Ibn Abbas said: "These verses were revealed as an excuse for those who came before and as a proof against those who remain. The excuse for those who came before is that they met Allah before alcohol was prohibited for them, and the proof against those who remain is that Allah says: { O you who have believed, indeed, intoxicants, gambling, [sacrificing on] stone alters [to other than Allah], and divining arrows are but defilement from the work of Satan, so avoid it that you may be successful }' (3), and then he recited to the end of the other verse. If he is among those who have believed and done righteous deeds, then feared [Allah] and believed, and then feared [Allah] and did good, then Allah has prohibited drinking alcohol.' Omar said: 'You have spoken the truth.' " (4)

Al-Shatibi mentioned a similar incident and then said: "In these two hadiths, there is an indication that neglecting the reasons for revelation leads to deviating from the intended meaning of the verses." (5)
