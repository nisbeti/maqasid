One example of the principle in necessities is the compilation of the Qur'an. He said: "The compilation of the Qur'an was something that was not addressed during his time (peace and blessings be upon him), but when disagreements about the Qur'an arose and became widespread to the extent that one would say to another: 'I disbelieve in what you read,' the compilation of the Qur'an became obligatory; otherwise, it would follow that examining every incident that did not occur in the earlier times would be considered an innovation (bid'a), which is unanimously false. However, such examination falls under the category of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) that aligns with the principles of the Sharia, even if there is no specific text supporting it, and this is what is referred to as public interest (maslaha). Everything that the righteous predecessors introduced in this regard does not differ from it in any way" (1). The evidence here is in his statement: "The compilation of the Qur'an became obligatory," as it was previously permissible, but a situation arose that put the preservation of the religion at risk unless the Qur'an was compiled. He said: "There is no text from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) regarding what they did in this matter, but they saw it as a public interest that aligns with the actions of the Sharia, undoubtedly, as it pertains to the preservation of the Sharia, and the command to preserve it is well known, and to prevent the means to disagreement regarding its foundation, which is the Qur'an, and it is known that there is a prohibition against disagreement in that, with no further elaboration" (2).
