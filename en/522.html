As for the necessity of achieving the relevant criteria (tahqiq al-manat) in every incident, even if its ruling is known, he said: "Actions do not occur in existence in an absolute manner, but rather they occur in a specific and defined manner, so the ruling does not apply to them except after knowing that this specific case falls under that absolute or that general ruling" (1). When this reasoning ceases, knowledge of the rulings is impossible, and thus the obligation is lifted. He said: "The obligation to act is lifted from the one seeking a ruling when the mufti is absent if he has no knowledge of it either through valid reasoning or through imitation" (2). This reasoning does not practically cease because it is attainable and only requires rational consideration and the necessary information that can be derived from the reasoning of the predecessors. He said: "This reasoning is necessary in every time, as the obligation cannot be established without it. If the obligation were to be assumed while the possibility of this reasoning ceasing exists, it would be an obligation to do the impossible, which is not permissible in Sharia just as it is not permissible rationally" (3).

As for the reasoning that can cease, it is of three types. He said: "The reasoning that can cease is of three types: the first is what is called the refinement of the relevant criteria (tanqih al-manat)" (4). Then he said: "The second is what is called the derivation of the relevant criteria (takhrij al-manat)" (5). These two types are useful in claiming the causes and in their examination and validation or rejection. Then he said: "The third is a type of achieving the relevant criteria" (6), and what he means is the specific achievement of the relevant criteria, which has been previously explained.
