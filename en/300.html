... Among the conditions is that the hardship must be actual or certain to occur; it is not sufficient for it to be based on conjectures and unproven estimates, for this is an illusion that has no validity. He says: "Indeed, conjectures and unproven estimates fall under the category of illusions, which are varied. Likewise, the desires of the souls estimate things that have no reality; thus, it is correct to adhere to the principle of determination (azimah) except in cases of severe and debilitating hardship, for patience is preferable unless it leads to a detriment to the intellect or religion of a person. The reality of this is that one cannot bear patience, for one is only commanded to be patient if one is capable of it. You will find through observation that severe hardship does not have its illusion attached to it; rather, its ruling is weaker based on the fact that illusion is often not truthful in many situations. Therefore, hardship is not real... and it is preferable to remain with the principle" (1). That is, with determination; however, if the occurrence of this hardship is uncertain between being realized and being conjectured, and it is likely to occur according to the course of customs, then this is a domain for the jurists.

... Among the conditions is that the determination of hardship should not align with desires. He says: "The principles of the Sharia are opposed to desires... and often hardships arise and increase due to opposing desires, and following desires is contrary to following the Sharia, and one who follows desires finds everything difficult, whether it is inherently difficult or not" (2).
