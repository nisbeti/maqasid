Then he said: "The reason is only prescribed in relation to a specific, imposed, known matter, not absolutely" (1). And he said: "Rather, we know that many of the reasons were legislated for matters that arise from them and were not legislated for matters, even if they arise from them and are contingent upon them... So when we know that it is legislated for specific matters, what is unknown to be legislated for it is unknown in ruling, thus the legitimacy of proceeding is not valid until the ruling is known" (2). Then he said: "If this is established and a causation is clarified that we do not know whether it is what the Legislator intended with the prescribed causation or not, it is necessary to pause until the ruling on it is known. For this, there is a principle by which what is intended by the Legislator from the consequences of causes and what is not prescribed can be clarified, and it is mentioned in the Book of Objectives (maqasid)" (3). Accordingly, al-Shatibi believes that achieving the legal objective is only valid through the legal method that the Legislator has established. And although the method is legal, it is not valid to adopt or utilize it to achieve a legal objective unless the Legislator has established that method for this objective. It is not valid to prevent theft except by cutting off the hand, and the punishment cannot be replaced by another. And cutting off the hand is only valid for what it has been legislated for. Likewise, applying jihad for other than what it has been legislated for is not valid. And it is not valid, for the sake of establishing the ruling by what Allah has revealed, which is a legal objective, to adopt means or methods that have not been legislated for it, such as charitable institutions, for example, even if they are permissible, they are not prescribed for this objective. Or like participating in governance through disbelief, for this was not established for this purpose, let alone that it is prohibited by law.
