He says: "The fact that the public interest (maslaha) is an interest intended by the ruling and that the harm (mafsada) is likewise a harm that pertains to the legislator is a matter that does not allow for rational consideration based on the principle of negating the good and the bad. If the legislator has enacted a ruling for a specific interest, then he is the one who establishes it as an interest; otherwise, it could rationally be that it is not so, since all things are equal in relation to their original state, and reason has no jurisdiction over their goodness or badness. Therefore, the existence of the public interest is from the legislator, such that reason affirms it and the soul finds reassurance in it. The interests, in their essence as interests, have been concluded to be matters of worship, and what is built upon acts of worship can only be worshipful" (1).
It may be said that many rulings observe the consideration of the interests of the servants in the sense of what they are inclined to according to their senses, nature, and habit, or that many rulings have objectives that align with the aims and purposes of the servants. Al-Shatibi's response here is that the public interest is what the legislator has sought or what was intended for it; if this coincides with the worldly interests of the servants, it is incidental, not fundamental. This is a grace from Allah, Exalted is He, to encourage obedience and worship. He says: "The rulings of the Sharia do not escape the five categories; as for obligation and prohibition, their opposition to the inclination that falls under choice is evident, as it is said: 'Do such and such, whether you have an interest in it or not.' And 'Do not do such and such, whether you have an interest in it or not.' If the obligated person has an interest that aligns with the desire that motivates the command or prohibition, it is incidental, not fundamental. As for the other categories, although their apparent nature falls under the choice of the obligated person, they have entered under the legislator's choice, and thus they revert to being removed from his choice" (2).
