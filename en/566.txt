Among the examples of necessities (hajiyat) is the liability of craftsmen. He said: "The rightly guided caliphs ruled that craftsmen should be held liable. Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) said: 'People cannot be right without that.' The rationale behind this necessity is that people have a need for craftsmen, and they are often absent from the goods, and they are mostly negligent and fail to safeguard them. If their liability were not established, despite the pressing need to employ them, it would lead to one of two outcomes: either a complete abandonment of contracting craftsmen, which would be burdensome for the people, or they would work without being held liable, claiming loss and destruction, leading to the loss of property, reduced caution, and the possibility of betrayal. Thus, the interest lies in establishing liability" (1). This example regarding the public interests (masalih mursala) is a subject of debate and has led to criticism of the principle, as it resulted in a false condition, which is the liability of the craftsman (2). The truth is that this does not pertain to the principle but rather to the disagreement over the detailed evidence. His statement here about the liability of craftsmen does not indicate that this matter is forbidden and permitted by the principle; rather, it is originally permissible, and due to the need to alleviate hardship, it became obligatory. Note his statement at the beginning of the example that the rightly guided caliphs did this; if this is authentic, it serves as a legal proof, as none of the Companions denied it. However, this is not authentic, and what he attributed to Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) is not established (3).

(1) The same source, 2/378.
(2) It is mentioned in al-Mabsut by al-Sarakhsi: "From Amr ibn Shu'ayb from his father from his grandfather that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'Whoever deposits a trust and it is lost, he is not liable.' And it was reported from Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said: 'There is no liability on a shepherd or on a trustee.' 11/109. Al-Shafi'i said in al-Umm: 'The trustee is not liable unless he violates.' 3/245, and the hadith was narrated by Ibn Majah in the chapter on deposits without the phrase 'and it is lost.'
(3) Al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) does not find an excuse for one who considers the hired worker liable because it contradicts the hadith, unless he says, for example: 'The trustworthy person is one to whom you have given something willingly, trusting in his honesty, not for a fee for anything of what you have given, and giving me this fee distinguishes him from the trustworthy person who took what he was entrusted with without compensation.' Al-Umm, 4/37-38.
