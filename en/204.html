The answer is that al-Shatibi avoids this response and rejects it, because if it initially appears that he is patching a gap in the methodology, in reality, he is destroying more than he is building. For if we find a public interest (maslaha) in the concession and claim that it serves a general principle, the law has stipulated that the original ruling is better: { and to fast is better for you } (1). Al-Shatibi believes that adhering to the original rulings (azaim) is preferable to taking concessions (rukhas). He stated in his argument that concessions are akin to permissibility in the sense of lifting hardship, not in the sense of offering choice: "And the evidence that choice is not intended in these matters is that the majority or all say: whoever does not utter a word of disbelief under coercion is rewarded and in the highest ranks" (2).
