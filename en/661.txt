The meanings of rulings do not exist; rather, there are meanings for the words and sentences that these words and sentences indicate in any type of indication(1). If the meanings are known, there is no point in searching for meanings for them, nor is it correct to say that these meanings are intended by the legislator, for if they were intended by him, he would have established a proof for them or set a sign for them. At that point, they would be among the indications of the texts, and there is no harm in allowing thought or imagination to roam regarding the reasons for the rulings, their causes, their objectives, and their wisdom, especially since there is something in the soul that drives one to this, leading to the assumption of meanings or rulings that one may find reassurance in or take delight in. However, it is not permissible in any case to attribute any legal value to these assumptions, nor to ascribe them to Allah - Exalted is He - by saying they are intended by Him, or that they are a cause for His ruling, for such assumptions are mere fantasies and are not based on any legal source, and since there is no evidence for them, they hold no significance.
Al-Shatibi does not disagree with this; rather, he affirms it. He does not accept legal conjecture (dhann shar'i) at all unless it is based on a definitive meaning (ma'na qat'i). Such justifications for rulings are mere conjectures that hold no weight. However, if the rulings converge on a meaning and there is a comprehensive examination (istiqra'), then the meaning becomes definitive, and it serves as a benefit (maslaha), a cause ('illa), a principle (asl), and an objective (maqsad), all in a definitive manner.
The objection is how this moral transmission, or the corroboration from individual rulings on this meaning, will occur, as there is no legal value for this meaning in any ruling, since rulings do not indicate meanings, and these meanings are not among the implications of the texts.

(1) The linguistic indication is confined to three: correspondence, implication, and entailment. The first: the indication of the word on the entirety of what it was designated for. The second: its indication of a part of the named thing. The third: its indication of what is outside the named thing but necessary for it. See: al-Zarkashi, al-Bahr al-Muhit, 1/417 and 1/420.
