Indeed, these outcomes do not prevent marriage, and if we were to consider the ruling based on this outcome in our time, it would lead to the prohibition of marriage, which is incorrect because it contradicts the established ruling supported by evidence. His statement: "and that is not correct" means that it is not permissible to invalidate or change a legal ruling under the pretext of outcomes. Furthermore, if we were to prohibit marriage, which is a legal ruling, we would fall into greater evils than those of these outcomes. The outcomes are considered in light of the Sharia, and we do not change rulings based on them; rather, we discover the Sharia's consideration of them. Since marriage is permissible, and every permissible matter is a benefit, its invalidation is a harm, whether we realize it or not. According to al-Shatibi, it is a fundamental principle, whether necessary, urgent, or complementary. Thus, if the consideration of these outcomes implies changing the ruling, it leads to the invalidation of a principle, which is incorrect. His statement: "Similarly, seeking knowledge, if it involves hearing disapproved matters, attending funerals, and performing religious duties if they can only be fulfilled by witnessing what is not acceptable, does not remove these matters from their principles" means that what has been said regarding marriage applies to seeking knowledge and the other mentioned matters. All of these are particulars that have been established as permissible by evidence. The fact that they may lead to something prohibited, such as viewing nakedness or hearing what is not permissible to listen to, does not invalidate the legality of these actions, because what invalidates or does not invalidate is the Sharia, and the Sharia has not invalidated them despite the existence of these outcomes. The Sharia has considered the outcomes, and with its consideration of the outcomes, thus were its rulings; they should not be tampered with under the pretext of outcomes, and it is permissible to engage in them with the possibility of their outcomes. This principle, that is, the consideration of outcomes, does not change a legal ruling; it neither makes the unlawful lawful nor prohibits the lawful. If a servant engages in these actions, they must avoid the prohibitions that may result from these actions to the best of their ability. His statement: "Because they are the foundations of religion and the principles of benefits, which is understood from the objectives of the Legislator" means that the foundations are not invalidated by particulars, because the foundations are general.
