Among its examples is the permissibility of combining the two prescribed prayers (salat) due to hardship. The ruling on the permissibility of combining during travel supports the description of hardship, and the permissibility of combining them in a settled place during rain also supports the description of hardship. The hardship of rain and the hardship of travel are two types of hardship that fall under the category of hardship, and considering hardship here is a consideration of the category of the description without evidence, but only through the extrapolation of rulings. It has been said that this example has its own complexities(1).

It is also exemplified by the exemption from the two rak'ahs (units of prayer) for the traveler due to hardship, and the exemption from making up prayers for the menstruating woman due to hardship. Thus, the consideration of general hardship here is a consideration of the category of the description. This example also has its own complexities(2).

C - The strange (ghareeb):

This is a suitable description, whose consideration has appeared in a single ruling. The scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) from the Shafi'i school have deemed it valid for reasoning, while the Hanafi school has rejected it. Al-Amidi said: "The legislator must have considered the particular description in a particular ruling without showing the consideration of its essence in the generality of that ruling in another agreed-upon origin, nor its genus in that same ruling, nor its genus in its genus, nor has it been indicated that it is a cause by text or consensus, neither explicitly nor implicitly, such as the meaning of intoxication" (3). He then said: "If we were to assume the absence of texts indicating that intoxication is a cause, it would not be considered by text either, and this is the suitable strange which is disputed among the analogists, and some have denied it, though their denial is not well-founded as it leads to suspicion regarding reasoning" (4).
