And his statement above: "And the regulated is the first" means that if some of those obligated (mukallaf) show an intention towards the prohibited, they are not judged by that, but rather by what is original or the original intention, which is predominant by custom and habit in society.
Then he said: "If the follower becomes predominant in intention and precedes in the custom of some times until what was originally the case becomes like the nonexistent, then the ruling changes. I do not think this can happen universally, but if it is assumed to happen, the ruling changes. The principle remains established as it was set in the Sharia, and if it does not happen but the intention towards the follower is prevalent, then the original consideration is what is intended like it by custom. The matter is generally disputed regarding the two possibilities, and the principle of means (dhara'i) is also based on the precedence of intention towards the prohibited" (1).
These texts clarify al-Shatibi's intent regarding the intended follower; it is not specifically the intention of the obligated person but rather the intention of the general public regarding the action. If the custom in society changes such that the original intention of someone buying grapes is that they buy them for wine, and for someone buying weapons that they buy them to commit robbery, then the ruling changes accordingly to the change in intention, and the follower becomes the followed. The considered intention is the original intention, and the ruling follows it. Al-Shatibi's statement: "And the principle of means is also based on the precedence of intention towards the followed" means that this reversal in ruling is supported by the principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i). This is the meaning of the second criterion for applying the principle, which is the emergence of intention towards that in society such that it becomes the original and everything else is the exception. What strengthens the consideration of this criterion for him is his statement: "For the principle of means is strengthened here since the intention towards the prohibited has been established" (2).
