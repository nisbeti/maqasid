This argument is invalid even if there are many such texts, because it is an incomplete induction that does not encompass every action or ruling. Just as there are many texts that indicate intention, there are also many texts in which intention is not apparent. Furthermore, the texts that al-Shatibi presented do not indicate causes, and it is not rational to link them to the reasoning of rulings based on their objectives. Additionally, much of what al-Shatibi used as evidence can be argued in the opposite direction of what he intended.

Thus, al-Shatibi's proof regarding the principle of reasoning is refuted from several angles:

First: This induction is incomplete, so it does not provide any benefit, nor does it achieve the desired outcome unless supported by a textual evidence. If such evidence exists, there is no need for the incomplete induction.

Second: The existence of many texts in which the legislator did not mention an intention contradicts the claim of induction, such as His saying, Exalted is He: { And when We said to the angels, "Prostrate to Adam" } (1), and His saying, Exalted is He: { And when Moses said to his people, "Indeed, Allah commands you to sacrifice a cow" } (2). And His saying, Exalted is He: { For those who take an oath, there is a waiting period of four months } (3), and His saying, Exalted is He: { And the divorced women wait for three menstrual periods } (4), and His saying, Exalted is He: { And Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest } (5). It is no more valid to interpret these texts as supporting an objective than it is to interpret those as negating it.
