It should be noted that he cites the hadith: "The killer does not inherit." The disagreement among the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) is regarding the consideration of the meaning of acting contrary to the intended purpose that the hadith supports. This meaning is not among the implications of the text of the hadith, while al-Shatibi discusses the application of the hadith itself, and his intent is that the idea of acting contrary to the intended purpose, if established definitively, would then allow for the application of the hadith because it would then refer back to a definitive meaning. We also observe that he considers this hadith to be an appropriate oddity, and we have previously explained that the scholars of usul who reason based on oddities regard the oddity as a description that is observed in a single ruling and does not have a witness, or has only one witness, and the term witness means a ruling that aligns with the meaning(1). Here, he has made the hadith odd, and it requires a definitive witness, meaning that the meaning of acting contrary to the intended purpose must be established definitively. Therefore, we found him in another place mentioning the principle of acting contrary to the intended purpose as a question with a tendency to accept it, as it has partial witnesses available to it, but it does not have the inductive reasoning that leads to certainty. He says: "But the question remains whether the causation should be considered as being contrary to the intention of the legislator in such a way that it does not result in what the cause intended, thus establishing the principle of acting contrary to the intended purpose, and the ruling is applied based on it if that intended purpose is determined, which is the implication of the hadith regarding the disinheritance of the killer, and the implication of the jurisprudence in the hadith prohibiting the gathering of the dispersed and the dispersing of the gathered out of fear of charity. Likewise, the inheritance of the divorced woman during illness, or the perpetuation of prohibition on one who marries during the waiting period, and many similar cases"(2). If acting contrary to the intended purpose were a universal principle that supports the meaning of the hadith "The killer does not inherit," then al-Shatibi's citation of the hadith as an example of the fourth category that does not have a universal principle supporting it would not be valid.
