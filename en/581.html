As for the necessity of preference (istihsan) due to necessity, it is stated: "Among them is what is established by necessity, which is the purification of ponds, wells, and vessels. For analogy (qiyas) negates the purity of these things after they have become impure because it is not possible to pour water on the pond or well to purify it. Likewise, the water entering the pond or springing from the well becomes impure upon contact with the impure, and the bucket also becomes impure upon contact with the water, thus returning to a state of impurity. Similarly, the vessel, if it does not have a hole at its bottom to let the water out when poured from above, will have the impure water collect at its bottom, so its purity cannot be established. However, they preferred to abandon the application of the analogy due to the necessity that requires it for the general public. And necessity has an effect in the abrogation of the ruling" (1).

Here, too, there is no analogy in the legal sense, but rather it is a discussion of reality or the basis, which is that the water has become impure and will not be purified. However, this ruling of the impurity of ponds, vessels, and wells is difficult for the people, so it is ruled to be pure due to necessity. Thus, the ruling of their impurity is specified by necessity, and necessity has its ruling and effect in Sharia. This is a type of preference, and it is evident that it is a specification. It is also evident that it is not a specification of analogy nor an application of the stronger of the two analogies. Although they have referred to the abandoned ruling as analogy and the applied ruling as preference, it is a naming they have given while knowing that there is no analogy or cause. However, there may be a resemblance between the application of the ruling in its various instances by general evidence and the extension of the ruling to different places through the cause. Likewise, the importance of the cause lies in its effect on the ruling; if the effect of a thing appears, it resembles the cause in that regard. Al-Bazdawi said: "Our aim here is to categorize the aspects of causes in relation to rulings. When the cause becomes a cause by its effect, we call that which weakens its effect analogy and that which strengthens its effect preference, meaning a preferred analogy. We give precedence to the latter even if it is hidden over the former even if it is apparent, for the consideration is for the strength of the effect rather than its appearance and clarity" (2).
