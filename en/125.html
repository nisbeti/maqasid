The appropriate meaning in deriving a specific ruling from a principle may be considered an analogy (qiyas), but the existence of other rulings that support that meaning makes it a foundational principle in legislation. This, according to those who accept it, is stronger than analogy. For this reason, al-Shatibi said: "Every legal principle that does not have a specific text to support it and is compatible with the actions of the Sharia and whose meaning is derived from its evidences is valid and can be relied upon, provided that this principle has become established through its collective evidences. Because the evidences do not necessarily have to indicate certainty about the ruling in isolation without the addition of others to them, as previously mentioned. This is because that is nearly impossible. This includes the unqualified reasoning (istidlal al-mursal) that Malik and al-Shafi'i relied upon, for although it does not have a specific principle to support the branch, it has a general principle that supports it, and a definitive general principle may equal a specific principle and may even surpass it depending on the strength or weakness of the specific principle" (1). Al-Juwayni said: "I take those causes as my refuge and make the arguments close to them, even if they are not their specific instances, so that they are as if they are principles. The reasoning is considered valid. Considering meaning by meaning is preferable to considering form by form in a comprehensive sense" (2).

Therefore, those who accept it responded to those who rejected it and said that it leads to reliance on reason and the wisdom of philosophers, stating: even if it does not have a supporting principle, it leads to reliance on reason and the wisdom of philosophers if we pursue it in an unregulated manner. If it is regulated by Sharia guidelines, then it is legitimate. These beneficial meanings, even if they do not have recognized meanings supporting them, are close to or similar to them. And even if they do not have specific principles supporting them, they are based on established foundational rulings, and even if those meanings are not established, their foundations are.
