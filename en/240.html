The determination of a person regarding benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) and the preference between them returns to two matters: the first is his characteristics and nature as a human being, which lead him to seek food, drink, shelter, wealth, pride, praise, security, and prosperity, and to aim at preserving his life and satisfying his desires, as well as protecting his children and family, and so on. He feels that these things are benefits or good, and their loss is a harm or evil. The second matter is what concepts are formed within a person, whether as a result of thought and conviction, such as someone who believes that gambling or cheating is forbidden, thus viewing it as a harm even though ownership is a benefit in itself, or that adultery is forbidden, leading him to judge it as a harm, or someone who does not believe in Islam or in resurrection and accountability, seeing gambling, usury, or adultery as a benefit. Or it may not be due to thought and conviction, but rather as a result of upbringing and environment, such as someone who grows up among Muslims and develops a sense of preserving honor and a revulsion towards adultery, and a disgust for pigs or carrion... or someone who grows up among non-Muslims and develops a veneration for idols and feels that destroying them is a harm, or does not feel disgust at kissing dogs or does not find adultery repugnant...

Based on this, people's perspectives on benefits and harms vary, and they contradict and differ in their preferences.

The second aspect from which the classification of objectives (maqasid) and the determination of their ranks arises is what is understood by one who has embraced Islam and realized that he is a servant of Allah, that Islam is Allah's law for all people, that there is no ruling except for Allah, and that it is not for a believing man or woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter, to have any choice in their affair. And that every legislation from anyone other than Allah is tyranny (taghut). Thus, he begins to understand Allah's objectives in legislation, and he starts to affirm what the Sharia has deemed good, and to condemn what it has deemed bad, to magnify what the Sharia has indicated is great, and to belittle what it has indicated is small, and so on. The judgment of what is a benefit and what is a harm thus becomes dependent on the Sharia, determining what is a benefit and what is a harm, and clarifying what is known (ma'ruf) and what is condemned (munkar), what is good and what is evil.
