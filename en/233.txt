He said: "As for the necessities, their meaning is that they are essential for the establishment of the interests of both religion and the world, such that if they are lost, the interests of the world will not proceed in a proper manner, but rather in corruption, chaos, and the loss of life. In the Hereafter, it results in the loss of salvation and bliss, and returning with manifest loss" (1). With this description, the necessities are defined by their specific entities. He states: "The total number of necessities is five: the preservation of religion, life, lineage, wealth, and intellect, and it has been said that they are to be observed in every community" (2). He also said: "As for the needs, their meaning is that they are required in terms of expansion and alleviating the constraints that generally lead to hardship and difficulty resulting from the loss of what is sought. If they are not observed, hardship and difficulty will generally befall the obligated, but it will not reach the level of the ordinary corruption expected in public interests." The public interests, that is, the necessities, and based on this definition, the principle of not imposing what is beyond one's capacity and the alleviation of hardship are foundational or essential rules.
