To clarify al-Shatibi's (may Allah have mercy on him) intention regarding the secondary meaning, he provided the example he mentioned. He said: "If an action occurs in existence for Zayd, such as standing up, then if every speaker wants to inform about Zayd's standing, he will achieve what he intends without any difficulty" (1). The meaning understood from the report here is the original meaning. He then said: "As for the second aspect, it is that which is specific to the Arabic tongue in that narration and that report, for every report in this aspect necessitates matters that serve that report" (2). He then said: "That is, you say at the beginning of the report, 'Zayd has stood up,' if there is no concern for the one being reported about, but rather for the report itself. If the concern is for the one being reported about, you would say, 'Zayd has stood up.' In response to the question, 'What is the status of that standing?' you would say, 'If Zayd has stood up.' In response to the denial of his standing, you would say, 'By Allah, Zayd has stood up'" (3). He then said: "All of this revolves around reporting Zayd's standing, so such actions, which change the meaning of the same speech according to them, are not the original intention. Rather, they are complements and supplements" (4). Based on this, if this difference in reporting were considered among the original meanings, it would not be possible to translate any speech from Arabic into the speech of the non-Arabs, let alone translate the Qur'an (5).
