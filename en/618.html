Thus, what is relied upon in Sharia is the roots and their branches, and the particular is not relied upon unless it refers back to a root to be a branch. The root has characteristics that must be noted, which are mentioned above: "general, consistent, established, unchanging, and governing rather than governed." He explained what he meant by these characteristics, saying: "The generality and consistency; thus, the legal rulings regarding the actions of the obligated individuals are absolute, even if their specific instances are countless. There is no action that is mandated nor any stillness that is claimed except that the Sharia governs it individually and collectively, which is the meaning of its generality. And if there is a specific case in its texts or its rationales, it refers back to the general" (1). That is, if a particular exists that can be considered and specifies this general, it must refer back to another general, and the specification of some generals by others is a form of clarification, which is confirmed by the second characteristic of the generals. He said: "The second is permanence without change; therefore, you will not find in them, after their completeness, any abrogation or specification of their generality, nor restriction of their absoluteness, nor lifting of any of their rulings" (2). He said: "Rather, what has been established as a cause is always a cause that does not cease, and what was a condition is always a condition, and what is obligatory is always obligatory, or recommended is recommended, and so on for all rulings; there is no cessation or change for them, and if the obligation were to remain indefinitely, its rulings would be the same" (3). As for the third characteristic, which is that the generals are governing and not governed, its meaning is that there is nothing in the texts of Sharia that necessitates diverting its meaning to a meaning that the texts do not support. He said: "You will never find in practice anything that governs over the Sharia; otherwise, its being governing would turn into being governed" (4).

These are the three characteristics that distinguish the roots, and any meaning stated in the sciences of Sharia that loses one of these characteristics is not a root and cannot be referred to.
