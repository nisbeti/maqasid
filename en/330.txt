... Among what the generality of the Sharia (Islamic law) necessitates is the stability of its rulings in every time and place; otherwise, it would not be general for those obligated by it. The revelation has ceased after the death of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), so there is no abrogation, and what is good does not become bad, nor does what is bad become good.
... If some rulings change, it is due to the change in their underlying reasons (manat), such as when the legislator has linked the ruling to a reason, and the ruling changes according to the presence or absence of that reason. Or it may be that the legislator has arranged the rulings based on certain attributes, so the ruling changes according to the attribute, such as puberty in legal responsibility (taklif), which may differ from place to place or from person to person. Legal responsibility may apply to one person at the age of eleven, for example, and to another at the age of fifteen. This change, in this sense, is not a change in the essence of the address, but rather a stability, as the condition of puberty in legal responsibility is fixed and does not change.
... Likewise, the rulings that are affected by the intention (niyyah) or purpose (maqsad) of the obligated person, such as prostrating to Allah, Exalted is He, or to an idol, or the utterance of the two testimonies (shahadatayn) in faith or hypocrisy, depend on the intention of the doer. Here, the intention or purpose is part of the underlying reason for the ruling, meaning it is part of the act, and the ruling is a ruling on an act that changes with its change according to the address.
... Similarly, the rulings that have words as their causes, such as marriage (nikah), divorce (talaq), and emancipation (itaq), occur with the utterance of the words that indicate them. The meanings of the words may change, as people may become accustomed to certain words for meanings or to meanings for certain words, and the customs of one group may differ from another, leading to a change in the ruling based on those words accordingly. This does not affect the fact that the words are causes, as the words were established for their meanings. If the connotations of the words change, the consideration is given to the connotations or meanings and the words that indicate them.
