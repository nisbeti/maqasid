One consequence of this is that the particulars derived from texts of uncertain authenticity or uncertain meanings will remain the sources of the universals, and they are the subject of induction. It is not correct to claim the preservation of the universals by any means, for the universals have no path except through these particulars. Just as it is impermissible to neglect the universals, neglecting the particulars is similar. On the other hand, neglecting the particulars is a neglect of the universals. He says: "If a universal principle is established in these three (1) or in any of them, it must be preserved in relation to what the universal entails, which are the particulars. The particulars are intended and considered in establishing the universal; if the universal is neglected, its intended benefit in legislation will also be neglected" (2). He then presented evidence for this and said: "And since this is the case, it indicates that the particulars are included in the demand and preservation of the universals" (3). He said: "If the particulars were not considered and intended in establishing the universal, the command regarding the universal would not be valid in the first place, because the universal, as such, cannot be the object of obligation" (4).
