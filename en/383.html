To clarify al-Shatibi's position here accurately, it is essential to point out his distinction between performing an action that aligns with the intent of the Lawgiver out of necessity, as in the marriage mentioned above, and performing the same action without regard for compliance or non-compliance, where the alignment occurred by coincidence, that is, without the agent intending it. There is also the performance of the same action but with the intention of non-compliance, where the alignment occurred by mistake. As for the first case, it has been clarified. The ruling for the second case is that it is invalid in acts of worship, while in customary matters, it is valid (1). The ruling for the third case is similar to that of the second... In all cases, the sin attaches to the one who intends to oppose the intent of the Lawgiver or to the one who does not care about it. He says: "If one intends to oppose the command of the Lawgiver, then whether in acts of worship he aligns or opposes, the opposition is not considered because it is a complete contradiction of intent. In customary matters, the original rule is to consider what aligns and not what opposes, because actions that do not require intention for their validity are not considered in terms of alignment with the legal intent or opposition to it, like someone who contracts with the intention that it is invalid, yet it turns out to be valid, or someone who drinks a syrup thinking it is wine, except that he incurs sin for intending to oppose. As for one who does not intend alignment or opposition, he is acting merely out of chance or negligence, and the ruling for him in acts of worship is invalid due to the lack of intention to comply, while in customary matters, it is valid if it aligns with the intent of the Lawgiver" (2).

This means that the intention to comply is a condition for the validity of acts of worship, while it is not for customary matters, and the actions of customary matters are not invalidated or rendered void by the absence of the intention to comply, nor if the intention to comply does not cross the mind. He said: "When an action occurs in accordance with the subsidiary objectives, it cannot be devoid of the original objectives or not. As for the first, it is an act of compliance without doubt, even if it is a pursuit of personal gain. As for the second, it is an act based on desire and whim in isolation" (3).
