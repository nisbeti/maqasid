To clarify al-Shatibi's position here accurately, it is necessary to point out his distinction between performing an action that aligns with the intention of the Lawgiver with the intention of compliance out of necessity, as in the marriage mentioned above, and performing the same action without regard for compliance or non-compliance, where the compliance occurred by coincidence, that is, without intention on the part of the obligated person, and performing the same action but with the intention of non-compliance, where the compliance occurred by mistake. As for the first, it has been clarified. As for the second, its ruling is invalidity in acts of worship, and in customary matters, it is valid(1). As for the third, its ruling is like the second... In all cases, sin attaches to one who intends to oppose the intention of the Lawgiver or to one who does not regard it. He says: "If one intends to oppose the command of the Lawgiver, then whether in acts of worship he complies or opposes, what he opposes is not considered because it is contrary to the intention in general. In customary matters, the original ruling is to consider what complies and not what opposes, because what does not require intention for its validity in actions does not regard compliance with the legal intention or its opposition, like one who contracts a contract intending it to be invalid but it turns out to be valid, or one who drinks a syrup thinking it is wine, except that he bears the sin for intending to oppose. As for one who does not intend compliance or non-compliance, he is acting merely out of chance or negligence, and its ruling in acts of worship is invalidity due to lack of intention to comply, and in customary matters, it is valid if it complies with the intention of the Lawgiver"(2).

That is, the intention of compliance is a condition for the validity of acts of worship, whereas for customary actions it is not. The actions of customary practices are not invalidated or rendered corrupt by the absence of the intention of compliance, nor if the intention of compliance did not cross one's mind.

He said: "When an action occurs in accordance with the subordinate objectives (maqasid al-tabi'a), it can either be accompanied by the primary objectives (maqasid al-asliya) or not. As for the first, it is an action of compliance without doubt, even if it is a pursuit for personal gain; and as for the second, it is an action driven solely by desire and inclination."(3)
