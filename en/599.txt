The original ruling regarding the purchase of wine is, by custom and habit, that it is for drinking, and thus it remains prohibited even if the intention is to ferment it. The same applies to the purchase of the fat of carrion, even if the intention is to use it for painting ships or for lighting. Accordingly, al-Shatibi does not see in the meaning of the principle the prohibition of what is permissible merely because the obligated person may intend to use it to reach something prohibited, even if the intended purpose is religiously forbidden. He says: "In the matter of dependency, what is related to it in terms of demand is disregarded; likewise here, unless the contracting party has a specific intention towards the prohibited, for this has two possibilities: the first is to consider the original intention and disregard the dependent one even if it is intended, and the second is to consider the incidental intention if it becomes prior or as if it were prior, and anything else is like the dependent, so the ruling applies to it. An example of the original permissibility of benefits is the purchase of a female slave with the intention of converting her to prostitution for profit, and the purchase of a boy for immoral acts, and the purchase of grapes to press into wine, and weapons to rob, and some items for deception. In terms of the original prohibition of benefits, the purchase of a dog for hunting and for grazing and farming according to the opinion of those who prohibit that. The purchase of carrion to destroy farms, and the purchase of wine for fermentation, and the purchase of carrion to paint ships or for people to use for light, and similar matters. The regulated principle is the first, and the evidence for it is more, because considering what is intended in terms of originality and habit is what has been established in the Sharia regarding what is intended for permissibility and prohibition, for the purchase of a female slave for benefit in concubinage if she is from the noble class of slaves, or for service if she is from the low class, and the purchase of wine for drinking, and carrion, blood, and pork for eating is the common and usual practice among the Arabs to whom the Qur'an was revealed. Therefore, the related matters of prohibition and permissibility are omitted in the verse: { Prohibited to you are your mothers } (1), until the verse: { And lawful for you is what is beyond that } (2)."(3).
