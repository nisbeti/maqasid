The right of the servant manifests in two aspects. The first: it includes the right of Allah and the right of the servant, but the right of the servant is predominant. This is what Allah has granted the servant in terms of the right of choice, as in his choice of types of consumables, such as food, drinks, clothing, and other things that are lawful for him, as well as in types of sales, transactions, and claims to rights. He has the right to waive them, to exchange them, and to dispose of what is in his possession without restriction, provided that his actions align with the good customs he is accustomed to (1). However, it is not permissible to forfeit the right of Allah; thus, it is not valid for the accountable person to consume what Allah has prohibited of foods, drinks, or clothing, nor to engage in what Allah, Exalted is He, has forbidden in transactions or in what he possesses.
The second: it includes the right of Allah and the right of the servant, but the right of Allah is predominant, yet the right of Allah has been waived, leaving only the right that Allah has granted to the servant. An example of this is the preservation of life and intellect, among other rights, which are rights of Allah over the servants and not rights of the servants over one another. The evidence for this is that Allah has not left this to their choice. If Allah, Exalted is He, completes a servant's life, body, and intellect, it is not valid for the servant to forfeit them. Unless the accountable person is afflicted with something of that nature without any fault or cause of his own, and as a result, he loses his life, intellect, or a member of his body, then the right of the servant is established, since the right of Allah has lapsed, as what has occurred cannot be reversed. What remains of the right of the servant is a right against others, like a debt among debts, which he may choose to collect or to forgo (2). An example of this is retribution (qisas) and blood money (diya), which serve as compensation for what the victim has lost in terms of his own interests or body. The right of Allah has lapsed, and there is no compensation for it, just as what has occurred that can be remedied, such as illnesses, if seeking medical treatment is not obligatory.
Here, the right of Allah, Exalted is He, has lapsed because it cannot be fulfilled, and the right of the servant remains, which the Sharia has granted him the right to collect or to waive.
