4- It must be a clear and defined description. Clarity and definition are the opposites of ambiguity and obscurity. The obscure and unstable cannot be used for reasoning. An example of this is the hardship of travel, which is not defined; therefore, it was not considered in the hardship of the porter and the baker in the city, making travel a defined description that includes the unstable wisdom of hardship. Thus, reasoning based on hardship is not valid, but travel is correctly counted as a cause that includes the reason. Al-Amidi said: "Most scholars have held that it is impermissible to reason a ruling based solely on wisdom that lacks a defining characteristic, while a minority have permitted it" (1). He said: "We know that the legislator only permitted ease in long travel to alleviate the defined hardship of long travel to a specific destination and did not link it to the hardship itself because it is something that fluctuates and varies. For this reason, he did not permit the heavily burdened porter in the city, even if it is assumed that his hardship exceeds that of the traveler every day by a mile, even if he is in utmost comfort and ease, because that is something that varies and fluctuates" (2).

5- It must be free from any legal evidence against it. Al-Zarkashi said: "It must be free from its condition, meaning that it should not be contradicted by a text or consensus, because analogy (qiyas) is a branch of it and is only used in its absence; thus, it cannot be a negation of it. If either of the two contradicts it, it is invalid" (3).

6- The reason must be consistent. Al-Zarkashi said: "It must be consistent, meaning that whenever it exists, the ruling exists" (4). If it exists and the ruling does not exist, it means that it is not a reason.
