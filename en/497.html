It is worth noting that what is being discussed here pertains only to the methodology of al-Shatibi or what he distinguished or focused on, and it is not intended to present or refute the opinions of the imams on this matter, as al-Shatibi himself refers to them and their books in matters where he agrees with them.

The First Topic

The Conditions of Ijtihad

Definition according to al-Shatibi:

Al-Shatibi defines ijtihad by saying: "Ijtihad is the exertion of effort in acquiring knowledge or conjecture about a ruling" (1). There is no doubt that he refers to the exertion of effort by the mujtahid (jurist) who has reached a level where his statements can be trusted. Al-Shatibi does not disagree that there is no ijtihad in definitive matters, as will be clarified, but his statement "in acquiring knowledge" relates to the conditions of the mujtahid, including the acquisition of knowledge of the general principles, and in this regard, he exerts effort. However, this effort does not grant him the authority to engage in ijtihad on detailed contentious issues nor to have a respected opinion in them to issue fatwas (legal opinions) for the people, as he is still progressing in knowledge and completing his understanding of the objectives (maqasid) of the Sharia. He said: "The ijtihad that occurs in the Sharia is of two types: one is the ijtihad that is legally recognized, which is issued by those qualified who have undertaken to understand what is required for ijtihad" (2). He then said: "The second is the unrecognized type, which is issued by someone who is not knowledgeable about what is required for ijtihad" (3).
