It is not a condition for ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) that the mujtahid (jurist) be proficient in every science that is necessary for it. Some of what is required for ijtihad can be acquired by the mujtahid from others and accepted from them. He said: "We take the four imams as examples; al-Shafi'i is considered a follower in hadith, having not reached the level of ijtihad in critiquing and understanding it, and Abu Hanifa is the same. Only Malik is counted among them, and you see him in rulings referring to others, such as those knowledgeable in experience, medicine, menstruation, and so on, and he builds upon that" (1). He said: "Most of what has been written in the principles of fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence) consists of Arabic topics that the mujtahid is responsible for answering, while other preliminaries may suffice with following, such as discussions on rulings in terms of conception and affirmation, like the rulings of abrogation and the rulings of hadith and similar matters" (2).
