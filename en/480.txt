Just as it was previously mentioned that the Qur'an contains the clarification of all matters of legal rulings, so too does the Sunnah, as it includes what is in the Qur'an along with additional clarification and explanation. It should be noted that the difference is that the Qur'an is definitively established in its entirety and in detail, while the Sunnah, in terms of detail, is presumed. The Qur'an presents the general principles, and the Sunnah elaborates on them. Al-Shatibi states in the context of explaining how the Sunnah clarifies the Qur'an: "One should look at what the Book indicates in general, and that it is present in the Sunnah in full, in addition to what it contains of clarification and explanation. This is because the Noble Qur'an came with the definition of the interests of both abodes, bringing them forth, and the definition of their harms, repelling them" (1). He then said: "The Book presented these as foundational principles to which one can refer, while the Sunnah elaborated on the Book and clarified what is contained within it. Thus, you will find in the Sunnah only what pertains to those categories (2); the five necessities, as they are established in the Book, are detailed in the Sunnah" (3). After indicating that the same applies to the needs, enhancements, and complements, he said: "The principles of the Sharia have been completed in the Qur'an and in the Sunnah, and nothing has been omitted from them, and induction clarifies this" (4).
