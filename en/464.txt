Therefore, some interpretations have been rejected, even if they were issued by scholars, such as interpreting the neighbor who is near as the heart, the neighbor who is distant as the natural self, and the companion who is near as the intellect that is followed in the Sharia, and the wayfarer as the limbs that are obedient to Allah, Exalted is He. He said that this: "is not known by the Arabs, neither by those who believe among them nor by those who disbelieve, and the evidence for this is that nothing has been transmitted from the righteous predecessors among the Companions and the Followers that resembles or approaches this interpretation; if it had been known to them, it would have been transmitted. For they were more deserving of understanding the apparent and hidden meanings of the Qur'an, by the consensus of the imams, and no one from the end of this nation will come with guidance more than that which was with its beginning, nor are they more knowledgeable of the Sharia than them. There is also no evidence indicating the validity of this interpretation, neither from the context of the verse, as it contradicts it, nor from outside it, as there is no evidence for it either. Rather, such a thing is closer to what has been firmly rejected and denied from the Qur'an, like the words of the Batiniyya and those similar to them."(1)
Examples of al-Shatibi's methodology in understanding the meanings of the Qur'an:
All the rulings of the Sharia are present in the Qur'an in universals that serve one another. Therefore, it is necessary for anyone who wishes to acquire knowledge of the Sharia to know its universals that are compiled in the Qur'an, as this enables them to apply them to their particulars. Knowledge of the universals necessitates knowledge of the Qur'an and constant engagement with it in order to be aware of the meanings it contains that do not become obsolete, and the rulings and truths that continue to emerge for those who reflect upon it. This can only be achieved through continuous reading and contemplation of what is in it. He said: "If we look at the return of the Sharia to its moral universals, we find that they are fully encompassed in the Qur'an."(2) He said: "The Book has been established as the universal of the Sharia, the foundation of the religion, the fountain of wisdom, and the sign of the message."(3) He then said: "If that is the case, it is necessary for anyone who seeks to gain insight into the universals of the Sharia and aspires to grasp its objectives and join its people to take it as their companion and friend, and to make it their companion throughout the days and nights, both in contemplation and action, not limiting themselves to one of them."(4)
