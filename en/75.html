Among the examples of this approach is "that the legislator mentions a description appropriate to the ruling, such as his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): 'The judge should not rule while he is angry' (1). This implies that anger is a prohibitive cause for ruling due to the confusion of thought and disturbance of state it brings" (2). The judge's work is to rule, and his sitting is for the purpose of ruling; thus, if he is prevented from ruling in a certain state, this indicates that this state is the cause of the prohibition.

This section contrasts with the second category of the explicit text's indication of reasoning, but that is from the stated and this is from the implied. These three approaches: consensus (ijma), text (nass), and indication and hint (tanbih wa imah) are agreed upon among the jurists. As for the subsequent approaches, there is disagreement regarding them; they are adopted and defended by the method of the theologians among the jurists, particularly the Imams of the Shafi'i school. The method of the jurists among the legal scholars, namely the Hanafis, rejects them.

The Imams of the Shafi'i school refer to the causes that come with these three approaches as the stated causes (al-‘ilal al-mansusah), while the causes that come with the other approaches that will follow are referred to as the inferred causes (al-‘ilal al-mustanbatah). The Hanafis, on the other hand, refer to the inferred causes specifically as those that come from the method of indication and hint, while the stated causes for them are those that come from the methods of consensus and text.

The fourth approach: probing and division (sabr wa taqsim). Some jurists have endorsed it. It is based on the principle that the original state of rulings is to be reasoned. Its meaning is that the investigator exhausts all the descriptions that could potentially be a cause for the ruling—based on the necessity of a cause for the ruling—and this is the probing. Then he examines these potential descriptions one by one and establishes that they are not a cause for the ruling except for one of them, and this is the division. Thus, the description that remains is the cause.
