This text indicates that those who reject public interest (maslaha) are rejecting something other than what its proponents intend. If the examples mentioned by al-Qarafi above refer to the topics of public interest, then these examples are matters of consensus, even if there is disagreement regarding detailed legal reasoning. Those who do not accept public interest refer it to other principles, such as the preservation of necessities or the principle that whatever is necessary for fulfilling an obligation is itself obligatory, or the principle of alleviating hardship or harm, or similar concepts. Therefore, there is no real disagreement regarding the principle of public interest; the source of the disagreement lies in the differing understandings of the principle among those who disagree.
The significant disagreement, however, is as previously mentioned, regarding the justification of legal rulings by their purposes or by their benefits. This may lead some followers of the imams and those studying the books of usul to misunderstand and impose their understanding on the school of thought or its imam, or to exploit the idea and use it without its constraints and inappropriately, perhaps even in a way that misrepresents its meaning. For instance, to understand public interest as merely the fact that something is beneficial or leads to a benefit is evidence of its permissibility, or to understand that there is a vacuum in the Sharia, and that there are occurrences without rulings, and that this is left to us to determine its ruling based on benefit or harm—such statements have no basis among any of the recognized imams. It is reported that there is consensus on rejecting this, and this matter will become clear upon the completion of this discussion, if Allah wills.
