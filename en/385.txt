It has been previously clarified that the objectives of the Legislator (al-Shari) are of two types: original and subsidiary. The original objectives are those for which there is no consideration for the obligated person (mukallaf). They mean performing an action or refraining from it based on a command or prohibition without regard to interests or outcomes, and interests only arise within the framework of the obligation. As for the subsidiary objectives, they are legal objectives that take into account the interests of the obligated person, such as ownership and enjoyment, and so forth. These are objectives that the obligated person may intend through their actions, and Allah - Exalted is He - has legislated rulings for their sake. Thus, the rulings are causes for them, and these interests are legal objectives, so it is valid for the obligated person to intend them through these causes, just as it is valid for them not to intend them, but rather to intend compliance only through those causes.
According to logical categorization, the actions of the obligated person may align with the command and prohibition, making the form of the action legal, but the intention of the doer may be legal or illegal, meaning it may align with the law or contradict it. Similarly, the action may contradict the command or prohibition, making the form of the action illegal, but the intention of the doer may be legal or illegal.
These are four categories detailed by al-Shatibi. If something of this has been previously mentioned in the earlier discussion, it pertains to alignment or non-alignment with the intention of the Legislator, not to the contradiction of the intention of the Legislator.
There is a fifth category that will be referred to after explaining these four categories, which is when the action is legal and the intention is legal, but the action was not legislated for this intention but for another. Likewise, the interest or intention in general was not intended by the Legislator with this action, but with another. This is like the cutting of the hand; it is a legal action, and the intention of deterrence is a legal intention. However, cutting the hand of the apostate or the adulterer is not legal, because the cutting of the hand, which is legal, was not established for deterring the apostate or the adulterer, even though deterring them is legal.
