The requirement for certainty in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) is well-known and established among jurists. Al-Shatibi referred to this in his first introduction. What the jurists mean by the principles that must be certain are the definitive evidences or the general sources of legislation, which are the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas). As for other topics in the principles, such as the paths of reasoning and their conditions, and discussions of generality and specificity, absolute and restricted, and the conditions of narration and narrators, these do not require certainty, and it suffices to rely on preponderance of evidence.
Al-Juwayni said: "If it is said: What is jurisprudence (fiqh)? We say: It is, in the terminology of the scholars of Sharia, the knowledge of the rulings of obligations. If it is said: Most of what is contained in the issues of Sharia is based on conjecture. We say: Conjectures are not jurisprudence; rather, jurisprudence is the knowledge (1) of the obligation to act when conjectures arise. Therefore, the scholars have stated: The reports of the singular (akhbar al-ahad) and the analogies of jurisprudence do not necessitate action by themselves; rather, action must be based on what necessitates knowledge of the obligation to act, which are the definitive evidences regarding the obligation to act when reporting singular reports and applying analogies. If it is said: What are the principles of jurisprudence? We say: They are its evidences, and the evidences of jurisprudence are the textual evidences from the Qur'an, the textual evidences from the mutawatir Sunnah, and consensus, all of which are based on the saying of Allah, Exalted is He. From this perspective, the principles of jurisprudence derive from discourse. If it is said: The details of singular reports and analogies can only be found in the principles and are not definitive. We say: The role of the jurist is to clarify the definitive in acting upon them, but it is necessary to mention them so that the meaning is clarified and the evidence is connected to it." (2)

(1) That is, the certain knowledge of the obligation to act based on the conjectural reports regarding their authenticity or implications, or both.
(2) Al-Juwayni, Al-Burhan fi Usul al-Fiqh. 1/78 - 79. Paragraphs: 4 - 5 - 6.
