... He says: "Continuous customs are of two types: the first type is the legal customs that are affirmed or denied by legal evidence. This means that the Sharia has either commanded them as obligatory or recommended, or prohibited them as disliked or forbidden, or permitted them in action or abstention. The second type consists of the customs that are prevalent among people for which there is no legal evidence for their denial or affirmation. As for the first type, it is always established like other legal matters... either good in the view of the Lawgiver or bad; they are among the matters that fall under the rulings of the Sharia, and they cannot be changed even if the opinions of the obligated individuals differ regarding them. It is not valid for what is considered good in this context to become bad, nor for what is considered bad to become good, until it is said, for example... that uncovering the private parts is neither a fault nor bad, so it is permissible, or something similar. For if such a thing were valid, it would be abrogation of the established and continuous rulings, and abrogation after the death of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is invalid, so the lifting of legal customs is invalid" (1). Al-Shatibi continues: "As for the second type, customs may be established or they may change, and nonetheless, they are causes for rulings that are derived from them. The established ones are like the existence of the desire for food, drink, and sexual intercourse... If they are causes for consequences that the Lawgiver has ruled upon, there is no doubt in considering them and building upon them and ruling according to them at all times. As for the changing ones: some may change in custom from good to bad and vice versa, such as uncovering the head, which varies according to the regions in reality. It is considered bad for those of honor in the Eastern lands but not bad in the Western lands. Thus, the legal ruling differs according to that, so it is a defect in justice among the people of the East and not a defect among the people of the West. Additionally, there are expressions that differ in conveying meanings, so the expression may shift from one meaning to another... like the differences in expressions according to the terminology of craftsmen... or in relation to the predominance of usage in certain meanings, until that term is understood to convey a specific meaning that it previously did not, or it was ambiguous and became specific..."
