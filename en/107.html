The transfer of scholars' statements without verifying the intended meaning for each of them has led to conflicting reports. Dr. Muhammad Hassan Hayto said: "Know that the issue of the transmitted reasoning has encountered much confusion, resulting in conflicting reports and divergent opinions" (1). The saying has become famously attributed to Imam Malik (may Allah be pleased with him), and an unregulated extension has been attributed to him in this regard. Some have attributed it to the Shafi'is and Hanafis, while others have attributed it solely to the Shafi'is without the Hanafis. Some have claimed its existence in all schools of thought, while others have agreed on its rejection and denied its attribution to Malik (may Allah have mercy on him). Below are some of those reports: Al-Juwayni said: "Al-Shafi'i and most of Abu Hanifa's companions relied on transmitted reasoning even if it did not rely on an agreed-upon ruling in principle" (2), while Al-Amidi went against this, saying: "The appropriate transmitted reasoning... and the jurists from the Shafi'is, Hanafis, and others have agreed on the impossibility of relying on it, which is the truth" (3). Al-Qarafi said: "What is misunderstood is the public interest (maslaha) that we advocate, and upon investigation, it is general in the schools" (4). The reports attributed to Al-Ghazali are perplexing, as he said: "Whoever seeks public interest has legislated, just as whoever deems something good has legislated" (5). He also said: "Any public interest that returns to preserving a legal objective known to be intended by the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus is not outside these foundations; however, it is not called analogy but rather a public interest... If we interpret public interest as preserving the objectives of the Sharia, there is no basis for disagreement on it, rather it must be affirmed as a proof" (6).
