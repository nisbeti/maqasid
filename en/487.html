The position of al-Shatibi is that the Sunnah does not bring about legislation that has no basis in the Qur'an. He faces objections that there are many things in the Sunnah that have no basis in the Book, so he mentions these objections to refute them and to affirm his position. He said, providing examples of what is objected to: "Induction has shown that there are countless things in the Sunnah that are not mentioned in the Noble Qur'an, such as the prohibition of marrying a woman’s aunt or maternal aunt, the prohibition of domestic donkeys, and every carnivorous animal with fangs, the ransom of a captive, and that a Muslim should not be killed for a disbeliever"(1).

He responded to the issue of the prohibition of marrying a woman’s aunt or maternal aunt by saying: "Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, has prohibited the combining of a mother and her daughter in marriage and between two sisters, and it has come in the Qur'an: { And lawful for you is what is beyond that } (2). Thus, the prohibition by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) of combining a woman with her aunt or maternal aunt is based on analogy (qiyas), because the reason for which the combination of those mentioned is condemned is present here. It may be narrated in this hadith: 'For indeed, if you do that, you will sever your ties of kinship'(3). The reasoning indicates the basis of the analogy"(4).
