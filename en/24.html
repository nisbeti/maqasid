Thus, the society of Granada witnessed a proliferation of sciences and knowledge, along with a multitude of scholars, which is a relatively advanced state. However, the important difference lies in the direction of the society in its cultural and scientific movement, which was one of regression and decline. This situation led to what al-Shatibi indicated regarding the spread of innovations (bid'ah) and closure, such that opposing or non-dominant opinions were not accepted even if supported by legal evidence, and to issuing fatwas based on blind imitation, applying the statements of jurists or the established rules of the school without delving into their foundations. Therefore, al-Shatibi depicts the state of affairs through the hadith: "Islam began as something strange and will return as something strange as it began, so blessed are the strangers" (1), and he speaks of the masters of ignorance, sectarian fanaticism, and the exaggeration in glorifying others besides Allah. He holds the scholars accountable for this ignorance, as they issue fatwas for the ruler according to his desires, or for their relatives or followers, and who alter rulings under the pretext of facilitation or necessity.
