Add to this another issue, which is that considering a certain description as a public interest (maslaha) and thus a legal objective (maqsad) and a reason for legislation is only for the purpose of building the legislation upon that. The reason is one of the pillars of analogy (qiyas), and analogy has other pillars, including the origin, which is the legal ruling that is being reasoned. The existence of the ruling and the existence of its reason leads to giving the same ruling to another incident that does not have a text, based on that reason or interest. However, in the matter of reasoning based on public interest, those who advocate for public interest may see benefits or harms in certain incidents that require a ruling, but without finding an origin or ruling to base it on. So, giving the ruling here based on the existing interest or harm in the incident without finding an origin to base it on—does this fall under analogy or is it something else? The existence of the origin is a pillar of analogy, and its absence invalidates the process of analogy. This is another issue that has posed a barrier for those who advocate reasoning based on public interests. Here, a disagreement arose among those who reason based on public interests; some called this matter analogy based on the premise that if a specific interest does not have an origin to be based on, there are origins for the category of interest. Others did not consider this a form of analogy but rather termed it: reasoning (istidlal). This matter will also be clarified in detail in its appropriate place.
It is generally held among the usul scholars that this is not analogy but rather reasoning, and more specifically, it is public interest (istislah) or unregulated public interest (maslaha mursala). The term reasoning is broader than unregulated public interests as it is also applied to preference (istihsan), presumption (istishab), and others that do not have a specific or direct evidence regarding the matter, but rather rely on a principle that is not specific to the issue, just as those who reason based on appropriate measures rely on the principle of the origin in the reasoning of rulings, and just as it is also relied upon in some jurisprudential matters based on the principle of presumption.
__________
(1) Public interest (maslaha) refers to the benefits that serve the community and society as a whole in Islamic jurisprudence.
(2) Legal objective (maqsad) denotes the goals and purposes of Sharia law.
(3) Analogy (qiyas) is a method of reasoning in Islamic jurisprudence that draws conclusions based on established precedents.
(4) Reasoning (istidlal) is the process of deriving legal rulings from general principles or established rules.
(5) Public interest (istislah) is a form of reasoning that seeks to achieve the welfare of the community.
(6) Unregulated public interest (maslaha mursala) refers to interests that are not explicitly mentioned in the texts of the Sharia but are considered beneficial.
(7) Preference (istihsan) is a juristic principle that allows for choosing a ruling based on a better alternative.
(8) Presumption (istishab) is a principle that maintains the status quo in the absence of evidence to the contrary.
