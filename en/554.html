Explanation of the text:

His saying: "The necessary matters or others that are of a needful or complementary nature, if they are surrounded by external matters that do not please the Sharia, then the act of bringing about benefits is valid on the condition of taking precautions according to one's ability without hardship." This means that the actions which the evidence indicates are permissible, whether by obligation, recommendation, or permissibility, do not fall outside the categories of necessity, need, or complementarity; they are benefits that fall under these three categories. These actions, if they have an outcome that is prohibited, then performing them is valid with the possibility of that outcome, meaning that the original consideration of outcomes does not affect the ruling on these actions. And since these actions may lead to something forbidden, one must take precautions against falling into these outcomes or prohibitions to the best of their ability, meaning one must avoid these prohibitions just as one must avoid any forbidden act. And Allah, Exalted is He, does not burden a soul beyond its capacity, so this text does not permit the commission of wrongdoing. His saying: "Like marriage, which necessitates seeking sustenance for dependents amidst the narrow paths of what is lawful, and the vast avenues of what is unlawful and questionable, often forcing one to earn for them through what is not permissible, but this does not prevent it." Marriage, for example, is permissible, and according to al-Shatibi, it is a legal ruling concerning the individual in its particularity, and it is a general, definitive principle concerning the public in its service to necessity. If it is established that it may lead in some cases to something prohibited, such as seeking sustenance for dependents through unlawful means, and it may lead to earning through what is forbidden, then the particularity conflicts with the principle, which is the permissibility of this marriage with regard to outcomes. In these cases, the outcomes of marriage do not invalidate the legal ruling on marriage; this act remains as it is, and its ruling is not changed by its outcomes. The obligated person strives to avoid falling into these outcomes according to their ability, and this does not mean that engaging with these outcomes becomes permissible. And his saying: "For what it leads to, taking precautions against the harm that is greater than the harm of exposure; if such a thing were considered in our time, it would lead to the invalidation of its principle, which is not correct."
