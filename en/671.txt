The fourth: What al-Shatibi used as evidence for the objective can be turned around to argue against it. In His saying, { And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me }. If someone asks: Why did Allah create people, and the answer is: So that they may worship Him. This does not satisfy what is in the heart if the goal is to know a reason or objective, and the question transforms into another question: Why does Allah want people to worship Him? What is the purpose of that? The text does not mention the purpose; rather, it commands worship without stating an intention or objective. Thus, in every proof presented by al-Shatibi, one could argue the opposite of what he intended, asking what the purpose is of testing people to see who is best in deeds, and likewise, what is the purpose of cutting off people's arguments on the Day of Judgment. Therefore, every text that al-Shatibi brought, which mentions intention or goal, also contains a lack of mention of that, indicating the invalidity of such reasoning.
In truth, there is no answer to such questions except to say: { He will not be asked about what He does, but they will be asked } (1), { He does what He wills } (2), { He is the doer of what He intends } (3), { Indeed, Allah judges what He wills } (4).
Therefore, those who argued about the prohibition of usury (riba), claiming it is like sales, were not given an answer that clarifies the differences between sales and usury, or explains the wisdom behind the prohibition of usury. Rather, the answer came as a decree, as He said: { Those who consume usury will not rise except as one rises whom Satan has struck with madness by his touch. That is because they say, "Trade is just like usury," but Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden usury } (5). Thus, what al-Shatibi used as evidence is not suitable for proving his claim.
