Conditions of concession:
... The fact that one is not obligated to do what is beyond their capacity, and that one is not tasked with burdensome difficulties, is a legal matter that does not involve reason. Likewise, the areas of concession and the type of concession are legal matters; therefore, there are conditions for the concession. Some of these conditions have been mentioned previously, namely that the difficulty must be burdensome. Below, these conditions are mentioned to complete the discussion.
... It is said: "If the difficulty leads to the disruption of a fundamental principle, then the principle of determination (azima) should not be considered, as completing the act of worship here and performing it as it should leads to its being nullified from its origin. Thus, performing what one is capable of from it, which is the essence of the concession, is what is required" (2). This difficulty is also associated with hardship, as it is said: "Hardship applies to one who is afflicted by hardship to the extent that it prevents them from their necessities and needs" (3). Likewise, there is necessity, as it is said: "The state of necessity has been clarified as that which one fears will lead to the loss of life, and this can only occur after being unable to perform acts of worship and daily activities, and it is, in itself, also an excuse" (4). These definitions clarify a condition of the difficulties, which is the disruption of a fundamental principle. He refers to it as burdensome or real, as has been clarified.
