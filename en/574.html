Al-Shatibi's response to this is: "The explanation of the answer is what Malik mentioned, that silence regarding the ruling of an action or abstention here, when the meaning necessitating the action or abstention is present, is an agreement from all who are silent that there is nothing additional to what existed, which is the ultimate in this meaning" (1). He said: "The existence of the necessitating meaning along with the absence of legislation is evidence of the intent of the Legislator to not add to what was already present. If there is an addition, it becomes evident that it contradicts the intent of the Legislator, and thus it is invalid" (2). The implication of this statement and what preceded it is that if there is to be an addition or change, it must be according to the evidence of the Sharia and its principles.

In summary, the principle of public interest (maslaha) according to al-Shatibi is applied to jurisprudence that is based on the principle "what is necessary for the fulfillment of an obligation is itself an obligation" in matters of necessity, and on the principle of alleviating hardship in matters of need, and it does not apply in acts of worship.

Section Two

Istihsan

Istihsan and the positions of the imams regarding it:

Istihsan, according to al-Shatibi, is a foundational principle (usuli) like public interest (maslaha), and it is a method in applying evidence and in legal reasoning. Its definitions are varied and differ. Before delving into it in detail, it is worth noting that its essence does not stray from acting upon the stronger of two evidences when they conflict. This was affirmed by Imam Abu Hanifa (may Allah have mercy on him) and his followers. It is well-known that it was rejected by al-Shafi'i and the Shafi'is, who strongly criticized it, considering it mere preference based on opinion, or abandoning evidence for opinion. However, in its essence as understood by the Hanafis, they accept it and consider it an act based on evidence. The practice of istihsan has also been attributed to the two imams, Malik and Ahmad (may Allah have mercy on them).
