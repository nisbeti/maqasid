He said: "The statement regarding the Qur'an returns to the fact that Allah intended such and such or meant such and such with His revealed speech, and this is of great importance" (1). He said: "The one who looks at and interprets it must be aware that what he says is an intention from the speaker, and the Qur'an is the speech of Allah; he is saying with this clear expression that this is the intention of Allah from this speech, so let him be certain that he will be asked by Allah, 'From where did you say this about Me?'" (2). Therefore, if the interpreter does not verify the correctness of his statement, it is not permissible for him to assert it. Rather, it should be that the interpretation aligns with the speech of the Arabs, meaning that it should be something that the text can accommodate, and it should be in accordance with the Qur'an and the Sunnah, meaning that it should return to a meaning that is certain. If the text can accommodate more than one meaning, and the possible meanings each return to a certain meaning, then there must be evidence to prefer one meaning over another; otherwise, the meanings remain possible, and it is not permissible for him to assert a meaning or prefer one over another, and he may say: "It is possible that the meaning is this or that." He said: "Let him be certain that he will be asked by Allah, Exalted is He, 'From where did you say this about Me?' It is not permissible for him to do so except with the clarification of the evidence; otherwise, mere possibility suffices for him to say it is possible that the meaning is this or that, based also on the validity of those possibilities in the core of knowledge; otherwise, the possibilities that do not return to a valid origin are not considered. In any case, there must be evidence for every statement he asserts or interprets; otherwise, it is invalid, and its speaker falls under the category of the blameworthy opinion, and Allah knows best" (3).

Accordingly, the accepted opinion in interpretation has two conditions. The first: it must align with the speech of the Arabs. The second: it must be in accordance with the Book and the Sunnah. The statement that fulfills the first condition is what al-Shatibi calls the apparent. As for the statement that fulfills both conditions, he calls it the hidden, which is what should be sought in interpretation, as will become clear.
