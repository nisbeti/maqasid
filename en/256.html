As for the other texts that Dr. Daraz saw as contradictory, what was mentioned in the fifth issue is not intended to define benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid), but rather the purpose of the issue is that benefits and harms are viewed from two perspectives. The first perspective is regarding their existence in reality, which here is not absolute; benefits are mixed with harms, and vice versa. That is, the action commanded for a purpose intended by the Legislator results in a legal benefit arising from the action, but the action may also produce other outcomes that are not intended by that command, which may be harms, and vice versa, because harms and benefits are not absolute in reality. For example, jihad is a benefit, and it results in benefits intended by the Legislator, such as the expansion of the authority of Islam, its honor, and the entry of people into it. It also results in the loss of lives, expenditure of wealth, and hardships; are these latter outcomes the intention of the Legislator in jihad? No. Rather, these are harms in the Sharia. Based on this, the saying of al-Shatibi is understood: "If that is the case, then the benefits and harms related to this world are understood according to what predominates." That is, one looks at the benefits, their extent, and the degree of their expectation. Is victory expected or not, and to what degree is that presumed? One also considers the costs in terms of lives and wealth, and the expected outcomes according to those with insight and experience. Therefore, he said: "If the predominant aspect is that of benefit, then it is the understood benefit in customary terms."
