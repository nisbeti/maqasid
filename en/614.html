Therefore, the five necessities are definitive principles of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), and likewise, the rules for removing harm, hardship, and difficulty, and similar matters are definitive rules in the needs (hajiyat). Additionally, the principle of the outcomes of actions (mala'at al-af'al) and the rules of public interest (maslaha mursala), preference (istihsan), and blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) are all definitive, both in their reality and in their conditions. This is not what the imams of usul intended when they stipulated definitiveness for the principles of jurisprudence; rather, they limited that to what is considered a source for legislation. Al-Shatibi indicates that his intention differs from others in this regard; at times, he directs his words to indicate that his meaning is what is relied upon by the earlier usulists, but they neglected to point it out. At other times, he explicitly states that his intention differs from theirs. He said: "However, the earlier usulists may have neglected this meaning and the indication of it, leading to its being overlooked by some later scholars, which caused confusion in deriving evidence from the verses in their singularity and from the hadiths in their isolation, as they did not take them in a collective manner, thus they were subjected to objections text by text. They weakened the evidence from them regarding the principles of usul intended for definitiveness" (1). He said in another place: "Every legal evidence is either definitive or speculative; if it is definitive, there is no doubt in considering it" (2). Then he said: "And if it is speculative, it is either related to a definitive principle or not. If it is related to a definitive principle, it is also considered; and if it is not related, one must be cautious about it, and it is not correct to broadly state its acceptance" (3). He said: "Know that the purpose of referring to the definitive principle is not to establish definitive evidence for the validity of acting upon it, like the evidence that acting upon the report of a single narrator or by analogy is obligatory, for example, but rather what is more specific than that, as previously mentioned in the hadith: 'There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm' (4), and the issues mentioned with it, which is a meaning contrary to that intended by the usulists, and Allah knows best" (5).
