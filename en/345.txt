One of them: What is known or presumed to be the reason for which the law was established, thus the causation of the one who causes it is valid because he approached the matter from its proper avenue and sought it through what the legislator permitted to seek in relation to what he also permitted to seek. This is because we have assumed that the legislator intended by marriage, for example, procreation primarily, followed by establishing a home and forming alliances with the family of the woman due to their nobility or religion or something similar, or providing service or attending to his interests, or enjoying what Allah has made lawful from women, or beautifying oneself with the wealth of the woman, or desiring her beauty, or being pleased with her religion, or refraining from what Allah has prohibited, or something similar as indicated by the Sharia. Therefore, what this causative agent intended is, in general, the intention of the legislator. (1)
