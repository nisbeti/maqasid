However, there is a matter that should be pointed out, which is that this flourishing in terms of science and culture does not necessarily indicate health and the continuation of progress. Indeed, Andalusia has gone through periods of scientific and cultural, civilizational and societal flourishing, and this situation in the eighth century is a remnant of that prosperity; it is a continuation of past progress. This is evidenced by the fact that Granada fell before the end of the ninth century, and that Muslims in Andalusia were gradually losing their authority and power. Therefore, it is noted that this advanced scientific and cultural state is limited to the explanation and preservation of existing knowledge, as evidenced by stagnation and lack of interaction with what is beyond the borders. New writings were nothing more than commentaries and summaries, and the high scientific level is a relative level, resulting from comparisons with the level of surrounding countries, whether in Europe or the Maghreb. However, in comparison to the situation in the fourth and fifth centuries of the Hijra, it indicates stability and stagnation in the legislative aspect. What confirms these conditions is the absence of any school of thought in Granada other than the Maliki school. Al-Shatibi said: "For the writings of the Hanafi school are as if they do not exist in the Maghreb, as are the writings of the Shafi'i school and others from the people of the schools."(1)
