What confirms what we have mentioned is his saying: "The report of an individual (khabar al-wahid) is acceptable in practice if it is based on an established principle; otherwise, one should refrain from acting upon it. Its being based on an established principle refers to it being a particular under a general Qur'anic meaning" (1). His statement about refraining means not applying the hadith and not rejecting it, as there may be an inductive reasoning that makes its meaning refer back to a definitive meaning. His saying: "refers to it being a particular under a general Qur'anic meaning" means the Sunnah clarifying the Book, and any meaning that has no basis in the Book is not taken (2). However, al-Shatibi's intent goes further than that, as he requires that any meaning of any text must refer back to a definitive meaning, even if it is a Qur'anic text.
Therefore, one should note his statement above: "under a general Qur'anic meaning." Not: "under a Qur'anic meaning." If we combine the issues that al-Shatibi asserts, which are that the meaning must be general, that individual pieces of evidence do not provide certainty, including the texts of the Qur'an, while knowing that all Qur'anic texts must be acted upon, a question arises: how is certainty established regarding the meanings of the Qur'an? This issue will be addressed in the next section - if Allah wills - which is al-Shatibi's methodology in understanding the Qur'an. The aim here is to establish al-Shatibi's position on the implications of texts regarding their meanings, which is that they are not taken or considered unless they refer back to a definitive meaning. These meanings are particulars, as are the meanings of rulings, which are the wisdoms. They are particulars, but not considering them does not mean rejecting them unless they oppose a definitive or general principle. If they do not oppose a general principle, there may be many particulars that agree with them and support them in meaning, thus making the meaning a definitive principle, and these particulars become branches referring back to it.
Previously, he mentioned that the characteristic of his book (al-Muwafaqat) is how to capture the universals from the particulars (3).
