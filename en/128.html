He said: "If the sources of evidence were limited to the texts and the meanings derived from them, the door of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) would not be wide enough, for the texts and their attributed meanings do not constitute more than a room from the sea of the Sharia. If those who came before had not relied on meanings in situations they had not encountered before, their abstention from issuing rulings would have exceeded their engagement, and this, if it coincided with a confirmation, would leave no room for the deniers of inference to be unsettled" (1). He said: "Then al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him) supported this by saying: Whoever examines the conditions of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), who are the role models in reasoning, will not find any one of them in the councils of deliberation establishing a principle or deriving a meaning and then building a case upon it. Rather, they delve into various opinions without regard to whether the principles existed or not. Therefore, if the vastness of ijtihad is established, and it is impossible to limit what is vast to the texts, and in addition to that, the scholars among the Companions did not concern themselves with seeking the principles, all of this leads to the conclusion of inference" (2).
