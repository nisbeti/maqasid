The evidence provided by continuous transmission (tawatur) does not rely on induction but rather on abundance with conditions (1). Al-Zarkashi said: "Continuous transmission, linguistically: is the succession of things one after another with a pause, and terminologically: it is the report of a group whose collusion on a lie is impossible due to their number, about something perceived" (2). He also said: "Tawatur can be verbal or it can be conceptual, which means that those previously mentioned come together on reports that refer back to a single report, such as the bravery of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and the generosity of Hatim" (3). He then mentioned that some have disputed Ali's bravery because his bravery is continuously transmitted both verbally and conceptually (4).
Thus, abundance in tawatur refers to a multitude united on a report. If the report is a narration from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), it is a hadith of definitive authenticity, and it may be definitively indicative or it may not be so. If the multitude is united on several reports, some narrating one report and others narrating another, and so on, and each of these reports does not reach the level of tawatur, they all share a certain meaning. This meaning would then be considered continuous, and this is the conceptual tawatur, and the meaning is definitive.
The particulars inferred by al-Shatibi are of two types: legal texts and legal rulings. If the induction is for legal texts, there is no issue regarding the result, and it may be the conceptual tawatur itself and not similar to it, as if it were an induction of the implications of singular reports, except that this is not induction but rather deduction from the texts. The discussion about the induction of particulars when they are legal texts has previously been addressed under the title: "Induction" in this section. The current discussion is about the induction of legal rulings and their meanings.
