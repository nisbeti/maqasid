This often occurs in oaths, contracts, and divorce, both implicitly and explicitly... Among them are those that differ based on matters external to the obligated person, such as puberty, which is considered based on the customs of people regarding wet dreams or menstruation, or reaching the age of one who experiences wet dreams or menstruates" (1).

... Then al-Shatibi emphasizes the stability of rulings, saying: "Know that what has been mentioned here regarding the difference in rulings due to differing customs is not, in reality, a difference in the essence of the command, because the Sharia is established as being eternal and everlasting, even if the world were to continue without end, and the obligation likewise does not require further elaboration in the Sharia. Rather, the meaning of the difference is that when customs differ, each custom returns to a legal origin that is judged accordingly, as in the case of puberty, for example, where the obligation is lifted from the child before reaching puberty. Once he reaches puberty, the obligation falls upon him... And similarly for other examples, the rulings are stable and follow their causes wherever they may be, and Allah knows best" (2).

The obligation of rulings is not an obligation of their objectives:

Al-Shatibi states that the legal ruling is related to the action of the servant, and the action is a cause for an effect. Since the effects are not from the actions of the servant and are not within his capacity, the obligation does not fall upon them. Therefore, when a command to act is given, it is not a command regarding its outcome.
