He establishes through this reasoning that the legislation of retribution (qisas) is for a purpose or outcome, which is to eliminate intentional murder or transgressions or to reduce them. Thus, this outcome is intended by the Legislator, and it is established that the actions or rulings of the Legislator are justified by their purposes. Since the purposes are intended by the Legislator, they are considered public interests (masalih).

This text and the one preceding it can be said to have as their aim a benefit according to common sense and nature or according to rational individuals, and this is correct. However, this is not the basis of al-Shatibi's reasoning, as it does not align with his other proofs. Rather, his intended purpose, which is supported by all his other arguments, is to establish the existence of a purpose for the ruling or action, regardless of whether it is rational or not. His next and final proof clarifies and emphasizes this matter.

His twelfth proof: { Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes, we bear witness, lest you should say on the Day of Resurrection: Indeed, we were of this unaware } (1).

The complete text is: { And when your Lord took from the Children of Adam, from their loins, their descendants and made them testify of themselves: Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes } (2) [the verse]. This is what al-Shatibi presented to prove that actions have purposes. The purpose here is to establish evidence against the Children of Adam and to cut off their excuses on the Day of Resurrection. The existence of a purpose for an action or ruling, and the affirmation of this in many actions and rulings, establishes for al-Shatibi that the actions and rulings of Allah have purposes. If this is established and there is a ruling for which there is no text regarding its purpose or the intention of the Legislator behind it, then one must search for a purpose or aim that the Legislator intends from it.
