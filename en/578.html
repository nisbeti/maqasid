The definition of al-Karakhi appears to be the most precise, as it encompasses all examples of istihsan (juridical preference). What reinforces this matter is that they say istihsan is one of the types of qiyas (analogy) or the strongest type of qiyas, but when they provide examples, we see them referring to the application of the general rule to its specific instances, or the general rule applying to its instances as qiyas, even though there is no qiyas or cause in the legal sense, which will become clear through the examples.

Categories of Istihsan:

They also categorize istihsan into sections, including istihsan based on the Sunnah, istihsan based on consensus (ijma), and istihsan based on necessity (darura). These examples do not involve qiyas, nor is it necessary for them to have any qiyas at all. Al-Bazdawi said: "Istihsan has categories, one of which is what is established by evidence, such as salam (forward sale) and ijara (lease), and the continuation of fasting with the action of the forgetful. Among them is what is established by consensus, which is istisna (manufacturing contract), and among them is what is established by necessity, which is the purification of water troughs, wells, and utensils" (1). The commentator in Kashf al-Asrar regarding istihsan based on evidence said: "For qiyas rejects the permissibility of salam because the subject of the contract, which is the object of the contract, is truly nonexistent at the time of the contract, and the contract does not take place outside its subject unless we abandon it based on the evidence that necessitates concession, which is the saying of the narrator: 'And he permitted salam' (2). And his saying, peace be upon him: 'Whoever among you sells, let him sell in a known measure' (3)." (4). His statement in the text: "For qiyas rejects salam" does not refer to legal qiyas, but rather to the generality of the text: "Do not sell what you do not possess" (5), which contradicts the sale of salam. Here, he refers to the application of the text to its instances as qiyas, where each instance is affirmed what is affirmed for others, which is the ruling. This is due to the generality of the text in its instances, so each instance takes the ruling of the other instance itself, as if the application of the ruling to all instances is qiyas. While the matter is, in reality, a general text that applies to its instances and is not qiyas.
