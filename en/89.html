As for Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni, he distinguishes between them. For him, "ikhalah" (الإخالة) is a descriptive term that can be imagined by the observer as a reason for the coming of the ruling. "Munasabah" (المناسبة) is the established fact that the description leads to the benefit or to what the Legislator intends. Thus, "ikhalah" is the claim of the description that leads to the coming of the ruling in accordance with the benefit, while "munasabah" is the emergence and establishment of the causal relationship between the description and the benefit that follows from it, as descriptions are signs and indicators of the ruling, and rulings have come for the sake of benefits. The establishment of the relationship between the description and the ruling makes the description a motivator.

He says: "If a ruling is established in a matter that is agreed upon, and the extractor claims that it is justified by a description he has presented, he is required to substantiate his claim regarding the original matter... If a claimant asserts that the meaning he has presented is a cause for the ruling, this claim is devoid of proof in that the determination of causes is not permissible as previously discussed in the refutation of the rejecters. There must be an appearance of a reason in the mind of the extractor that necessitates the imagination of a specific meaning in its establishment, and he is required to present it" (1). Then he said: "What the scholars have relied upon and what the esteemed Abu Ishaq has accepted is the establishment of the cause of the original matter by considering its 'ikhalah' and its 'munasabah' with the ruling, while being free from impediments and invalidators and conforming to the principles" (2). He said: "If it is said: If the one who establishes a cause presents an acceptable reason in 'ikhalah', it is accepted, and it is said to him: Not every 'mukhil' (مخيل) is a cause, and not every public interest (maslaha) is an acceptable reason in rulings, from where do you claim that what you have presented is of the kind that can be relied upon? For 'ikhalah' is divided, and the reasons for public interest are absent, and the Sharia does not see the ruling as connected to all of them, and the narrators have not documented the paths of conjectures for the Companions and their directions. So if the claim of connection to every benefit is invalidated and it is not clear to us what the predecessors relied upon, how does the very 'ikhalah' indicate anything?" (3). Then he said: "If 'ikhalah' appears and the meaning is free from invalidators and the presumption is strong, then that is certainly of the kind that the predecessors relied upon" (4). Then he said: "If it is said: Then 'ikhalah' with safety is what indicates. We say: No, but if 'ikhalah' is established and the 'munasabah' is apparent and the invalidators are removed, then that aligns with the path of the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them" (5).
