Al-Shatibi divides the permissible (mubah) into two categories: the first meaning is choice (takhyir), and the second meaning is the lifting of hardship (raf' al-haraj). What is meant by choice is that it is permissible in part, while the action is required in total. What is meant by the lifting of hardship is that it is permissible in part, while the abandonment is required in total. Therefore, it is noted in the text mentioned on the previous page that the term "permissible" (jائز) is used in relation to what is recommended (mandub) in total, as well as in relation to what is obligatory (wajib) in total. As for the third category, which is what is permissible in part but disliked (makruh) in total, the term "permissible" is not mentioned; rather, it states: "there is no hardship in it" (1). He says: "The permissible is used in two senses: one is in terms of being given the choice between action and abandonment, and the other is in terms of saying: there is no hardship in it" (2).
