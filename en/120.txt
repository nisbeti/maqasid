And since this appropriate description is a hypothetical description and there is no evidence from the Sharia to indicate that it is a cause, those who do not accept the method of appropriate reasoning rejected the reasoning by the appropriate description altogether. On the other hand, most of those who reason by the appropriate description rejected reasoning by the transmitted (marasil) and the strange (gharib), and they spoke of the suitable (mulayim): which has been supported by many rulings, or at least two rulings. From the existence of evidence for the description and its abundance, they argued that the strength of the appropriate description comes from the fact that the rulings of the Sharia have been in accordance with it, or have testified to it, or that this description or meaning aligns with the principles of legal interests (masalih shar'iyya). Thus, a new issue arose, which is that considering this description in reasoning does not return to a specific origin, and consequently, it is not part of analogy (qiyas) because the existence of an origin is a pillar in analogy, and the origin is absent here. Hence, the expressions of unregulated interest (maslaha mursala) and unregulated reasoning (istidlal mursala) arise, which refers to a meaning that does not have a specific origin but whose meaning aligns with the principles of the Sharia (1).
