As for the claim that disagreement on a matter is evidence of its permissibility, this exceeds the limit, and it is something that al-Shatibi finds objectionable. He says: "This matter has exceeded the limit of sufficiency to the point that disagreement on issues has been counted among the evidences of permissibility, and reliance on the permissibility of an action based on it being disputed among the scholars has occurred both in the past and in the future" (1). He also said: "Sometimes a fatwa is issued prohibiting a matter, and it is said: Why do you prohibit it when the matter is disputed? Thus, disagreement is made a proof of permissibility merely because it is disputed, not due to any evidence" (2). An example of this is the statement of those who said that since people have disagreed about drinks and have unanimously agreed on the prohibition of grape wine while differing on other drinks, we have prohibited what they have agreed upon and permitted what is otherwise (3). After he quoted such statements as being a gross error, and that if they were correct, similar reasoning would apply to usury (riba), currency exchange (sarf), and temporary marriage (nikah al-mut'ah) because the community has disagreed on them, and that disagreement is not a proof (4), he said: "The one who says this is returning to following his desires and making the opinion that aligns with him a proof for himself, using it to shield himself; he has taken the opinion as a means to follow his whims, not as a means to attain piety, and this is further from being one who is obeying the command of the Lord and closer to being one who has taken his desires as his god" (5). What is surprising is that the proponents of these opinions criticize those who adhere to what they see as correct or preferable. He said: "Sometimes the proponent of this opinion explicitly criticizes those who adhere to the well-known opinion or the one that aligns with the evidence or is preferred among the scholars and that which the majority of Muslims follow, saying to them: You have restricted what is vast and led the people to hardship, and there is no hardship in religion, and similar statements" (6). He then commented: "All of this opinion is erroneous and ignorant of what the Sharia was established for" (7).
