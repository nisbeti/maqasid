He also states, affirming this meaning and that the ranks of the objectives (maqasid) are derived from the Sharia: "An action is considered in the Sharia based on the benefits and harms it entails. The Sharia has clarified this and distinguished between actions whose benefits are significant, making them a pillar, or whose harms are significant, making them a major sin. It has also distinguished what is not so, calling the benefits good and the harms minor sins. In this way, what is part of the fundamentals of religion and its principles is distinguished from what is part of its branches and details, and what is known as major sins is distinguished from minor sins. What the Sharia has elevated in the commandments is part of the fundamentals of religion, while what it has placed below that is part of its branches and complements. What it has deemed significant in the prohibitions is part of the major sins, while what is below that is part of the minor sins, and this is according to the extent of benefit and harm" (1).

From the previous two texts, we conclude that al-Shatibi refers to the necessities, which are five, or what cannot be established without these five, calling them the pillars or principles of religion, while what is below them he calls the complements or branches of religion. Violating the necessities is considered a major sin in terms of harms or sins.

This difference in the ranks of the objectives does not mean neglecting any objective under the pretext of preserving what is more certain, nor does it justify contradicting or changing rulings under the guise of objectives, because these objectives are established based on the extrapolation of rulings and their meanings. The rulings serve as the foundations for the objectives, and the objectives are built upon them. By nullifying the foundation, what is built upon it is also nullified. Thus, the objectives are the objectives of the Sharia, and the Sharia is fixed and does not change or alter. It is not correct to invoke the objectives for that reason. This is what is implied in al-Shatibi's statement that the Sharia is infallible, which is the subject of the next discussion.
