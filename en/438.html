Therefore, the arrival of the second text: { Indeed, those for whom the good word has preceded from Us } is an explanation and further clarification, and not an abrogation or cancellation of some of what the wording addressed.

Accordingly, al-Shatibi rejects the specification (takhsees) that the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) speak of, whether it is specification by connection (muttasil) or by disconnection (munfasil). He says: "When what has preceded is established, specification is either by disconnection or by connection. If it is by connection, such as exception, description, limit, and substitution of part, and the like, it is not in reality an exclusion of anything but rather an explanation of the speaker's intent in the generality of the wording so that the listener does not misunderstand it as anything other than what was intended, as he refers to Sibawayh's statement: 'Zayd the red' to one who does not know him is like 'Zayd alone' to one who knows him" (1). He then said: "As for specification by disconnection, it is likewise a return to clarifying the intended meaning in the general forms as previously mentioned at the beginning of the issue, and it is not in the true sense of specification as mentioned by the usuliyun (scholars of usul)" (2).

In order to reinforce his opinion, he aims to clarify a matter, which is that the forms established for generality in the language may be understood by some as general according to the linguistic convention. If a conflicting legal text is found, it is assumed that this is a specification in the sense of abrogating part of the linguistic meaning. He clarifies that what is relied upon in the meanings of words and structures is the pragmatic meaning, not the conventional meaning, and this has been clarified in the third section of the fourth chapter.
