The second: What is considered is the description that has been affirmed by a ruling but not by a text or evidence. In other words: what is considered is what has been established in accordance with a ruling or rulings. In a third expression, it is the description that can be imagined in a ruling of the established rulings supported by legal evidence. This meaning is what is intended here.

Al-Amidi said: "The description is either considered in the view of the legislator or it is not considered; if it is considered, its consideration is either by text, consensus, or by arranging the ruling in accordance with it in a case of text or consensus" (1). Al-Shawkani said: "What is meant by consideration is to bring forth the ruling in accordance with it, not to explicitly state it or allude to it; otherwise, the cause would not be derived from the correlation, which is what is meant by their saying that it has been affirmed by a specific origin. Al-Ghazali said in Shifa al-Ghalil: The meaning of the affirmation by a specific origin for the description is that it is inferred from it in that the ruling has been established legally in accordance with it" (2). For example: in the ruling of the non-inheritance of the murderer, the meaning of treating contrary to the intended purpose is observed; based on the fact that the murderer, by killing his ancestor, intends to obtain the inheritance or hasten it, and treating him contrary to his intention is a meaning present in the ruling, this meaning (treating contrary to the intended purpose) is a considered meaning. The legal ruling is the witness to this meaning, or it can be said that this meaning, which has not been indicated by a text or consensus, has come with a ruling in accordance with it. This is the meaning of consideration, which is different from the consideration that is indicated by a text or consensus regarding the description or meaning. The latter has no disagreement, unlike the testimony of the ruling for the description or the coming of a ruling in accordance with the description, regarding the acceptance of this consideration there is significant disagreement.

Types of consideration:

If the consideration is for the very description present in the ruling or its basis, it is a consideration for the very description or its particularity in the ruling. If the consideration is for a description broader than the description present in the ruling or its basis, it is a consideration for the generality of the description or its genus in the ruling.
