... One of the conditions for seeking a concession (rukhsah) is that the concession must be in accordance with the Sharia, so one should not escape from the hardship that the Sharia has allowed him to escape from except through the Sharia itself. He should take what the Sharia has granted him in terms of alleviation and not follow his whims. This matter pertains to the concepts of good and evil, and there is no room for reason in it. He said: "Every difficult matter for which the legislator has provided an exit, the legislator intends for the obligated person to seek that exit if he wishes, as is the case with the legal concessions that provide an exit from hardships. If the obligated person seeks to exit in the manner prescribed, he will be complying with the command of the legislator and taking precautions in his matter. If he does not do so, he will fall into two prohibitions: the first is his opposition to the intention of the legislator, whether that opposition is in a mandatory, recommended, or permissible act; and the second is that he will close the doors of ease upon himself and lose the exit from that difficult matter from which he sought to escape in a manner not prescribed for him" (1).

... And he said: "The obligated person in seeking alleviation is commanded to seek it from the prescribed means, because what is sought in terms of alleviation is certainly attainable in both the present and the future in general. If he seeks it from another path, what he seeks in terms of alleviation will not be certain nor even probable, neither in the present nor in the future, neither in general nor in detail" (2). And he said: "Returning to the means established by the legislator is a return to the means of attaining benefit and alleviation in its entirety, unlike returning to what contradicts it" (3).
