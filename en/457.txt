The first condition for understanding the inner meaning (batin) is understanding the outer meaning (zahir), and the indications of the texts regarding their meanings are not definitive according to al-Shatibi, but they are confined to the outer meanings of the texts. If the conditions for understanding the outer meaning are fulfilled as explained above, then there are other conditions to determine the intended meaning of the text, that is, the inner meaning, whether with certainty or preference. If these conditions are met, an understanding that is acceptable in Sharia is achieved.
The text can have more than one meaning based on the outer meaning, and the potential outer meaning has conditions that must be met for it to be accepted. As previously explained, the meaning must refer to a definitive meaning established through induction, in that it must either be included within it or serve it. If this potential outer meaning contradicts a general principle in Sharia, it must be abandoned and disregarded.
One of the conditions for accepting the potential outer meaning is that it has a witness that favors it over other possible meanings. This is because the potential meanings of the outer text—those meanings that meet the conditions of the outer meaning—may have more than one general principle associated with them, and selecting one over the others without a basis for preference is an unjustified preference, which is not valid, as it constitutes a statement in Sharia and in the Qur'an without evidence. The final condition is that there should be no opposition to the meaning, as opposition serves as an indication of its lesser validity.
