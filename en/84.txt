As for the distinction between the specified cause (illa mansusah) and the inferred cause (illa mustanbatah), it is based on the fact that the specified cause, as in the above hadith, is called a cause even if it is a cause for the ruling of the original only, meaning it is a limited cause. The prohibition regarding the inferred cause is because the inferred cause is based on the principle of reasoning, not on the evidence of texts or consensus. Accordingly, the establishment of a second cause is considered a refutation of the first cause.
9- Among the matters they differed on is the permissibility of specifying the cause. Al-Amidi said: "They differed on the permissibility of specifying the inferred cause; most of the followers of Abu Hanifa, Malik, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal permitted it, while most of the followers of al-Shafi'i prohibited it. It has been said that this is reported from al-Shafi'i. Then, those who permit its specification agreed on the permissibility of specifying the specified cause, and they differed on the permissibility of specifying the inferred cause if there is no impediment or loss of condition in the place of deviation. Most prohibited it, while a minority permitted it. Those who prohibit the specification of the inferred cause differed on the permissibility of specifying the specified cause."(1)
The most important factor in this difference, in general, is the difference in terminology regarding the meanings of specified and inferred. Those who consider the specified cause to be that which comes through one of the two paths of text or consensus permit the specification of the specified cause and consider it akin to the specification of other texts. These individuals consider the inferred cause to be that which comes through the path of indication and hint, and they differ on the permissibility of specifying it, rejecting all other paths.
