As for the intention of compliance and drawing closer to Allah devoid of any other intention, this is the highest rank of deeds and the purest. As for the intention of seeking personal gain, if it stems from ostentation (riya), it is blameworthy (1), whether in acts of worship or in customary practices, and whether it is the ostentation of associating partners with Allah or mere ostentation. Al-Qarafi said: "Know that ostentation in acts of worship is associating partners with Allah, and it leads to sin, wrongdoing, and invalidation" (2). He also said: "The purposes of ostentation are three: seeking honor, gaining worldly benefits, and averting worldly harms. The latter two stem from the first, for when one seeks honor, worldly benefits are drawn to him and harms are repelled from him; this is the ultimate purpose in reality. Thus, this is the principle of ostentation that invalidates deeds, as agreed upon by consensus" (3). Al-Ghazali said: "Whoever is motivated solely by ostentation is at risk of destruction" (4).

As for seeking personal gain without ostentation, if it is devoid of the intention of compliance, it is invalid in acts of worship because acts of worship— as previously clarified— require intention. Al-Ghazali said: "Acts of obedience are linked to intentions in their validity and in the multiplication of their merit" (5).
