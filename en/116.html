The second: It must be free from opposition and nullification. This means that the description should not be annulled. Nullification is the text that states the cancellation of the description, as in the fatwa regarding fasting for two consecutive months for the sultan who engaged in sexual relations during the month of Ramadan. As for opposition, it refers to the existence of another ruling or rulings that contradict or oppose the description. This is similar to the concession for breaking the fast and combining the two obligatory prayers while traveling for the indulgent king. These rulings opposed the description of hardship.

Therefore, those who reason based on the appropriate must present the description against the principles, meaning against the rulings, to clarify its lack of opposition. Al-Bazdawi said: "Justice for us is the effect... Some of al-Shafi'i's companions said that his justice is based on being capable, then presenting it against the principles as a precaution for its safety from contradiction and opposition. Some of his companions said rather that his justice is based on presenting it against the principles, and if no principle contradicts or opposes it, it becomes just. It must be presented against two or more principles. According to the first view, it is permissible to act upon it before presentation, and according to the second, it is not permissible because it becomes a proof through it. According to the first opinion, it became a proof by being capable" (1). Then he said: "As for presenting it against the principles, it does not achieve the adjustment" (2) meaning according to him.

The commentator in Kashf al-Asrar said: "The contradiction of the description: nullifying itself by an effect, text, or consensus that contradicts it, or presenting a case where the ruling deviates from the description. The opposition of the description: presenting another description that necessitates a contradiction to what that description required without addressing the description itself. The meaning of presenting the description against the principles is that it is compared with the laws of Sharia; if it matches and is free from nullifications and impediments, then the principles have testified to its validity and it has become a proof" (3).
