The principle of matters is based on their objectives (1):

__________

(1) Some books on analogies and similarities mention that some jurists trace certain legal schools back to a few dozen principles. Refer to the book of analogies and similarities by al-Suyuti, pages 35 - 38, and the book of analogies and similarities by Imam Taj al-Din al-Subki, pages 10 - 12. Al-Suyuti mentioned that the entire Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) school can be reduced to four principles: the first: certainty is not overruled by doubt, the second: hardship brings about ease, the third: harm must be eliminated, and the fourth: custom is authoritative. Among what he said is: "Some later scholars have remarked that the status of these four as the foundations of jurisprudence is debatable, as most of it does not return to them except through intermediaries and effort." Some jurists added a fifth principle to these, which is: "matters are based on their objectives," based on the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Actions are judged by intentions." He also said: "Islam is built upon five [pillars]." [This hadith was narrated by al-Bukhari in the Book of Faith, number (7), (4153), and by Muslim in the chapter on the pillars of Islam and its great foundations, number (16), (20), (21), and by Ahmad, number (4567), and by al-Tirmidhi, number (2534), who said it is good and authentic, and by al-Nasa'i, number (4915)]. Jurisprudence is based on five, and al-'Ala'i said: this is very good, for Imam al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "This hadith encompasses a third of knowledge." page 37.
