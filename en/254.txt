Thus, the issue in this matter has been presented, and before explaining its solution and removal, I would like to point out that Dr. Daraz did not grasp the true meaning intended by al-Shatibi with the terms "maslaha" (public interest) and "mafsada" (harm), understanding them in the sense of benefit and harm. Therefore, he attributed to al-Shatibi the statement that what predominates in terms of benefit is the maslaha, and what is predominant in terms of harm is the mafsada. This is incorrect, and this was not al-Shatibi's statement; rather, he said that what predominates in terms of maslaha is the maslaha understood by custom, and similarly for mafsada. The two matters are different. The maslaha according to al-Shatibi is what has been definitively established as intended by the Lawgiver, and the mafsada is what has been definitively established as prohibited by the Law. As for benefits and harms, they pertain to the interests of the servant and what he loves and dislikes; thus, some benefits are prohibited and some harms are permissible. Therefore, al-Shatibi objected to al-Razi, stating after establishing that the masalih (benefits) and mafasid (harms) are considered in relation to the sustenance of this life for the afterlife: "It is not valid to maintain that the principle regarding benefits is permissibility and regarding harms is prohibition as established by al-Fakhr al-Razi, for there is hardly any true benefit or true harm; rather, most of them are relative" (1), and he adds: "And the masalih and mafasid, if they are related to the address of the Lawgiver... how can it be permissible to generalize this statement that the principle regarding benefits is permissibility and regarding harms is prohibition" (2). Additionally, there is another error, which is the failure to distinguish between al-Shatibi's intention in the fifth issue and his intention in the eighth issue. Dr. Daraz based his objection on al-Shatibi's statement in the fifth issue: "By masalih, I mean what pertains to the establishment of human life and the completion of his existence and attaining what is necessitated by his carnal and rational attributes in general, so that it is a blessing in general."
