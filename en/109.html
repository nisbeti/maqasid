Dr. Hito says: "As for attributing the statement to Malik with this type of remote reasoning, it is a disputed attribution" (1). Dr. Hito has proven the error of attributing many statements to Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) (2).

This degree of contradiction arises from the differing meanings of "remote" (mursal) among the disputants and the transmitters. The term "remote" has been used to mean that which is not known to be abrogated or disregarded at all, meaning no ruling has been witnessed for it. It has also been used to mean appropriate or strange.

As for the first meaning: everyone agrees on rejecting it. This is what is meant by denying its attribution to the Shafi'is and Hanafis and by denying it from Malik.

If it is remote in an unrestricted sense, here are some statements regarding it: Al-Amidi said: "The interests (masalih) are divided according to the testimony of the Sharia into those that are considered, those that are abrogated, and those that have not been witnessed for either consideration or abrogation. The latter is referred to as the appropriate remote... This category is uncertain between the two categories, and its attachment to one is not more valid than the other. Therefore, it is not permissible to use it as evidence without a witness of consideration that is known to be of the considered category rather than the abrogated" (3).

Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam said: "And there is no ruling except for Him (i.e., for Allah), so His rulings are derived from the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, sound analogies, and accepted reasoning. No one has the right to prefer or use a remote interest, nor to follow anyone whom he has not been commanded to follow" (4).
