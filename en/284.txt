The Third Topic: ... Between the Sharia and its Objectives or Between Worship and Following Meanings.

Chapter Five
The Intention of the Legislator in Establishing the Sharia for Obligations According to It
And in the Inclusion of the Obligor Under Its Rules

Introduction:
This chapter is to discuss the third and fourth types of the objectives of the legislator. What is noteworthy here is al-Shatibi's expression in the third type: "for obligations according to it," especially when we consider the title of the fourth type: "the inclusion of the obligor under the rules of the Sharia." What is the difference between the two types?
The difference between them can be understood if we refer to the fourth type as the intention of the legislator in establishing the Sharia for obligations through it, which means the intention of worship and compliance. As for the third type, it is the obligation according to it, meaning the implication of worship and compliance, which is burden and hardship. It has been understood from the legislator that the intention is for the benefit of the servants and for ease, which is contrary to hardship and pain. The legislator intends through the Sharia to alleviate hardship. Is there a contradiction here? Does obligation cease with hardship? Is there a limit to the hardship that would lift the obligation, and how can we reconcile the original intention of worship and compliance with the original intention of benefit and alleviation of hardship?
This is the subject of the chapter, which I have divided into three sections: the first to clarify al-Shatibi's intent and methodology in explaining the intention of the legislator in establishing the Sharia for obligations according to it, and the second and third to clarify his methodology in explaining the intention of the legislator in including the obligor under the rules of the Sharia.

The First Topic
The Intention of Obligation According to the Sharia

The Meaning of This Type:
The subject of this type is that the legislator does not impose obligations that are beyond the capacity of the obligor, nor those that entail excessive hardship, because one of the objectives of the legislator is, from the outset, to alleviate hardship and remove constraints. However, absolute hardship cannot be entirely avoided, as it is inherent in every obligation. What, then, is the criterion for the hardship that Allah does not impose obligations upon, and the hardship for which Allah, Exalted is He, obligates actions that are accompanied by similar hardship?
