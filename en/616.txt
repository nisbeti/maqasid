The principles (usul) according to al-Shatibi are definitive matters intended by the Legislator. They are established through the induction of particulars, and the particulars are meanings that the texts can bear, or wisdoms and interests that may be intended by the Legislator through the rulings. If many particulars are surveyed and a definitive meaning is established based on the induction, then this meaning is a universal principle. The particulars whose meanings relate to this principle, that is, those that are included in it or serve it, are branches of the principle. However, before the definitive meaning is derived, the particular remains a particular and cannot be considered a branch nor can it be deemed as legislation. Accordingly, the principles are definitive universals. He said: "Some knowledge is from the essence of knowledge, and some is from the embellishments of knowledge, not from its essence, and some is neither from its essence nor from its embellishments. These are three categories: the first category is the principle and the relied-upon, which is the basis of inquiry and to which the objectives of the firmly rooted (rasikhun) ultimately lead, and that is what is definitive or related to a definitive principle. The blessed Muhammadan Sharia is revealed in this manner. Therefore, it is preserved in its principles and branches" (1). He then said: "They are the necessities, the needs, and the enhancements, and what completes them and supplements their aspects, and these are the principles of the Sharia, and definitive evidence has been established for their consideration, and all branches are based upon them, so there is no doubt that it is a foundational science with a solid basis and stable pillars" (2). He then said: "Because knowledge of them is derived from the general induction that organizes the scattered individuals until they become in the mind a collection of consistent, general, stable universals, not vanishing or changing, and ruling rather than being ruled upon, and these are the characteristics of rational universals. Furthermore, the universals are derived from existence, which is a conventional matter, not a rational one, thus they are equal to the legal universals in this regard, and the distinction between them is removed" (3).
