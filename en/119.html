This amount of disagreement, like that between al-Razi and al-Shatibi, is where the former asserts the invalidity of the principle of reasoning, while the latter asserts its validity and its presence in all details of the Sharia. He firmly states that al-Razi is mistaken by saying: "Induction is something that neither al-Razi nor anyone else disputes," emphasizing the importance of the principle of reasoning and its significance for the method of suitability and for the use of general evidence. Therefore, it is necessary to address this principle with sufficient detail.

It should be noted that there is a significant difference between the methodologies of al-Amidi and al-Shatibi regarding reasoning. There is also a distinction between al-Shatibi's concept of public interests (masalih) and harms (mafasid) and the concept of those who preceded him who spoke of reasoning based on public interests and harms. First Topic

The arguments that led to the idea of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)

The evolution of the debate from the method of suitability to general evidence:

We previously discussed suitability as a method of reasoning. We covered the categories of suitable descriptions and their conditions, as well as the disagreement regarding them. The suitable description, according to those who advocate it, must be considered valid in Sharia, and its validity is established by the coming of a ruling in accordance with it. Thus, the ruling that attests to the description serves as a principle, and the description itself is the reason, making reasoning based on suitability akin to analogy (qiyas).
