Since understanding the inner meaning cannot be achieved without understanding the outer meaning, to grasp al-Shatibi's methodology in interpreting the Qur'an, one must understand his approach to both the outer and inner meanings. It is always important to note what he stipulates for the application of evidence, which is that the evidence must be either definitive (qat'i) or related to a definitive meaning; and here, the meaning is that the inner meaning must be either definitive or related to something definitive. He said: "In every issue for which one seeks to acquire knowledge in the most complete manner, one must refer to its origin in the Qur'an. If it is explicitly stated or its type or genus is mentioned, then that is sufficient; otherwise, the levels of consideration in it are multiple" (1). He then said: "It has been previously established in the first section of the Book of Evidence that every legal evidence is either definitive or related to something definitive, and the highest reference for the definitive is the Noble Qur'an, which is the first point of reference" (2).

How to understand the outer meaning:

The texts of the Qur'an are Arabic in their words, structures, and styles, and understanding their implications is constrained by this. Although they may not be definitively indicative, their possible implications are limited to the apparent meanings of the texts, as clarified earlier. He said: "The fact that the inner meaning is intended in the discourse has also been demonstrated from what was mentioned in the previous issue, but it is subject to two conditions. The first is that it must be valid according to the implications established in the Arabic language and must align with Arabic objectives" (3). He stated that this is "evident from the principle that the Qur'an is Arabic; for if it had an understanding that is not implied by the speech of the Arabs, it would not be described as Arabic in the absolute sense, and because it is a concept attributed to the Qur'an, there is nothing in its words or meanings that indicates it. What is like this cannot be attributed to it at all, as its attribution is not more valid than the attribution of its opposite, nor is there a criterion that indicates one over the other. Thus, affirming one of them is mere assumption and speculation about the apparent Qur'an, and in this case, its speaker falls under the sin of speaking about the Book of Allah without knowledge, and the evidence mentioned that the Qur'an is Arabic applies here" (4).
