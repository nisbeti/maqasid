If these objectives are limited, according to others besides al-Shatibi, to the objectives of the Sharia, then the objectives of the legislator, according to al-Shatibi, are of four types, one of which is the objectives of the Sharia. He says: "The objectives that are considered fall into two categories: one pertains to the intention of the legislator, and the other pertains to the intention of the obligated person. The first is considered from the perspective of the legislator's intention in establishing the Sharia from the outset, from the perspective of his intention in establishing it for understanding, from the perspective of his intention in establishing it for obligation according to it, and from the perspective of his intention in bringing the obligated person under its ruling. These are four types" (1).

The definitions mentioned revolve around the necessary, need-based, and enhancement objectives of the Sharia, which are only the first type, that is, the intention of the legislator in establishing the Sharia from the outset. This is one aspect of the shortcomings in attempts to define the objectives, and there is another matter in these definitions that contradicts al-Shatibi's intent regarding this type of objectives.

That is, all these definitions suggest that the objectives are the interests and rulings, meaning they are the appropriate meanings that the mind accepts. This understanding, once again, if it is correct according to what is held by others besides al-Shatibi, does not hold true according to what is held by al-Shatibi. For example, Sheikh Ibn Ashur defined them as objectives for achieving beneficial objectives for people or for preserving their public interests. Dr. al-Raisuni's definition is that they are the goals for which the Sharia was established to achieve the benefit of the servants. Dr. al-Abidi defined them as the rulings. As for Professor Allal al-Fasi, he limited them to the goals of the rulings.

Whereas the objectives according to al-Shatibi are what is established as intended by the legislator regardless of whether it is an interest in the habitual sense, or whether it is something that minds accept, or whether it is appropriate or inappropriate.
