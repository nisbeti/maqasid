Al-Shatibi mentions cases that may be raised against his previous statement, where it is thought that the interests (masalih) or the intended meanings of the rulings are more deserving than the apparent text or wording. He says: "It cannot be said that neglecting the meanings is a disregard for the known objectives of the Legislator, as in the saying that it is not permissible to perform ablution with water that a person has urinated in; if he urinated in a vessel and then poured it into the water, it is permissible to perform ablution with it" (1), because the prohibition here regarding the second matter does not arise from following the meanings, that is, the meanings of the rulings, but rather from the indication of the wording itself; this is a type of linguistic indication (2). He said: "Following the very texts of the rulings, which are the original, is obligatory because they, along with the meanings, are like the original with the branch. It is not correct to follow the branch while disregarding the original" (3). However, it is the meaning of the wording that must be considered; its indication is not definitive, thus it must be understood in light of other matters that assist in this understanding. He said: "The second of the two considerations is from the perspective of what is understood from the commands and prohibitions as a legal intent based on induction and the accompanying contextual or verbal evidence that indicates the specific interests in the commanded actions and the harms in the prohibited actions" (4).
