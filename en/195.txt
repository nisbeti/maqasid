He says: "If it is said that there is no harm in what is permissible (mubah), then it is not included under the choice between action and inaction" (1). Then he said: "The category that requires action in its entirety is the one in which choice is given between action and inaction, as in His saying: { Your women are a tilth for you, so go to your tilth as you will } (2), and His saying: { And eat from both of them abundantly wherever you wish } (3)... This is indeed a true choice... As for the category that requires inaction in its entirety, we do not know of anything in the Sharia that indicates a true choice in it textually; rather, it is either silent about it or refers to some of it with a phrase that removes it from the ruling of explicit choice, such as naming this world as play and amusement in the context of reproach for those who lean towards it, for it implies that amusement is not something one has a choice in" (4). He said: "In general, it (i.e., the permissible) is of four categories: the first is that it serves a matter that requires action, the second is that it serves a matter that requires inaction, the third is that it serves a matter that allows for choice. The fourth is that it does not contain any of that" (5).

We may wonder, regarding this classification of the permissible and this distinction between the two categories, whether there is some confusion in Al-Shatibi's thought. We have cited his text in defining the permissible as it appears in the first part, p. 68: "What is permissible according to the legislator is that which one has a choice in between action and inaction," and here he mentions that some of the permissible is not something one has a choice in!!
