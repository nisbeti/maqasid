... It clarifies the meaning of the hardships that the legislator intended to alleviate, and thus it is forbidden for the servant to seek them. These are the hardships that are beyond the norm, which lead to a cessation of the required actions or some of them, or that cause a disruption in the individual. Such is the case with hardship. It is intended to be alleviated by law. Among the hardships is that the obligated person imposes upon themselves actions that, due to their nature or their abundance, lead to a shortcoming in their duties. He says: "Know that hardship is lifted from the obligated person for two reasons: the first is the fear of being cut off from the path and the aversion to worship and the dislike of obligation, which encompasses the fear of causing corruption to their body, mind, wealth, or state. The second is the fear of shortcoming when competing obligations related to the servant of different types arise, such as their duty to their family and children alongside other obligations that may come in the way. Perhaps delving into some actions will distract them from others and prevent the obligated person from fulfilling them, and perhaps they intend to burden both sides with excessive scrutiny, thus cutting them off from both" (1). Here, there is a conflict between the principle of compliance and the principle of alleviating hardship, which is a genuine hardship that is legally termed as hardship, meaning that which the legislator intended to alleviate.
... For such hardships, it is legislated to ease the obligation or grant concessions, making these hardships reasons for licenses. However, it should be noted that some ordinary hardships may be burdensome for certain obligated individuals, while some burdensome hardships for the general public may be ordinary for some. Likewise, there are places in the Sharia where licenses exist without any significant or burdensome hardship. So what is the meaning of a license, and how can the obligation be eased or lifted if its cause is found?
