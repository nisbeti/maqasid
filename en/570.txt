The mentioned point in this regard often leads to error, as it is considered among the public interest (maslaha) based on its definition, which states that it is something that the Sharia has remained silent about, neither affirming nor negating it. Here, it is rejected, and consensus on this is conveyed. Then it clarifies what the public interests (masalih mursala) are that the specific evidences have remained silent about, neither affirming nor negating them, saying: "Secondly, it must align with the actions of the Sharia, which means that there exists a type of this meaning that the legislator has considered in general without specific evidence, which is the general reasoning known as public interests (masalih mursala)" (1).
Thus, it is incorrect to define public interests as those that the legislator has left unclarified and has not mentioned either affirmatively or negatively. With this definition, it is rejected without disagreement, and therefore, it is not correct to make a public interest a proof merely because it is a public interest. Rather, this is the view of the proponents of rational good and evil, and this issue is fundamentally resolved. Among the evidences in this topic is al-Shatibi's statement: "The correspondence does not necessitate a ruling for itself; rather, this is the view of the proponents of rational good." He also conveyed the consensus on the prohibition of reasoning solely based on public interest, even if it were not negated, as stated earlier regarding what the specific evidences have not affirmed or negated: "Thus, it is not correct to justify it or base a ruling upon it by consensus, and such a thing is legislation from the one who claims it, so it cannot be accepted." Al-Razi conveyed the consensus on this matter when he said: "It is appropriate, but does not align, nor does it have a specific foundational evidence; thus, this is rejected by consensus. An example of this is the disinheritance of the killer, opposing him with the contradiction of his intention, if we assume that there is no text regarding it" (2).
