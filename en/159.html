As for the technical term, there is no agreed-upon or established definition. The accepted definition in terminology is the linguistic amount present in the term. It is not possible to establish a technical definition for the objectives (maqasid) except after understanding what is meant by them, as the judgment on a thing is contingent upon its conception.

Al-Shatibi did not provide a technical definition for the objectives, but what is found in his book (al-Muwafaqat), especially in its second part, serves to define what he meant by the objectives. There is no harm in this; in fact, there is little benefit in presenting a text that does not help the reader understand the defined reality. Such definitions can only be grasped by someone who has previously understood their reality. Therefore, defining by means of a limit does not benefit him at all; rather, it judges the definition according to his understanding of the defined. Some later scholars have attempted to provide a definition for the objectives, limiting them to the objectives of the Sharia, and perhaps some have referred to objectives for the Sharia and objectives for people. However, they did not highlight the distinction between the objectives of the Sharia and the objectives of the legislator. Sheikh Muhammad al-Tahir ibn Ashour (may Allah have mercy on him) spoke of general objectives and specific objectives, defining each of them. He said: "The general objectives of legislation are the meanings and rulings considered by the legislator in all circumstances of legislation, which include the characteristics of the Sharia and its general purpose, and the meanings that legislation cannot be devoid of considering; this also includes meanings of rulings that are not considered in all types of rulings but are noted in many of them" (1). He said regarding the specific objectives: "The intended modalities for the legislator to achieve the beneficial objectives of people or to preserve their public interests in their private actions so that their efforts in their private interests do not nullify what has been established for them in obtaining their public interests" (2).
