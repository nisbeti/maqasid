And based on that, if a follower (muqallid) is faced with an issue, he should not proceed until he knows Allah's ruling on it, and this is the requirement of obligation and piety (taqwa) (1). Therefore, he must ask those who are knowledgeable about it, who are the people of insight and deduction. He said: "When a religious issue arises for the follower, it is not permissible for him in religion except to ask about it" (2). And he said: "The questioner should not ask someone whose answer is not considered valid in the Sharia" (3). And he said: "If it becomes necessary for him to ask, it is his right to ask only from someone who is among the people of that particular matter he is inquiring about" (4).

(1) Al-Shatibi argued that Allah's statement: { And fear Allah, and Allah will teach you } (Surah Al-Baqarah, 282) is not as many people understand, but rather as the scholars in the field of grammar establish, meaning that Allah teaches you in every case, so fear Him. Thus, the second, which is knowledge, is the cause of the first, which is piety. The command to be pious is therefore contingent upon the attainment of knowledge, indicating that knowledge precedes action in a meaningful way. See: Al-Muwafaqat, 4/151-152.
