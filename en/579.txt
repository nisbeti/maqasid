The specification in the sale of salam means that the text did not apply to the sale of salam, which is one of the forms of sale, so it was a specification of a general text and not a specification of analogy (qiyas). It is a separation of the sale of salam from its counterparts and granting it a ruling different from that of its counterparts. Therefore, it is said that the definition of Abu al-Hasan al-Karakhī is the most precise of definitions. The same applies to leasing (ijara) and the continuation of fasting (sawm); the lease contract is a contract for a benefit that does not exist at the time of the contract and is only fulfilled later. He believes that analogy necessitates the prohibition of the lease contract. The implication is that leasing is a type of sale, and applying the rules of sale to it contradicts the lease contract. This is what he calls analogy, and since the texts have permitted leasing, it takes precedence over analogy. This is, in fact, a specification, and specification always involves a conflict between the general and the specific. Here, there is a conflict between the general text that he called analogy and the specific text, which can also be referred to as its application in its instances as analogy. Therefore, this process of specification, in which the specific is given precedence over the general, is called acting according to the stronger of the two analogies, and he refers to it as istihsan (juridical preference). Some have called it acting according to the stronger of the two evidences. Hence, it was also said that the strongest opinion is the opinion of al-Karakhī: it is the separation of the issue from its counterparts, etc.
This is more clearly illustrated in his example regarding the continuation of fasting with the act of one who forgets. He said in Kashf al-Asrar: "Similarly, eating forgetfully invalidates the fast according to analogy because something cannot remain in existence with the presence of what contradicts it, like purity with ritual impurity and seclusion (i'tikaf) with exiting without necessity. However, it is set aside by the evidence, which is his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): 'Continue your fast, for indeed Allah has fed you and given you drink' "(1), and this indicates the implication of Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with him): 'Were it not for the saying of the people, I would have said: he must make up the fast,' referring to the narration of the evidence "(2). Here, too, there is, in fact, no analogy that necessitates the invalidation of the fast, which means that what is meant by analogy is not the legal analogy in the technical sense, but rather the application of the ruling in all its instances. The ruling is that eating invalidates fasting, and eating forgetfully is still eating, so it should invalidate the fast. What is meant by analogy is the general text, and then it was specified by the evidence, so he did not rule on this eating as he would on its counterparts because it was forgetful eating, and the evidence exempted the forgetful person. Thus, istihsan was a specification and a separation of the issue from its counterparts.
