The first: that what is meant by it is general in what is not possible to achieve and in what is possible to achieve if it is unbearable, like a disabled person who attempts to stand, or a person who attempts to fly in terms of it being a hardship that is of no benefit; thus, its ruling is like that of what is not possible to achieve, and this has been clarified.

The second: that what is meant by it is the burdensome hardship, that is, the one that is beyond the usual, which leads to a disturbance in the mind or soul, or compels one to abandon obligations or commit prohibitions. Such hardship does not warrant a demand for what necessitates it, and it is the place of concession (rukhsa).

The third: that what is meant by hardship is the ordinary hardship, that is, the non-burdensome one, which accompanies ordinary actions, and what these actions require in terms of effort and cost. Such hardships have no effect on the obligation.

The fourth: that what is meant by hardship is the opposition to desires, in that the obligation takes the obligated person away from indulging in their desires, and such hardship has no effect on the obligation. The legislator does not intend to remove it.
