He said: "The intention of the legislator (shari') for the accountable person (mukallaf) is that their intention in action should align with the intention in legislation" (1), meaning that the accountable person, even if they act with the intention of compliance in accordance with the command and prohibition, that is, according to the original objectives (maqasid), must also ensure that the intended outcomes, if they exist for the accountable person (2), align with the subsidiary objectives. If the accountable person intends from the action a benefit, a gain, or an outcome, then this outcome must be one that the legislator intended to achieve through this action. Otherwise, intending it through this action or this means signifies a contradiction to the intention of the legislator. The objectives of the legislation have been established by the legislator through specific actions, so if the accountable person intends through these actions objectives other than those, this constitutes a violation in that the accountable person has made these objectives means to others, which they are not in the law, and it is also a violation in that the legal objectives intended by the accountable person have specific actions designated by the legislator, thus adopting actions other than what the legislator has established is neglecting what Allah has legislated. Al-Shatibi said: "Whoever seeks in the obligations of the Sharia something other than what it was legislated for has contradicted the Sharia, and whoever contradicts it, their action in contradiction is void. So whoever seeks in the obligations of the Sharia what was not legislated for them, their action is void" (3). He also said: "If the accountable person intends exactly what the legislator intended by permission, they have sought the path of benefit in its most complete form, and they are worthy of attaining it. But if they intend something other than what the legislator intended, this is usually due to the misconception that the benefit lies in what they intended, for the rational person does not directly intend the path of harm. They have made what the legislator intended neglected in consideration, and what the legislator neglected as an intended consideration, which is a clear opposition to the Sharia" (4).
