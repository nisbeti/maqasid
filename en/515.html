As for the second point, which is the consideration of the consequences of actions, it also relates to the alignment with the intention of the Legislator (Shari') from the Sharia. The Legislator has enacted the ruling for a purpose. There may be situations where the ruling itself undermines the purpose for which it was originally established, as in the case of legal tricks (hiyal) and means (dhara'i). Therefore, it is incumbent upon the jurist (mujtahid) during the process of deriving legal rulings to consider the action itself and its consequences. Otherwise, he would undermine the intention of the Legislator in his legislation. He said: "The consideration of the consequences of actions is a legally intended objective, whether the actions are in agreement or in opposition. This is because the jurist does not rule on an action performed by the accountable person (mukallaf) with either approval or disapproval except after considering what that action will lead to. It may be permissible due to a benefit it brings or a harm it prevents, but it has consequences contrary to what was intended. Conversely, it may be impermissible due to a harm that arises from it or a benefit that is repelled by it, but it has consequences contrary to that." (1)
