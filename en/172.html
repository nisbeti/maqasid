These texts above clarify his intention regarding induction, and that induction is the only way to reach certainty in religious matters, and that induction is not for the evidences which are the texts, but rather for their meanings, and their meanings are their linguistic implications, rulings, and consequences. All of these are speculative if one relies on isolated texts; thus, they should not be relied upon in legislation unless they are transmitted through continuous narration (tawatur). The tawatur intended here is the tawatur of meanings. Therefore, we see him say: "It is similar to the meaningful tawatur" (1), and look at his statement in the previous text: "Unlike the principles, which are derived from the induction of the implications of the evidences in general." He did not say: "from the induction of the evidences." Rather, he said: "from the induction of their implications," and their implications are their implications or meanings. Then his statement: "in general," does not limit the meanings to linguistic implications but also includes their rulings, objectives, or causes.
