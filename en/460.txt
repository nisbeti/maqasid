As for what does not appear to be the case, al-Shatibi provided examples of it, including his statement: "There are problematic interpretations in the Qur'an that may fall into this category (1) or into the category of the correct hidden meanings, which are attributed to people among the scholars, and perhaps some of them are attributed to the righteous predecessors. Among these are the initials of the chapters such as { Alif } and { Lam } and { Meem } "(2). He said: "They narrate from Ibn Abbas that { Alif } is for Allah, { Lam } is for Gabriel, and { Meem } is for Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him). If this is correct in transmission, it is problematic because this pattern of interpretation has not been established in the speech of the Arabs in this manner at all; rather, it has appeared similarly only when supported by verbal or contextual evidence" (3). He said: "It has also been reported that these initials are secrets whose interpretation is known only to Allah, and this is the most apparent opinion, so they fall into the category of ambiguous verses" (4). Another example he provided is found in the interpretation of the verse { So do not make equals to Allah } (5), meaning opposites, where it was said that the greatest of the equals is the commanding soul (6). This is problematic for al-Shatibi, because the apparent meaning according to his conditions, based on the reasons for revelation and the implications of the contextual clues, is that the equals are the idols and not the soul.
