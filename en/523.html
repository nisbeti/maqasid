These three types can be interrupted by the cessation of the mujtahids (scholars capable of independent reasoning), but the obligation does not cease with their interruption. He said: "The new occurrences that have no precedent in earlier times are few compared to what has preceded due to the broad scope of understanding and ijtihad (independent reasoning) among the earlier scholars, so it is permissible to follow them in this matter because it is the essence of the Sharia (Islamic law). The Sharia does not become inactive due to the cessation of some particulars, just as if one were to assume the inability to establish the criteria in some particulars while others remain, there is no harm to the Sharia in that."(1)

The Second Topic

Conditions of Ijtihad(2) and Fatwa

The subject of this topic:

The intention of the legislator in enacting laws is to free the obligated person from the call of their desires so that they may be a servant of Allah by choice. Therefore, they must seek the ruling of Allah in their actions. If they are capable of deriving the rulings themselves, they are a mujtahid; if not, they are a muqallid (follower) and must strive to know the ruling of Allah through someone capable of doing so, which is the mujtahid. The mujtahids are not infallible from error, and they differ in knowledge, jurisprudence, and piety, as well as in what makes them mujtahids whose opinions can be followed. They also differ in their ijtihadat (independent reasoning) and fatwas (legal opinions). Just as the mujtahid must strive to understand the intention of the legislator through the understanding of the legal evidences, so too must the muqallid strive to align with the intention of the legislator by following the one whom they believe will achieve that for them. Therefore, it is necessary to know the characteristics and signs that the muqallid must verify or seek assistance with in order to identify the mujtahid they will follow or the fatwa they will adhere to.
