"The Sharia (law) operates in its obligations according to the middle, most just path, taking from both sides with a share that has no bias in it, falling under the earnings of the servant without hardship upon him or dissolution" (1). With this meaning, al-Shatibi concludes this type of objectives of the legislator, and this meaning has been clarified previously, and there is no need to reiterate it and the intention of al-Shatibi behind it, except for what sometimes circulates from meanings that have no reality concerning the middle and moderation or the moderation of the Sharia. The concept of the middle is the actual matter between two extremes, such that one of them signifies strictness and the other signifies dissolution, or one signifies excess and the other signifies deficiency, or what may be imagined from two sides on either side of the middle. Accordingly, the term middle is used to mean or with the intention of improvement or beautification or acceptance of what occurs between two extremes. The same can be said about justice. Dr. Muhammad Said Ramadan al-Bouti states in his presentation of evidence regarding the consideration of the Sharia for public interests: "His Exalted is He's saying: { Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice. Excellent is that which Allah instructs you. Indeed, Allah is ever Hearing and Seeing }" (2). The essence of justice between two things or two persons is the equation and balance between them in a matter, so what is meant by it is then to observe moderation between the extremes of excess and deficiency in everything, and the true organization of people's interests and their harmony with one another is nothing but a straight line that separates the extremes of excess and deficiency in their affairs, and these are two extremes that inevitably lead to corruption" (3).
