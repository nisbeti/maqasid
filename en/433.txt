Thus, the issue of the forms of generality and whether generality has a form or not is not a significant matter for al-Shatibi, because generality is the generality of the overall meaning, and this is definitive. If the forms indicate generality according to linguistic usage, then definitive indications are not established by individual texts in his view. He says: "There must be a premise that clarifies the intended meaning of generality and specificity here, and the intended generality is whether it has a specific form or not. If we say regarding the obligation of prayer or other obligations, and regarding the prohibition of injustice or others that it is general, this means that it is established absolutely and generally, based on evidence that has a form of generality or not, on the basis that the evidence used here is merely inductive, which, when combined, leads to certainty in the ruling as clarified in the premises, and specificity is contrary to generality"(1).
