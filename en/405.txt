The third: differentiating between the rights of Allah and the rights of human beings. The rights of human beings refer to the rights of the servant himself who performs the action that is a means. The effects of the action are based on whether it is a right of the servant. Its effects are nullified if it is a right of Allah. An example of the right of the servant is traveling to shorten prayers or to break the fast. An example of the right of Allah is the distinction between the community and the gathering of those who are separated in zakat. He said: "And the third is to differentiate between the rights of Allah, Exalted is He, and the rights of human beings"(1). However, if both rights are combined in one action, the right of Allah, Exalted is He, and the right of the servant, then the matter becomes one of consideration and ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) to favor one of the two sides, and the ruling will be for the dominant side according to the jurist. He said: "And what remains is that if both rights are combined, it is a matter of consideration and ijtihad to favor one of the two sides according to what appears to the jurist, and Allah knows best"(2).
The right of Allah and the right of the servant:
These two expressions are used to refer to what the obligated person has no choice in, which is the right of Allah, and to what they have a choice in, which is the right of the servant.
The meaning of the right of the servant is that he is legally free to either claim his right or to waive it. As for the right of Allah, the servant does not have the authority to waive it. The right of Allah also includes the rights of others among the servants, so the servant cannot waive the rights of others. Al-Shatibi said: "Everything that pertains to the rights of Allah does not allow for any choice on the part of the obligated person in any case, whereas what pertains to the right of the servant in himself allows him the choice. As for the rights of Allah, the evidence that they are not waived and do not revert to the choice of the obligated person is abundant, and the most prominent of it is the complete induction in the provisions of the Sharia and its sources, such as purification in its various forms, prayer, zakat, fasting, pilgrimage, enjoining good, and forbidding evil, the highest of which is jihad, as well as the related expiations, transactions, eating, drinking, clothing, and other acts of worship and customs that establish the right of Allah or the right of others among the servants. Likewise, all offenses are on this scale; it is not permissible to waive the right of Allah in them at all."(3)
