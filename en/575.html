As for Imam Malik, al-Shatibi attributed to him in several places in the Muwafaqat that "istihsan (juridical preference) is nine-tenths of knowledge" (1). Al-Zarkashi said: "And from Ibn al-Qasim: Malik said: 'Nine-tenths of knowledge is istihsan.' Asbagh ibn al-Faraj said: 'Istihsan in knowledge can be more effective than qiyas (analogy)' "(2). Among what al-Zarkashi reported is: "The istihsan that Malik referred to is the opinion based on the stronger of the two evidences" (3). He also said: "Al-Abyari stated: What appears from Malik's school is the opinion of istihsan not as previously mentioned, but its essence is the use of a partial benefit (maslaha) in contrast to a general qiyas, thus preferring the unqualified evidence over the qiyas" (4).

As for Imam Ahmad, Dr. Abdullah bin Abdul Mohsen al-Turki clarified his position in his book (The Foundations of Imam Ahmad's School), where he cited the opinions of prominent Hanbalis and their imams regarding the principles, then concluded that the majority of them chose from the definitions of istihsan that it is abandoning qiyas for a stronger evidence than it, and some say it is the stronger of the two qiyas. They all, based on this definition, affirm istihsan and consider it, stating that it is the position of Ahmad and that it is not an independent evidence, but rather a matter of weighing the evidences against one another. However, if it is based on mere desire, they reject it and consider it as legislating what Allah has not permitted (5).
