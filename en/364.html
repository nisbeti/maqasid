Regarding the meaning of the hadith, actions are of two types: acts of worship (ibadat) and customary practices (adat). Those who said that intention (niyyah) means purpose and did not specify intention in a legal sense, which is the intention to seek Allah's pleasure and comply with His command, have interpreted the meaning as applying to the validity of actions whether they are acts of worship or customary practices based on the validity of the intention, which is the seeking of Allah's pleasure. If actions lack this intention, then the intention is invalid, and thus the actions are invalid because actions are judged by intentions. Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani said: "The intention in the hadith is understood in its linguistic meaning" (1), and he said: "The implication is: there is no action except by intention, so the intent is not to negate the existence of the action, as it can exist without intention, but rather to negate its rulings such as validity and completeness; however, the interpretation as negating validity is more appropriate" (2). Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, in his explanation of the hadith, said: "With this, al-Bukhari began his book (al-Sahih) in the position of its introduction, indicating that every action not intended for the sake of Allah is void and has no fruit in this world or the Hereafter" (3). His statement: every action, means whether it is from acts of worship or customary practices, which is what al-Ghazali also held, as will be clarified.
