Regardless of al-Shatibi's methodology or his idea, what he intended is indeed worthy of pursuit, which is returning to the roots, refining them, and reconciling them by removing the disagreements based on differing terminologies and misunderstandings, and by eliminating foreign ideas that bear no fruit or that have no relevance to the foundations of jurisprudence but were inserted into its discussions due to speculative theology (kalam).

This reform can only be carried out by true scholars who return to the roots, to the Qur'an and the Sunnah and what they guide towards, learning from them and acting upon them, liberating themselves from the constraints of Western thought and its conformity, giving no weight or consideration to customs that are not endorsed by Islam, even if widespread, nor to thoughts or rulings that do not stem from its creed, even if dominant. They are neither ashamed nor disturbed by the contradiction of Islamic rulings that have become alien to the ideas and concepts of the backward and degraded Western civilization, whether they are prevalent or new; they strive for the supremacy of Islam as a creed and system, not to reconcile it with others under the pretexts of false flexibility and spurious development.

This reform is carried out by true scholars who liberate themselves from the alien understandings of terms such as moderation (wasatiyya), objectives (maqasid), renewal (tajdid), moderation (i'tidal), dialogue (hiwar), freedom (hurriyya), and similar concepts, which are taken and called for with truncated meanings that deviate from what Islam has brought and from the understanding of the noble companions (may Allah be pleased with them) and the righteous scholars.

This reform is carried out by true scholars who realize that they ought to be the heirs of the prophets, so they rise with zeal and determination, moving forward confidently and speaking truthfully, to bear witness to humanity, { And thus We have made you a just nation that you will be witnesses over the people and the Messenger will be a witness over you } (1).

Yes, the Sharia has objectives, but they are the objectives of the Sharia and the objectives of the Legislator, not the whims of souls. The meaning of the objectives of the Sharia is not the flexibility of Islam or its rulings that can be folded, twisted, expanded, or contracted to conform to any reality or to be shaped in a way that pleases this party or that, and then it is said: this cannot be denied, for Islam is evolving!
