These conditions of division, weakness, conflict, and ambitions encouraged the Christian states in the north to pounce on the Muslims.

And when the danger intensified in Andalusia and the kings of the taifa (small kingdoms) were unable to repel the attack of the Christian kingdoms that united in northern Spain, al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad, the king of Seville, sought help from the Almoravids, the rulers of Morocco. Their forces crossed into Andalusia and inflicted a crushing defeat on the armies of the Christian kingdoms at the Battle of Zallaqa in the year 479 AH - 1086 CE. The assistance of the Almoravids and their overwhelming victory played a crucial role in saving Andalusia and maintaining the Islamic presence there.

Once the danger subsided, the Muslim princes returned to infighting and disputes over power, and the contagion spread to the rulers of Morocco, leading to discord among them and weakening their authority. The Christian rulers in northern Spain seized the opportunity and prepared armies to invade the southern states, resulting in the fall of all of them except for Granada, which remained steadfast like a towering mountain. The fall of Córdoba occurred in the year 634 AH - 1236 CE, and Seville in the year 646 AH - 1248 CE (1).
