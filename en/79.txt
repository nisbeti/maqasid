The eighth method: the principle of correlation (al-tard) and some usul scholars have argued that correlation without the condition of contrariety is a cause. Thus, correlation becomes a method for them. Al-Razi stated this in al-Mahsool. He said: "What is meant by it (i.e., correlation) (1) is the description that is not known to be suitable or necessitating the suitable if it is present with the description in all cases differing from the place of dispute, and this is what is meant by correlation and continuity"(2). Al-Juwayni rejected it.

Those who rejected the method of correlation and contrariety also rejected the method of correlation as it is more deserving of that. Al-Amidi said: "And if it is known that correlation and contrariety do not serve as evidence for causation, then correlation by itself is more deserving of not being evidence"(3). Al-Zarkashi said: "Correlation: and it is not meant by it that the cause does not get negated, for that is the claim of contrariety, but it is meant that its cause is neither suitable nor effective"(4)... And those who say that correlation and contrariety are not evidence, in the case of correlation not being evidence, it is more so from the first perspective... and those considered by the scholars to hold that reliance on it is invalid because it is a form of nonsense, Imam al-Haramayn said: And the judge went to extremes in condemning those who believe in linking God's judgment to it"(5). He said: "Ibn al-Sam'ani said: And Abu Zayd named those who consider correlation as evidence and continuity as proof of the validity of causation 'the Hushawiya of the people of analogy.' He said: And these are not counted among the jurists"(6).

Al-Ghazali said: "Correlation must be rejected by every analogist"(7), this is his statement in al-Mustasfa, but in Shifa al-Ghalil his statement differs(8).
