It has become clear that the Sharia (Islamic law) returns to a single intention in its foundations and branches, meaning that the purpose of the legislator in every incident is one, and it is upon the jurist (mujtahid) to exert their utmost effort to align with it in their reasoning (ijtihad). Likewise, it is upon the obligated person (mukallaf) to seek the intention of the legislator by weighing the opinions of the jurists. Accordingly, al-Shatibi rejects fatwas (legal opinions) that do not rely on weighing the evidence. He also rejects the principles that some scholars rely on in issuing fatwas that do not rest on the basis of exerting effort to align with the intention of the legislator. This includes, for example, the follower (muqallid) choosing between the opinions of the jurists when they differ, or the scholar choosing between the opinions of the Companions or the recognized imams, or the mufti selecting for the questioner the easier and lighter option based on the notion that religion is ease and there is no hardship in it, and that the Sharia is lenient and the differences among the imams are a mercy, as well as the generalization of the principle that necessities permit the prohibited and applying it in inappropriate contexts. This also includes pursuing the concessions of the schools of thought (madhahib) and making the differences in a matter a proof for permissibility. Al-Shatibi has followed such opinions and elaborated on them in detail and in refutation.
It should be noted that such fatwas are clearly present in our time, but what is striking is that some of their proponents claim to base them on al-Shatibi and his methodology. They issue fatwas that align with personal desires or the interests of the ruler, sometimes justifying it with renewal and at other times with necessities and needs, citing public interests (masalih) at one time and objectives (maqasid) and the chief of objectives (Sheikh al-Maqasid) at other times, undermining the rulings of the Sharia under the pretext of its objectives, oblivious to the fact that the primary objective of the Sharia is the application of the Sharia itself, and that one of the most important points emphasized by al-Shatibi is that there is no path to the objectives of the Sharia except through the Sharia. Furthermore, they are either neglectful or ignorant of al-Shatibi's concept of public interests and harms and his methodology in reasoning and establishing the objectives based on inductive reasoning that leads to certainty, as well as the fact that the objectives are those of the legislator and not the objectives of those with personal interests and agendas.
