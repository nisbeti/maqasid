As a result of this debate, al-Juwayni came up with the idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), which form the criteria for appropriate description. The assertion of an objective or objectives for the Sharia and the attachment of the appropriate description or meaning to this objective strengthens the reasoning based on description, as this allows for the application of the appropriate description according to the objectives of the Sharia. Or as his student al-Ghazali later expressed it, that the public interest (maslaha) should follow the established interests, which are the five universal principles.

However, this idea did not negatively or positively affect the principles of those who reject this approach, namely the esteemed Hanafis. Therefore, we find them mentioning it by saying: "And they said."

The idea of the objectives of the Sharia and reasoning based on the appropriate description necessitated another discussion. This is that the original principle in legal rulings is reasoning. Therefore, whatever does not have its reason established by the agreed-upon methods among most jurists, such as text, consensus, indication, and hint, must have a reason established for it if possible. If the ruling allows for reasoning, it must be reasoned.

Al-Amidi elaborated on this principle, the disagreement regarding it, and the evidence for each group in his book al-Ihkam, while al-Razi discussed it in al-Mahsool, where al-Amidi affirmed the principle of reasoning, and al-Razi declared it invalid, even though he accepts analogy (qiyas) and reasoning and supports the approach of appropriateness. The reality of the matter— as will become clear— is that what al-Razi definitively deems invalid is reasoning concerning the essence of Allah, and I have referred to the meaning of reasoning according to him in the previous chapter(1).

Then al-Shatibi came with his Muwafaqat, where he referred in the book of objectives— from his book al-Muwafaqat— to al-Razi's position and his rebuttal, affirming reasoning for the actions and rulings of Allah— Exalted is He— and that this is definitive. He said: "We have inferred from the Sharia that it was established for the interests of the servants, an inference that al-Razi and others do not dispute... If the inference indicates this and is beneficial for knowledge in such a matter, then we conclude that the matter is consistent in all details of the Sharia"(2).
