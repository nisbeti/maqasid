The permissible (mubah) that is chosen seems to be akin to the obligatory (wajib) that is chosen, like the attributes of expiation (kafara). He says: "It is summarized that every permissible act is not permissible in an absolute sense, but rather it is permissible in part specifically, and as for the whole, it is either required to be acted upon or required to be refrained from. If it is said, 'Is this explanation not a contradiction to what was previously stated, that the permissible is that which has equal sides?' The answer is no, because what was previously mentioned is in terms of looking at it in itself, without considering any external matter, and this perspective is in terms of considering it with respect to external matters. So, when you look at it in itself, it is what is called here the permissible in part. And when you look at it according to external matters, it is what is called the required in whole. You see that this nice garment, for example, is permissible to wear; it has equal consideration in the view of the Sharia for both acting and refraining, so there is no intention in either matter. This is a rational reality when considered in this limited regard to the essence of the permissible as it is. And from the perspective of being a protection from heat and cold, a cover for the private parts, and beauty in appearance, it is required to be acted upon. This perspective is not limited to this specific garment nor to this specific time; it is a perspective in the whole, not in part."(1)
