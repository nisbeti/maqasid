If al-Shatibi referred the optional permissible (mubah) to a general principle by directing attention to it being sought in its entirety, then the concession (rukhsah) and the permissible in the sense of lifting hardship did not return to general principles according to him. Therefore, he proposed for them, as we explained in the previous discussion, the rank of exemption, and he stated that partial rulings do not violate general principles. He also said this regarding concessions after confirming that they do not belong to general principles. He stated: "The necessities are not called concessions by the scholars" (1). He said: "The legitimacy of concessions is partial, limited to the place of need. If the traveler’s journey is interrupted, he must return to the original ruling of completing the prayer and observing the fast. The sick person, if he is able to stand in prayer, does not pray sitting, and if he is able to use water, he does not perform tayammum. Likewise, other concessions, unlike loans, partnership, and agricultural contracts, which resemble concessions, are not truly concessions in the sense of this terminology" (2).
The conclusion is that concessions are particulars that do not revert to universals, and even though they are such and do not align with his methodology or his idea of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), the world will not be disrupted. He says: "The firm ruling returns to a general principle in obligation because it is absolute and general in its essence for all obligated individuals, while the concession returns to a particular based on some obligated individuals who have an excuse, and based on certain conditions and times for those with excuses, not in every situation, nor at every time, nor for everyone. It is like an incidental occurrence to the general principle. The established rule in its place is that if a general matter and a particular matter conflict, the general matter takes precedence because the particular entails a partial benefit while the general entails a general benefit, and the order of the world is not disrupted by the disruption of the partial benefit" (3). He then said: "The legitimacy of the concession is that it should be partial" (4).
