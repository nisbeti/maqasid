It was indeed deserving of compassion, for the feeling of the greatness of invention, in isolation, due to the absence of supporters and followers, along with the lack of evidence and the passing of years, and the scarcity of those who discuss and advise, leads one to believe in fantasy and to free the mind from its constraints, like a thirsty person who sacrifices his life for a mirage. Perhaps al-Shatibi (may Allah have mercy on him and reward him for his efforts) came upon induction (istiqra) or induction came upon him, thus he leaped over the reality that if this is valid in rational matters, it is not valid in religious matters, so he was like one who jumped into the sea fleeing from death by burning to death by drowning, then began to weave from his imagination ropes to cling to. Al-Shatibi's discovery of induction was like breathing life into the body of his ideas. Therefore, he referred to induction as a spirit in more than one place in his book, and what indicates this is his description of the hardships he faced in writing his book: "Indeed, I have traversed in pursuit of this objective paths that are both pleasant and unpleasant, and I have endured the trials of my journey, both good and bad, and I have encountered from its opposing faces both frowning and smiling, and I have suffered from its differing riders, both prohibitive and permissive. If you wish, you can find it to be a toil of travel, or a result of the hardships that have laid me low, or from battling the hindrances that have left me wounded; there is neither a pleasant life nor a comforting death. In summary, the most difficult thing that the traveler on the path encounters is the loss of guidance, with a mind that is dim due to the lack of the light of discernment, and a heart afflicted by the shocks of illusions, so he walks without a path and belongs to no tribe, until from the Generous Lord, the Kind Benefactor, the Guide of whom He wills to the Straight Path, the spirits of those bodies were revived for him, and the realities of those forms appeared, and the meanings of those labels became evident" (1).
