This text is important in the methodology of al-Shatibi. It contains several points, including that particulars (juz'iyyat) are the source of universals (kulliyyat), and without particulars, universals would not be known. There is no meaning in the universals that is not present in the particulars, and if a universal conflicts with a particular, this means that the universal has not yet been realized as a universal. Therefore, it is necessary to return to the particular to verify the true meaning of the universal and to regulate it in order to resolve the conflict. If the universal is firmly established as a universal and it contradicts the particular, then the contradiction must be resolved, and neither can be disregarded. The universal is firmly established and definitive, so its contradiction is not valid, and the particular cannot be disregarded for the sake of considering the universal. The universal, as he said, is sourced from the particulars, and doubt in the particular is doubt in the universal.

But this differs in another context. He said: "The established principles are generalities, and the particulars cannot invalidate the generalities; therefore, the rulings of the generalities remain applicable to the particulars even if the meaning of the generalities does not specifically manifest in them."(1)

And this is a contradiction with what has preceded, for how can the meaning of the universals (kulliyat) be applied to the particulars (juz'iyat) without that being evident, when the universals are known only through the particulars, and the particular is the manifestation of knowledge of the universal?! And he said: "If they oppose each other, then either both are to be applied together, or both are to be neglected, or one is to be applied without the other, meaning in the case of opposition. Applying both together is invalid, and likewise neglecting them both, because that is applying the opposition between the speculative (dhanni) and the definitive (qat'i), and applying the particular without the universal gives preference to the particular over the universal, which is contrary to the principle. Thus, the only remaining option is to apply the universal without the particular, which is what is required"(2). And this is a clearer contradiction with what has preceded, for here he says: "Applying both together is invalid," whereas there he said: "It is not possible to apply one and disregard the other." Here he says: "Applying the universal without the particular is what is required," while there he stated that doubt in the particular is doubt in the universal, and that one must consider how to reconcile between them. This contradiction is due to the methodology, which is induction (istiqra).
