This is al-Shatibi's position on legal tricks (hiyal), which is that they are not permissible based on the fact that they contradict the intent of the Legislator. As for his statement: "not permissible in general," it means that there are cases in which legal tricks are permissible. The decisive factor in this is the intention of the obligated person; if their intention in lifting or changing the ruling contradicts the intent of the Legislator, then it is a blameworthy trick, but if their intention in the trick, that is, in changing the ruling, preserves the intent of the Legislator, then it is a permissible trick. An example of a permissible trick according to him is a believer uttering a word of disbelief under compulsion with the intention of preserving their life. He says: "If this is established, then the tricks that have been previously invalidated, condemned, and prohibited are those that undermine a legal foundation and contradict a legal interest. If we assume that the trick does not undermine a legal foundation nor contradict a legal interest recognized by the Sharia, then it is not included in the prohibition and is not invalid" (1). This is the criterion or principle for him regarding the permissibility or invalidity of tricks. Based on this, tricks in terms of their ruling are divided into three categories. He said: "The matter regarding them is that they are of three categories: the first is unanimously agreed upon as invalid, such as the tricks of hypocrites and show-offs; the second is unanimously agreed upon as permissible, such as uttering a word of disbelief under compulsion, for the ratio of trickery in preserving blood with the first intention without believing in its implications is like the ratio of trickery with the word of Islam in preserving blood with the first intention as well. However, the latter is permitted due to being a worldly interest with no inherent corruption either in this world or the Hereafter, unlike the former, which is not permitted due to being an inherent corruption in the Hereafter. Worldly interests and corruptions in the Hereafter are prioritized in consideration over worldly interests and corruptions by consensus, as it is not valid to consider a worldly interest that undermines the interests of the Hereafter. It is known that what undermines the interests of the Hereafter is not in accordance with the intent of the Legislator, thus it is invalid. This is why there has come condemnation of hypocrisy and its people as it has. And likewise, all that follows this reasoning. Both categories reach the level of certainty.
