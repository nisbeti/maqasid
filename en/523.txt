These three types can be interrupted by the interruption of the mujtahids (scholars capable of independent reasoning), but the obligation does not cease with their interruption. He said: "The new occurrences that have no precedent in earlier times are few compared to what has preceded, due to the broad perspective and ijtihad (independent reasoning) of the earlier scholars. Therefore, it is permissible to follow them in this matter because it is the essence of the Sharia (Islamic law), so the Sharia does not become inactive due to the interruption of some particulars, just as if one were to assume the inability to achieve the criteria in some particulars while it remains possible in others; there is no harm to the Sharia in that."(1)

Section Two
Conditions of Ijtihad (independent reasoning) and Fatwa (legal opinion)

The subject of this section:
The intention of the legislator in enacting laws is to free the obligated person from the call of their desires so that they may be a servant of Allah by choice. Thus, they must seek the ruling of Allah in their actions. If they are capable of deriving the rulings themselves, they are a mujtahid; if not, they are a muqallid (follower) and must seek to know the ruling of Allah through someone capable of doing so, which is the mujtahid. The mujtahids are not infallible from error, and they vary in knowledge, jurisprudence, and piety, as well as in what makes them mujtahids whose opinions can be followed. They differ in their ijtihadat (independent reasoning) and fatwas (legal opinions). Just as the mujtahid must strive to understand the intention of the legislator by comprehending the legal evidence, so too must the muqallid strive to align with the intention of the legislator by following the one whom they believe will achieve that through their following. Therefore, it is necessary to know the characteristics and signs that the muqallid must verify or use to identify the mujtahid they will follow or the fatwa they will adhere to.
