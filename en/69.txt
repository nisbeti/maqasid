Similarly, the description may come in one of the paths of reasoning (ta'lil), but it is not suitable for extending the ruling based on it, because the description must have conditions for it to be considered a reason (illah) or for the ruling to be extended based on it (1). For example, Allah, Exalted is He, says: { Establish the prayer at the decline of the sun } (2), and He says: { That they may witness benefits for themselves } (3). The "lam" in both texts is for reasoning, and the path of reasoning here is the text. However, the descriptions that come after the "lam" are not suitable as reasons for analogy, as the decline of the sun in the first verse is merely an indication; it is a cause but not a reason for the obligation of prayer. The testimony of benefits in pilgrimage is a result that may or may not occur, and it is not a suitable reason for the obligation of pilgrimage. Thus, the descriptions in the two verses do not mean the motivator. For a description to be a motivator, it must include a wisdom that is intended by the legislator in enacting the ruling, which is one of the conditions of the reason. Therefore, even if the reason appears in one of the paths of reasoning, it is not considered so until its conditions are met. Al-Shawkani said: "It has twenty-four conditions" (4). Al-Amidi mentioned under the title "On the Conditions of the Original Reason" twenty issues (5). The books of usul (principles) are filled with mentioning and discussing them.
Below are some paths of reasoning and their conditions to the extent necessary to understand the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi, based on what has been clarified in the introduction to this chapter that the objectives of the Sharia for him are its reasons.
Paths of reasoning:
The first path: consensus (ijma). This is like the consensus of the Companions on the fact that childhood is a reason for establishing guardianship over a minor in establishing the guardianship of marriage by analogy to the guardianship of property. (6)
