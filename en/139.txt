"The second type: that which pertains to public need and does not reach the level of necessity. This is like the validation of rental agreements, which are based on the pressing need for housing while being unable to own it and the reluctance of its owners to provide it as a loan. This is a clear need that does not reach the level of necessity prescribed in sales and others" (1). Al-Juwayni has classified sales under the first type.
This section is what has been termed the rank of needs.
"The third type: that which does not pertain to an urgent necessity or a public need, but it indicates an objective in seeking a virtue or in negating its opposite. It is permissible for this category to include the purification from ritual impurity and the removal of filth" (2). This section is what has been termed the improvements.
"The fourth type: that which is not based on need or necessity, and the attainment of its objective is explicitly recommended from the outset" (3). This section resembles the third in that it does not rely on necessity or public need and is considered recommended. It differs from it in that the third is supported by innate inclinations such as cleanliness, and its attainment does not fall outside the first and second types, such as the writing of a contract by a slave and his meeting with his owner regarding his ownership, which goes against the principle that the slave is owned by the master" (4).
