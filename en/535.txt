Therefore, this research, in addition to clarifying some of al-Shatibi's views, warns those who mistakenly claim to rely on al-Shatibi and his methodology that al-Shatibi was in opposition to these views and claims.
As for the prohibition of choosing between opinions when they conflict, whether for the mujtahid (independent jurist) or the muqallid (follower), he states: "The benefit of establishing the Sharia is to remove the obligated person from the call of his desires, and allowing him to choose between two opinions contradicts that principle, which is not permissible. The Sharia has been established to encompass a specific benefit in every issue and a general benefit in general. As for the specific, it is expressed by the evidence of each ruling and its wisdom, and as for the general, it is that the obligated person should be under a specific law of the Sharia in all his actions, beliefs, and statements, so that he is not following his desires like a free-roaming animal until he is trained by the reins of the Sharia. If we allow the muqallid to choose from the schools of the imams to select what is most pleasing to them, they will have no reference except to follow their desires in their choices, and this contradicts the objective of establishing the Sharia, so the claim of permissibility in choosing is not valid" (1).
