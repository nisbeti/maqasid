This is because the understanding of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) is more complete than that of others and is more deserving of precedence. They witnessed the reasons for revelation and the contextual circumstances that others did not witness, and they were eloquent Arabs.

He said: "Reliance on them in explanation is preferred for two reasons: the first is their knowledge of the Arabic language, for they are eloquent Arabs whose tongues have not changed, nor have they descended from their high rank of eloquence; thus, they are more knowledgeable in understanding the Book and the Sunnah than others"(1). Then he said: "The second is their direct engagement with events and incidents and the revelation of the divine guidance through the Book and the Sunnah; they are more adept at understanding the current contexts and are more knowledgeable about the reasons for revelation, and they perceive what others do not because of that. The witness sees what the absent does not see, so whenever there comes from them a specification of some generalities or a restriction of some absolutes, acting upon it is correct, unless there is a report of disagreement from any of them regarding the issue; if some of them disagree, then the matter is one of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning)"(2). Then he said: "However, if it is known that the situation is one of ijtihad that does not depend on those two matters, then they and others are equal in it"(3).
