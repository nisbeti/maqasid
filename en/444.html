This is because the understanding of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) is more complete than that of others and is more deserving of precedence. They witnessed the reasons for revelation and the contextual circumstances that others did not, and they were eloquent Arabs. He said: "Reliance on them in explanation is preferable for two reasons: the first is their knowledge of the Arabic language, for they are eloquent Arabs whose tongues have not changed, nor have they descended from their high status of eloquence; thus, they are more knowledgeable in understanding the Book and the Sunnah than others" (1). He then said: "The second reason is their direct engagement with events and occurrences and the revelation of the divine message through the Book and the Sunnah; they are therefore more adept at understanding the contextual clues and are more knowledgeable about the reasons for revelation, perceiving what others cannot due to this. The witness sees what the absent does not see, so whenever there comes from them a specification of some generalities or a restriction of some absolutes, acting upon it is correct, unless there is a report of disagreement among them regarding the issue; if some of them disagree, then the matter is one of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning)" (2). He then said: "However, if it is known that the situation is one of ijtihad that does not depend on those two matters, then they and others are equal in it" (3).
