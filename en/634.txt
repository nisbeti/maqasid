Indeed, al-Shatibi did not provide evidence for this statement; rather, he presented it as a report, linking the principles to the beliefs in a formulation that could be accepted at first glance. When reading his statements on this matter, the mind shifts to the commonly understood meaning in the words of the imams regarding the necessity of certainty in the principles, which are the sources of legislation, namely the general evidences of jurisprudence, which are the four evidences: the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas). However, al-Shatibi went further, intending the principles and rules of jurisprudence in the sense of the discussions of the principles of jurisprudence, such as the indication of a command for obligation or otherwise, or the rule that what is necessary for the obligation to be fulfilled is itself obligatory, or the rule that the default state of things is permissibility unless there is evidence for prohibition, or the rule of public interest (maslaha), or preference (istihsan), or presumption of continuity (istishab). These rules could indeed be subjected to the condition of certainty if they were meant as sources of legislation, that is, evidences standing on their own; however, this is not what al-Shatibi intended. The linking of the principles in the sense of sources of legislation to beliefs and the condition of certainty in them is based on Allah's condemnation of following conjecture and the reproach of those who follow conjecture, and this pertains to beliefs. Allah, Exalted is He, said: { They are nothing but names you have named them, you and your forefathers; Allah has not sent down any authority for them. They follow nothing but conjecture and what their souls desire, and guidance has certainly come to them from their Lord } (1). And He, Exalted is He, said: { Indeed, those who do not believe in the Hereafter will name the angels with female names (27) and they have no knowledge of it; they follow nothing but conjecture, and indeed, conjecture does not avail against the truth at all } (2). And He, Exalted is He, said: { And most of them follow nothing but conjecture; indeed, conjecture does not avail against the truth at all } (3).
