The disagreement regarding public interest (maslaha) in the context of the unregulated interests (masalih mursala) partly stems from the differences in the validity of reasoning based on the sent analogy (al-munasib al-mursil) or the analogy itself. This matter has been discussed in detail. On the other hand, it also relates to differences in terminologies. If we establish that unregulated interests do not revert to analogy, which is the correct view, but rather to reasoning, then the differing opinions on this matter are almost merely disagreements in terms and terminologies. This is without taking into account the position of those who reject analogy, and the application of the meaning of unregulated interests — in that case — is almost unanimously agreed upon(1). The meaning that was rejected by those who opposed unregulated interests is not the same as that intended by those who advocate for it in the context of reasoning. The meaning intended by its proponents was also acknowledged by its opponents under different names. For example, al-Amidi rejected unregulated interests, stating: "The interests, as we have explained, are divided into those that the legislator has recognized and those that he has annulled. This category (i.e., unregulated interests)(2) is ambiguous between the two categories, and it is not more appropriate to associate it with one than the other, thus it is not permissible to use it as evidence without a witness of recognition that indicates it belongs to the recognized rather than the annulled"(3). It has been previously established that al-Amidi and the Imams of the Shafi'i school were among the most ardent defenders of the principle of analogy and its consideration in reasoning, as well as the consideration of the nature of the description in relation to the nature of the ruling, which is what unregulated interests signify for those who advocate for them in reasoning and analogy.
