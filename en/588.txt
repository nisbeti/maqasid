The blocking of means (sadd al-dhara'i) is the prohibition of what is permissible in order to prevent reaching what is prohibited. Al-Shatibi said: "It pertains to the request to abandon what has been established as a request to perform due to a hindrance that arises, and this is a principle generally agreed upon" (1). Al-Zarkashi quoted: "It is the issue that appears to be permissible but leads to the commission of the prohibited, such as selling an item for one hundred on credit and buying it back for fifty in cash; thus, he has reached fifty by mentioning the item" (2). Al-Qarafi said: "Blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) means cutting off the sources of corrupt means to prevent them. Whenever an action that is free from corruption serves as a means to corruption, the owner is prohibited from that action in many cases" (3). The means (dhara'i) are actions that are causes for others, thus they are means. Al-Qarafi said: "And sometimes the means are referred to as dhara'i, which is a terminology of our scholars" (4).
