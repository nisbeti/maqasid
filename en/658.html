It may be said: However, what al-Shatibi has proposed is the relied-upon and established position among the imams, which is the application of the legal rules and principles when applying the detailed texts, as well as in cases of conflict between the definitive (qat'i) and the speculative (dhanni), where the speculative is rejected if reconciliation between them is not possible.

The response is that this is correct, but there is no contradiction in the methodology of derivation (istinbat) because the doubt regarding the speculative and its rejection does not imply skepticism about the definitive, as the definitive is not derived from the induction of the speculative or the particulars. Furthermore, if the universal (kulli) conflicts with the particular (juz'i) or the general (aam) with the specific (khass), one can specify one by the other, and this is not considered a contradiction or a negation of anything, because the universal is not derived from the induction of the particulars. Therefore, it was said at the beginning of the discussion of this issue that these two matters according to al-Shatibi are correct in terms of their outcomes, but they do not align based on his methodology. In addition to this contradiction in methodology, the negation also arises from two aspects:

First: The particular that he suggested neglecting, if it conflicts with the universal and reconciliation is not possible, may be a text of a sound hadith or the meaning of a Qur'anic text. The universal he speaks of is neither truly universal nor definitive, as has been clarified, since the induction he refers to is incomplete and not comprehensive. Therefore, interpreting the meaning of the text or rejecting the hadith due to its opposition to this universal is not valid in an absolute sense.

Second: This methodology necessitates reliance on the universals at the expense of the particulars, which is what al-Shatibi attempted to evade. This matter will be discussed, God willing, in the third section of this chapter.

The moral transmission (tawatur al-ma'nawi):

Al-Shatibi held that induction provides a transmission similar to moral transmission, and he likened the certainty derived from induction to the certainty of the bravery of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and the existence of Hatim, which is a certainty caused by transmission due to the abundance of reports that support it.

__________

(1) The term "moral transmission" refers to a type of transmission in which the meaning is conveyed through multiple reports, even if the exact wording differs.

(2) The reference to Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) highlights his renowned bravery in Islamic history, while Hatim is known for his generosity.
