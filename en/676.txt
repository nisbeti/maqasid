The second: The intention of the decision-maker can be understood by following his statements and actions and through understanding his mentality and analyzing his psychology, as this falls within the limits of human perception. This cannot be applied to the Creator, so it cannot be said that He has an intention or a cause without revelation from Him. Attempting to comprehend a method for Him in legislation or laws involves an error in comparing the Creator to the created. This is what al-Shatibi did through what he called the universal laws, the fundamental principles, and the true interests (masalih). He built all of this on the objectives of the Legislator (maqasid al-sharia), which he sought to understand through induction and established as definitive causes.
This methodology also responds from this perspective, which is considering the objective (maqasid) as a cause and what is built upon that from the analogy (qiyas) of the Creator to the created.
This methodology has led to a dangerous outcome, and indeed it is a sign of the error in the methodology that necessitates a reconsideration of it, which is the reliance on the general principles (kulliyat) over the specific details (juz'iyat), which is the next topic.
Reliance on the universals over the particulars:
It is evident from al-Shatibi's methodology that legislation has objectives (maqasid) which are its reasons or general principles upon which the particulars are derived or for which they exist, and these particulars are the texts and rulings. The rulings are among the objectives of the texts, and both of them share those general principles as their objectives.
The universals (kulliyat) can only be understood through the induction of particulars (juz'iyat), for they are their source and evidence. Were it not for the particulars, the universals would not be known. It is also clarified that it is necessary to apply both particulars and universals together, for applying the universal without the particular is an error, just as applying the particular without the universal is(1).
It may be objected against al-Shatibi that what is the need to apply the particular (juz'i) if the general (kulli) has been understood from it, for the meaning of the particular is present within it, and examining the particular thereafter is a burden. If a contradiction is assumed between the particular and the general, or if there is a specificity of the particular that is not considered in the general, this means that the general is not truly general after all, which contradicts the assumption; thus, there is no need to apply the particular(2).
