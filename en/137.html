Among this is the statement of his student al-Ghazali: "As for public interest (maslaha), it originally refers to bringing benefit and repelling harm, and we do not mean that. For bringing benefit and repelling harm are the objectives of creation and the welfare of creation in achieving their objectives. Rather, we mean by public interest the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, and the objectives of the Sharia concerning creation are five: to preserve their religion, their lives, their intellect, their lineage, and their wealth. Everything that involves the preservation of these five foundational principles is considered public interest, and everything that jeopardizes the preservation of these five foundational principles is considered corruption, and repelling it is public interest. When we use the terms 'appropriate' and 'suitable' in the book of analogy, we mean this category" (1). He said: "The fact that these meanings are intended is known not by one proof but by many countless proofs... And if we define public interest as the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, there is no room for disagreement on it, and it must be established as a proof" (2).

This defense of the approach of appropriateness and the idea of inference led to the emergence of the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in the hands of al-Juwayni. It was later crystallized by his student al-Ghazali, based on texts such as: "Considering the meaning by the meaning in approximation," "interests similar to the recognized interests by consensus," "interests based on established and fixed rulings in the Sharia," and "intended meanings known not by one proof but by many countless proofs from the Book and the Sunnah" (3).

The classification of rulings according to their outcomes or their public interests:

The assertion of these objectives is based on presenting the Sharia, that is, its rulings, in a manner that highlights its care for public interests. In other words, it is based on adapting the Sharia in a way that supports the claim of the validity of considering appropriateness, whether it is a specific analogy in a ruling or a general one.
