He said: "Everything that pertains to the Arabic meanings, upon which the understanding of the Qur'an depends, falls under the apparent meaning. The rhetorical issues and the disputes of eloquence cannot be separated from the apparent meaning of the Qur'an" (1). He said: "And everything that pertains to the meanings that necessitate the realization of the addressee in the state of servitude and the acknowledgment of Allah's lordship is the intended inner meaning, which is the purpose for which the Qur'an was revealed" (2). Then he said: "In general, everyone who deviates and strays from the straight path does so to the extent that they miss out on understanding and knowledge of the inner meaning of the Qur'an. And everyone who attains the truth and hits the mark does so in proportion to the understanding they have gained of its inner meaning" (3).

Understanding the intended meaning, that is the inner meaning, can only occur after understanding the apparent meaning. The intent of Allah in the address, even if it is not definitive according to the interpreter, must still be something that the apparent meaning can accommodate or necessitate. He said: "The fact that the apparent meaning is the Arabic concept in its abstract form is without doubt, as both the supporters and opponents agree that it was revealed in clear Arabic language" (4). He said: "Any meaning derived from the Qur'an that does not conform to the Arabic tongue is not part of the sciences of the Qur'an, neither in what can be derived from it nor in what can be derived by it" (5).
