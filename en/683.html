After presenting the arguments of both sides, he said: "And if it is established that each possibility has its basis, then the issue, according to true consideration, remains problematic" (1). This position is strange and confused, for based on what has been established regarding the necessity of applying both universals and particulars together, he should have rejected the arguments of those who permit without hesitation, rather than finding the matter problematic. Furthermore, based on what has been established regarding the method of obtaining universals, he should have refuted the evidence of the forbidders that discusses the specificity of particulars and the specificity of the obligated individuals, for this evidence does not only prevent the ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) of one at this rank, but it also undermines the methodology itself as it prevents the acquisition of universals from particulars. He had previously rejected it when the matter pertained to the correctness or error of his methodology. He said: "If it is said: extracting the universal meanings from particular occurrences is not clear from several aspects: one of them is that this is only possible in rational matters, not in religious matters" (2). "And the second: that the specificities entail, in terms of specificity, a meaning additional to that general meaning or many meanings, and this is clear in rational matters because what is shared is not the same as what distinguishes" (3). "And the third: that the specifications in the Sharia are many, so a place is specified by one ruling and a similar one by another ruling, and likewise, different matters are combined under one ruling" (4).

The second and third aspects here are the second and third arguments in the evidence of the forbidders against the ijtihad of one who does not apply particulars. He responded to these three aspects by saying: "The response to the first is that it is possible in religious matters as it is in rational matters" (5). "And to the second, they did not organize the general meaning from the specific cases until they knew that the specificities and what distinguishes them are not considered" (6). "And to the third, it is the problem raised against the opinion of analogy, and what the usuliyyin (jurisprudential scholars) have said is the answer here" (7).
