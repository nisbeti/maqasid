Likewise, some muftis rely in their fatwas on principles or foundations that they pursue for their own purposes, or they mistakenly believe them to be correct or legitimate when they are not. Therefore, it is necessary to warn these muftis about their methodologies or what they do in issuing fatwas, as well as to caution against such fatwas, and to inform the inquirer or the follower of this so that it becomes a sign of distinction between the mujtahids (scholars capable of independent reasoning).
In order to clarify how to ascertain the condition of the mufti or mujtahid, as well as to indicate the places or some places of deviation in fatwas, this section was written.
The mujtahids and the evidence for the followers:
Since people are obligated to follow the rulings, and the sources of rulings are the legal texts, the followers do not have the capacity to derive rulings from the texts; thus, the texts are nonexistent for them, and they must understand the rulings through someone who comprehends them from the texts. The loss of the mujtahid for the follower is like the loss of evidence for the mujtahid. Al-Shatibi said: "The fatwas of the mujtahids for the common people are like the legal evidences for the mujtahids, and the evidence for this is that the existence of evidences for the followers and their absence is the same since they do not benefit from them at all; thus, examining the evidences and deriving rulings is not within their capacity, nor is it permissible for them at all" (1). He also said: "They are, therefore, the ones who stand in the place of the Legislator, and their statements stand in the place of the Legislator" (2). He further stated: "There is no obligation except with evidence; if there is no evidence for an action, the obligation is lifted. Likewise, if there is no mufti for the action, he is not obligated to it. Thus, it is established that the statement of the mujtahid is evidence for the common person, and Allah knows best" (3).
