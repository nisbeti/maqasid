Al-Istihsan (juridical preference) according to al-Shatibi does not differ from what has been mentioned above except in that it is a general principle, and general principles are definitive. For him, istihsan is a departure from applying the general principle in one or some of its particulars. Accordingly, the ruling in this particular that deviates from the general must refer back to another general meaning; otherwise, this deviation is not valid. It is the removal of the particular from its dependence on one general to another general, and this is akin to a conflict between general principles or the specification of some general principles by others. General principles are definitive and do not conflict, nor can they be specified, as has been previously explained. Therefore, this conflict is not real but rather exists from one perspective or in one state rather than another. Similarly, specification, in his view, means clarification; thus, the specification of some general principles by others is the clarification of the limits and conditions of the application of each one.

As for the fact that istihsan is a definitive general principle and that which is preferred (mustahsan) refers to a meaning that is definitively established through induction, the evidence for it is akin to the evidence of the unqualified (mursal) evidence, as indicated by his statement: "For although it does not have a specific origin that testifies for the branch, it has a general origin that testifies for it. And the general origin, if it is definitive, may equal the specific origin or exceed it depending on the strength or weakness of the specific origin, just as it may be the case that in some issues, the ruling of all the specific origins that conflict in the matter of preference may exist. Likewise, the principle of istihsan according to Malik is based on this principle because its meaning refers to giving precedence to unqualified evidence over analogy" (1). He also said: "And what is based on this principle (i.e., the principle of the outcomes of actions) (2) is the principle of istihsan, which in Malik's school is taking a partial interest in opposition to a general evidence, and its implication is to return to giving precedence to unqualified evidence over analogy. For the one who prefers does not revert to mere taste or desire but rather to what is known of the intent of the Legislator in general in similar hypothetical matters, such as issues that analogy would require a matter unless that matter leads to the loss of an interest from another perspective or brings about a harm as well" (3). Thus, giving precedence to the partial interest over the general evidence is not istihsan based merely on opinion or desire but rather through the precedence of unqualified evidence; the partial interest refers back to a general meaning that is established by Sharia.
