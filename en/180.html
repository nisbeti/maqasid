And the third category is that which is neither from the fundamentals (usul) nor from the ancillary matters (milah), which does not return to a definitive (qat'i) or speculative (dhanni) origin. Rather, its nature is to revert to its origin or to another by way of nullification of what is established as being from the recognized sciences and the principles referred to in actions and beliefs, or what is inclined towards nullifying the truth and establishing falsehood. This is not knowledge because it reverts to its origin through nullification; it is neither stable nor authoritative nor consistent, nor is it from the ancillary matters, because the ancillary matters are those that are appreciated by minds and are pleasing to souls(1). And he said about this category: "If it sways a people and they find it pleasing and seek it, it is due to a passing resemblance and confusion between it and what preceded it, so the foolish may consider it to be based on an origin, and they may lean towards it for that reason. The reality of its origin is delusion and imagination, with no reality to it, along with the ulterior motives and desires that accompany it"(2).

These texts about al-Shatibi indicate or establish his position on the approach of analogy (qiyas) and suitability (munasaba), or how to consider public interest (maslaha), and the following research clarifies his stance on how to consider interests or wisdoms in legislation.

The meaning of returning to a definitive origin:

Al-Shatibi said that the foundation and what is relied upon, which the demand revolves around, is what is definitive or refers back to a definitive origin(3). In his first introduction, he stated: "By the universals here, I mean the necessities, the needs, and the enhancements"(4). In his ninth introduction, he said: "The necessities, the needs, and the enhancements, along with what complements them and completes their aspects, are the foundations of the Sharia, and definitive proof has been established for their consideration, with all branches relying upon them"(5).
