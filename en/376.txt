As for al-Shatibi, his opinion will be clarified in detail below; however, two matters should be noted: First, actions that are devoid of intention altogether, whether the intention is to comply or otherwise, such as the actions of the coerced, the forgetful, the sleeping, the insane, the child, and similar cases, are outside the scope of this discussion. Second, the actions that are dependent on intention for their validity and for the hope of reward in the Hereafter, or for their validity and acceptance in this world, are only the actions that are originally lawful. If the action is unlawful, then the validity of intention or the goodness of purpose does not rectify it. Al-Ghazali said: "Sins do not change their nature by intention, so the ignorant should not misunderstand this from the general statement of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), 'Actions are judged by intentions,' thinking that a sin can turn into an act of obedience by intention, like one who backbites another person to please someone else's heart, or feeds a poor person with someone else's money, or builds a school or a mosque or a ribat with unlawful money while intending good. All of this is ignorance, and intention does not affect its being oppression, aggression, and sin; rather, intending good through evil, contrary to the requirements of the Sharia, is another form of evil. If one knows this, he is opposing the Sharia. If he is ignorant of it, he is sinful due to his ignorance, as seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim. Good deeds are only recognized as good by the Sharia, so how can evil be good? Never!"(1)

(1) Al-Ghazali, Ihya Ulum al-Din, 4/368 - 369.
