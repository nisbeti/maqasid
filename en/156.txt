Al-Ghazali mentioned something of these arguments regarding this matter, saying: "As for the strange appropriateness, this is a matter of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), and it does not seem unlikely to me that some mujtahids (jurists) may be inclined to think so, and there is no decisive evidence indicating the invalidity of their ijtihad. If it is said: 'It indicates its invalidity because it is an arbitrary reasoning without evidence that supports attributing the ruling to its cause,' we say: 'Establishing the ruling in accordance with it indicates that the Sharia (Islamic law) has taken it into consideration and is likely to be true.' If it is said: 'Your statement that establishing the ruling in accordance with it is misleading, for it means that the ruling was sought based on an appropriateness and the legislator prompted the ruling, so he responded to its prompt and acted in accordance with it,' this is arbitrary reasoning because it is possible that the ruling of the Sharia regarding the prohibition of wine is either an act of worship or arbitrary reasoning, similar to the prohibition of pork, carrion, blood, domestic donkeys, and all predatory animals with fangs, and all birds of prey, while the hyena and fox are permissible according to some schools of thought, which are arbitrary reasonings. However, the meaning of intoxication in wine is agreed upon, so it is assumed to be for the sake of intoxication, while a similar agreement does not exist regarding carrion and pork, so it is said that this is arbitrary reasoning, and this is on the assumption that there is no indication in the Qur'an mentioning enmity and hatred. It is possible that there is another meaning appropriate that has not been revealed to us, and it is possible that it is for the sake of intoxication. So these are three possibilities; thus, ruling based on one of these three is arbitrary reasoning without evidence, otherwise, what would favor this possibility? This does not change in the effective cause, as it is known to be a cause by attributing the ruling to it textually or by consensus, such as childhood and the precedence of the brother over the father and mother. The response is: 'We favor this possibility over the possibility of arbitrary reasoning with what we have countered the view of those who deny qiyas (analogy).'"(1) He then said: "As for their statement that perhaps there is another appropriate meaning that is the cause and has not been revealed to us, our souls have inclined towards the meaning that has appeared due to the non-appearance of the other, not due to any evidence that indicates it, for it is pure conjecture. We say: The predominance of conjectures in every case relies on such conjecture and depends on the absence of the appearance of another meaning; if it were to appear, the predominance of conjecture would be invalidated. If this door were opened, qiyas would not be established."(2)
