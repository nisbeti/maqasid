Despite the critique of al-Shatibi's idea and methodology, the book al-Muwafaqat is almost a comprehensive work in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh). Although it does not elaborate on the research and issues of consensus (ijma) and analogy (qiyas), what is presented therein serves as foundational principles for them, and a thorough examination of his book indicates his views on these matters. It is filled with foundational and jurisprudential benefits, well-structured in its expression, formulation, and arrangement. The author embarked on it earnestly and with determination, carrying a scientific and objective intent. His ideas are interconnected, with the latter built upon the former, and the depth of thought is profound to the utmost degree, featuring an exploration that only the diligent, patient, and broad-minded can achieve. This does not contradict the assertion of the methodological errors and contradictions. These errors and contradictions stem from incomplete induction and the illusion that it is complete, as well as from the piety that sometimes leads him to refrain from a ruling based on the methodology, either out of doubt or relying on the notion that the rare has no ruling. The errors of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and inference do not diminish his scholarly status and intellectual capacity. Few are those who do not err, but the danger of his error lies in the principles rather than in the branches.
Studying al-Muwafaqat is an analytical endeavor, following his ideas in various contexts while considering that his words, sentences, issues, and their arrangement are all intentionally chosen. This confirms that al-Shatibi carries a concern and aims for a significant matter—in his view—and that he spent many years contemplating ideas, during which he took the Qur'an as a companion, friend, and confidant, reflecting and pondering, yet he found no foundational principle upon which his ideas could stand nor any connection that could form them into a coherent entity or methodology, wandering in thought in search of evidence to support his ideas and breathe life into them. When he found none, he settled his thoughts on induction.
