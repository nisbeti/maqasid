And despite the errors in his methodology, in its foundations and pillars, he did not build upon it any deviant branches. Whenever his principles led to results that were not acceptable, he would pause and not proceed, unconcerned, as his piety prevented him from continuing. Thus, when his methodology led him to rely on generalities over specifics, he halted and did not allow the fact that he had exerted his utmost effort in formulating his idea, which was the fruit of his lifetime, to push him toward a disgraceful statement; rather, he paused in contemplation, as if he anticipated that someone would follow his methodology and provide a solution to this dilemma.
Similarly, when he determined that a report from a single source (khabar ahad) should be paused upon if it did not refer to a definitive meaning, it seemed that deep down he could not accept pausing on the application of a hadith that had no legal opposition, merely because it did not refer to a definitive meaning. Therefore, he stated that in this case, there is no harm in applying such a hadith, arguing that the scholars have applied the unusual analogy and associated this with it. Thus, despite the apparent corruption of the methodology, no deviant branches emerged in al-Shatibi's work. It was as if, despite his belief in his methodology and principles, he felt that safety lay in conforming to the predecessors among the righteous predecessors and the good scholars regarding what they issued in jurisprudence; he strived to avoid contradicting them, despite the accusations of inconsistency that this might bring upon him.
