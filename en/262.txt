As for the clarification of the integration between the two issues, this text confirms it - for example - taken from the eighth issue, which states: "The benefits that are legislated and the harms that are repelled are only considered in terms of establishing worldly life for the afterlife, not in terms of the desires of souls in seeking their ordinary benefits or averting their ordinary harms. The evidence for this consists of several matters: the first is: { And if the truth had followed their desires, the heavens and the earth and whoever is in them would have been corrupted } (1). The second is what has been previously mentioned, that the benefits obtained by the obligated person are usually mixed with harms, just as harms are surrounded by benefits. For instance, we say that souls are respected, preserved, and sought to be revived, such that if the matter is between reviving them and destroying wealth, reviving them is preferable. If reviving them opposes the destruction of religion, then reviving religion is preferable even if it leads to their destruction, as in the case of fighting the disbelievers and killing the apostate, and so on. Similarly, if the revival of one soul opposes the destruction of many souls in a conflict, for example, then reviving the many souls is preferable. Likewise, if we say that eating and drinking revives souls and has apparent benefits, even though it involves many hardships and pains in obtaining it initially, in using it at the moment, and in its requirements and consequences ultimately. Nevertheless, what is considered is the greater matter, which is the aspect of benefit that is the foundation of both religion and worldly life, not in terms of the desires of souls" (2).
