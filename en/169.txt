Thus, al-Shatibi does not rely on anything other than induction (istiqra) to establish certainty in legal matters, and this is one of his innovations; he adopted it in his book and considered it the only way to achieve certainty, stating that it is a characteristic of his work. He said, referring to this third introduction of his: "It has also been explained how to seize certainty from the conjectural, and this is a characteristic of this book, and praise be to Allah" (1).
Induction is when there are numerous pieces of evidence indicating a specific meaning, or rulings that are presumed to have a particular wisdom or result. The indication of the evidence is conjectural, and the notion that the legislator intends a specific meaning or a particular wisdom is also conjectural. It is not valid to take conjectural matters as a basis in jurisprudence or legislation, nor to build upon them. When the evidence or rulings on a specific meaning become numerous and corroborative, this leads to beneficial induction that results in certainty, and these meanings become foundational principles. He said: "When the evidence becomes abundant for the observer, supporting one another, it becomes collectively beneficial for certainty; likewise, the matter is in the sources of evidence in this book, which are the sources of the principles" (2). He added: "However, the earlier scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) may have neglected to mention this meaning and to point it out, leading to its oversight by some later scholars" (3). He warned that through this oversight, we do not attain certainty regarding a legal ruling at all, and that the indication of consensus (ijma) on legal rulings is not established (4).
