As for the description of the one who possesses this rank, he said: "He should conclude by reflecting on the realization of the meaning of what he has attained according to what the legal proof has led him to, such that he achieves certainty and is not opposed by doubt; rather, doubts that arise for him become like the proofs indicating the correctness of what he holds" (1). Then he said: "However, his condition continued until his memorization slipped from his retention in a ruling, even if it was present with him, so he does not care about making definitive rulings on the issues whether he has stated them or opposed them" (2). Then he questioned: "If the student reaches this rank, is it permissible for him to exercise independent juristic reasoning (ijtihad) in legal rulings or not? This is a matter of consideration and ambiguity and one in which disagreement arises" (3). He then clarified what he sees as arguments for those who permit it, saying: "The one arguing for permissibility may say: If the legal objective has become clearer to this student than the sun, and the meanings of the legal texts have become evident to him until they cohere and some support others, and there remains no need for him in understanding their realities, then what he has attained is the entirety of the Sharia and the foundation of the doctrine and the source of obligation. Therefore, it does not matter whether he looks into its specific provisions or its particular issues or not. For his examination of that does not increase him in knowledge; if it were so, he would not have reached this rank, and we have assumed him to have reached it, which is contradictory.
