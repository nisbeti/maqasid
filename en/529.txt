As for the second description, if the actions of the mufti (Islamic legal scholar) align with his statements perfectly, he is in the highest ranks in this description. If there is a contradiction, this is a descent from that rank, and it is necessary to weigh the extent of this contradiction or its locations among the jurists. However, it should be noted here that this opposition does not undermine justice (adala), because if it did, the fatwa (legal opinion) would not be trustworthy at all. He said: "Whoever meets the conditions for being appointed to issue fatwas falls into two categories: one of them is the one whose actions, statements, and conditions are in accordance with his fatwa; he is characterized by the attributes of knowledge and stands in the position of complete compliance, so that if you wish to follow him without asking, you are often free from the need to ask in many actions, just as the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was followed in knowledge through his words, actions, and approvals. This category, when found, is more deserving than one who is not like that" (1). He also said: "Whoever has renounced the superfluous matters that do not undermine justice and is ascetic in them, his renunciation is more beneficial than the renunciation of one who has renounced them but is not abandoning them, for that is a contradiction, even if it is permissible. In opposing the statement with the action here, it prevents reaching the rank of one whose statement matches his action" (2).
As for when the jurists descend from this rank, the one who is preferred among them is the one whose statements align with his actions most frequently. He said: "If the ranks of the muftis differ in this alignment, it is preferable for the follower (muqallid) to follow the one whose statement predominates in alignment with his action" (3). Among the factors for preference is that the follower looks at the locations of alignment and contradiction, for alignment in prohibitions is more preferable than alignment in commands. He said: "The alignment or lack thereof is considered in relation to commands and prohibitions. If he aligns in both, that is perfection; if there is a difference in this regard, meaning aside from the conditions of justice, the alignment in prohibitions is more preferable" (4). This is because avoiding prohibitions is more emphatic and significant in the legal intent (5).
