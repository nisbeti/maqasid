Among the examples regarding conditions, he said: "If his action or inaction is intended as a condition to negate the effect of the cause so that no consequence follows from it, then this is an invalid action and a futile endeavor"(1). And he said: "This action renders what has established a cause for a legal ruling, whether for the sake of bringing about a benefit or preventing a harm, as mere play with no ruling or benefit in it. This contradicts what has been established in the principle of interests (masalih) that they are taken into account in rulings, and it is also contrary to the intention of the Legislator (Shari') in that when the cause has been established and exists, it becomes a legal requirement for its effect, but it is contingent upon the fulfillment of a condition that completes the cause. Thus, this actor or one who refrains, intending to negate the ruling of the cause, is aiming to oppose the Legislator in establishing a cause. It has become clear that opposing the intention of the Legislator is invalid, so this action is void"(2).

Accordingly, if the accountable person (mukallaf) performs an action with the intention of nullifying a ruling, then their action is blameworthy because it opposes the intent of the Lawgiver. However, if they perform it for a reason related to lawful benefit (manfa'a mashru'a), then it is permissible even if it results in nullifying a cause or changing a ruling, and this does not fall under legal tricks (hiyal) at all.

He said in response to those who claim that it is permissible to use legal tricks, such as spending all or part of one's wealth before the due date to avoid the obligation of zakat: "This reasoning only applies if one does not intend to negate the ruling of the cause; however, if the intention is to do so, it is an invalid reasoning because the Sharia has definitively annulled it"(3). He then said: "This is only because he has introduced a condition or removed a condition that negates what the first cause necessitated; likewise, the one who spends his due amount with the intention of negating what is required of him in terms of obligatory payment"(4). He then said: "Thus, bringing about conditions or removing them with that intention is what is prohibited, and if it is prohibited, it contradicts the intention of the Lawgiver, and therefore it is invalid"(5).
