{ And when the prayer has been concluded, disperse throughout the land } (1). It is certainly known that the intent of the Legislator is not merely to engage in hunting upon the permissibility or to disperse after the conclusion of the prayer, but rather that the reason for the prohibition of these things has been removed, which is the conclusion of the prayer and the lifting of the state of ihram. This perspective is also supported by inductive reasoning (istiqra) (2).
That is, the phrases "hunt" and "disperse" are in the imperative form, but the intent of the text is not a command but rather permissibility. This is not about following the meanings of rulings or interests, but rather it is the meaning of the text itself. This is based on the intent of the Legislator in the address, not in the legislation.
Therefore, there must be a method for understanding commands and prohibitions, and al-Shatibi's approach in this regard is to return the particulars to definitive meanings and to apply both particulars and universals together, along with inductive reasoning for the meanings, and to utilize current and contextual indicators.
He said: "Rather, we say: The speech of the Arabs in general must consider the meaning of the context in the indication of the forms, otherwise it would become a source of mockery and ridicule. Do you not see their saying, 'So-and-so is a lion or a donkey or a great ash or a cowardly dog,' and 'So-and-so is far from the place of the earring,' and countless other examples? If one were to consider the word in isolation, it would have no rational meaning. What do you think of the words of Allah and the words of His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him)? On this basis, the distinction between urination in stagnant water and pouring from a vessel into it is made" (3).
Generality and specificity:
The concept of generality and specificity in al-Shatibi's thought differs significantly from the conventional understanding in the science of usul al-fiqh. Although he uses these terms in his book, he has a different intention than other scholars of usul.
For him, generality is one of the characteristics of universals, and universals can only be derived through inductive reasoning. Universals are consistently applicable to particulars and do not fail. Therefore, universals cannot be specified. When al-Shatibi uses these terms, as in his statement that necessities are specified by needs, it will become clear in a certain sense.
