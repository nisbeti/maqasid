The topic of this discussion:

Here, we continue what we began in the previous section, which is the discussion of the concept of objectives (maqasid) according to al-Shatibi. What we will discuss in this section is the foundation of the idea, which is that His actions - Exalted is He - are purposeful, and that they are purposeful for their ends or objectives. This section will also address one of the important outcomes of this methodology, which is the independence of the jurist (mujtahid) from the particulars in jurisprudence (ijtihad) if they have comprehended the objectives in general and in detail.

Al-Shatibi - may Allah have mercy on him - was not the only one who spoke about the principle of purposefulness or the objective behind actions, so we will briefly discuss those statements. It is worth mentioning here that this topic was presented in some detail in the first chapter.

The principle of purposefulness:

There are two directions in this research that may be confused by some, so it is necessary to distinguish between them. The first is that the actions of Allah - Exalted is He - are purposeful, for He does not act except for a purpose, and this is what wisdom necessitates. The opposite of this is frivolity, and Allah is far above that. If it is established that He does not act except for a wisdom or purpose, this applies to His rulings, so there must be an intention behind every ruling, which is the reason for the ruling, thus necessitating the purposefulness of rulings.

The second is that the legal rulings (ahkam) are purposeful, based on the evidence indicating that the Sharia is a mercy for people and a healing. Also, based on observation, one finds that the rulings of the Sharia are aligned with interests or reasons for them, and one also finds rulings that are justified by reasons that indicate consideration of interests, which shows that the principle is the purposefulness of rulings based on interests.

As for the first direction, it is a theological discussion that has no relation to the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), even if it is included in its studies, nor is it related to analogy (qiyas), although some have linked it to that. It has been clarified previously that al-Razi rejected the purposefulness of Allah's actions and the term "purpose" in relation to Allah's actions categorically, even though he supports analogy and the principle of purposefulness for rulings.

(3) The term "ahkam" refers to the legal rulings in Islamic jurisprudence.

(4) The term "usul al-fiqh" refers to the principles of Islamic jurisprudence.

(5) The term "qiyas" refers to analogy, a method of reasoning in Islamic jurisprudence.
