And based on this introduction, one of the most important principles according to al-Shatibi is established, a principle that serves as the spirit of his entire methodology. He says: "And based on this introduction, there is another meaning, which is: every legal principle that does not have a specific text supporting it and is suitable for the actions of the Sharia (1), and whose meaning is derived from its evidence is valid and can be relied upon, and it is referred to if that principle has become established through its collective evidence, because the evidence does not necessarily have to indicate certainty in the ruling by itself without the addition of others to it, as previously mentioned, because that is somewhat impossible. This falls under the category of the general reasoning (istidlal al-mursal) that was adopted by Malik and al-Shafi'i (2).

(1) The meaning of suitability for the actions of the Sharia is clarified in the first chapter, which is that more than one ruling must support the appropriate description. As noted in al-Shatibi's texts, he does not merely use the term "more," but rather stipulates that the abundance must result in a beneficial induction leading to certainty, and it is not necessarily the case—according to him—that the suitable is appropriate. This will become clear in its place, if Allah wills.

(2) In the first chapter, the conflicting reports about Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) regarding the mursal principle or reasoning were mentioned, as well as the conflicting opinions of the Shafi'i scholars regarding the mursal or reasoning, and their differences concerning what is attributed to Imam al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him). We then clarified the differing meanings of mursal among the scholars and the differing positions based on that. The text above attributes to the two Imams (may Allah have mercy on them) the saying of general reasoning, but it restricts that to what is established with certainty and what is consistent with his methodology. This suggests that this inductive methodology in establishing certainty and relying on its results is practiced among the scholars, and perhaps this is part of what he meant by saying: "However, the earlier scholars of usul may have neglected to mention this meaning and to point it out, leading to its oversight by some later scholars." 1/15. Likewise, what was stated in the preface of his book in response to the denial of those who may deny what is presented in his methodology: "Indeed, by the grace of Allah, this is a matter confirmed by the verses and reports, strongly established by the righteous predecessors, delineated by the scholars, and its foundations built by the discerning. When the path is clear, denial is not permissible, and acceptance of what it contains is obligatory." Preface of the book. p. 9.
