Al-Shatibi's response to this statement means that he does not suffice with the presumed wisdom. Therefore, he said: "This is not permissible, for there remains the possibility of another wisdom for which the ruling was legislated, so we are, from that perspective, standing with the act of worship" (1), and he said: "If a reason appears to us that is suitable for the independence of the ruling's legitimacy and we are not required to deny anything other than it, then the usuliyyin (jurisprudents) allow for the possibility that the reason may differ from what has appeared to them" (2).

What confirms this in his methodology is his consideration that wisdom is a consequence. He said: "As for the cause, it refers to what has been legislated for a ruling due to a wisdom necessitated by that ruling" (3). He also said: "The wisdoms of the causes are the consequences" (4).

The consequence:

It is the wisdom intended by the Legislator through the legislation of the cause. It is the result of the commanded action that the Legislator intends to achieve through the occurrence of the action, and it is the result of the prohibited action that the Legislator intends not to achieve through the non-occurrence of the action that causes it.

However, not everything that can result from an action or from the omission of an action is a consequence of the cause; therefore, the consequence is what is established as being intended by the Legislator through inductive reasoning that leads to certainty. It is not sufficient for it to be something that can result from the action.

The reason:

The reason is the consequence or the result that the Legislator intended by legislating the ruling. Thus, it is the objective and the wisdom.

One might ask how the reason can be the consequence, while it is closer in meaning to be a cause rather than a consequence? The answer is that in the terminology commonly used among the usuliyyin, the reason is neither a cause nor a consequence; rather, the reason is the motivator for legislation. This has been clarified in the previous chapter. However, for al-Shatibi, the matter is different, and the reason is the consequence according to his methodology.

The explanation of this is that the Legislator only legislated the rulings to achieve objectives, and the objectives are the consequences of the rulings. If we want to know the objective of the Legislator, we look into these rulings and their consequences and intended outcomes, and what is established as such is considered a principle of legislation.
