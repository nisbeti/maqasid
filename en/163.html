The objectives (maqasid), according to him, are not limited to the goals of the rulings, but rather the rulings themselves are among the objectives, as the rulings are intended by the legislator through the texts. All definitions revolve around the objectives of the rulings, which is the first type according to al-Shatibi, while one of the main focuses of al-Shatibi is the objectives of the legislator in understanding the commands and prohibitions in addition to the intention of worship and compliance. What these definitions mention are the goals of legislation, which are results or causes for it, and the Sharia or rulings are means to achieve the goals or reasons for them.

Therefore, these definitions, in addition to their shortcomings, do not accurately define what al-Shatibi intended, nor the first type of the objectives of the legislator according to him. For example, Dr. al-Raisouni's definition: "They are the goals that the Sharia was established to achieve for the benefit of the servants" is not valid due to the restriction to the benefit of the servants. If this restriction is removed, the definition becomes: "They are the goals that the Sharia was established to achieve," which is correct for al-Shatibi's intended meaning of the objectives of the Sharia, not the objectives of the legislator.

Additionally, what some have referred to as the division of objectives into specific, general, and partial does not exist in al-Shatibi's work, nor is there any evidence in his texts to support it. In fact, his texts refute this claim. This will become clear upon further elaboration, which is central to the idea of objectives according to al-Shatibi. The objectives according to al-Shatibi are universal principles, and they are general in all circumstances, at all times, and for all accountable individuals, while the particulars are not objectives because the objectives are universal principles; rather, the particulars may serve as branches that support the objectives or relate back to them.

Moreover, the objectives, according to al-Shatibi, are definitive principles and not presumed rulings. This is one of the most important pillars of the idea of objectives according to al-Shatibi, which none of the mentioned definitions or published research on the objectives according to al-Shatibi have noted.
