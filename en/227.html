He says: "If it is accepted that the purpose of the Sharia (law) is for the interests of the servants, then it returns to them according to the command of the Lawgiver and within the limits He has set, not according to their whims and desires. Therefore, the legal obligations are burdensome on the souls, and the senses, customs, and experience bear witness to this. The commands and prohibitions lead one away from the inclinations of their nature and the pursuit of their desires until they take them from beneath the prescribed limit... As for the fact that the interests of the obligation return to the obligated in both the immediate and the delayed sense, this is correct, and it does not necessitate that attaining them is outside the bounds of the Sharia or that one can attain them by oneself without the Sharia providing them, which is evident" (1). He says: "The pursuit of desires and interests does not contradict the purpose of the Sharia in this regard because the Sharia is also established for the interests of the servants. If the interest is made subordinate, there is no harm on the doer, except that there is a significant condition, which is that the means by which they achieve their interest must be shown to be legislated by the Lawgiver for the attainment of such, otherwise, the prior command is that of the Lawgiver" (2). He says: "Actions and abstentions, in themselves as actions or abstentions, are rationally equivalent concerning what is intended by them, as there is no rational commendation or condemnation. When the Lawgiver specifies one of the equivalents for the interest and the other for the harm, He has clarified the means by which the interest is achieved, thus commanding it or permitting it, and He has clarified the means by which the harm is achieved, thus prohibiting it out of mercy for the servants. If the obligated person intends exactly what the Lawgiver intended by permission, they have aimed at the means of interest in its most complete form, and they are deserving of it. However, if they intend something other than what the Lawgiver intended, this often occurs due to the misconception that the interest lies in what they intended, for a rational person does not directly intend the means of harm. Thus, they have neglected what the Lawgiver intended and considered what the Lawgiver neglected as a valid aim, which is a clear contradiction to the Sharia" (3).
