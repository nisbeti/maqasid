The influential factor (mu'athir) according to the usuliyyin (jurisprudents) of the Shafi'i school and in the principles of theologians (mutakallimin) in general is a counterpart to the appropriate (mulayim) and the strange (gharib), unlike the usuliyyin of the Hanafi school. The latter have made influence a condition of the cause (illa), and therefore do not accept the path of appropriateness (munasaba) at all; rather, appropriateness for them is a condition for reasoning (ta'leel) based on description (wasf). Thus, they do not accept merely the consideration of the description as a testimony (shahada) for the ruling (hukm) but require the testimony of the text (nass), which they refer to as justice (adala), meaning the justice of the description and its suitability for reasoning. The term appropriate (mulayim) in their texts does not mean what others among the theologians intend; rather, they mean exactly what others refer to with the term appropriate (munasib). Al-Ghazali said: "Know that the influential factor is accepted by consensus among those who advocate analogy (qiyas), and Abu Zayd al-Dabusi restricted analogy to it, saying: only the influential factor is accepted. However, he provided examples of the influential factor, indicating that he accepted the appropriate but also called it an influential factor" (1). The meaning of this text is that the Hanafis accept only the influential factor.

__________

(1) Al-Ghazali, Al-Mustasfa. 2/299. This statement by Al-Ghazali, that Al-Dabusi accepts the appropriate and calls it an influential factor, indicates that the Hanafis reason based on the appropriate, which is strongly rejected by the Imams of Hanafi jurisprudence.
