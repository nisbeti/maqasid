Thus, the universals cannot do without the particulars, and likewise, the particulars cannot do without the universals. For example, His saying, Exalted is He: {Say, "Be stones or iron"} (1), the verse. The text allows for meanings, including obligation and despair, but the prohibition of imposing obligations that cannot be borne or are impossible is a universal principle. To interpret the text as an obligation would contradict the universal, so it is necessary to apply the universal and interpret the text in another way, which is despair. Similarly, in His saying, Exalted is He: {Then bring a surah like it} (2), the verse. The universal principle, which is the prohibition of imposing what cannot be borne, must be preserved, so the meaning shifts from obligation to the challenge intended to induce incapacity. The same can be said for His saying, Exalted is He: {Taste, indeed you are the honored, the noble} (3), where the meaning shifts from obligation or otherwise to humiliation. In His saying, Exalted is He: {And Allah will never give the disbelievers a way over the believers} (4), the text allows for the meaning of reporting and the meaning of obligation, but the universal principle, which is the necessity of the truth of the reporter, shifts the meaning from reporting to obligation, which is prohibition. This is the meaning of al-Shatibi's statement: "It is necessary to consider those particulars in light of these universals when applying the specific evidences from the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy."

The universal is not violated by the opposition of individual particulars to it:
