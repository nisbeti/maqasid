Thus, it becomes clear that he agrees with the Hanafis, for example, in that the mere existence of meaning in a ruling or in some rulings does not benefit in the process of reasoning, and he agrees with the Shafi'is in the validity of reasoning or searching for the meanings intended by the Legislator, while he disagrees with the Hanafis in their rejection of this reasoning, and he disagrees with the Shafi'is in being satisfied with the meaning being a presumption of being intended by the Legislator and being satisfied with a few evidences based on the meaning, as in the appropriate cases, or with a single evidence as in the rare cases, or merely its existence in the ruling as in the mursal (unattributed hadith). We have explained this matter in detail in the first chapter.
With these criteria, al-Shatibi enters into the discussion of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), and he explained these criteria in the first part of his book, dividing them into two sections: the first is the introductions that he deemed necessary for understanding his book, and the second is the Book of Rulings. As for the objectives, he made them the third section of his book, which occupied the second part of it. The third and fourth parts, which complete his book al-Muwafaqat, contain the fourth and fifth sections, which are applications of his idea regarding the objectives, or his methodology in establishing the principles based on the sciences of the Qur'an and the Sunnah.
The idea of the objectives of the Sharia and their division into three categories: necessary, need-based, and embellishing, along with the addition of supplements for each category, is not new to al-Shatibi; it first appeared through Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni, and Allah knows best. Many scholars followed this matter after him. However, what is new in al-Shatibi's approach is his stipulation of certainty for the objective to be considered legal, and his requirement of induction as a means to that end, as well as his understanding of benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid). Furthermore, his discussion on the objectives is characterized by detail, arrangement, and classification that is absent in his predecessors.
According to him, the objectives are divided into two categories: the objectives of the Legislator and the objectives of the obligated person (mukallaf). The objectives of the Legislator are of four types: the first is His intention in establishing the Sharia from the outset, which is the type that the legal theorists (usuliyyun) referred to before him, and in it, the division of objectives into necessary, need-based, and embellishing occurs.

(1) mursal: a type of hadith that is not attributed to a specific narrator.
(2) maqasid al-sharia: the objectives of Islamic law.
(3) masalih: benefits or interests.
(4) mafasid: harms or detriments.
(5) mukallaf: a person who is legally responsible for their actions in Islamic law.
