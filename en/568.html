(5) Narrated by al-Bukhari in the chapter on selling food before it is received and in the chapter on selling what you do not have (2020), Abu Dawood (2979), and al-Darimi (2454).

Based on this, the principle of public interest (maslaha mursala) is a foundational principle of Islamic law according to al-Shatibi and is not a fifth source in itself; it is not a source of rulings but rather a legal methodology applied or utilized during deduction, similar to the principle of presumption of continuity (istishab), the principle of permissibility of things (asl fi al-ashya), or the principle of removing harm. However, if it is taken as a source of rulings such that the requirement of legislation becomes merely looking at whether an action is beneficial or harmful, or whether it is so with the slightest difficulty or cost, then this is a method of innovation (bid'ah) and not legislation.

Categories of interests:

Al-Shatibi discussed in his book (al-I'tisam) those who invoked public interest (maslaha mursala) for their violations and innovations, clarifying the matter further. He said: "Since the place is a slippery slope for the people of innovation to argue for their innovation from this angle, the right approach is to examine the basis of the error that occurs for these people until it becomes clear that public interest is not part of innovation in its origin or conclusion, by the will of Allah" (1). He then said: "The appropriate meaning that connects the ruling does not escape three categories:

The first is that the Sharia witnesses to its acceptance; there is no doubt about its validity nor disagreement in its application, otherwise it would contradict the Sharia, such as the law of retribution (qisas) for the preservation of lives and limbs, among others" (2). This is not part of public interest but rather what is considered either by the testimony of evidence or by the testimony of the ruling. For al-Shatibi, consideration is by the testimony of evidence, or by numerous rulings that collectively form evidence in his view, and consideration is not accepted by the testimony of few rulings.
