"The third approach: what indicates causation through indication and allusion" (1). This is what the evidence indicates in a suggestive manner rather than explicitly. That is, the text indicates the cause by its implied meaning rather than its literal wording. It is divided into two categories:
The first: that the implied meaning indicates causation, whether it is a concept of agreement or a concept of disagreement. This is like His saying, Exalted is He: {Indeed, the alms are for the poor and the needy and those employed to collect them and for bringing hearts together} (2). The implication is that those who are not among these mentioned have no right to the zakat, and these descriptions are suitable for causation; therefore, they serve as causes. The bringing together of hearts is a reason for giving zakat, and if this quality is removed, the right to zakat is also removed. The same can be said for their being poor, needy, or workers collecting it.
This category corresponds to the first category of the explicit text indicating causation, but that is from the literal meaning while this is from the implied meaning.
The second: "that the causation is necessary from the meaning of the term by its placement, not that the term indicates causation by its placement" (3).
In this case, the text may not have a concept of agreement or disagreement, and it may have a concept but does not indicate causation.
