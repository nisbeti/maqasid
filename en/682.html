The second: "That particularities have characteristics suitable for each of their respective contexts that are not suitable for other contexts, as in marriage, for example, it is not permissible for it to be treated like exchanges in every respect, just as it is not permissible for it to be treated like gifts and donations in every respect" (1). He said: "And for every particular there is a characteristic that is suitable for it and not suitable for others, as in the concessions in acts of worship, customs, and other rulings. If that is the case, and we know that all of them refer, for example, to the preservation of necessities, needs, and embellishments, then applying the preservation of these in every context in a uniform manner is not possible; rather, it is necessary to consider the particularities of circumstances, categories, and other specific particulars" (2).

The third: "That this rank necessitates that if particularities are not considered, then their contexts, which are the actions of the accountable individuals, should not be considered either. Just as universals apply to every particular absolutely, it would necessitate that they apply to every accountable individual absolutely without considering their particularities, and this is not correct" (3).

Then he said on behalf of the opponents: "The holder of this rank cannot descend to what is required by the rank of the mujtahid (scholar), so it is not consistent with this for him to be among the people of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning)" (4).

Upon examining the arguments of the opponents, it becomes clear that they all return to the failure to apply particulars, and that particulars have their own specific characteristics, whether in actions or in the accountable individuals.
