Al-Shatibi held that legal ruses (hiyal) in religion are generally not permissible, and this means that there are very few cases in which they are allowed. Therefore, it is necessary to have a criterion to determine the cases in which legal ruses are permissible. Legal ruses are actions performed by a responsible person with the intention of changing a legal ruling, which contradicts the intent of the Lawgiver. So, what are the cases in which this contradiction is valid? Al-Shatibi's answer is that if this contradiction is intended to oppose the objectives of the Lawgiver, then it is prohibited. However, if this contradiction is intended to preserve the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), then it is permissible. He said, "Legal ruses in religion, in the mentioned sense, are not permissible in general" (1). It has been previously established that legal ruses can occur in causes, conditions, and impediments. An example of this in causes is as he said: "In the school of thought, if someone swears by divorce that so-and-so will fulfill his right by a certain time, and then fears breaking the oath due to non-fulfillment, he divorces his wife until the time passes, and the breaking of the oath does not apply to him, even though his intention is blameworthy and his action is blameworthy because he has employed a ruse that invalidated a right. Thus, the violation is prohibited even if it resulted in avoiding the breaking of the oath" (2). Another example is: "The ruses of the people of 'ainah in making the commodity a means for selling one dinar for two dinars for a deferred payment" (3).
