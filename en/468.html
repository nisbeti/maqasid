The second principle is: "When there is encouragement (targhib) in the Qur'an, it is accompanied by deterrence (tarhib) in its subsequent or preceding verses or in its context, or vice versa, as well as the combination of hope (tarjih) with fear (khawf), and what relates to this meaning is similar" (1). This is because a person is inclined to lean towards either the side of laxity or the side of strictness. Thus, encouragement and deterrence come as a form of discipline, guiding him towards what is moderate and just, keeping him between fear and hope. If he leans towards laxity, he is met with fear, and if he leans towards strictness, he is met with hope. Al-Shatibi mentioned that this principle is consistent in the Qur'an as it is one of the actions of the Wise and All-Aware Creator. He said: "And this is how he (peace and blessings be upon him) disciplined his companions. When a group of people became overly fearful, they were told: { O My servants who have believed, do not despair of the mercy of Allah } (2), and when another group neglected certain matters, they were warned and admonished, as in the saying: { Indeed, those who harm Allah and His Messenger - Allah has cursed them in this world and the Hereafter } (3). Therefore, if this is established from the arrangement of the Qur'an and the meanings of its verses, then it is upon the accountable person to act according to that discipline" (4).

This pattern in deriving rulings regarding the principles of conduct and its etiquette has examples that al-Shatibi refers to in several places in his book, and they return - in his view - to a foundational principle he calls the principle of embodying the attributes of Allah and emulating His actions, where each of these attributes serves as a principle derived through induction.
