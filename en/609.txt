The awareness of al-Shatibi regarding this led him to attempt to trace the roots of the various schools of thought back to their origins, meaning that his methodology should serve as a foundation for all. This is where the name of his book, al-Muwafaqat, comes from, which will be clarified in the next section.
The innovation and depth in al-Shatibi's research, along with his seriousness and discipline, have motivated many in our time to highlight al-Muwafaqat and its author. Perhaps some have even attempted to draw from it and rely on it. However, the reality is that no one has built upon his methodology or adopted it; on the contrary, what has been attributed to him is what he consistently warned against.
Indeed, what he feared has occurred, as he said: "And thus, it is not permissible for the one who examines this book to look at it with a beneficial or advantageous perspective until he is well-versed in the knowledge of Sharia, its principles and branches, both transmitted and rational, not clinging to imitation and partisanship to a school of thought. For if it is like this, there is a fear that what has been deposited in it may turn into a trial by mere appearance, and if it is wisdom in itself" (1).
The reason for the lack of dissemination of the methodology and its development is, and Allah knows best, due to the methodology itself, specifically the novelty of the methodology that may make the student of knowledge feel a thirst to understand it; once he understands it, he turns away from it. He stated in the conclusion of his book: "The intended purpose has been achieved, and thanks to Allah, the promised accomplishment has been realized, although there remain things that could not be included, as it has not been easy for many of the seekers to grasp them, and few are those who, despite their great thirst for them, have sought them. I feared that they would not reach their source and that they would not be organized in the path of verification of its stray points" (2). If the matter is as al-Shatibi said in this text: "Few are those who, despite their great thirst for them, have sought them," it seems to indicate that the methodology was born dead. This will be clarified through discussion in this chapter. It may also be beneficial before that to briefly present al-Shatibi's idea and highlight its novelty in a comprehensive manner in an independent section, which will be the first section of this chapter, followed by a discussion in the next two sections, and upon Allah we rely.
