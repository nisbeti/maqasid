He said: "If the clarification is achieved through speech and action that corresponds to the speech, then it is the ultimate in clarification" (1). In purification (taharah), for example, and in prayer (salah), fasting (sawm), pilgrimage (hajj), and other acts of worship and customs, speech does not convey the manner as action does, just as action does not convey what speech conveys in terms of generality or specificity, or in terms of recommendation, obligation, or permissibility (2). He said: "Speech clarifies generality and specificity in circumstances, times, and persons, for speech has forms that necessitate these matters and similar ones, while action is confined to its doer and his state and does not extend beyond its place at all" (3).
Action, unlike speech, indicates absolute permission of obligation, recommendation, or permissibility, and does not include dislike, because dislike falls under prohibition, and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) does not do what is prohibited. Action is distinguished from speech in that it is more eloquent in demonstrating emulation and compliance (4). He said: "Absolute permission includes the obligatory, the recommended, and the permissible; thus, his actions (peace be upon him) do not fall outside of that; they are either obligatory, recommended, or permissible" (5).
