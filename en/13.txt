During this time, one of the kings of the taifa states emerged, namely Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Hud, who was titled Amir al-Muslimin Sif al-Dawla, the one who relies on Allah. He controlled a number of important Andalusian cities, including Cordoba and Granada. In the year 635 AH - 1238 CE, Ibn Hud passed away, and Abu Abdullah Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Nasr, known as Ibn al-Ahmar (1), appeared, the founder of the Nasrid dynasty, and he declared himself the Amir of southern Andalusia, making Granada the capital of his state. This was in the year 635 AH - 1238 CE (2).
The era of Muhammad ibn al-Ahmar was characterized by the strengthening of his state and the growth of its wealth. The Nasrids stood against the armies of the northern Christians, repelling them with determination and faith. Granada became the center of the Muslims and their major stronghold in Andalusia after the kingdoms and states fell before the attacks of the Christian kings who united to eradicate Islam. The Muslims were expelled from their lands and paid tribute to the infidels, yet the Islamic presence in Andalusia continued for more than two and a half centuries after Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Nasr (Muhammad ibn al-Ahmar), nicknamed al-Shaykh al-Ghalib bi-Allah, managed to establish and strengthen his state in southern Andalusia (3).
