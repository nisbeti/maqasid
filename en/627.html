The objectives of the Legislator (al-Shari) are broader than the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), as the reasoning behind God's actions means that the Legislator has an objective in every action He performs. He has objectives in the creation of the heavens and the earth and in the creation of man, and these are objectives of the Legislator, not objectives of the Sharia. Among the objectives of the Legislator are also His objectives regarding how to understand the discourse, meaning the principles of understanding the meanings of words and structures, and these are not objectives of the Sharia. Therefore, the objectives in His view are of different types, and all of them are only considered valid if established through induction.

Objectives:

The objectives (maqasid) are of two types: the objective of the legislator (al-shari) and the objective of the obligated person (al-mukallaf). The objective of the legislator has four types. He said: "The objectives that are considered are of two types: one relates to the objective of the legislator and the other relates to the objective of the obligated person. The first is considered from the perspective of the legislator's intention in establishing the Sharia (law) from the outset, and from the perspective of his intention in establishing it for understanding, and from the perspective of his intention in establishing it for obligation according to it, and from the perspective of his intention in bringing the obligated person under its ruling. These are four types."(1)

The first type is the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), which are the necessary, need-based, and enhancement-related interests, along with what is supplementary and complementary to them(2). The second type pertains to the foundational and linguistic rules relied upon to understand the implications of the legal text. The third type is to clarify that the legislator did not intend hardship with the obligation, even though it may be a consequence of the obligation, and to distinguish between the hardships that the legislator intended to alleviate and those that the legislator did not intend to alleviate. The fourth type is that one of the objectives of the legislator is the servitude of the servants and their submission to the command of the legislator, and to divert them from the inclinations of their desires, even if this contradicts what they perceive to be in their interest.

However, two new matters stand out and should be noted regarding al-Shatibi that differ from other scholars of usul (usul al-fiqh) who argue for the justification of rulings based on public interest (maslaha) or the pursuit of benefits and the prevention of harms. These are his understanding of public interest and harm, as well as the meaning of justifying the Sharia (Sharia) based on public interest.
