The objectives of the Legislator (al-Shari) are broader than the objectives of the Sharia (al-Sharia), as the reasoning behind God's actions means that the Legislator has an objective in every action He performs. He has objectives in the creation of the heavens and the earth and in the creation of man, and these are objectives of the Legislator, not objectives of the Sharia. Among the objectives of the Legislator are also His objectives in how to understand the discourse, meaning the principles of understanding the meanings of words and structures, and these are not objectives of the Sharia. Therefore, the objectives in his view are of different types, and all of them are not considered unless established through induction.

Objectives:

The objectives are of two types: the objective of the Legislator and the objective of the obligated person (mukallaf). The objective of the Legislator is of four types. He said: "The objectives that are considered are of two categories: one relates to the objective of the Legislator and the other relates to the objective of the obligated person. The first is considered from the perspective of the Legislator's intention in establishing the Sharia from the outset, from the perspective of His intention in establishing it for understanding, from the perspective of His intention in establishing it for obligation according to it, and from the perspective of His intention in bringing the obligated person under its rule. These are four types" (1).

As for the first type, it is the objectives of the Sharia, which are the necessary, need-based, and enhancement-related interests, along with what is supplementary and complementary to them (2). The second type pertains to the linguistic principles and rules relied upon to understand the meaning of the legal text. The third type clarifies that the Legislator did not intend hardship in the obligation, even if it is a necessity of the obligation, and distinguishes between the hardships that the Legislator intended to alleviate and those that He did not intend to alleviate. The fourth type is that among the objectives of the Legislator is the servitude of the worshippers and their submission to the command of the Legislator, removing them from the inclinations of their desires, even if this contradicts what they perceive as interests.

However, what is noteworthy, and should be pointed out, are two new matters in al-Shatibi's thought that differ from other scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) who argue for the reasoning of rulings based on interests or the pursuit of interests and the prevention of harms, which are his understanding of interest (maslaha) and harm (mafsada) and the meaning of reasoning the Sharia with interest.
