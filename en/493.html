As for the approval of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) regarding an action, it can be understood that the action may be obligatory, recommended, or permissible. The permissible (mubah) may be permissible in part while being required in total, or it may be permissible in part while being prohibited in total. In this case, if it is prohibited in total, it is permissible in the sense that there is no harm in it. This meaning has been clarified in the third chapter of this book. Therefore, approval does not indicate absolute permissibility but rather indicates that there is no harm in the action. He said: "In summary, the approval itself does not indicate absolute permissibility without consideration" (1). He also said: "As for approval, it is understood to mean that there is no harm in the action that he (peace and blessings be upon him) saw and approved of, or heard about and approved of" (2). He said: "What is not harmful is a category that includes types: the obligatory, the recommended, and the permissible in the sense of being allowed and in the sense of there being no harm in it. As for the disliked (makruh), it is not included in this context, because his silence about it indicates its permissibility, equating the action with abstention, and the disliked action cannot be treated that way because the disliked action is prohibited" (3).

Accordingly, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) does not do what is disliked nor does he approve of what is disliked. He may approve of an action and do it, which represents the highest level of emulation. He said: "His approval (peace and blessings be upon him) when it coincides with the action is valid in emulation, without any blemish or degradation from the highest levels of emulation" (4). He may approve of an action without doing it; his approval indicates that the action falls within the four types mentioned above, and his not doing it negates its being obligatory, recommended, or permissible in the sense of being allowed. It remains that it is permissible with no harm in it, and he does not do it because he (peace and blessings be upon him) is at the highest levels of following and emulation. Al-Shatibi said: "An example of this is his turning away from listening to entertainment, even though it is permissible, and his distance from indulging in it, even if he does not feel any harm in using it" (5).
