It should be noted that taking an appropriate description or public interest (maslaha) as a reason for a ruling is a form of reasoning based on indicators that have not been proven to be a cause. Therefore, reasoning based on them is negated if a more appropriate description is found, or if it does not apply consistently. Al-Shatibi said: "The purpose of establishing laws is for the interests of the servants in both the immediate and the ultimate, and this is a claim that must be substantiated as true or false, and this is not the place for that, and there has been disagreement about it in the science of theology" (1). He then said: "When it became necessary in the science of usul al-fiqh to establish reasons for rulings specifically, they were established as indicators, and there is no need to delve into this matter" (2). Therefore, these interests or reasons are understood as indicators of knowledge, not as causes, and thus they are not suitable for analogy and reasoning based on them. Consequently, those who reasoned with them aimed to establish the principle of reasoning, as it then becomes akin to a cause and is useful in analogy. However, al-Shatibi did not suffice with the establishment of the principle of reasoning, which he affirmed through induction; he also stipulated that induction must apply to the reason itself or the indicator itself, thus making this reason substantiated in its own right, which is a condition of the Hanafis, making it a cause for legislation and a basis for it. This will become clear during the explanation and elaboration of the concept of objectives (maqasid) according to him.
The meaning of objectives (maqasid) according to al-Shatibi:
Objectives linguistically are the plural of maqsad, which is the thing that is intended. He said in Lisan al-Arab: "Ibn Jinni said: The root of (q-s-d) and its usages in the speech of the Arabs are determination and direction, and moving towards something in a straight manner, whether that is just or unjust. This is its essence in reality, even if it may be specified in some contexts to mean the intention of uprightness without deviation. Do you not see that you sometimes intend injustice just as you intend justice at other times? Thus, determination and direction encompass both" (3).
And he said in al-Qamus al-Muhit: "Al-qasd: the straightness of the path, reliance, and intention; he intends it, and to it, he aims" (4).
