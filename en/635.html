And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Indeed, those who have differed concerning it are in doubt about it; they have no knowledge of it except the following of assumption.} (1). And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {And if you obey most of those upon the earth, they will mislead you from the way of Allah. They follow not except assumption, and they are not but falsifying.} (2). And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Say, "Do you have any knowledge that you can produce for us? You follow not except assumption, and you are not but falsifying."} (3). And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Are you disputing with me concerning names you have named, you and your forefathers, for which Allah has not sent down any authority?} (4). And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Say, "Produce your proof, if you should be among the truthful."} (5). These verses and others are explicit in condemning assumption and in condemning the belief and faith in things without authority or proof, and the authority and proof is the decisive evidence, for if it is based on assumption, it is condemned. However, this applies to beliefs and not to legal rulings, as it has been established in the latter— as will be clarified— that it is permissible to follow assumption based on certainty.

The principles that are related to beliefs are the general sources of jurisprudence, for if someone claims that legal obligations are derived from such-and-such person or from such-and-such book or from any source without a decisive proof for that, this is following assumption, which is condemned. This leads to the permissibility of deriving legislations from visions and dreams if there is an assumption that they are good visions for the righteous, and it may lead to the permissibility of deriving legislations from the Torah and the Gospel if there is an assumption regarding some of their texts that they are not altered. All of these are general sources, but these are assumptions, and no decisive proof has been provided to validate these assumptions, and therefore they are rejected.
