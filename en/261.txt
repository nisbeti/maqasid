Thus, it becomes clear that the subject of the fifth issue is consistent and aligned with what is mentioned in the eighth issue, and it is indeed a prelude to it. This is confirmed by al-Shatibi's explanation of how to prioritize and weigh between the two sides where interests and harms conflict in terms of existence, as he states: "In summary, whenever the evidence contradicts, it must be that the two sides are either equal or one is preferred over the other. If they are equal, then there is no ruling from the perspective of the obligated person regarding one side over the other if the equality is evident according to the evidence. Perhaps this does not occur in the Sharia, and if it is assumed to occur, there is no preference except by desire without evidence, and this in matters of the Sharia is invalid by consensus." (1)
As for the eighth issue, it is in harmony with the fifth issue; however, Dr. Daraz's understanding of interests and harms as benefits and detriments, and that they are such according to custom and tradition and based on the views of rational individuals, led him to see an irreconcilable contradiction. Consequently, he made the following comments: "This alone does not benefit after considering previously that what predominates in the direction of benefit is the interest, and what is preferred in the direction of harm is the detriment." He also said: "If the basis of the argument is that rational individuals agree that what is considered is the greater matter, which is the direction of interest that is the foundation of religion and worldly affairs, regardless of the desires of souls, then this can be valid as evidence, but there is no need to mediate the previous premises before it." This indicates that he did not grasp al-Shatibi's intent regarding the fifth issue.
