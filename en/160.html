As for Professor Allal al-Fassi, he said: "The intended meaning of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) is the purpose behind it and the secrets that the legislator has placed with each of its rulings" (1).

Dr. Ahmed al-Raisuni referred to these definitions and others, stating: "Based on the various usages and statements found among the scholars who have discussed the topic of objectives, it can be said that the objectives of the Sharia are the goals that the Sharia has set to achieve for the benefit of the servants" (2).

The Saudi writer Khalid bin Saleh al-Saif referred to these definitions in an article he began with the question: What are the objectives? (3).

Dr. Hamadi al-Abidi points to the definition of Sheikh Muhammad al-Tahir bin Ashour, saying: "This is not, in fact, a definition of the objectives because definitions do not take this form; rather, it is a clarification and elaboration of the areas where the objectives of the Sharia are sought. It is more appropriate to limit the definition to saying: 'The objectives are the intended rulings of the legislator in all circumstances of legislation'" (4).

In reality, all these definitions fall short of conveying the intended meaning. This is because the meaning of objectives according to al-Shatibi differs from its meaning according to others who preceded him or from contemporary scholars discussing the objectives. All these definitions, if they are correct, are only valid in the sense that exists in the minds of the definers. They are all far from accurately reflecting al-Shatibi's intent.
