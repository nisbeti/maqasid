As for what some rely on, such as the saying that "differing is a mercy," and what is narrated about the caliph Umar ibn Abdul Aziz that he said: "I would not have liked that the companions of the Messenger of Allah did not differ," and the interpretation of this as indicating the permissibility of choosing between the sayings of the companions and the sayings of the imams, al-Shatibi refutes this meaning and clarifies that the meaning is that if the companions (may Allah be pleased with them) had not differed and their opinion had been one on every issue, it would indicate the prohibition of differing and consequently the prohibition of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning). Thus, the door to ijtihad was opened after them; the breadth and mercy in the differing of the companions lies in the opening of the door to ijtihad and in the fact that the mujtahid (jurist) acts according to what he believes is correct, and among his actions is his fatwa (legal opinion). After mentioning the opinions of some scholars that support his view, he said: "If it were assumed that the companions did not consider these ambiguous subsidiary issues and did not speak about them, while they are the role models in understanding the Sharia and acting according to its objectives, then those who came after them would not have the right to open that door" (1). He then said: "The field would have narrowed for those after the companions, but when they exercised ijtihad and the differing that arose from their striving for correctness made it easier for those who came after them to follow the path. Therefore, Umar ibn Abdul Aziz - and Allah knows best - said: 'I would not be pleased to have the differing of them equivalent to the red camels,' and he said: 'I would not have liked that the companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not differ'" (2). It is narrated from Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) that he said: "There is no breadth in the differing of the companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), rather the truth is in one" (3).
