8- That a single ruling should not have two causes simultaneously in one scenario. Al-Amidi said: "There is a disagreement regarding the permissibility of attributing a single ruling in one scenario to two causes simultaneously; some have prohibited this absolutely... while others have permitted it absolutely... and some have differentiated between the stated and inferred causes, permitting it in the stated causes and prohibiting it in the inferred ones... The preferred view is that of the first opinion" (1).
And al-Zarkashi said: "It is permissible to attribute a single ruling to different types based on different causes for one person by consensus, such as attributing the permissibility of killing Zayd for his apostasy, and Amr for retribution, and Khalid for adultery" (2). He also said: "The Prophet explicitly stated different causes, each of which is independent in permitting blood, as he - peace and blessings be upon him - said: 'The blood of a Muslim is not lawful except in one of three cases: disbelief after faith, or adultery after chastity, or killing a believer unjustly'" (3) (4).
It appears from the texts of the imams that there is a prohibition against attributing a ruling to two causes, and the consensus reported, as mentioned by al-Zarkashi, does not pertain to the cause by which analogy (qiyas) is established. The causes mentioned in the hadith are all limited causes, so a branch cannot be analogized to a root through them, and the application of the ruling in any case is based on the evidence of the text and not on analogy. Or the attribution may be understood in the sense of an indicator rather than a motivator. Al-Zarkashi, when presenting the differing opinions regarding attributing more than one cause, said: "The permissibility is absolute, which is the correct view and the view of the majority, as the judge said in al-Taqrib, and then he said: 'And based on this, we say, considering that the causes are signs and indicators of the rulings, not causes that necessitate them'" (5).
