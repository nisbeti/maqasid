As for their statement that the appropriate description or inference leads to conjecture, and that conjecture is applicable in legal matters, they also rejected it. Al-Bazdawi said: "As for imagination, it is a false matter because it is mere conjecture without any reality, and it is internal and does not serve as evidence against the opponent nor as a legal proof" (1).

He also said in Kashf al-Asrar: "As for imagination... it is a false matter because it is merely a form of conjecture, as imagination and conjecture are one and the same, and conjecture does not provide anything of truth. It cannot be said that conjecture is considered in the law in terms of the obligation to act upon it like the report of a single narrator or analogy, for we say that what is considered is that which has a definitive proof for its obligation to act upon it, not mere conjecture, and there is no proof here for its consideration in the law, thus it must be disregarded" (2).

Al-Ansari said: "Contrary to the Hanafis, who do not accept imagination at all, because although it may lead to conjecture, it is not binding according to what the legislator has established... And if it does not lead to the conjecture of the legislator's consideration, it is not a legal proof; rather, it is merely a product of the mind and thus not considered. Perhaps this is what al-Nasafi meant, that it is mere conjecture, meaning it is not the conjecture of the legislator's consideration, so it does not provide anything of truth. If you say: 'Imagination certainly leads to conjecture and consensus has been established on the consideration of conjecture,' he said: 'The consensus on acting upon conjecture is only on the condition that it is - that is, conjecture - legal and derived from the law, and a conjecture for the consideration of the legislator'" (3). He also said, quoting Imam al-Nasafi: "The fact that the description is imaginative, meaning it evokes in the heart the imagination of correctness: it does not provide anything of truth, and its utmost is to be like inspiration. It is not a valid proof" (4).
