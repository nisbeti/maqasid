If one intends to comply without considering what worldly benefits may come from it, then that is the purest and highest of deeds. Nevertheless, his benefits will still be attained within that. If he considers his benefits and intends them in his action, but he aims for compliance and does not seek his benefits except through the lawful means, then his action is legitimate, and there is no doubt about its validity. He says: "An action that aligns with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) is either in accordance with the original objectives or the secondary objectives" (1). He also said: "If it aligns with the requirements of the original objectives in such a way that they are taken into account in the action, there is no doubt about its validity and soundness, whether it is free from personal gain or whether personal gain is considered, because it corresponds to the intention of the Legislator in the original legislation" (2).
He said: "The primary objective, when sought by the obligated person, includes the intention of everything the Legislator intended in the action, whether it is to achieve a benefit or to avert harm. The one acting upon it intends to fulfill the command of the Legislator, either after understanding what was intended or merely out of obedience to the command. In any case, he is intending what the Legislator intended. If it is established that the intention of the Legislator is the most comprehensive and foremost of objectives, and that it is pure light untainted by ulterior motives or personal gain, then the one receiving it in this manner is taking it in a pure, complete, and untainted manner, not falling short of the Legislator's intent... As for the secondary intention, it does not entail all of that, because the one who takes the command and prohibition with the intention of personal gain has limited his intention to that gain, thus restricting its generality and failing to rise to the level of the first" (3). Therefore, he says: "When actions are based on the original objectives, obedience becomes greater, and if they are opposed, disobedience becomes greater" (4).
