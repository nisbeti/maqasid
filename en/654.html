The rising and setting of the sun and the moon, and their repeated occurrence in a regular manner, leads to the feeling that there is a system governing this movement, which is what causes it to recur regularly and without fail. This system serves as the cause for the occurrence of this regular movement. This cause or system is what prompts the ruling tomorrow or the day after, based on the perceived system. Since the cause present today will also be present tomorrow, the matter will repeat. If it were known that the one who established this system would change it tomorrow, and that the sun would not rise or would rise from the west, the ruling would change, which confirms that the generalization and its application to what has not been examined is not due to induction but rather to the cause. Two points affirm this interpretation:

First: The examined particulars are very few and hardly worth mentioning compared to what has not been examined; it suffices for the examined to be a single individual or one particular so that the desired description or ruling can be understood from it, and then the cause (the logical analogy) thereafter becomes the reason for the general ruling to be applied to the rest. Therefore, the cause is the reason for the generality, not induction.

__________

(1) The concept of "cause" here refers to the underlying principles that govern phenomena.

(2) The term "induction" refers to the method of reasoning from specific instances to general principles.
