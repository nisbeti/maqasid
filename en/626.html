The issue of justifying rulings through wisdom, interests, and appropriate descriptions has been the subject of considerable debate among theologians. This issue was introduced into the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) due to its impact on the discussions regarding the pathways of causation, which are the non-prescriptive pathways (1). Al-Shatibi came into this fray to establish a definitive principle, which is that the actions and rulings of Allah, Exalted is He, have purposes, and that He does not act except for a purpose He intends. He aimed to respond to those who rejected this justification, particularly Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, who himself firmly denied the notion that the actions of Allah are justified, while maintaining the claim of justifying rulings and through analogy. Al-Shatibi said: "The establishment of the laws is for the interests of the servants in both the immediate and the ultimate. This is a claim that must be substantiated as true or false, and this is not the place for that. Disagreement has arisen regarding it in the science of theology. Al-Razi claimed that the rulings of Allah are not justified by any cause whatsoever, just as His actions are not. And the Mu'tazila agreed that His rulings are justified by considering the interests of the servants, and this is the choice of most later jurists" (2). He then said: "What is relied upon is that we have inferred from the Sharia that it was established for the interests of the servants through an inference that al-Razi and others do not dispute" (3).

Thus, the justification of the Sharia by the interests of the servants, according to him, can only be achieved through inference, which differs from the reasoning of the earlier scholars who advocated for this justification (4).
