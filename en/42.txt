Then, after Imam al-Shafi'i, works in this science continued to emerge, and some disagreed with him on the principles, while others disagreed with him on the branches and related matters without disagreement on the principles themselves. Some agreed with him and followed him in his principles and branches and what relates to them. The principles referred to here are the sources of legislation or its general proofs, which are the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy (qiyas). Sheikh Muhammad Abu Zahra states: "In truth, the jurists of the four schools did not disagree with al-Shafi'i regarding the proofs he established, which are the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy. These principles are agreed upon, and any additions to them are a matter of disagreement between him and most of them" (1).
As for those who opposed him, they are the Zahirites and the Shi'a. The Zahirites reject all forms of analogy (2) and rely only on the apparent meanings of texts, while the Shi'a have a significant disagreement as they consider the sayings of the Imams to be a legal proof akin to the Book and the Sunnah. The words of the Imams, in their view, specify the legal texts (3).
As for the Hanafis, Malikis, and Hanbalis, their principles align with those of al-Shafi'i, albeit with differences in details (4). Those who followed al-Shafi'i in his principles and details, and embraced his opinions, are his followers who were active in the science of usul al-fiqh and produced numerous works in it.
